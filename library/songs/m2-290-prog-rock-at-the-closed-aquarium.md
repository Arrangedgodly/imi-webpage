# Prog Rock at the Closed Aquarium

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: progressive rock's shifting meters, long suites, and elaborate instrumental sections that outlast the event they describe*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Start in 7/8, switch to 5/4 for the aquarium alarm, then build a grand finale that stops when the doors close.*

**Verse 1 (7/8)**
At seven, the aquarium doors are locked for the night.  
A band of macaques arrives with a keyboard full of light.  
The ticket says the concert starts at six, the date says Friday.  
The manager points at the sign: "Closed on every Monday."  
They launch a suite in seven beats, the lobby starts to sway.  
A fish tank's bubble pump keeps time and steals the downbeat away.  

**Chorus (5/4)**
Turn the tide, turn the dial, let the reef be our stage.  
Long guitar, longer bridge, one more instrumental page.  
The doors are shut, the tanks are dim, the clock has lost its hands;  
We play the scale that spans the sea and never leaves the stands.  

**Verse 2 (7/8)**
The keyboardist builds a coral chord; the drummer shifts the pace.  
The singer names each jellyfish and gives them all a place.  
The maintenance crew is mopping up; they coil the cable line.  
The band adds twenty bars of rain and calls the floor sublime.  
A turtle turns its back to them and glides behind a rock.  
The bassist counts a coda; the turtle checks the clock.  

**Chorus (5/4)**
Turn the tide, turn the dial, let the reef be our stage.  
Long guitar, longer bridge, one more instrumental page.  
The doors are shut, the tanks are dim, the clock has lost its hands;  
We play the scale that spans the sea and never leaves the stands.  

**Bridge (free time)**
The manager taps the exit sign; the band begins a solo.  
The cleaner moves the wet-floor board; it becomes a rolling halo.  
They follow it through the lobby, instruments in a train.  
The final note rings out beyond the aquarium again.  

**Ending**
The last chord fades; the crew locks up and turns the hallway light.  
The band asks for a second set; the tanks are closed for night.  
A bubble rises in the dark and pops against the glass.  
The drummer counts it as one beat; the band has time to pass.
