# The Cabaret Singer Books the Barber

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: theatrical cabaret confessionals and singers whose dramatic reinvention is chiefly a routine haircut*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-013*
*Performance note: Piano, spoken asides and theatrical pauses; the barber answers in plain short sentences.*

[Opening Recitative]
Tonight I leave the old self in the shadow of the square.  
Tonight a new identity shall rise from what was there.  
The barber looks at me and asks how much I'd like to lose.  
I tell him there are many selves from which I have to choose.  
He says, "Around the ears?"  
I whisper, "All my wasted years."  
He reaches for a towel.  
I give a small and tragic howl.  

[Refrain]
Change me, rearrange me,  
Leave the things that still explain me.  
Cut away the long regret,  
The barber asks how short I want to get.  
Mirror, let the old world fall,  
He needs a length before he changes anything at all.  

[Verse]
I show him photographs of heroes with their hair blown high,  
He shows me four quite ordinary styles for me to try.  
I choose the second picture with a tidy little fringe,  
He puts the cape around my neck and checks the chair's small hinge.  
The scissors make a rhythm as I speak about my past,  
He asks me to keep still so that the haircut might go fast.  
The little fringe grows shorter while the rest takes tidy shape,  
I speak of liberation underneath a fastening cape.  

[Refrain]
Change me, rearrange me,  
Leave the things that still explain me.  
Cut away the long regret,  
The barber asks how short I want to get.  
Mirror, let the old world fall,  
He needs a length before he changes anything at all.  

[Spoken Interlude]
He holds a second mirror up behind my little head.  
"That's what the back looks like," is all the barber said.  
I turn to see a person with a little less of hair.  
My eyebrows and my old regrets are absolutely there.  

[Final Refrain]
Change me, rearrange me,  
Those who knew my fringe might fail to name me.  
Cut away the long regret,  
He matches my old photograph; I owe him no regret.  
Mirror, let the old world fall,  
I pay the posted price and tip for listening to it all.  

[Coda]
I leave a new identity beneath the street's old light.  
The barber sweeps the old one to the dustpan for the night.  
He writes my next appointment underneath a simple name,  
Six weeks until the reinvention reaches him again.
