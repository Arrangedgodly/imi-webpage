# The Courtly Dance Buys a Checkout Bag

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: courtly dance songs and formal performers attempting elaborate manners at an ordinary checkout*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-014*
*Performance note: Measured recorder-like melody, light hand drum and polite ensemble voices; the cashier interrupts the bows.*

[Verse 1]
We practiced courtly dancing in the town's old costume hall,  
Our sleeves were long and elegant, our gestures rather tall.  
We stopped to buy some oranges before the evening show,  
The checkout had a narrow lane with room for little flow.  
I bowed to greet the cashier with my feathered cap in hand,  
My partner turned a full circle as the dancers had all planned.  
The customer behind us held a basket full of bread,  
The cashier asked us kindly if we'd pay for fruit instead.  

[Refrain]
Bow low, turn slow, let the fine sleeves fall,  
The checkout isn't built to hold a courtly dance for all.  
Bow low, turn slow, greet the little day,  
The cashier has a question that the bow cannot convey.  
Graceful step, gentle hand, let the small bells sway,  
Would we like a checkout bag, and how would we like to pay?  

[Verse 2]
I gave a shorter bow and said a paper bag was fine,  
My partner paid the total without adding any line.  
We packed the little oranges and moved beyond the till,  
The customer could buy his bread with all the dancers still.  
We took the bags to practice and we set them by the door,  
The courtly steps had room enough upon the wooden floor.  
The cashier came that evening with the customer for the show,  
They watched us use a whole wide stage for every turning bow.  

[Refrain]
Bow low, turn slow, let the fine sleeves fall,  
The checkout isn't built to hold a courtly dance for all.  
Bow low, turn slow, greet the little day,  
The cashier has a question that the bow cannot convey.  
Graceful step, gentle hand, let the small bells sway,  
Would we like a checkout bag, and how would we like to pay?  

[Bridge]
We thanked the cashier afterward and offered her some fruit,  
She asked if all the grand bows could be done in a smaller suit.  
We taught her one small greeting with the feathered cap held low,  
She said she'd try it at the till when queues were moving slow.  

[Final Refrain]
Bow low, turn slow, greet the little day,  
The cashier says the bag costs extra in a most courtly way.  
Graceful step, gentle hand, let the small bells sway,  
The customer applauds her bow, then uses his card to pay.  
Bow low, turn slow, let the fine sleeves fall,  
The paper bags have brought more useful grace than sleeves to all.
