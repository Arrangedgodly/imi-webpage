# Ballad of the Failed Signup

*Format: Song parody*  
*Parody target: protest folk songs and earnest public-square choruses*  
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*  
*Batch: IMI-M1*

## Verse 1
We gathered at the square with clipboards high.  
The council planned a signup form by Friday.  
The website asked our birthdays, then our shoe size.  
The password box rejected honest replies.  
We brought a banjo, thermos, and a sign.  
The banner read, "Let neighbors stand in line."  
The mayor said the printer needed rest.  
We sang the little grievance from our chest.  

## Chorus
Write my name where everyone can see.  
Let a troopmate save the list for me.  
We came to join the garden club today.  
We shall not be bounced by fields of gray.  

## Verse 2
Old Mr. Bell could not recall his login.  
The system said his surname needed hyphen.  
He brought the seed packets for the plot.  
The form demanded he describe each pot.  
Young Nina tried to help him with the mouse.  
The popup opened windows through the house.  
The help desk played a loop of elevator strings.  
The crowd began to hum about these things.  

## Chorus
Write my name where everyone can see.  
Let a troopmate save the list for me.  
We came to join the garden club today.  
We shall not be bounced by fields of gray.  

## Bridge
The clerk unlocked the side door by the stairs.  
She brought a paper sheet and folding chairs.  
We passed the pencil, one hand to the next.  
No field appeared to ask us to be vexed.  
We wrote our names and marked the garden beds.  
The banjo found a tune beneath our heads.  
The mayor took the laptop off its stand.  
He signed the sheet with his own hand.  

## Final chorus
Write our names where everyone can see.  
Let a person keep the list with care.  
We came to plant some beans and marigolds.  
We leave with dirt beneath our fingernails.  
Write our names; the paper has room.  
Save one square for basil when it blooms.  
The form can rest until it learns our names.  
The seeds will grow without its games.  

## Ending
By spring, the rows were green and straight.  
The list was pinned beside the gate.  
The banjo rested by the shed.  
The beans climbed higher than we said.  

*Performance note: Keep the melody easy to learn, with a chorus the crowd can sing after hearing it once.*
