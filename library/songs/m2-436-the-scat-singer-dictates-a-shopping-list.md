# The Scat Singer Dictates a Shopping List

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: virtuoso jazz scat vocals and singers who cannot stop improvising long enough for speech recognition to understand a simple list*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-011*
*Performance note: Swinging piano and upright bass; sing original nonsense syllables between real grocery items, then let a dry voice read the resulting list.*

[Verse 1]
I had a tune inside my head while checking what to buy,  
The phone was taking notes for me beneath a kitchen light.  
I sang "Milk, ba-da-lem, and bread, da-doo-da-day,"  
The phone wrote "Mill battle them and red a holiday."  
I added eggs on one high note and butter on a fall,  
The phone recorded "X" and "better" halfway down the wall.  
My partner heard the melody and joined my little beat,  
The shopping list grew longer than the things we planned to eat.  

[Chorus]
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, tell it what we need,  
The phone can hear the music but the words are hard to read.  
Shoo-ba-doo, milk and bread, put it on the line,  
There is nothing in the pantry called a better X of mine.  
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, let the chorus fly,  
We have got a whole jazz number where the groceries should lie.  

[Verse 2]
My partner took the phone and read the list aloud quite plain,  
It asked for "Seven velvet moons" instead of grain.  
I said the transcription captured quite a lovely state of mind,  
She said it wouldn't help her find the things she had to buy.  
We cleared the notes and spoke each item without any tune,  
The phone wrote milk and bread and eggs; the list was finished soon.  
I put the nonsense on a sheet beside the piano stand,  
Its syllables were excellent for rehearsing with the band.  

[Chorus]
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, tell it what we need,  
The phone can hear the music but the words are hard to read.  
Shoo-ba-doo, milk and bread, put it on the line,  
There is nothing in the pantry called a better X of mine.  
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, let the chorus fly,  
We have got a whole jazz number where the groceries should lie.  

[Bridge]
That evening we performed the notes the phone had once misheard,  
The audience applauded every gloriously mistaken word.  
My partner brought the groceries and set them by the door,  
The phone asked if we'd like to buy a velvet moon once more.  

[Final Chorus]
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, tell it what we need,  
The phone has got a memory that nobody can feed.  
Shoo-ba-doo, milk and bread, now the list is clear,  
The shopping has a spoken voice; the singing happens here.  
Ba-da-lem, doo-da-day, let the chorus fly,  
Our bassist asks for better X and points toward the sky.
