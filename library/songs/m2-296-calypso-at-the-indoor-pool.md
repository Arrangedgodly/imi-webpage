# Calypso at the Indoor Pool

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: calypso's buoyant call-and-response and resort performers who narrate the entire holiday*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Bright steel-pan pulse; the lifeguard call-and-response should cut through the vacation patter.*

**Verse 1**
The pool band's on the shallow end, the ceiling's painted blue.  
The singer wears a flower shirt and tells the guests what to do.  
"Come swim beneath the tropic moon, though it is half past three."  
The lifeguard blows a whistle: "Walk. No running by the sea."  
The steel pan answers every rule with one bright ringing note.  
A guest in flippers waves along and paddles on the float.  

**Chorus**
Hey, calypso, count the strokes and mind the lane;  
Hey, calypso, don't dive twice into the drain.  
Call the tide, call the time, call the guests to shore;  
The pool is closing, but the singer wants one more.  

**Verse 2**
The band describes a coral reef behind the locker door.  
The actual pool has painted tiles and one wet towel on the floor.  
The singer calls, "A sea turtle glides beneath your chair."  
A child points at the turtle sticker on the lifeguard's spare.  
The lifeguard says, "That's on my case; please do not feed the art."  
The drummer taps a gentle beat; the swimmers make a start.  

**Chorus**
Hey, calypso, count the strokes and mind the lane;  
Hey, calypso, don't dive twice into the drain.  
Call the tide, call the time, call the guests to shore;  
The pool is closing, but the singer wants one more.  

**Bridge**
The final whistle sounds; the guests climb from the pool.  
The band plays them to the towels, the room grows calm and cool.  
The singer folds his flower shirt and checks the empty lane.  
The steel pan's last note rings once, then dries like summer rain.  

**Ending**
The lifeguard closes both the gates; the lights go dim and low.  
The singer bows to empty chairs; the pans stop their glow.  
A drip falls from the diving board and lands exactly on the beat.  
The band calls it encore; the lifeguard sweeps the street.
