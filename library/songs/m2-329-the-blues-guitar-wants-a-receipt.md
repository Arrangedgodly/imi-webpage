# The Blues Guitar Wants a Receipt

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: twelve-bar blues tales of loss reduced to a shop's return policy*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-009*
*Performance note: Slow twelve-bar blues; guitar answers each sung complaint with one increasingly expensive bend.*

[Verse 1]
I took my broken guitar to the shop beside the track,  
I took my broken guitar, said I'd like my money back.  
The clerk laid down his sandwich, said the proof was in the sack.  
I searched my coat for paper, found a ticket and a comb,  
I searched my coat for paper, found the key to my old home.  
But the little strip of purchase was the one that chose to roam.  

[Chorus]
Oh, receipt, where did you go?  
You were curled up in my pocket just a little while ago.  
My guitar has lost its voice, my wallet knows the tune,  
The refund train is leaving at a quarter after noon.  

[Verse 2]
I played the clerk my heartbreak on the one remaining string,  
I played the clerk my heartbreak; he said pain proves not a thing.  
He needed date and price before the cash drawer bell could ring.  
I brought a friend to witness when I bought it on that day,  
I brought a friend to witness; he remembered what I'd pay.  
He couldn't name the guitar, but he hummed the shop's display.  

[Chorus]
Oh, receipt, where did you go?  
You were curled up in my pocket just a little while ago.  
My guitar has lost its voice, my wallet knows the tune,  
The refund train is leaving at a quarter after noon.  

[Bridge]
The clerk looked through his records, found the sale at last,  
He asked how long I'd owned it; I recalled a distant past.  
The guarantee was over by a decade and a spring,  
He offered me a discount on a brand-new second string.  

[Verse 3]
I bought the string reluctantly, the clerk began to smile,  
I bought the string reluctantly, he tore a paper mile.  
He handed me a receipt that reached halfway down the aisle.  

[Final Chorus]
Oh, receipt, don't you stray,  
I'll wrap you round this guitar till the morning turns to day.  
My guitar has found its voice, my wallet knows the tune,  
The paper rattles louder than my solo every afternoon.
