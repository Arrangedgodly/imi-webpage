# The Reggae Gardener Waits for the Rain

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: laid-back reggae gardening advice that keeps finding reasons to postpone watering*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-009*
*Performance note: Offbeat guitar and warm bass; the watering can rings against a fence post during the final chorus.*

[Verse 1]
I planted little beans beside the house before the spring,  
My neighbor brought a watering can and asked a simple thing.  
She asked if they were thirsty; I said patience makes them grow,  
The sky would send its blessing when the clouds were ready so.  
I sat beneath a parasol and played a mellow chord,  
The beans stood in the sunshine looking steadily more bored.  

[Chorus]
Wait for the rain, wait for the sky,  
Let a little cloud come drifting by.  
Roots in the earth and hopes in the blue,  
There's a little job the weather ought to do.  
Wait for the rain, easy and slow,  
My neighbor's beans are twice as tall across the row.  

[Verse 2]
The clouds arrived on Tuesday and I pointed up with pride,  
They crossed beyond the hillside and the garden stayed bone dry.  
I told the beans a story about rivers finding sea,  
My neighbor held the watering can and looked from them to me.  
I said the song would strengthen them; she hummed the chorus through,  
Then tipped her can upon her plants and watched them rise anew.  

[Chorus]
Wait for the rain, wait for the sky,  
Let a little cloud come drifting by.  
Roots in the earth and hopes in the blue,  
There's a little job the weather ought to do.  
Wait for the rain, easy and slow,  
My neighbor's beans are twice as tall across the row.  

[Bridge]
I filled the can at sundown and I walked beside my beans,  
I watered every little root beneath their drooping greens.  
The neighbor kept the rhythm with a tap upon the rail,  
The first raindrop hit my nose as I set down the pail.  

[Final Chorus]
Wait for the rain, wait for the sky,  
Let a little cloud come drifting by.  
Roots in the earth and hopes in the blue,  
I did the little job the weather ought to do.  
Wait for the rain, easy and slow,  
It fills my empty watering can while I dance down the row.
