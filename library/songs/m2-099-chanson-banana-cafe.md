# Chanson at the Banana Cafe

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: French chanson cabaret conventions, intimate storytelling, and a singer whose tiny cafe set keeps being interrupted by real customers*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-003*

*Performance note: Accordion, brushed snare, and close vocal; keep the verses intimate until the cafe interrupts.*

Verse 1
The singer sat beside the window of the Banana Cafe,
She sang about a paper boat that drifted far away.
A waiter crossed behind her with a tray of tea and pears,
The singer gave the boat a line about the cafe stairs.

Chorus
Little boat, sail under the chair,
Past the cup and through the air.
Turn the page and let it go,
A small song finds a place to row.

Verse 2
A capuchin came in to buy a bun for afternoon,
He hummed the melody and tapped a spoon against the tune.
The waiter asked the singer if the table could be cleared,
She added one refrain about the dishes being near.

Chorus
Little boat, sail under the chair,
Past the cup and through the air.
Turn the page and let it go,
A small song finds a place to row.

Bridge
The paper boat was folded from the menu by the door,
It sailed across a puddle to the baker's shoes on floor.
The singer stopped mid-phrase; the waiter lifted it away,
The boat had found its harbor in a saucer meant for tea.

Verse 3
The cafe settled softly; the customers drank their tea,
The singer folded up her song and let the ending be.
The waiter set the menu down; the bun was passed around,
The paper boat remained afloat in one small saucer crown.

Chorus
Little boat, sail under the chair,
Past the cup and through the air.
Turn the page and let it go,
A small song finds a place to row.

Ending
The singer left the cafe as the accordion played low,
The boat stayed by the window, with nowhere else to go.
