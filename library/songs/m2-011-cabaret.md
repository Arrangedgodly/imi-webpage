# The Velvet Queue

*Format: Song parody*  
*Parody target: cabaret patter, smoky piano, and theatrical audience flirtation*  
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*  
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS*

*Performance note: A piano thumps like a heartbeat; the singer shares every joke with the front table.*

## Verse 1
At the Velvet Beetle, curtains smelled of dust.  
A langur in a waistcoat sang because he must.  
The piano player wore a rose behind one ear.  
The singer bowed to every empty chair.  
He praised the mayor's hat, the soup, the weather vane.  
The back row coughed; he turned it into a refrain.  
A waitress balancing five cups stopped in the aisle.  
He tipped his glass and gave her half a smile.  

## Chorus
Take your time, take your seat, take the joke.  
The velvet is old, but the piano's not broke.  
Take your time, let the room lean in.  
A song can start where the night gets thin.  

## Verse 2
A tourist asked if cabaret meant dancing bare.  
He said, "Only if you brought a folding chair."  
The pianist struck a chord that rattled every spoon.  
The waitress spun a tray like Saturn's little moon.  
The singer told a story of his first bad gig.  
He'd played to three bored bats and someone's wig.  
The crowd leaned close, then laughed before the end.  
He let their laughter serve as his old friend.  

## Bridge
A spotlight failed, the stage went dark and still.  
He sang the next verse softly by sheer will.  
The room lit up with matches, phones, and glow.  
He took his final bow beneath their row.  

## Chorus
Take your time, take your seat, take the joke.  
The velvet is old, but the piano's not broke.  
Take your time, let the room lean in.  
A song can start where the night gets thin.  

## Ending
The chairs were stacked, the last red bulb grew dim.  
The singer helped the waitress carry cups with him.
