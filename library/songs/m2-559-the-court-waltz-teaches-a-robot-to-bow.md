# The Court Waltz Teaches a Robot to Bow

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: courtly musical spectacle and performers programming mechanical politeness without considering the room around it*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-014*
*Performance note: Measured three-quarter piano and two voices; the robot announces each gesture with precise spoken counts.*

[Verse 1]
We hired a little dancing robot for the winter ball,  
Its polished metal coat reflected lanterns in the hall.  
The dance teacher gave the bow and asked it to repeat,  
The robot bent at the waist with both feet planted neat.  
Its long hat touched a vase of flowers on the little stand,  
The teacher caught the vase before it left her ordinary hand.  
The robot said the bow was perfect by the measured plan,  
The vase required a shorter bow than such precision ran.  

[Counting Refrain]
One, two, three, bow to the guest,  
The robot gives its manners to the utmost of its best.  
One, two, three, hold the flowers high,  
The hat has got a feather that the vase would like to pass by.  
Step, turn, pause, let the little ballroom glow,  
The robot needs the nearby things included in its bow.  

[Verse 2]
The teacher set a smaller bow for greeting at the door,  
The robot tested it with no hat sweeping near the floor.  
The vase stayed on the little stand with flowers in their place,  
The robot greeted guests with a distinctly measured grace.  
It danced the simple opening with a partner by its side,  
The teacher gave the turns the room that all the guests supplied.  
The hat feather swayed above the dancers in the light,  
The vase had no more meetings with the robot's hat that night.  

[Counting Refrain]
One, two, three, bow to the guest,  
The robot gives its manners to the utmost of its best.  
One, two, three, hold the flowers high,  
The hat has got a feather that the vase would like to pass by.  
Step, turn, pause, let the little ballroom glow,  
The robot needs the nearby things included in its bow.  

[Bridge]
The robot asked why space mattered to each guest in the hall,  
"Leave room for hats and flowers; then the bows can greet them all."  
It wrote a note about the space beside each little stand,  
The teacher said politeness had a use beyond a programmed hand.  

[Final Counting Refrain]
One, two, three, let the little ballroom glow,  
The robot greets the vase as well before its smaller bow.  
Step, turn, pause, hold the flowers high,  
The teacher has a quiet stand that lets the feather pass by.  
One, two, three, bow to the guest,  
The vase receives a greeting card before the robot takes its rest.
