# The Medieval Bard Reads the Parking Map

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: medieval bardic travel ballads and the festival performer finding a designated unloading bay*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-015*
*Performance note: Lute and a measured narrative melody; a parking marshal answers the refrain with one spoken bay number.*

[Verse 1]
I rode into the festival with costumes in my van,  
My lute lay in a padded case beside the folded stand.  
I sang of gates and castles as I reached the market square,  
The marshal waved a printed map and bade me stop right there.  
I told him I had traveled from a kingdom far away,  
He asked if I was booked to play the midday show that day.  
I showed the little artist pass attached beside my name,  
He pointed to the loading bay and marked it with my name.  

[Refrain]
O gate of stone, O road of gold,  
The bard has many stories of the journeys brave and old.  
O gate of stone, O journey's end,  
The map has got a loading bay around the second bend.  
Sing my passage, mark my stay,  
The marshal says, "Unload in twelve, then park in lot A."  

[Verse 2]
I drove around the second bend and stopped in number twelve,  
The porter took the costume box; I carried lute myself.  
I set the lute beside the stage and passed the artist pass,  
The porter pointed to the van still parked beside the grass.  
I moved it to the public lot before the show began,  
I took the footpath back again and left the parked-up van.  
The midday crowd was waiting when I walked up to the stand,  
I sang the tale of gates and kings with my lute in my hand.  

[Refrain]
O gate of stone, O road of gold,  
The bard has many stories of the journeys brave and old.  
O gate of stone, O journey's end,  
The map has got a loading bay around the second bend.  
Sing my passage, mark my stay,  
The marshal says, "Unload in twelve, then park in lot A."  

[Bridge]
The marshal came at lunchtime and he heard the closing verse,  
He said the road to kings and castles sounded much less terse.  
I offered him a ballad for the map he'd kindly drawn,  
He asked me to collect the boxes when the final crowd was gone.  

[Final Refrain]
O gate of stone, O road of gold,  
The bard can tell a loading tale beside the stories old.  
O gate of stone, O journey's end,  
The loaded van departs again beyond the second bend.  
Sing my passage, end my stay,  
The marshal checks the empty bay and puts his map away.
