# The Stamp Has a Backbeat

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: Motown call-and-response, handclap backbeats, and the night-shift factory turning routine work into a chart-ready groove*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-005*
*Performance note: Use tight harmonies and claps; build a full chorus from the stamping machine's rhythm.*

[Verse 1]
At the parcel plant, the night crew clocked in at ten,
A lemur named Jo stamped each box and passed it down the pen.
The machine made a rhythm; the floor made a reply,
And the parcels kept on dancing as the midnight hours went by.

[Chorus]
Stamp that label, clap it twice, send it down the line,
Keep the beat and keep the boxes landing square and fine.
Stamp that label, turn around, hear the rollers sing,
Night shift, hold that groove until the morning bell can ring.


[Verse 2]
The lead said, "Make it faster," and the chorus picked up speed;
A tambourine was missing, so Jo shook a box of seed.
The box marked "fragile teacups" took the solo on the track,
While the actual cups marched past with bubble wrap around their backs.

[Chorus]
Stamp that label, clap it twice, send it down the line,
Keep the beat and keep the boxes landing square and fine.
Stamp that label, turn around, hear the rollers sing,
Night shift, hold that groove until the morning bell can ring.


[Bridge]
The missing shaker surfaced in a coat sleeve by the door;
It rattled when the break bell rang, then rattled even more.
The foreman called for silence, but the rollers hummed along;
Jo stamped the final shipping label right in time with the song.

[Verse 3]
At dawn the crew had shipped the lot and earned a bonus meal;
The mislabeled seed box went to a band who liked its feel.
They planted it behind the loading dock to grow a stage-side tree;
The night shift took their clapping hands and started track number three.

[Final Chorus]
Stamp that label, clap it twice, send it down the line,
Keep the beat and keep the boxes landing square and fine.
Stamp that label, turn around, hear the rollers sing,
Night shift, hold that groove until the morning bell can ring.


[Ending]
The foreman punched out last, still tapping on his lunch tin.
