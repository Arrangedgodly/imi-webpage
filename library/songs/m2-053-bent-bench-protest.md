# Protest Song for the Bent Bench

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: earnest folk-protest anthems, communal choruses, and verses that outgrow the grievance*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-002*

*Performance note: Plain acoustic guitar and handclaps; begin with one complaint, then let the chorus recruit the whole park.*

Verse 1
We gather by the garden where the old bench tilts to west,  
A macaque brings a sign that says, "This seat deserves a rest."  
A robin joins the chorus, then the gardener joins the song,  
The bench has leaned for seven years; we've let it lean too long.  
We sing for every sitter who slid toward the flower bed,  
The mayor takes a folding chair and places it instead.  

Chorus
Oh, straighten up the bench, let the weary sit in line,  
Set the bolts and level legs; the shade will still be fine.  
Raise your voice, raise the wrench, pass the tools around,  
A song can start a movement when the feet are on the ground.  
Oh, straighten up the bench, make a place for two,  
The gardener has the hardware; the mayor has the glue.  

Verse 2
The first bolt turns too easily, the second will not budge,  
The mayor says, "A symbol's more important than a grudge."  
The robin pecks the signpost; the sign falls on his head,  
He keeps on singing underneath, the loudest in the bed.  
A tortoise brings a level and a wrench that's twice his size,  
He sets the seat exactly flat while we harmonize.  

Bridge
The bench is straight, the chorus swells, the crowd begins to cheer,  
The mayor asks for one more verse to thank the volunteers.  
We sing about the broken sign, the tools, the bolts, the shade,  
The final line turns into thanks for lemonade.  

Chorus
Oh, straighten up the bench, let the weary sit in line,  
Set the bolts and level legs; the shade will still be fine.  
Raise your voice, raise the wrench, pass the tools around,  
A song can start a movement when the feet are on the ground.  
Oh, straighten up the bench, make a place for two,  
The gardener has the hardware; the mayor has the glue.  

Ending
The mayor sits to give a speech; the bench holds firm and true,  
The crowd stands up to sing again, so he can sit there too.
