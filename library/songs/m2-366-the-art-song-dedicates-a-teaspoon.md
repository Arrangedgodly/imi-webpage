# The Art Song Dedicates a Teaspoon

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: intensely interpreted recital songs that assign enormous emotional importance to a tiny household object*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-010*
*Performance note: Earnest concert voice and restless piano; linger dramatically over the teaspoon's every reflected detail.*

[Verse 1]
Upon the cloth a teaspoon lay beneath the afternoon,  
The pianist discovered grief within its silver moon.  
I sang of all the mornings that its little bowl had known,  
Of every cup it stirred and every hour it spent alone.  
The audience held still as I considered its despair,  
A waiter needed cutlery and waited by my chair.  

[Chorus]
O little spoon, O slender fate,  
You held the dawn beside a plate.  
What bitter sweetness did you bear?  
What secret sorrow waited there?  
O little spoon, receive my song,  
The waiter has a table waiting and the line is growing long.  

[Verse 2]
I turned the spoon and found a mark engraved upon its back,  
The pianist heard doom within that crooked little track.  
I sang the letters slowly as a lover's hidden plea,  
The waiter said the letters named the catering company.  
I held it toward the window for a vision of the past,  
The waiter reached to take it but my cadence still would last.  

[Chorus]
O little spoon, O slender fate,  
You held the dawn beside a plate.  
What bitter sweetness did you bear?  
What secret sorrow waited there?  
O little spoon, receive my song,  
The waiter has a table waiting and the line is growing long.  

[Bridge]
He gave me one small wooden stick to finish up the verse,  
I took it very gently and described a simpler curse.  
The spoon returned to serving tea; the audience watched it go,  
The pianist followed with his eyes and played a trembling low.  

[Final Chorus]
O little spoon, O slender fate,  
You hold the dawn beside a plate.  
What bitter sweetness do you bear?  
The waiter stirs a cup without a single secret care.  
O little stick, receive my song,  
The pianist orders coffee and the recital carries on.
