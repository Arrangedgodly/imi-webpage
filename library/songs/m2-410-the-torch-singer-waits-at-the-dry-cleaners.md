# The Torch Singer Waits at the Dry Cleaner's

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: smoky torch songs about abandonment, longing and fragile promises, centered on a dress delayed at the dry cleaner's*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-011*
*Performance note: Slow piano, muted trumpet and an aching contralto; a clerk reads the collection ticket with devastating calm.*

[Verse 1]
I brought my velvet evening gown to have its collar cleaned,  
The clerk said Thursday afternoon; I took that as a dream.  
I came at four on Thursday with my ticket in my hand,  
The rail was full of other coats from other people's plans.  
She searched beneath the counter, then she checked the hanging row,  
My gown was at the pressing room across the town below.  
The concert started promptly; my blue gown was the cue,  
I wore a shirt of gray regret; the audience heard it through.  

[Chorus]
Come back, velvet, come back home,  
You're hanging in a pressing room and I am singing alone.  
Come back, collar, soft and fine,  
The promise on this ticket had a very different time.  
Slow night, bare rail, hear me plead,  
I have got a room of listeners and a dress I cannot see.  

[Verse 2]
At noon on Friday I returned to find the gown was there,  
Its velvet lay quite beautiful beneath the hanging glare.  
The clerk removed the plastic and I reached to take it down,  
The hem was much too short for me; she'd fetched a different gown.  
She read my little ticket and she checked the printed name,  
The other owner came inside; her ticket looked the same.  
Two sisters with one surname had been filed upon one hook,  
The clerk found my long evening gown and asked us both to look.  

[Chorus]
Come back, velvet, come back home,  
You're hanging in a pressing room and I am singing alone.  
Come back, collar, soft and fine,  
The promise on this ticket had a very different time.  
Slow night, bare rail, hear me plead,  
I have got a room of listeners and a dress I cannot see.  

[Bridge]
My sister took her shorter dress and booked a front-row chair,  
That evening I wore velvet with the cleaner's loving care.  
I sang about the empty rail; the crowd adored my pain,  
The clerk requested tickets when I brought the gown again.  

[Final Chorus]
Come back, velvet, come back home,  
The dry cleaner knows the ballad and can sing it on her own.  
Come back, collar, soft and fine,  
She gives me Tuesday mornings now with extra pressing time.  
Slow night, full rail, let it be,  
My sister wants the ballad sung about her own short sleeves.
