# The Jingle That Stuck to the Parade Float

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: advertising jingles engineered to be catchy enough to follow listeners out of the commercial*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Bright novelty-commercial bounce; repeat the hook until the parade crew has to physically interrupt it.*

**Verse 1**
A sponsor gives the marching band a tune to play at noon.  
It sells a brand of polished bells that promise to ring soon.  
The band rehearses four short bars; the jingle sticks at three.  
The drummer hums it down the block; the floats hum in a sea.  
A monkey paints the route sign, but the hook slips in his hand.  
He paints the chorus on the curb and starts the band again.  

**Chorus**
Ring it once, ring it twice, bright bell, brighter day;  
Buy a bell, buy a bell, the jingle wants to stay.  
The tune goes round the corner, then it circles back once more;  
The parade forgets its ending and marches past the store.  

**Verse 2**
The band reaches the town hall; the mayor raises his hand.  
The jingle fills the quiet hall and recruits the brass band.  
The drummer tries a different beat; the hook fits every bar.  
The float crew hums it while they load the painted star.  
A pigeon carries one refrain above the courthouse roof.  
The clerk writes down the melody as evidence of proof.  

**Chorus**
Ring it once, ring it twice, bright bell, brighter day;  
Buy a bell, buy a bell, the jingle wants to stay.  
The tune goes round the corner, then it circles back once more;  
The parade forgets its ending and marches past the store.  

**Bridge**
The sponsor asks for one last play; the band says, "We are done."  
They change the final note to silence; no one hums the run.  
The floats arrive at sunset; the route is finally clear.  
The mayor rings a plain old bell; the jingle disappears.  

**Ending**
The crew unloads the polished bells, the instruments go home.  
The pigeon drops one shiny note onto the bandstand dome.  
The drummer hears it, starts to hum, then bites his lip instead.  
The parade is over now; the tune stays in his head.
