# Arena of the Last Banana

*Format: Song parody*  
*Parody target: stadium rock excess and audience-call climaxes*  
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*  
*Batch: IMI-M1*

## Verse 1
At dawn the fruit truck rattled through the gate.  
One golden banana rolled out, carrying everyone's fate.  
The band plugged in beneath a plywood moon.  
Our capuchin singer raised his fist and promised doom.  
The baboon snack committee counted every trembling peel.  
The drummer hit a tom; the rafters learned to feel.  
We built a crown from napkins, tape, and pride.  
Then seven hundred cousins stormed the loading side.  

## Chorus
Raise your hands for the last banana!  
Let its yellow glory shake the town!  
If you can hear us past the rafters,  
Make a noise that knocks the moonbeam down!  

## Verse 2
The mayor brought a forklift, dressed in silver lace.  
His campaign slogan read, "Fruit for every face."  
A cloud of confetti clogged the fire escape.  
The keyboard player played a chord shaped like a grape.  
Our roadie climbed a speaker stack to bless the prize.  
He slipped, caught a cable, and swung past our eyes.  
We sang about redemption, rent, and fiber.  
The floor manager shouted, "Someone call the climber!"  

## Chorus
Raise your hands for the last banana!  
Let its yellow glory shake the town!  
If you can hear us past the rafters,  
Make a noise that knocks the moonbeam down!  

## Bridge
A spotlight struck the fruit; the crowd fell still.  
One child asked if we could split it with a bill.  
The singer stared, then lowered both his arms.  
The band unplugged and faced the snack alarms.  
We found a dull knife in the merch booth.  
It bent in half, which felt like honest truth.  
So teeth went to work in a tidy line.  
Each cousin got one bite and called it fine.  

## Final chorus
Raise your hands for the shared banana!  
Let its yellow crumbs run through the town!  
If you can hear us past the rafters,  
Make a noise that knocks the moonbeam down!  
Raise your hands, then pass the napkins.  
Tell the back row they can take a turn.  
The last big note can wait till morning.  
Tonight we eat; tomorrow we return.  

## Ending
The truck rolled out beneath a quiet sky.  
The mayor sold his forklift tie.  
We packed our amps beside the peels.  
The best encore was a room with meals.
