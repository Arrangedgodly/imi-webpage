# Double Dutch, Single Shoe

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: playground skipping rhymes, hand-clap patterns, and the escalating dare to jump in*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-002*

*Performance note: Handclaps and turning-rope rhythm; the rhyme should speed up until the lost shoe forces everyone to pause.*

Verse 1
Two lemurs turn the rope in time beside the schoolyard wall,  
A capuchin recites the rhyme and says he won't trip at all.  
He jumps on one, he lands on two, he skips the third beat clean,  
His left shoe flies above the fence and lands beside the dean.  
The rope keeps turning, children chant, the shoe is out of sight,  
The dean keeps up the rhythm and calls the landing right.  

Chorus
Turn it once and turn it twice,  
Jump in careful, roll the dice.  
Skip the stone and mind the shoe,  
The next refrain belongs to you.  
Clap your hands and tap your feet,  
Don't outrun the steady beat.  
Turn it once and turn it twice,  
The lost shoe gets a turn in the rhyme.  

Verse 2
The dean steps in to help us search; the rope goes round his hat,  
He jumps one time, he jumps two times, then forgets all that.  
A squirrel finds the missing shoe inside the hopscotch square,  
The capuchin says, "That is mine," the squirrel says, "It is a pair."  
They match the laces carefully, the rope slows to a crawl,  
The dean says, "No one jumps again until we find them all."  

Bridge
The shoe comes back, the rope turns slow, the circle clears the space,  
The capuchin ties both laces up before he joins the pace.  
The dean recites the final line, the schoolyard claps in time,  
He skips the rhyme's last couplet and makes up one that's fine.  

Chorus
Turn it once and turn it twice,  
Jump in careful, roll the dice.  
Skip the stone and mind the shoe,  
The next refrain belongs to you.  
Clap your hands and tap your feet,  
Don't outrun the steady beat.  
Turn it once and turn it twice,  
The lost shoe gets a turn in the rhyme.  

Ending
The rope is hung, the class goes in, the dean walks with a hop,  
He finds one shoe beside the door and tells the rhyme to stop.
