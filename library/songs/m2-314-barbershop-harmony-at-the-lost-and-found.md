# Barbershop Harmony at the Lost and Found

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: barbershop quartet close harmony and lost-property clerks who need an exact description before returning an item*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Four-part barbershop chords and a held tag; the clerk's questions interrupt every attempted description.*

**Verse 1**
A hat is on the counter, folded brim and feathered band.  
A quartet sings a chord to help the clerk understand.  
The clerk asks for its color; the tenor sings it blue.  
The bass says, "It is green," the lead says, "Could be two."  
The baritone holds a vowel till the owner enters late.  
He says, "That's mine," and the quartet asks him to describe the shape.  

**Chorus**
Lost, found, turn around, tell us what you see;  
Four-part harmony cannot replace a receipt.  
Hold that note, hold that hat, keep the feather high;  
The chord is very certain, but the tag won't tell us why.  

**Verse 2**
The owner says a feather bends toward the left-hand side.  
The quartet sings a leftward chord; the clerk begins to slide.  
She checks the inside lining; there is a stitched-in name.  
The bass sings every letter; the vowels sound the same.  
A second guest walks in and says the hat looks just like his.  
The clerk asks for the ticket; he has a matching quiz.  

**Chorus**
Lost, found, turn around, tell us what you see;  
Four-part harmony cannot replace a receipt.  
Hold that note, hold that hat, keep the feather high;  
The chord is very certain, but the tag won't tell us why.  

**Bridge**
The first guest shows a photo with the feather bent in rain.  
The second sees his own hat still resting on the train.  
The clerk returns the proper one; the quartet sings a ring.  
The owner wears it out the door, still hearing them sing.  

**Ending**
They file the matching hat away, its brim against the wall.  
The quartet holds one perfect chord and lets the echo fall.  
The clerk tags the lost one; the owner waves goodbye.  
Four voices sing "You're welcome," and the feather points awry.
