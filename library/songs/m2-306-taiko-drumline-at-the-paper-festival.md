# Taiko Drumline at the Paper Festival

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: taiko ensemble spectacle, synchronized precision, and festival organizers who keep adding cues to a finite performance*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Powerful ensemble calls and synchronized hits; let the stage manager's cue cards become part of the rhythm.*

**Verse 1**
The festival has one short stage beneath a paper moon.  
The taiko crew arrives at dawn to test the opening boom.  
The stage manager holds up cards: begin, then turn, then stop.  
The drummer sees the first cue twice and gives a thunder drop.  
The lanterns shake, the banner flaps, the paper cranes all bow.  
The organizer adds a cue: "A softer ending now."  

**Chorus**
Don, don, don, let the big drums answer;  
Call, call, call, make the paper dance here.  
One hit, two hits, the whole stage knows;  
The cue cards flutter wherever the rhythm goes.  

**Verse 2**
A second card says bow; the third says bow again.  
The first bow knocks the stand; the fourth card says, "Begin."  
The crew plays one clean pattern, then the stagehands move the screen.  
The paper moon rolls sideways, blocking every drum machine.  
They turn the moon around; it covers up the drum.  
The manager waves five new cards; the drummers keep it dumb.  

**Chorus**
Don, don, don, let the big drums answer;  
Call, call, call, make the paper dance here.  
One hit, two hits, the whole stage knows;  
The cue cards flutter wherever the rhythm goes.  

**Bridge**
The crew sets down their sticks and reads one card: "Play through."  
The stage manager drops the stack; the signal is now true.  
They play the final phrase together, clean and strong and brief.  
The paper cranes stay on their strings; the moon stays on its reef.  

**Ending**
The drums are packed; the cue cards fold; the crowd leaves in a line.  
One paper crane keeps bobbing to the last low beat in time.  
The manager raises one blank card; the crew all understands.  
They bow once, then carry every drum away by hand.
