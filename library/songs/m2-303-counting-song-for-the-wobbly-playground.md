# Counting Song for the Wobbly Playground

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: children's counting songs that pretend every real-world problem can be solved by one more repeated number*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Bouncy handclaps, simple call-and-response; the group keeps counting the loose bolts instead of securing the seesaw.*

**Verse 1**
One little monkey climbs the ladder to the slide.  
Two little monkeys count the rungs from side to side.  
Three little monkeys start a verse about the sun.  
Four little monkeys see the seesaw wobble when they run.  
Five little monkeys clap the beat and count the bolts below.  
Six little monkeys sing the number, but the bolts still go.  

**Chorus**
Count them up, count them down, clap on every four;  
One more verse and one more count will fix the playground floor.  
One, two, three, the children all repeat;  
The seesaw keeps on wobbling to the counting beat.  

**Verse 2**
Seven little monkeys count the swings that squeak in time.  
Eight little monkeys make the squeak become a rhyme.  
Nine little monkeys list the bolts beside the gate.  
Ten little monkeys sing them twice and call the checklist great.  
The teacher asks them to step back and let the caretaker through.  
They sing eleven, twelve, thirteen; the caretaker says, "Move."  

**Chorus**
Count them up, count them down, clap on every four;  
One more verse and one more count will fix the playground floor.  
One, two, three, the children all repeat;  
The seesaw keeps on wobbling to the counting beat.  

**Bridge**
The caretaker locks the gate and tightens every joint.  
The children hold the ladder steady at its point.  
They count the turns together, then test the seesaw slow.  
It stays level; now the counting has a place to go.  

**Ending**
One little monkey takes a turn; the rest wait in a line.  
The group sings one short number song and ends it right on time.  
The swings still squeak, the ladder stands, the seesaw stays in line.  
They count the last child through the gate, then leave the park behind.
