# The Doo-Wop Group Courts a Vending Machine

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: street-corner doo-wop romances with extravagant promises and an unresponsive object of affection*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-010*
*Performance note: Four-part finger-snapping harmonies; the bass singer supplies the vending motor's low hum.*

[Verse 1]
We gathered by the station where the evening trains depart,  
Four jackets, matching haircuts, and one hopeful little heart.  
Our tenor saw a chocolate bar behind a shining pane,  
He dropped a coin and chose it, but the spiral stayed the same.  
He pressed his hand against the glass and sang a tender plea,  
The bass man hummed the motor while the rest supplied the key.  
A cleaner paused beside us with her bucket and her broom,  
She said, "It takes exact change," and we harmonized the gloom.  

[Chorus]
Come down, sweet thing, come down to me,  
You're hanging on a spiral where my sweetheart ought to be.  
Sha-la, click-clack, hear our little song,  
We've been waiting at your window all the hungry evening long.  
Come down, sweet thing, don't leave me blue,  
I put my only coin inside; my lunch depends on you.  
Doo-wah, low hum, promise me a sign,  
Your expiration date is near, but darling, you look fine.  

[Verse 2]
Our baritone bought peanuts to persuade the coil to turn,  
The peanuts fell beside the bar; we watched the tenor learn.  
He said the two were holding hands and wouldn't come apart,  
The alto sang of rivals with a roasted peanut heart.  
The cleaner found a maintenance card and called the posted line,  
The service voice said Friday; it was Monday, half past nine.  
We tried a rising key change and an extra pleading chord,  
The station clock struck ten and drowned the harmonies we scored.  

[Chorus]
Come down, sweet thing, come down to me,  
You're hanging on a spiral where my sweetheart ought to be.  
Sha-la, click-clack, hear our little song,  
We've been waiting at your window all the hungry evening long.  
Come down, sweet thing, don't leave me blue,

Your expiration date is near, but darling, you look fine.  

[Bridge]
The cleaner bought us sandwiches from her familiar shop,  
We took them to the platform and the tenor let it drop.  
A passing train shook every pane; the bar fell with a thud,  
We ran back for our sweetheart and returned with chocolate love.  

[Final Chorus]
Come down, sweet thing, come down to me,  
You needed locomotive bass instead of harmony.  
Sha-la, click-clack, hear our little song,  
The peanuts have come down as well; we brought them both along.  
Come down, sweet thing, no longer blue,  
Our tenor shares his chocolate with the cleaner and the crew.  
Doo-wah, low hum, now the train is late,  
We serenade the timetable; it will not change its date.
