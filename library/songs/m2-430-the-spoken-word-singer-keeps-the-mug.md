# The Spoken-Word Singer Keeps the Mug

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: earnest spoken-word tracks that assign vast personal meaning to an ordinary object borrowed from a café*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-011*
*Performance note: Sparse hip-hop beat beneath measured spoken verses; sing the refrain gently while the barista interrupts with plain requests.*

[Verse 1]
This mug has held the weather of a thousand mornings past,  
It knows the shape of waiting and the warmth that doesn't last.  
I hold it by its handle like a promise made of clay,  
The barista says it's closing and to leave the cup that way.  
I say the cup is memory, the vessel of my voice,  
She says the next customer wants their coffee, not my noise.  
I turn toward the audience and give the rim a name,  
She turns toward the dish rack and requests the mug again.  

[Chorus]
Hold what you carry, carry what you know,  
The little mug has taught me how the longest feelings grow.  
Hold what you carry, let the warm things stay,  
The barista needs the vessel for the washing-up today.  
Old glaze, new voice, hear the story hum,  
She has heard my whole philosophy and still would like her mug.  

[Verse 2]
I tell her that its tiny chip resembles all our scars,  
She says the chip means it is due to leave the serving bar.  
She takes a little replacement from a carton by her feet,  
The old mug's handle cracks a little where it meets the heat.  
I ask if I can keep it as a pencil pot at home,  
She says, "For pencils, yes; for coffee, leave that cup alone."  
I place it in my canvas bag and thank her for the gift,  
The audience claps politely as the last cafe chair lifts.  

[Chorus]
Hold what you carry, carry what you know,  
The little mug has taught me how the longest feelings grow.  
Hold what you carry, let the warm things stay,  
The barista needs the vessel for the washing-up today.  
Old glaze, new voice, hear the story hum,  
She has heard my whole philosophy and still would like her mug.  

[Bridge]
At home I put my pencils where my coffee used to be,  
The mug became more useful than its lofty history.  
I wrote a track about it and returned to play next week,  
The barista handed coffee in a plain disposable sleeve.  

[Final Chorus]
Hold what you carry, carry what you know,  
The little mug holds pencils while my newest verses grow.  
Old glaze, new voice, hear the story hum,  
The barista has a policy: no sentimental mugs.  
Hold what you carry, let the warm things stay,  
The paper cup says "Thank you." She says, "That means please pay."
