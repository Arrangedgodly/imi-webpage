# The Folk Orchestra Tunes to a Doorbell

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: folk orchestra tuning rituals and musicians adopting the first convincing note they hear*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-013*
*Performance note: Fiddle, mandolin and concertina; a two-note shop bell interrupts the tuning and becomes the unintended reference.*

[Verse 1]
We met behind the music shop to play the autumn fair,  
The fiddles and the mandolins were waiting on a chair.  
The leader sought his tuning fork and searched his woolen coat,  
The shop bell rang two little chimes; he caught the lower note.  
He hummed it to the fiddles and the fiddles took the pitch,  
The mandolins came in behind and made a little switch.  
The concertina held its fixed notes firmly in the air,  
It couldn't change its tuning just because a bell was there.  

[Refrain]
Ding, little bell, tell the strings your name,  
The concertina cannot follow every changing game.  
Ding, little bell, let the doorway ring,  
The shop has bought a door chime, not a tuning thing.  
Bow the note, pluck the string, find the proper key,  
The leader's woolen pocket has the answer we should see.  

[Verse 2]
The shopkeeper found the tuning fork beneath the leader's gloves,  
He'd set it on the counter with the music that he loves.  
The fork gave out a clean note that the concertina met,  
The fiddles shifted down again; the mandolins reset.  
We played our opening together and the doorway bell rang through,  
The shopkeeper closed the inner door to give the tune its due.  
The music filled the little room without a changing pitch,  
The leader put the fork away beside the pocket's stitch.  

[Refrain]
Ding, little bell, tell the strings your name,  
The concertina cannot follow every changing game.  
Ding, little bell, let the doorway ring,  
The shop has bought a door chime, not a tuning thing.  
Bow the note, pluck the string, find the proper key,  
The leader's woolen pocket has the answer we should see.  

[Bridge]
We played the fair that afternoon with every note in place,  
The shopkeeper stood listening with a smile upon his face.  
He asked if we could write a tune to match his little bell,  
The concertina chose a key that all the strings knew well.  

[Final Refrain]
Ding, little bell, let the doorway ring,  
The lower note now starts a tune the little shop can sing.  
Bow the note, pluck the string, find the proper key,  
The fork stays with the leader; the bell stays where it should be.  
Ding, little bell, let the customers arrive,  
The shopkeeper requests no tuning when they come inside.
