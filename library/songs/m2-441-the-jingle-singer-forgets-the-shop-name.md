# The Jingle Singer Forgets the Shop Name

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: relentlessly catchy advertising jingles that remember every sales promise except the business they are meant to promote*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-012*
*Performance note: Bright commercial pop, smiling vocals and a bell accent; the shop owner's corrections gradually occupy more of the hook.*

[Verse 1]
I wrote a jingle for a little shop beside the square,  
It promised sturdy tables and a dozen sturdy chairs.  
It had a lovely melody that everyone could hum,  
The owner asked to hear it and I sang the opening run.  
I named the chairs and tables and the lovely lacquer sheen,  
Then sang "Come to our little shop" without the name between.  
The owner said the chairs were right, the tune was very sweet,  
But people couldn't find his shop with no name and no street.  

[Chorus]
Come on down, take a chair,  
The friendliest furniture shop is waiting for you there.  
Come on down, take a seat,  
It is somewhere by the square along a very pleasant street.  
Bright wood, fair price, let the little bell ring,  
We have everything you need except the name that I should sing.  

[Verse 2]
He held a card that read "Moss Furniture, eighteen Cedar Lane,"  
I tried to fit it in the hook and tangled up the refrain.  
The street went where the chairs had been, the number took the rhyme,  
The shop name got one syllable; the others fell behind.  
He asked me for a simpler tune with fewer claims to make,  
I put the name at the beginning for the listener's sake.  
The owner joined the second line and gave the full address,  
The lovely lacquer lost its verse; the name survived the test.  

[Chorus]
Come on down, take a chair,  
The friendliest little furniture is waiting for you there.  
Come on down, take a seat,  
It is somewhere by the square along a very pleasant street.  
Bright wood, fair price, let the little bell ring,  
We have everything you need except the name that I should sing.  

[Bridge]
The radio played the simpler tune and callers found the shop,  
The owner said the long one made his neighbors hum a lot.  
He kept it for the holding line when customers rang through,  
They heard the lovely lacquer while he fetched the chairs they knew.  

[Final Chorus]
Moss Furniture, eighteen Cedar Lane,  
There are chairs at Moss Furniture, eighteen Cedar Lane.  
Come on down, take a seat,  
The address finally fits inside our shorter little beat.  
Bright wood, fair price, let the little bell ring,  
The owner gets the name right; I can harmonize the thing.
