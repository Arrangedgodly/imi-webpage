# Harp Song for the Rain Gauge

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: Celtic harp ballads that romanticize weather while a field station needs a usable reading*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Gentle harp arpeggios under a lyrical refrain; let the singer's weather metaphors interfere with the station's actual measurement.*

**Verse 1**
The cloud comes over heather hill, the harp begins to sigh.  
A macaque checks the rain gauge as the first drops pass him by.  
The singer calls the weather tears the mountain keeps inside.  
The gauge reads two small marks; the song says, "Let it ride."  
The field tech asks for millimeters, writes the number down.  
The harpist turns the drizzle into rivers through the town.  

**Chorus**
Rain on the string, rain on the stone,  
The mountain keeps its weather to itself alone.  
Measure the fall, let the melody flow;  
The gauge says three, the chorus says snow.  

**Verse 2**
The drops become a steady patter; the harpist speeds the phrase.  
The tech checks the glass tube in a careful little gaze.  
A gust tips over the rain card; the numbers blur and run.  
The singer calls it silver ink beneath the setting sun.  
The tech turns the gauge toward shelter, keeps the tube upright.  
The harp makes every raindrop sound like a star at night.  

**Chorus**
Rain on the string, rain on the stone,  
The mountain keeps its weather to itself alone.  
Measure the fall, let the melody flow;  
The gauge says three, the chorus says snow.  

**Bridge**
The cloud lifts off the hill; the final drops fall slow.  
The tech records the total while the harp plays low.  
The number is exact; the singer adds no more.  
The field log dries beside the station door.  

**Ending**
The weather clears; the harp is cased; the gauge is set upright.  
The tech files the reading for the forecast later tonight.  
The singer hums a quiet tune beneath the open sky.  
The mountain keeps its secret, while the rain gauge tells us why.
