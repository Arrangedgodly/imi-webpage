# The Crosswalk Jingle

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: advertising jingles, irresistible product hooks, and the claim that a slogan solves everything*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-002*

*Performance note: Bright thirty-second commercial tune; repeat the slogan cheerfully while the actual crossing signal keeps cycling.*

Verse 1
The city hired a chorus to promote a safer street,  
We clap beside the traffic light and sing a catchy beat.  
A macaque writes the lyric, "Wait until the walking sign,"  
The producer wants a rhyme for "sign" that sounds more fine.  
We try "divine," we try "design," the traffic starts to flow,  
A little kid says, "Wait right here," and stops the whole show.  

Chorus
Wait, wait, watch the light,  
Cross together, cross it right.  
Green means go and red means stay,  
Don't let jingles lead the way.  
Wait, wait, take your time,  
That's the whole and only rhyme.  
Look both ways, then cross the street,  
The catchy hook is just a beat.  

Verse 2
The ad director adds a dance, the crew begins to spin,  
They dance into the bike lane and the crossing guard steps in.  
He stops the band and moves them back behind the yellow stripe,  
The producer says, "That moment has authentic safety hype."  
The chorus sings the slogan twice; the signal changes red,  
The crew keeps singing in the road; the guard waves them instead.  

Bridge
We trim the spot to twenty seconds, remove the spinning feet,  
We leave the words about the sign and wait beside the street.  
The camera shows the actual light, a child begins to hum,  
The ad ends with the crossing guard before the cars can come.  

Chorus
Wait, wait, watch the light,  
Cross together, cross it right.  
Green means go and red means stay,  
Don't let jingles lead the way.  
Wait, wait, take your time,  
That's the whole and only rhyme.  
Look both ways, then cross the street,  
The catchy hook is just a beat.  

Ending
The ad wins one small award for clarity and restraint,  
The producer asks for sequels; the guard says, "Please don't paint."
