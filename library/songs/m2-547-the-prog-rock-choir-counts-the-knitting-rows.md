# The Prog Rock Choir Counts the Knitting Rows

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: progressive rock vocal suites and singers applying ornate time changes to a simple knitting count*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-014*
*Performance note: Shifting rock pulse and choral numbers; the knitter interrupts with a plain count in steady time.*

[Movement One]
Seven footsteps to the gateway, five stars in the sky,  
Three forgotten kingdoms where the little shadows lie.  
The choir sings the numbered worlds beside a knitted sleeve,  
The drummer makes a time change and the knitter has to grieve.  
She counted forty rows before the choir began to sing,  
The numbered worlds had interrupted her own counting string.  
She lays the needles on her lap and asks them all to wait,  
The music has been counting quite a different kind of fate.  

[Refrain]
Count the stars, count the years,  
The knitter has one sleeve and a different count to hear.  
Count the stars, count the rain,  
The forty knitted rows have got no kingdom in their name.  
Long song, small stitch, let the changing measures roam,  
The sleeve would like a plain count to bring its next row home.  

[Movement Two]
The band takes one quiet break while she checks every row,  
She marks the fortieth with a thread so she can see it show.  
The choir resumes its seven worlds and five great seas beyond,  
The knitter keeps her marked row as a small and steady bond.  
She finishes the sleeve before the final movement starts,  
The choir reaches eighty-seven in its interlocking parts.  
The drummer asks which number gives the sweater its last thread,  
She says the pattern has a count, not the numbers that he said.  

[Refrain]
Count the stars, count the years,  
The knitter has one sleeve and a different count to hear.  
Count the stars, count the rain,  
The forty knitted rows have got no kingdom in their name.  
Long song, small stitch, let the changing measures roam,  
The sleeve would like a plain count to bring its next row home.  

[Final Movement]
She holds the finished sleeve when the choir performs next week,  
The singer asks if all its stitches have a number they can speak.  
She gives them one short counting pattern for a four-bar little theme,  
The choir sings it simply in the middle of the dream.  
The drummer adds a time change and she points him to the page,  
He plays the four bars steadily before the next large age.  

[Final Refrain]
Count the stars, let the changing measures roam,  
The sleeve has its own count and the knitter has gone home.  
Long song, small stitch, let the last chord shine,  
The choir buys a copy of the pattern for its next ambitious line.
