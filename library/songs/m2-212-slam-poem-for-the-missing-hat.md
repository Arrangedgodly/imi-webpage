# Hat, I Have Known You

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: spoken-word slam poetry, dramatic pauses, audience snaps, and a poet who treats a missing hat like a former friend*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-006*
*Performance note: Speak the verses over a sparse handclap; leave room for the crowd to snap after each image.*

[Verse 1]
I, Nema the capuchin, wore you on the bus, hat.
You rode the same seat, hat.
Then the doors opened, and you were gone.
I checked my head.
I checked the seat.
I checked the person checking me.

[Refrain]
Hat, where did you go?
The wind knows something I do not know.
Hat, return to the chair,
I left you a space and a little fresh air.

[Verse 2]
The crowd snapped twice.
I paused three times.
A macaque in the front row raised a hand:
"Is the hat blue?"
I said, "It is now."
The spotlight turned the curtain blue.
The audience snapped again.

[Refrain]
Hat, where did you go?
The wind knows something I do not know.
Hat, return to the chair,
I left you a space and a little fresh air.

[Bridge]
The poet climbed down from the stage and looked under the chair.
A ring-tailed lemur lifted the hat from the floor.
It had been holding the microphone cable in place.
The crowd stopped snapping.
The poem was holding the sound.

[Verse 3]
I checked the coat rack, the ticket booth, the aisle beside the door.
The bus had made one careful loop and stopped at our old store.
My hat was on the signpost, where the driver set it down;
The poet wore it backward as we rode across the town.

[Final Refrain]
Hat, where did you go?
You kept the whole set from losing its flow.
Hat, return to my head,
The cable is safe and the poem is fed.

[Ending]
The poet wore it backward; the audience snapped on beat.
