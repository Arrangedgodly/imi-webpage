# Cabaret at the Museum Statue

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: cabaret torch songs, knowing asides, and a performer who treats a small inconvenience as a grand betrayal*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-004*

*Performance note: Piano vamp and smoky spotlight; the marble statue never moves but gets the best reaction.*

Verse 1
A lemur in a feather hat sang softly by the stairs,
A marble statue watched him with its thousand-year-old stares.
He sang, "You never clap for me, you never buy a drink!"
The tour guide said, "It's stone," and he replied, "That's what I think."

Chorus
Oh, monsieur, won't you applaud?
You have both hands, though made of stone.
I sing my heartbreak to the crowd,
And you just sit there all alone.

Verse 2
He circled round the pedestal to catch a better light,
The statue's shadow stretched across his face that night.
He took it as a gesture, bowed, then sang another verse,
The visitors all tipped the stone and made his mood much worse.

Chorus
Oh, monsieur, won't you applaud?
You have both hands, though made of stone.
I sing my heartbreak to the crowd,
And you just sit there all alone.

Bridge
The curator came to move the rope; the singer cried, "Betrayal!"
He wrapped himself around the post and sang his grand finale.
The statue stood in perfect pose; the singer struck the same,
The curator turned the lights down and announced them both by name.

Verse 3
The audience applauded; the stone received the cheers,
The singer took a bow and billed the statue for its years.
A child put on a paper crown; the singer called it art,
The curator sold the program with his autograph apart.

Chorus
Oh, monsieur, won't you applaud?
You have both hands, though made of stone.
I sing my heartbreak to the crowd,
And you just sit there all alone.

Ending
The statue stayed in silence as the singer left the hall;
His portrait joined the exhibit, with no applause at all.
