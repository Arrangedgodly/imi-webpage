# The Baroque Soloist Turns the Page

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: ornament-heavy baroque arias in which a trivial obstacle receives an extravagant musical response*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-010*
*Performance note: Harpsichord and strings under a solemn solo voice; extend each word about turning the page into a ridiculous flourish.*

[Verse 1]
I sang beside the harpsichord beneath a golden shell,  
My first two pages promised that the third would go quite well.  
I reached the final measure with a flourish in the air,  
My fingers held a feather fan; the next page waited there.  
The player gave a little nod and kept the bass in place,  
I held a note through seven bars and gazed into his face.  
My page-turner had gone to fetch a glass of cooling tea,  
The orchestra repeated while I sang the letter E.  

[Chorus]
Turn, turn, merciful leaf,  
Deliver me from ornament and musical grief.  
Rise, rise, paper so pale,  
My aria has reached a most stationery tale.  
Turn, turn, flutter and fly,  
The court has heard this single vowel enough to wonder why.  
Rest, rest, grant me a breath,  
This page is more resistant than my character's death.  

[Verse 2]
I laid the fan upon the stand and reached across the score,  
A gust from my enormous sleeve sent page four to the floor.  
The violinists saw it fall and lengthened every bow,  
The cellist put his foot on it; he wouldn't let it go.  
I turned to page three safely and began its flowing line,  
The page-turner returned with tea and whispered, "Just in time."  
He saw the missing final sheet and crawled behind the chair,  
The court believed this pantomime was written in the air.  

[Chorus]
Turn, turn, merciful leaf,  
Deliver me from ornament and musical grief.  
Rise, rise, paper so pale,  
My aria has reached a most stationery tale.  
Turn, turn, flutter and fly,  
The court has heard this single vowel enough to wonder why.  
Rest, rest, grant me a breath,  
This page is more resistant than my character's death.  

[Bridge]
He rescued page four gently from the cellist's polished shoe,  
And set it on the music stand exactly when it was due.  
I sang the final sentence with my fan against my heart,  
The court applauded him as well; he bowed and stole my heart.  

[Final Chorus]
Turn, turn, merciful leaf,  
The page-turner saved the day and claimed my laurel wreath.  
Rise, rise, paper so pale,  
The court requests an encore of the crawling-paper tale.  
Turn, turn, flutter and fly,  
I sing a grand conclusion while he holds the pages dry.  
Rest, rest, grant me a breath,  
He charges per appearance now; my aria pays his rent.
