# The Barbershop Quartet Counts Three Coats

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: barbershop quartets and harmonious ensembles undone by the simplest arithmetic at the cloakroom*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-012*
*Performance note: Close four-part harmony; each voice counts the coats until the fourth singer notices what he is wearing.*

[Verse 1]
Four voices met for singing at the town's old winter fair,  
Three coats went up on numbered hooks beside the wooden stair.  
We sang of sunny avenues and friends who never part,  
Our bass supplied a booming floor beneath a tenor's heart.  
The concert ended warmly and we gathered by the rack,  
We counted three fine overcoats and wondered what we lacked.  
The tenor asked the baritone to count them once again,  
Three coats made three, however much we harmonized the end.  

[Chorus]
One for the tenor, one for the lead,  
One for the baritone, warm as he needs.  
Where is the bass coat, where can it be?  
Four voices make a chord but there are only three.  
Hum a little question, let the answer stay,  
The bass has sung through winter in a most peculiar way.  

[Verse 2]
The bass declared his coat was dark with buttons made of brass,  
The lead searched underneath the chair and in the passage past.  
The baritone asked the usher if a coat had walked away,  
The usher pointed at the bass and had a word to say.  
He stood there in a dark coat with its buttons shining bright,  
He'd left it on for singing since the stage was cold that night.  
We counted all four overcoats with one upon his back,  
The usher shut the cloakroom and removed the empty rack.  

[Chorus]
One for the tenor, one for the lead,  
One for the baritone, warm as he needs.  
Where is the bass coat, where can it be?  
Four voices make a chord but there are only three.  
Hum a little question, let the answer stay,  
The bass has sung through winter in a most peculiar way.  

[Bridge]
We walked out to the square with every singer warmly dressed,  
The bass hummed us a low note while the tenor did the rest.  
The baritone wrote a new verse about a well-attached coat,  
The lead said it was difficult to fit inside one note.  

[Final Chorus]
One for the tenor, one for the lead,  
One for the baritone, warm as he needs.  
One for the bass voice, buttoned to the throat,  
The greatest solo in the show was finding his own coat.  
Hum a little question, let the answer stay,  
Tomorrow we shall count our hats before we sing away.
