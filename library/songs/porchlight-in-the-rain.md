# Porchlight in the Rain

*Format: Song parody*  
*Parody target: country breakup songs and roadside heartbreak imagery*  
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*  
*Batch: IMI-M1*

## Verse 1
You left your key beside the feed-store phone.  
The porch light buzzed; I had to walk home.  
Your truck kept coughing halfway down the lane.  
I waved from my macaque porch, pretending sane.  
The dog still checks your side of the bed.  
He likes the pillow where you rested your head.  
I kept the dented skillet from our fights.  
It makes good eggs on lonely Tuesday nights.  

## Chorus
That porch light burns through weather and regret.  
It knows the shape your leaving made in me.  
I miss your laugh, your boots, your unpaid vet.  

## Verse 2
The county fair came back without your name.  
I lost at darts and blamed the wind for shame.  
The pie judge said my crust was much too tough.  
I told him love had made my hands rough.  
He gave me second place and half a pie.  
I ate it by the cattle gate at five.  
Your sister called to ask about the shed.  
I said the hinges squeak, the roof leaks red.  

## Chorus
That porch light burns through weather and regret.  
It knows the shape your leaving made in me.  
I miss your laugh, your boots, your unpaid vet.  
I miss the person I was trying to be.  

## Bridge
Then May arrived with mud up to my knees.  
I fixed the shed, repainted both oak trees.  
The dog found someone's glove beside the creek.  
It wasn't yours, but I let myself speak.  
"We'll be all right," I told his muddy face.  
He licked my thumb and took your old seat's place.  
No train came back, no letter crossed the plain.  
Just thunder rolling off the grain.  

## Final chorus
That porch light burns; I'll change the bulb tonight.  
It doesn't owe your shadow any more.  
I miss your laugh, then turn the switch to bright.  
The dog and I can guard another door.  
That porch light shines above a swept clean step.  
The skillet cools; the dishes wait inside.  

## Ending
The rain moved east across the sleeping grain.  
I shut the door and slept through rain.  
At dawn the bulb still did its part.  
That was enough to start a heart.  

*Performance note: Keep the vowels wide and the guitar plain; let the skillet line land without a wink.*
