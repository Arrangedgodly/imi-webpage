# The Kora Player's Lost String

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: West African kora performance with cascading plucked patterns and a story carried between repeated motifs*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-007*
*Performance note: Let the kora pattern flow steadily under the story; a missing string should alter the melody without stopping the player's hands.*

[Verse 1]
A musician sat beneath a broad tamarind tree,  
The kora's bright strings carried a tale beside the sea.  
A macaque brought a stool and placed it by the shade,  
The player found a broken string and paused the tune half-played.  
The crowd leaned close to hear the gap inside the sound,  
A gibbon searched the grass where the spare strings might be found.  
The musician kept the pattern with the notes that still would ring,  
The story turned around the space left by the missing string.  

[Chorus]
Pluck the pattern, let it run,  
The story moves beneath the sun.  
One note missing, still we know,  
The stool is sinking very low.  
Pluck the pattern, carry on,  
The gibbon cannot hold it long.  

[Verse 2]
A capuchin found a cord wound round the woven seat,  
He pulled it out; the stool collapsed beneath the player's feet.  
The gibbon caught one corner, the macaque held the rest,  
The player fitted that new string and launched his longest quest.  
He sang about a journey with a thousand hills to climb,  
The gibbon said, "My elbows cannot last another rhyme."  
The player sang, "The travelers approached a distant shore,"  
The audience called, "From the beginning! Tell us more!"  

[Chorus]
Pluck the pattern, let it run,  
The story moves beneath the sun.  
One note missing, still we know,  
The stool is sinking very low.  
Pluck the pattern, carry on,  
The gibbon cannot hold it long.  

[Bridge]
The player told of travelers who crossed a windy plain,  
Each time they reached the village, they set off again.  
The gibbon tried to wave goodbye; the seat began to slip,  
The player sang an extra verse about a tighter grip.  
A child brought two sturdy bricks and set them under the seat,  
The gibbon stretched his aching arms and staggered to his feet.  

[Final Chorus]
Pluck the pattern, let it rise,  
The gibbon names his new supplies:  
One brick left and one brick right,  
No more holding stools all night.  
Pluck the pattern, carry on,  
I'll be sitting down at dawn.  

[Ending]
The player packed the kora; the crowd sang the motif,  
The gibbon sold reserved brick seats to monkeys seeking relief.
