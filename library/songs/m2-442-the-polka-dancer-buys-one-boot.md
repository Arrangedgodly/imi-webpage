# The Polka Dancer Buys One Boot

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: exuberant polka dance songs whose cheerful momentum encounters a shoe-shop display mistaken for a complete purchase*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-012*
*Performance note: Accordion, tuba and a bouncing two-beat chorus; one heavy boot creates an unbalanced thump beside the dancer's old shoe.*

[Verse 1]
I saw a shining boot for sale beside the market door,  
Its leather looked quite splendid and its price was less than before.  
I paid the seller quickly and I tucked it in my bag,  
He said it was a sample as I turned away to brag.  
At home I found one right boot with a lovely polished toe,  
The left was still my old shoe, which had nowhere else to go.  
I dressed for evening dancing with the pair I'd managed to make,  
The boot came down like thunder; the shoe made a little shake.  

[Chorus]
Step, little boot, answer the shoe,  
The left foot has a whisper and the right foot has a boom.  
Round, little room, hear the uneven beat,  
I have bought a fine new half of my intended dancing feet.  
Step, little boot, shine upon the floor,  
The matching left is waiting in a shop I must explore.  

[Verse 2]
My partner heard the difference as we circled round the hall,  
She asked if I was adding percussion to it all.  
I said the right foot led the dance, the left had simpler taste,  
She said the seller might still have the other half to place.  
At morning I returned to ask about the missing mate,  
He brought the left boot from the stockroom, where it lay to wait.  
The sample price applied to one; the left one cost the same,  
I paid and took them both home with a less triumphant claim.  

[Chorus]
Step, little boot, answer the shoe,  
The left foot has a whisper and the right foot has a boom.  
Round, little room, hear the uneven beat,  
I have bought a fine new half of my intended dancing feet.  
Step, little boot, shine upon the floor,  
The matching left is waiting in a shop I must explore.  

[Bridge]
We danced again that evening with two heavy polished toes,  
My partner missed the little whisper from the old left shoe.  
I brought the old pair back next week and set the boots aside,  
The bargain pair kept waiting by the door with matching pride.  

[Final Chorus]
Step, little shoes, answer as a pair,  
The heavy boots are by the door and make a handsome glare.  
Round, little room, hear the steady beat,  
The shoes that cost me nothing have the lightest little feet.  
Step, little boots, wait beside the floor,  
I wear them for the rainy market, not the dancing anymore.
