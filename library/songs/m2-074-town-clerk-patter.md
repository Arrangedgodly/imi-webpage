# Patter Song for the Town Clerk

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: comic patter songs, impossible verbal speed, and lyrics crammed with administrative detail*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-002*

*Performance note: Light piano and clipped consonants; accelerate the list until the clerk's stamp runs out of ink.*

Verse 1
The town clerk reads the agenda at a pace that makes it blur,  
A lemur sings the meeting minutes faster than the mayor.  
We move the bins, approve the lamps, replace the park's old gate,  
Then list the chairs by color and the benches by their weight.  
The clerk stamps every paper with a flourish and a spin,  
The stamp runs dry at item nine; the chorus can't begin.  

Chorus
File it, pile it, sign the sheet,  
Keep the clauses short and neat.  
Read it, stamp it, pass it on,  
The next agenda starts at dawn.  
Talk it fast, but check the date,  
The clerk can make the meeting wait.  
File it, pile it, don't forget,  
The minutes aren't approved just yet.  

Verse 2
A capuchin brings three motions and a question on a napkin,  
The clerk recites them backwards while the mayor starts clapping.  
The first motion says the fountain needs a gentle repair,  
The second asks for quieter birds to gather over there.  
The third is just a sandwich list the clerk has misfiled,  
He sings the lunch ingredients and the council is beguiled.  

Bridge
The stamp is filled with ink again; the clerk resumes the flow,  
He lists the meeting adjourned, then adds "please don't go."  
The mayor says the meeting's done; the clerk sings one more line,  
"The minutes need approval," and the room begins to whine.  

Chorus
File it, pile it, sign the sheet,  
Keep the clauses short and neat.  
Read it, stamp it, pass it on,  
The next agenda starts at dawn.  
Talk it fast, but check the date,  
The clerk can make the meeting wait.  
File it, pile it, don't forget,  
The minutes aren't approved just yet.  

Ending
The clerk closes up the ledger, sings his last procedural phrase,  
The mayor asks for an encore; the clerk says, "Not today."
