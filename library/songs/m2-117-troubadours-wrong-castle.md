# The Troubadour's Wrong Castle

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: medieval troubadour ballads, heroic refrain conventions, and a traveling singer who keeps serenading the wrong castle gate*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-003*

*Performance note: Lute and frame drum; sing the refrain with noble certainty while the herald redirects the performer.*

Verse 1
A traveling singer reached a castle in the rain,
He raised his lute and sang of love and noble pain.
The gate swung wide; a goat walked out and shook its silver bell,
The singer bowed and asked the goat if this was where love dwelled.

Chorus
O castle, hear my distant plea,
Open up your gate to me.
If the drawbridge takes its time,
I'll turn the waiting into rhyme.

Verse 2
A herald stepped onto the wall and pointed down the road,
The singer thanked him with a verse and shifted his old load.
He reached another castle just before the setting sun,
The herald there was off for tea; the gate was held by one.

Chorus
O castle, hear my distant plea,
Open up your gate to me.
If the drawbridge takes its time,
I'll turn the waiting into rhyme.

Bridge
The singer strummed a noble chord; the drawbridge started down,
It stopped halfway above the moat and made a wooden frown.
The goat from the first castle appeared beside the chain,
It leaned its weight against the lever and lowered it again.

Verse 3
The singer walked inside the hall, the goat received a bell,
The herald returned from tea and said the song rang well.
He played a final verse for all the guards upon the wall,
The goat bowed at the refrain and answered every call.

Chorus
O castle, hear my distant plea,
Open up your gate to me.
If the drawbridge takes its time,
I'll turn the waiting into rhyme.

Ending
The singer found his audience, the goat found hay to eat,
The lute went quiet at the gate; the bell kept time beneath.
