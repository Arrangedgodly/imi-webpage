# The Baroque Duet Shares a Bookmark

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: baroque-style duets and singers ornamenting a simple dispute over their place in a shared book*
*By: IMI Records Executive Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-014*
*Performance note: Harpsichord-like accompaniment, two elaborate vocal lines and a joint refrain; ornamental runs delay every page number.*

[Voice A]
I left my little ribbon at the place where love begins.  
[Voice B]
I moved it to the chapter where the second traveler wins.  
[Voice A]
My chapter had a meadow and a very gentle rain.  
[Voice B]
My chapter had a bridge, and we must find the page again.  

[Verse]
We shared a single storybook beside the evening fire,  
Two voices chased its chapters with a very fine desire.  
One ribbon held the place for both the chapters we had read,  
We moved it back and forth until its useful work was dead.  
I sang a long ornament around the number twenty-three,  
You sang the number forty with a greater turn than me.  
The ribbon lay at thirty in the middle of the book,  
The story had no singer where the marker chose to look.  

[Refrain]
Turn, little ribbon, turn in the light,  
The readers have two chapters and one marker for the night.  
Turn, little ribbon, hold the proper line,  
The meadow waits at twenty-three; at forty, bridges shine.  
Two voices, one book, let the counterpoint grow,  
The paper has a page number that each of us should know.  

[Verse]
We cut a second ribbon from a little roll of blue,  
I kept the red at twenty-three; the blue marked forty true.  
We read our chapters quietly with all the singing done,  
The meadow met its rain; the traveler crossed the bridge he'd won.  
At night we put the book away with both the markers there,  
The next day's pages waited for our voices and our care.  
I wrote a shorter number song without the added run,  
You kept the grand ornament for the tale when it was done.  

[Refrain]
Turn, little ribbon, turn in the light,  
The readers have two chapters and one marker for the night.  
Turn, little ribbon, hold the proper line,  
The meadow waits at twenty-three; at forty, bridges shine.  
Two voices, one book, let the counterpoint grow,  
The paper has a page number that each of us should know.  

[Final Duet]
A: Red at twenty-three, let the meadow stay.  
B: Blue at forty, keep the bridge in view.  
Both: Two voices, two ribbons, one book by the chair,  
The next reader finds both and asks why we need a choir there.  
A: I sing the page number.  
B: I sing it too.  
Both: The next reader makes a card that marks a third place too.
