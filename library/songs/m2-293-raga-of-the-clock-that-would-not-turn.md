# Raga of the Clock That Would Not Turn

*Format: Song parody*
*Parody target: Hindustani raga development and accompanists who keep elaborating after the household is ready to begin*
*By: IMI Records Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SONGS-008*

*Performance note: Begin with a slow alap, add tabla in a steady cycle, then let the singer's improvisation collide with the clock's stuck hand.*

**Alap**
Before the first bright note, the house is still and wide.  
A tanpura hums a single string; the moon hangs by its side.  
A gibbon tunes the wall clock, but the minute hand won't move.  
The singer tests one rising note; the shadows start to groove.  

**Verse**
The family waits for breakfast; the kettle starts to steam.  
The tabla taps a cycle like a river in a dream.  
The singer bends the melody, then circles back around.  
The clock hand holds at seven; the cups are set for sound.  
The cook asks, "Is it morning?" The singer answers, "Soon."  
The raga blooms in careful turns beneath a changing moon.  

**Chorus**
Sa re, re ga, let the morning climb;  
Dha ni, ni sa, we have plenty of time.  
Round the beat and rise again, the melody will show;  
The clock remains at seven, and the porridge starts to glow.  

**Verse 2**
A macaque taps the clock face with a spoon to free the gear.  
The singer takes the knock and weaves it through the air.  
The tabla player speeds a little; all the guests lean in.  
The hour hand moves at last, but lands where it began.  
The cook turns down the burner; the pan begins to smoke.  
The singer finds a final phrase and sings it as a joke.  

**Chorus**
Sa re, re ga, let the morning climb;  
Dha ni, ni sa, we have plenty of time.  
Round the beat and rise again, the melody will show;  
The clock remains at seven, and the porridge starts to glow.  

**Ending**
The cook serves bowls; the clock strikes eight, the meal begins at last.  
The singer holds one note so long the breakfast hour has passed.  
The tabla gives a final beat; the spoons all hit the rim.  
The family starts to eat while the raga carries him.
