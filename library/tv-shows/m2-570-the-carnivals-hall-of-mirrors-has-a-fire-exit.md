# The Carnival's Hall of Mirrors Has a Fire Exit

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: carnival adventure specials, maze challenges, and safety inspectors whose exit signs appear in every reflection*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: INSPECTOR VELA, an orangutan; CARNIVAL OWNER, a baboon; HOST, a gibbon; RUNNER, a capuchin; TECHNICIAN, a macaque; non-speaking camera operator, a gibbon.

COLD OPEN

INT. HALL OF MIRRORS - LATE AFTERNOON

The host leads a camera crew into the mirror maze. A red EXIT sign appears in every direction.

HOST: We are surrounded by exits.

INSPECTOR VELA: One is real.

The runner walks toward a reflection and bumps gently into the glass.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The carnival owner says the maze is ready for its preview. Inspector Vela checks the emergency route. The host wants a dramatic challenge: find the exit before the clock runs out. The technician says all reflective panels are identical from inside. The real EXIT sign is mounted above the push-bar door and reflected in the mirrors. The owner proposes covering it with a hinged mirror panel for the show; Vela refuses to let the preview begin until the real sign is visible.

CARNIVAL OWNER: It is obvious from outside.

VELA: The audience is inside.

The runner tests the route with a bright red ribbon tied to the actual door handle. The ribbon appears in several mirrors. The host follows the wrong reflection and reaches a padded wall. The owner asks the technician to add arrows to the floor, but the maze rules forbid markings that spoil the challenge. The owner briefly swings the mirrored panel over the real sign to demonstrate the game effect. Vela stops the preview and orders it folded back open.

ACT TWO

The technician leaves the hinged panel folded open, exposing the one real EXIT sign, and places a small emergency light over the door. With the show lights off, reflections of decorative lamps disappear. The EXIT sign still reflects, but the downward emergency light reveals the real doorway and push bar. The host tries the challenge again, but the camera operator's red tally light reflects in every panel. Vela asks the crew to turn off equipment lights and place the camera at the entrance.

VELA: A safety route cannot depend on the camera angle.

The runner walks the route and reaches the door. The push bar works. The inspector asks the owner to add tactile floor markers along the path. The owner fears they will be visible during the game. Vela suggests a low ridge strip that can be felt underfoot but does not alter the mirror reflections. The technician installs the strip once along the real route. The runner tests it with eyes closed and finds a gap at a mirrored corner. The technician joins that segment, then the runner follows the continuous ridge to the exit in one clean pass.

The host stands at the start and watches the camera monitor. A reflected EXIT sign on the monitor points toward a dead end, so Vela covers that corner of the preview monitor with a small opaque card, telling the host to inspect the actual room rather than navigate by the image. The actual floor ridge remains clear beneath the host's feet. The owner agrees that the safe route can stay marked without spoiling the visual maze.

INT. HALL OF MIRRORS - EVENING

The technician closes the maze to visitors after the preview. Vela checks that the installed tactile strip remains uncovered and the emergency light is visible from the entrance. The mirror reflections create three apparent doorways. Only one has a visible push bar.

VELA: A reflection is not an exit.

OWNER: The guests like the choice.

VELA: They should choose the route, not the door.

The host proposes a timed run to demonstrate the maze. Vela asks the technician to test the door with the lights on and off. The push bar opens in both conditions. The technician checks that the tactile strip is not curled and that the path is wide enough for two people to pass. The runner watches from the entrance while the host practices the camera introduction, following the real sign rather than the false image on the monitor.

The host rehearses a safety explanation at the entrance. The preview image includes the camera operator's reflection pointing toward a false door. The technician moves the camera back and checks a new preview. This time the host names the actual exit and shows the push bar. The carnival owner accepts that the maze can still be confusing without concealing the safety route.

TECHNICIAN: We can challenge people without trapping them.

OWNER: We can put the clock farther from the exit.

The host approves the new timing point, away from the doorway.



The next morning, before opening, the host checks the camera monitor from the entrance. It still shows the false reflection, so the opaque card stays clipped to the monitor corner while the technician adjusts the preview angle. The host follows the installed floor ridge and the single real EXIT sign to the actual push bar.

HOST: I found it without the monitor.

VELA: You followed the raised edge.

OWNER: The camera will want a better angle.

The camera operator moves to the entrance, where the real exit light is visible. The owner tries the run wearing a feathered carnival cape that hides the floor strip from the camera. It also catches on a panel. The runner frees the cape, folds it over one arm, and escorts the owner along the clear path. Vela asks the technician to confirm that the fire door closes behind them without locking. The technician opens it, checks the closer, and leaves it ready for visitors.

TECHNICIAN: The exit works from inside.

OWNER: And the cape works better on the poster.

The host rehearses the last part of the route demonstration, then steps outside through the real door. The show clock remains at the start point, away from the doorway.

ACT THREE

The inspector approves the maze after one final check of the exposed sign, working push bar, and clear route. The host records the single finished introduction explaining that reflections multiply the one real sign. The runner completes the maze and leaves without hitting a panel.

HOST: I found the real exit.

VELA: You followed the floor.

FINAL IMAGE: The mirror maze glitters around the one exposed EXIT sign, multiplied in the panels but reachable by a clear tactile path.
