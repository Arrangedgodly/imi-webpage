# Everybody Loves a Macaque

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: multi-camera family sitcom convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: A family-run laundromat's downstairs apartment becomes the neighborhood's unofficial lost-and-found, to the annoyance of the owner who wants one quiet evening.

Cast: Rena Macaque, practical laundromat owner; Sol, her teenage gibbon nephew who repairs machines by listening to them; Aunt Yumi, an elderly colobus who has opinions about everyone's folding; Dev, Rena's neighbor and a meticulous pigeon courier; Kit, a child capuchin who leaves objects wherever a better game appears.

## Episode: "The Sock Drawer"

### Cold open

INT. LAUNDROMAT, EVENING

Rena pulls one red sock from a dryer. She waits. No matching sock appears. She checks the lint trap, then the basket marked LOST PROPERTY. Three people watch her search.

RENA: If the sock is in here, we have a problem.

Sol opens the basket. It contains one hundred and six single socks, two mittens, and a door key tied to a note that says "not mine."

AUNT YUMI: That key is mine.

She takes it and walks out. The audience laughs. Rena looks into the camera crew.

### Act one

The laundromat's regulars are arriving for the neighborhood's annual wash-and-fold fundraiser. Rena has agreed to donate the evening's profits to replace the broken ramp at the community garden. She has one rule: nobody leaves an unclaimed item in her apartment. The rule lasts until Kit appears with a shoebox of orphaned objects from the sidewalk.

Kit says he is collecting treasures for a game. In the box are a toy car, a tiny notebook, three buttons, and an old photograph. Rena sends him to return the box to its owner. Kit says all the owners are probably playing somewhere else.

Dev arrives with a letter for the garden committee. He has sorted his deliveries by street, urgency, and handwriting quality. He cannot identify the notebook's owner because the cover says only "Things I Don't Want to Forget." Aunt Yumi says the handwriting looks like hers, but she has never owned a notebook. She begins a story about the flood year and stops when she sees the photograph.

The photo shows a young Aunt Yumi beside the original ramp, holding a box of seed packets. She admits she wrote the notebook while helping at the garden. She had given it to the former groundskeeper, who moved away. The objects in Kit's box belonged to children who played there while the gardeners worked.

Rena turns the notebook over. A phone number is written inside, half rubbed away, beside Aunt Yumi's note: "Lark has the seed-locker key." Sol recognizes the last digits from a service tag on the washer.

### Act two

Sol needs the service tag to fix a dryer before the fundraiser begins. The tag is on a machine Aunt Yumi says she has used since before Sol was born. She refuses to let him take it apart until he can explain what the strange humming means. Sol listens. The hum repeats every twelve seconds.

He thinks a coin is trapped behind the drum. Rena says the dryer has always hummed. Sol asks why it only started after someone placed a metal stool on top. Everyone looks at Kit. He moved it there to reach a jar of buttons. The stool vibrates against the panel and makes a second sound.

Sol lifts it. The hum stops. The dryer still needs a belt, but he can finish the repair without ordering a new motor. Rena grumbles that he has saved the fundraiser and lost them a perfectly good excuse to replace the machine.

Dev uses the full service tag number to contact the former groundskeeper. The person who answers is Lark, now living two blocks away. Lark still has the seed-locker key mentioned in the notebook and brings it to the laundromat. The key opens the garden's old seed locker. The locker holds envelopes of seed money from a fundraiser years ago, carefully labeled and never deposited because the garden committee changed its bank account.

Rena worries the discovery means the current fundraiser is unnecessary. Lark explains the old money was enough for lumber but not for a ramp that meets the new safety rules. Rena looks at the envelope and admits that a useful surprise can still need more work.

### Act three

The fundraiser starts with a short line of neighbors, then fills the shop. Rena's rule against apartment drop-offs turns into a new plan: the lost-property basket stays by the door, and anything with a name gets returned before closing. Kit offers to make labels. His first label reads "stuff from outside." Dev helps him add an address and a date.

Aunt Yumi uses the old notebook to call past gardeners. They agree to come by Saturday and help clear the locker. Lark brings the key, and the committee writes down who will count the old cash with two witnesses. Rena says she is glad they found it. She is less glad that the ledger requires her signature because she owns the laundromat next door.

RENA: I wash clothes. I do not audit gardens.

AUNT YUMI: You have been doing both since 1998.

Sol finishes the dryer repair. The last load is a stack of fundraiser aprons, all clean except one red sock that snuck into the whites. Rena holds it up. Kit reaches into his shoebox and produces its mate. He says he found it by the garden gate.

The group laughs. Rena hands him the matching pair and gives Dev the sock basket to deliver to the committee. He adds it to his route under "urgent laundry." The audience applauds as the shop's lights dim and the ramp donation jar fills.

### Tag

Rena opens her apartment drawer for a clean apron. It is packed with labeled objects from the laundromat. A card on top reads "Rena's things, probably." She turns to Sol.

RENA: Did you label my apartment?

SOL: The drawer was becoming a collection.

The audience laughs. Aunt Yumi opens the drawer and takes the spare key she thought she had lost.
