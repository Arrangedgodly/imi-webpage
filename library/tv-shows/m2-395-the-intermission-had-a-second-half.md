# The Intermission Had a Second Half

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: televised classical concerts, whispering presenters, instrument close-ups, and intermissions filled with solemn trivia*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-014*

Logline: A concert broadcast's second half is delayed by one loose music stand, and the presenter must fill the intermission without interrupting the orchestra.

Cast: Presenter Nala, a gibbon; conductor Omi, a macaque; cellist Rena, an orangutan; camera operator Piri, a capuchin; stage manager Velo, a baboon.

COLD OPEN

INT. CONCERT HALL - EVENING

Omi raises the baton. One music stand slowly tilts. Rena catches the pages with her bow hand. The orchestra holds its breath.

NALA (WHISPERING): We are about to begin.

VELO (WHISPERING): The stand is still falling.

ACT ONE

Velo enters, lifts the stand upright and tightens its knob. The orchestra resets. Omi asks whether the cameras are ready. Piri zooms in on the baton, then on Rena's bow, then on the music stand.

OMI: Please frame the players.

PIRI: The stand has been the most active performer.

The concert begins. Nala narrates in a whisper about the composer. Her microphone is still live in the hall. Rena hears it through her monitor and plays a louder note to cover the voice. Omi stops the piece, lowers the baton and asks Nala to mute.

NALA: We can continue after intermission.

RENA: We were three bars in.

ACT TWO

The orchestra leaves the stage. Nala begins the intermission segment. She stands beside a display of the instruments and explains that the strings are tuned before each performance. Velo hands her a small tuning key. She asks whether it is part of the exhibit. It belongs to the bass stand.

NALA: We have found another stand.

VELO: This one is not falling.

Piri zooms in on the key. Nala whispers that the key was used to tune the bass. She holds it as if it were an artifact. Velo gently takes it back and returns it to the stage. Nala asks Rena for a short interview. Rena says she can discuss the piece after she checks the bridge on her cello.

NALA: How does the bridge feel?

RENA: Wooden.

NALA: A profound answer.

The stage manager asks for another five minutes. Nala returns to camera and introduces a short feature about instrument care. Piri zooms in on Rena's cello case, which is already closed. Velo hands Nala a rosin cake, then takes it back when he sees the orchestra needs it.

NALA: We are looking at the materials musicians use.

RENA: That is rosin. It helps the bow grip the strings.

NALA: Is it edible?

RENA: It is not a snack.

Piri tries to show the rosin close-up. The camera focuses on Nala's sleeve instead. Nala holds still while Velo adjusts the focus ring. The image snaps to the tiny block. Velo asks if they can film a demonstration. Rena says she can show the bow hair, but not while the cello is in its case.

NALA: We will save that for the concert.

Piri pans back to Nala. She begins a quiet interview with an audience member, but the microphone is still attached to the stand. The attendee speaks; the broadcast hears the orchestra tuning backstage. Nala asks Velo to move the microphone. He does, and the tuning stops as the musicians wait.

VELO: We have found the intermission.

NALA: We will let them finish tuning before the concert.

ACT TWO-B

The second half is still delayed because the stand's pages are in the wrong order. Omi asks the stage crew to sort them by measure number. Nala fills the silence by explaining the order of the program. She does so twice. Piri films the empty seats, then a close-up of a program booklet. The camera zooms so far in that the page numbers become abstract shapes.

PIRI: We have a circle.

NALA: That is the zero in page ten.

Rena checks each page while the orchestra waits. One sheet is upside down. Velo rotates it; the next page is also upside down. He turns the entire stand around. Omi calls for a brief tuning note. Every player gives a soft note at once; the sound becomes a low cluster.

OMI: One instrument at a time.

Rena plays her A. The others tune to it. Nala starts a sentence, stops when she hears the note, and lets the sound carry. Piri holds a wide shot of the orchestra tuning together.

NALA: That is our update.

ACT THREE

Omi raises the baton again. The pages are in order, the stand is secure and Nala's microphone is muted. The orchestra begins the second half without interruption. Rena's cello anchors the first phrase. Piri stays on the players rather than the stands. Nala watches from the side of the stage.

Velo whispers that the broadcast must return after the final note. Nala nods and does not speak. Omi lowers the baton. The audience applauds. Nala enters only after the applause settles and names the next movement.

TAG

The broadcast cuts to a close-up of the stand's tightened knob. Velo checks it one last time and steps out of frame.

FINAL IMAGE: Omi lifts the baton for the encore while Nala keeps her microphone lowered and Piri frames the whole orchestra.
