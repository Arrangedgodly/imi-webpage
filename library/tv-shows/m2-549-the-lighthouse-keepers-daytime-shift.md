# The Lighthouse Keeper's Daytime Shift

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: stormy maritime dramas, lighthouse mystery tropes, and keepers who insist every weather change is ominous*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: KEEPER ODO, an orangutan; ASSISTANT LARK, a gibbon; CAPTAIN VELA, a macaque; ENGINEER BEX, a baboon; RADIO OPERATOR, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

EXT. LIGHTHOUSE - DAY

The lighthouse beam sweeps across a sunny harbor. Keeper Odo peers through binoculars.

ODO: A vessel approaches from the east.

LARK: It is the mail boat.

ODO: It is always the mail boat until it isn't.

The radio crackles.

RADIO OPERATOR: The mail boat is two minutes away.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

INT. LIGHTHOUSE LANTERN ROOM - DAY

Odo insists the beam must remain on. Lark explains that the harbor is in daylight and the lamp is due for a maintenance check. Odo sees a white flash from the water and calls it a distress signal. Captain Vela radios that she is polishing a mirror on the mail boat.

ODO: The mirror is signaling.

VELA: I am signaling that I am polishing.

Bex checks the lamp motor. The lighthouse lens continues rotating even after the switch is off. The keeper says this is a warning. Bex finds a mechanical crank still engaged. The motor is disconnected, but the lens coasts for a long time.

LARK: It is not a ghost. It is momentum.

ODO: Momentum has a suspicious schedule.

ACT TWO

The mail boat docks. Vela brings a parcel addressed to the keeper. Odo refuses to open it until the weather changes. Lark reads the label: replacement mirror cleaning cloths. Odo says the cloths may be a coded message. Bex unwraps them and finds an instruction card with a diagram of the lens.

BEX: The beam has a service position.

ODO: That is where it points when nothing is wrong.

The radio operator reports a fog bank beyond the headland. Odo orders the lamp switched on. Bex says the daytime test should continue first. They use a dark panel to check the rotating beam safely. Lark watches the bearings and finds that one is dry, causing the lens to stutter once each rotation.

ODO: There. The flash.

VELA: It was your lighthouse.

LARK: The mirror on the boat was innocent.

The crew lubricates the bearing, then tests the lamp. The beam turns smoothly. Odo prepares to send a warning signal to the harbor. Vela says the mail boat has already left; the only vessel is a fishing skiff with a working radio.

EXT. LIGHTHOUSE BALCONY - DUSK

The fog thickens over the water. Odo watches the repaired lens through a small inspection window. The radio operator receives a call from the fishing skiff, whose compass is unreadable in the mist. Vela asks for the lighthouse bearing. Odo gives the safe channel, but the skiff repeats the number incorrectly.

VELA: They heard four as fourteen.

LARK: Send the signal twice, with a pause.

Odo rotates the lens to the approved harbor mark. Bex watches the bearings. The first beam passes across the headland. The second arrives after the pause. The radio operator confirms the skiff has corrected its course.

ODO: The light works.

BEX: The bearing works.

LARK: Both are important.

The keeper writes the signal time in the log. He asks whether the mail boat's mirror could have caused the earlier flash. Vela says the boat is already beyond the point. Odo circles the old note and adds "polished mirror, not distress." The radio operator calls the skiff again to confirm it reached sheltered water.



INT. LANTERN ROOM - DUSK

The fog alarm rings. Odo reaches for the lamp switch, but Bex stops him until the shutters are set. Lark checks the lens for condensation and wipes the inspection glass, not the optical surface. The radio operator keeps contact with the skiff while Vela reads the harbor lights from the balcony.

VELA: The west marker is visible.

ODO: Is it moving?

VELA: It is fixed to a rock.

BEX: The boat is moving.

The keeper adjusts the beam one notch. The skiff confirms the signal is now ahead on its left side. The radio operator repeats the harbor instructions in shorter phrases. The crew keeps the lamp on the verified route and does not chase every glimmer on the water. After the skiff reaches shelter, Odo logs the fog alarm, the bearing, and the safe arrival.

LARK: That is one real emergency.

ODO: A manageable one.

VELA: And not a mirror.



ACT THREE

The fog reaches the harbor mouth. Odo switches on the lamp at the correct time. Vela confirms the fishing skiff sees the beam and turns toward safe water. The mail boat's mirror sends one last bright flash as it heads around the point.

ODO: A signal from the east.

LARK: A mirror.

Odo writes both possibilities in the log. Bex locks the daytime crank away from the motor switch.

FINAL IMAGE: The lighthouse beam sweeps through the first fog while the keeper's log lists mirror and mail boat under "not a crisis."
