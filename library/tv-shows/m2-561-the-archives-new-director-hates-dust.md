# The Archive's New Director Hates Dust

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: workplace dramas about cultural preservation, new management, and records that must stay untouched*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: DIRECTOR VELA, a macaque; ARCHIVIST ODO, an orangutan; ASSISTANT KIP, a capuchin; DONOR, a baboon; CURATOR, a gibbon; PRESS REPORTER, a capuchin; CAMERA OPERATOR, a gibbon.

COLD OPEN

INT. TOWN ARCHIVE - MORNING

Director Vela enters the record room wearing white gloves. A thin layer of dust covers an old filing cabinet.

VELA: Who left this dust?

ODO: The cabinet is older than the building.

KIP: We were preserving it.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The new director announces a clean archive policy. Every surface must be dust-free by the afternoon press tour. Odo warns that some documents are fragile and should not be moved. The donor arrives with a box of family letters and insists they be displayed immediately.

DONOR: They are important.

CURATOR: They are not catalogued.

VELA: Catalog them before lunch.

Odo shows Vela the proper gloves, soft brush, and humidity log. The room humidity is safe for handling, but the display cabinet's own gauge reads too dry. The director looks at the brush and asks whether it can be replaced with a vacuum. Odo says not near paper. Kip plugs in a small vacuum anyway; it sucks a label off the cabinet and draws it into the hose.

KIP: We have lost the label.

VELA: We have cleaned it.

ODO: We have removed evidence.

ACT TWO

The press tour begins. Vela shows a polished table with nothing on it. The donor asks where the letters are. Odo has placed the letters in the safe storage cabinet. Vela opens the separate, empty display case and finds its gauge still reads too dry. Odo closes it and keeps the letters in storage until the display control can be adjusted.

VELA: Why is the table empty?

ODO: Because the table is clean.

The curator retrieves the cabinet label from the vacuum bag. It is stuck to a receipt, not damaged. Kip reads the label aloud: "Dust cover is part of the exhibit." The director checks the catalog and realizes the cabinet is a historical artifact. Its dust was documented and measured as evidence of age, but the cover is removable.

The donor offers to let the archive digitize the letters. Vela asks whether they can be handled without display. Odo explains the scanning process and prepares a clean table with a support cradle. Kip places the brush, scanner, and gloves in the correct order. The team scans one letter without bending it.

DONOR: Now I can read the letter.

VELA: And the original stays protected.

INT. DIGITIZATION ROOM - AFTERNOON

The archivist places the next fragile letter in the scanner cradle. Vela asks whether the dust on the cabinet should be cleaned before the tour resumes. Odo explains that the dust is not loose dirt but a recorded layer; brushing it away would erase the evidence of the cabinet's storage history. The curator checks the humidity meter at the display cabinet. Its reading is too low for the letter. Kip leaves the vacuum outside the room and checks the separate room humidity log; the room reading is already safe. Odo resets only the display cabinet's humidifier and waits until its gauge returns to the storage range.

DONOR: Is the letter safe?

ODO: It is safer when we wait.

PRESS REPORTER: Can we get a close-up of the letter?

VELA: Film the display case instead of the paper.

CAMERA OPERATOR: The light is too close to the glass.

ODO: Move it back until the reflection disappears.

[The operator moves the light.]

The donor reads the letter's first line from the digital copy. It is a note from the donor's grandparent, who served as the archive's first archivist, about borrowing the same cabinet when the archive opened. The curator checks the catalog and finds the grandparent's signature in an old accession ledger. The letters are not a new display item; they complete an existing collection.

CURATOR: We should add them to the ledger before the tour resumes.

VELA: Then the dust has to wait.

Kip seals the empty vacuum bag, labels it EMPTY, and carries it out of the room. Vela watches Odo return the brush to its case and updates the clean-room checklist to say "preserve documented surfaces."



The donor reads the next line of the grandparent's memo: "Do not put the cleaning equipment in the correspondence cupboard." Kip looks toward the vacuum in the hall. Odo adds the memo's catalogue number to the matching accession entry. Vela agrees to place a digital copy on the public screen, while the original remains in controlled storage. The press crew records the screen, not the paper.

ACT THREE

The press tour resumes with a display of scanned letters and the historical cabinet. Vela permits the cabinet's dust cover to remain, labeled and protected. Kip stores the vacuum outside the record room. Odo updates the catalog, giving the new director a blank field for future dust measurements.

FINAL IMAGE: The director points at a dust mote under the lights while Odo labels it "not part of the collection."
