# The People vs. the Puddle

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: courtroom television*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A small-town animal court hears a case over a puddle that keeps appearing in the market square, forcing neighbors to prove who owns water that refuses to stay put.

Cast: Judge Mallow, a calm old macaque; Clerk Brist, a precise tamarin; Ada, a capuchin fruit seller suing over spoiled melons; Finch, a gibbon roofer accused of diverting rain; and Jo, a young baboon juror who asks practical questions.

## Episode: "The Matter of the Moving Water"

### Cold open

INT. TOWN COURT, DAY

Clerk Brist calls the case. Ada places a wet melon on the evidence table. Water drips through a crack onto Judge Mallow's shoes.

JUDGE MALLOW: Is the melon entering a plea?

ADA: It is the victim.

Finch stands. A drop falls from his hat. The judge asks for a towel. Brist produces a document labeled "Towel, Exhibit A." The audience murmurs.

### Act one

Ada says a puddle has formed outside her stall every market day for three weeks. Her fruit sits low to the ground, and the damp has spoiled a basket of melons. She believes Finch's new roof gutter empties into the square. Finch says his gutter points toward the public drain and that he has already paid a fine for a leak that was not his.

At the square, the court sketch shows water flowing from Finch's roof to Ada's stall, but Jo spots that the map is upside down.

Back in court, Finch demonstrates his gutter using a tin roof model. He pours a cup of water. It follows the channel into a bucket, then leaks from a seam onto the floor. Finch says he patched the seam two days ago. Ada asks why the puddle still appears. He has no answer.

### Act two

The market sweeper says the puddle appears before Finch opens. Another witness heard a frog at night.

At the square, Jo follows the sound to a cracked watering trough behind Ada's stall. It is fed by a hand pump shared by market sellers. The pump's handle has a loose pin. Each time someone pumps water, the trough spills through a crack and trickles down the slight slope toward Ada's melons.

Ada used the pump for water. Finch filled flower pots. The sweeper rinsed the stones. All three shared the trough.

A frog croaks from the drain. Finch asks whether it has a license.


INT. COURTROOM, LATER

Brist unfolds the map. The puddle drawing slides off the table and lands in the bucket. Ada retrieves it before Finch can step on it.

MALLOW: Call the first witness.
BRIST: The melon is wet.
ADA: It has suffered.
FINCH: It is a melon.
JO: Does it have counsel?
MALLOW: It has a towel.
BRIST: Exhibit A.
ADA: I lost three.
FINCH: I lost none.
JO: The stall is uphill.
BRIST: The sketch is upside down.
MALLOW: Turn the sketch.
JO: The puddle stays put.
FINCH: My gutter is clear.
ADA: The trough leaks.
FINCH: How do you know?
ADA: My melons are wet.
JO: The pump handle drips.
MALLOW: Is that evidence?
BRIST: It is evidence.
ADA: The gate was open.
FINCH: The gate is always open.
JO: That seems relevant.
MALLOW: Order in the square.
BRIST: The frog is croaking.
MALLOW: The frog is not sworn.
FINCH: It can testify.
ADA: It lives in my trough.
JO: The crack is there.
BRIST: Please mark the crack.
FINCH: I did not make it.
ADA: I used the pump.
SWEEPER: I rinsed the stones.
MALLOW: We found the users.
JO: Not the culprit.
MALLOW: Water is not guilty.
BRIST: The summons can be closed.
ADA: Who pays for melons?
MALLOW: The market fund will.
FINCH: Who repairs the trough?
JO: All of us.

### Act three

The judge finds that Finch did not cause the puddle. The sellers collectively maintained the pump, so they will share the repair. Ada agrees to move her fruit crates onto a dry shelf until the trough is patched. Finch offers a piece of spare gutter lining. The market sweeper volunteers to clear the drain, but Brist reminds him to remove the frog only if it leaves on its own.

They repair the pump with a new pin and seal the trough crack with clay. The water now runs into the drain. Jo suggests marking a line on the pump handle to show how much water each seller uses. Ada proposes a simple schedule instead. Finch will water the flowers after the market closes, and the sweeper will rinse the stones only after checking the drain.

In the courtroom, Judge Mallow reads the ruling. The puddle is dismissed as a defendant because it has no address and did not receive the summons. Ada receives compensation from the market fund for the spoiled fruit. Finch's fine is returned. Brist files the wet melon as a court snack, then changes the label when Ada retrieves it.

Jo asks the judge whether the case is over if water forms in the square after rain. The judge says rain is free to appear. The town is responsible for giving it somewhere to go.

### Tag

The next morning, Brist steps into the courthouse and finds a small puddle beneath the rain barrel.

BRIST: New case?

Judge Mallow studies the roof. A leaf blocks the downspout. Jo removes it with a stick.

JUDGE MALLOW: The court recognizes a leaf's right to fall. The maintenance crew has a duty to notice.

Brist writes this on a card and pins it above the door.
