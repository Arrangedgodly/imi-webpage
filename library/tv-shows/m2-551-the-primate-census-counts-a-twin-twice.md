# The Primate Census Counts a Twin Twice

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: investigative census procedurals, data dashboards, and interviewers who mistake one monkey's quick change for a duplicate resident*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: ENUMERATOR KET, a gibbon; SUPERVISOR RUMA, an orangutan; RESIDENT PIP, a macaque; TWIN PAX, a macaque; ANALYST, a baboon; BUILDING MANAGER, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. APARTMENT HALLWAY - DAY

Ket knocks on a door. Pip answers wearing a green scarf. Ket marks the form, turns to leave, then sees the same face in the stairwell wearing a yellow scarf.

KET: You were just inside.

PAX: I was just upstairs.

KET: There are two of you.

PAX: There are.

The dashboard flashes DUPLICATE HOUSEHOLD.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

INT. CENSUS OFFICE - DAY

Supervisor Ruma studies a map with two identical dots over the same apartment. The analyst says the system has counted Pip twice, but the household count has not changed. Ket insists both interviews had different answers. Pip works nights and sleeps during the day; Pax works at the community theater and changes costumes.

RUMA: The system says two residents, one address, one identical birthday.

KET: The birthday is identical.

ANALYST: They are twins.

RUMA: Then it is not an error.

The software still flags the records because Ket entered the apartment number in one field and the floor number in another. The dashboard has assigned Pip to apartment 4 and Pax to floor 4. Ruma sends Ket back to verify the unit. The show follows Ket with a dramatic map animation that shows two dots merging into one.

ACT TWO

INT. APARTMENT BUILDING - DAY

Ket returns to the hallway. Pip and Pax stand in identical coats. They have switched scarves. Ket asks each to identify themselves without saying their names. Pip describes the apartment's leaky kitchen tap. Pax describes the theater's rolling scenery. The answers are accurate but both are currently standing in the apartment.

KET: Who lives here?

PIP: I do.

PAX: I am visiting.

Pip admits Pax is staying for the week because the theater's roof is being repaired. The form has a temporary-resident section, but the dashboard hid it below the fold. Ket scrolls down and finds a checkbox for household stays of more than one month. Pax is staying seven nights.

PAX: I am a guest.

KET: The form called you a resident.

PAX: The form also called the stairwell a floor.

Ruma arrives with the analyst. They compare the theater roster and the apartment lease. The twins are separate people, but only Pip resides at this address. The identical birthday and similar names triggered the duplicate warning; the misread floor number made it look like one person entered twice.

RUMA: We need both records and one household count.

ANALYST: I can merge the dots.

KET: Do not merge the people.

The analyst corrects the apartment number, adds Pax as a short-term guest, and preserves two separate records. The dashboard recalculates: one household, one resident, one temporary guest. It then flags the guest as a possible second resident.

RUMA: The system is learning.

KET: It is learning to be suspicious.

INT. BUILDING LOBBY - DAY

Ket asks the building manager to confirm the residents. The manager gives two keys to the same unit. One has a green tag and one a yellow tag; both open the apartment. Pax borrowed the spare yellow-tagged key while staying for the week. Ket asks whether Pax sleeps in the apartment. Ruma checks the temporary-stay rule and asks both twins separately.

KET: How many nights?

PIP: Seven.

PAX: Six, if the roof crew finishes early.

RUMA: The form asks for planned nights.

PAX: Then seven.

The analyst tries to mark both as residents. The system asks for a lease number. Pip provides the lease; Pax gives a theater call sheet. The software rejects the call sheet, then accepts it after the analyst selects VISITOR. It immediately asks whether the visitor lives at another address.

PAX: My usual address is the theater dorm.

ANALYST: Is that on the call sheet?

PAX: On the back.

Ket enters the theater's legal address as Pax's existing household. The software links his temporary visit to Pip's apartment without creating another theater household or merging the twins. Ruma asks the analyst to verify both portraits. Ket checks each name; the twins answer at the same time, in different voices.

KET: Two people.

PAX: One temporary stay.



ACT THREE

INT. CENSUS OFFICE - EVENING

Ket submits the corrected count. The twins appear on a video call from different places: Pip in the apartment, Pax on the theater stage. The dashboard finally removes its warning. Ruma signs the case closed and sends the form team a request to move the temporary-stay question above the fold.

FINAL IMAGE: Two separate portraits share one apartment dot; Pax's existing theater address appears once in the household list.
