# The Mountain Rescue Team Finds a Picnic

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: high-stakes rescue dramas and search teams who follow an emergency beacon to a very ordinary lunch*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: RESCUE CAPTAIN NELL, a gibbon; NAVIGATOR, an orangutan; HIKER PIP, a capuchin; RADIO OPERATOR, a macaque; RANGER, a baboon.

COLD OPEN

EXT. MOUNTAIN RIDGE - DAY

A rescue beacon flashes in the snow. The team rushes toward it. They find a picnic basket.

NELL: We have a signal.

PIP: I have sandwiches.

The basket's beacon keeps flashing.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The rescue team receives a distress call from a hiker near the ridge. The radio operator triangulates the signal. The team reaches the ridge and finds no hiker, only a basket with a red beacon strapped to its handle. Navigator says the signal came from an old emergency transmitter used in training.

NELL: Why is it here?

PIP: I borrowed it to mark our lunch.

The ranger says the beacon should not be activated unless there is a real emergency. Pip insists he only turned it on because the basket was sliding downhill. The captain asks whether anyone saw the hiker. The radio operator hears a faint whistle. The team moves toward it, leaving the basket under a rock.

ACT TWO

The whistle comes from the lower trail. The team finds a hiker sitting beside a broken trekking pole. The hiker is not injured but cannot find the marked path after fresh snow covered the trail markers. The rescue captain checks the map. The team can escort the hiker down, but the beacon is still flashing and might bring another team to the wrong location.

NELL: We have two problems.

PIP: One is a sandwich.

NAVIGATOR: One is a live signal.

The radio operator calls base and reports both locations. The captain asks Pip to switch off the beacon. Pip tries, but the latch is under the basket handle. The basket slides when he lifts it. The navigator braces it with a boot. The captain reaches the latch and powers it down.

RANGER: It is off.

The radio operator confirms that base has stopped dispatching the second team. The hikers begin the descent with the rescue team. Pip carries the basket. Halfway down, the actual hiker realizes the trail markers are missing because the maintenance crew removed them for winter. The ranger radios the crew to place reflective markers before the next storm.

EXT. LOWER TRAIL - AFTERNOON

The rescue captain asks the hiker to describe the last marker they saw. The hiker remembers a red stripe, but the trail crew had removed red markers for repainting. Navigator checks the map and finds that the group is one ridge west of the picnic site. The radio operator calls base with their location. The weather has started to close in, so the captain chooses the lower path rather than climbing back to the beacon.

NELL: We can get everyone down before the cloud line reaches us.

PIP: The sandwiches can wait.

The hiker has a twisted boot lace but no injury. The ranger checks the ankle and reties the lace. The rescue team sets a pace that the hiker can maintain. Pip straps the beacon to the inside of the basket, where it cannot be activated by accident. The captain tells base that the original alert came from a training unit, not a distress call. Base confirms that no second team is being sent.

RADIO OPERATOR: Signal is clear.

NELL: Keep the line open until we reach the ranger station.

The group follows the contour trail. At a fork, two routes are marked with temporary tape. The hiker points to the wrong one. The ranger checks the map, removes the old tape, and replaces it with a directional arrow. They reach the lower path before the clouds cover the ridge. The captain adds the missing marker report to the trail log.



At the ranger station, Pip notices the training beacon has a second switch. He has already packed it, but its red indicator blinks through the canvas. The rescue captain asks him to leave it off. Pip says the basket needs to be visible for the incident photograph. The radio operator puts the basket on the table and frames a shot. The beacon flashes, and the base dispatcher calls back.

RADIO OPERATOR: Base sees a new alert.

NELL: It's the camera flash.

PIP: That's a very responsive camera.

The captain turns the beacon face down and asks the operator to call base with the serial number. The dispatcher confirms the signal is now fully off. The ranger puts a strip of bright tape around the switch so the next trainee can see it before picking it up. Pip asks if the tape is approved for lunch baskets. The ranger says it is approved for switches. Pip puts the sandwiches in his pack instead, where the next photo cannot set off a rescue.

ACT THREE

At the ranger station, the hiker signs the trail report. The rescue captain logs the beacon as a false alert and the missing markers as a real hazard. Pip gives everyone a sandwich. The radio operator labels the beacon "TRAINING ONLY" and stores it in the supply box.

PIP: Do we need to mark the basket?

NELL: Only if you lose it.

FINAL IMAGE: The rescue team walks past a newly installed trail marker while the beacon remains dark in its box.
