# The Ferry Captain's Map Has an Island Missing

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: maritime adventure dramas, missing-map cliffhangers, and navigators who distrust ordinary tide charts*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: CAPTAIN ORA, an orangutan; NAVIGATOR FENN, a capuchin; DECKHAND LUZ, a macaque; HARBOR MASTER, a gibbon; PASSENGER, a baboon.

COLD OPEN

INT. FERRY WHEELHOUSE - DAWN

Navigator Fenn spreads a chart across the table. A small island is missing from the map.

FENN: The island has vanished.

ORA: It is at low tide.

The passenger peers through the window.

PASSENGER: I can see it.

The captain turns the chart upside down.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

EXT. FERRY DECK - DAWN

The ferry is due to take passengers around Gull Island. Fenn says the chart shows open water where the island should be. Ora asks him to check the chart date. It is eight years old and labeled LOW WATER SURVEY. The harbor master radios that the island's sandbar is exposed at low tide and submerged when the tide rises.

HARBOR MASTER: The island has not moved.

FENN: It is not on the page.

LUZ: The page is not the island.

The captain refuses to navigate without a visible coastline. The passenger says the island is still visible through the window. Fenn insists the map must be correct because it has a blue stamp. Luz notices the chart's transparent overlay has slipped out of alignment. It shows a tidal channel over the island, making the island appear missing.

ACT TWO

INT. WHEELHOUSE - MORNING

Ora asks Fenn to trace the route on the new tide table. Fenn lays the overlay on top of the chart, but its corner is torn. The island moves three inches west on paper. The passenger holds the sheet by the other side while Luz aligns the registration marks. The corrected map now shows Gull Island with a narrow passage around it.

FENN: It has returned.

ORA: It was under the overlay.

The harbor master asks whether the ferry can continue. Ora checks depth readings. The passage is deep enough at rising tide but too shallow near the eastern marker. Luz places a red clip on that section. Fenn starts to mark a new course, but his pencil rolls across the table and draws a line through the passenger manifest.

PASSENGER: I am now on the island.

Luz finds the pencil and uses a grease marker on the glass chart board instead. Ora plots a safe route outside the marker buoy. The passenger watches the real island slide slowly across the horizon as the ferry turns.

EXT. FERRY BRIDGE - MORNING

Fenn uses a hand compass to check the plotted route. The compass needle turns when it passes a metal toolbox. He sets the toolbox on the floor and tries again. This time the bearing matches the chart. The captain asks him to identify three fixed points: harbor light, east buoy, and the visible ridge on Gull Island.

FENN: The island should be behind the buoy.

ORA: It is beside it.

The ferry passes the buoy. The island appears on the other side of the ridge. The passenger points out that the chart has a second overlay for low tide. Luz holds it up to the window. The blue tidal channel masks the green island shape.

LUZ: We found it by removing a layer.

The harbor master asks for the updated course. Ora reads the bearing back. The harbor master corrects one degree, because the ferry has drifted while the chart was being repaired. Fenn plots the correction on the glass board and wipes away the earlier pencil line. The passenger says the island moved again. The crew explains the boat has moved relative to it.

PASSENGER: Then which one is lost?

FENN: The old line.

Ora logs the compass check, overlay alignment, and tide. The deckhand pins the old map to the notice board as a reminder to check dates.



The captain asks the passenger to compare the island through the window with the island symbol on the chart. The passenger points to the ridge and says both are in the same place, but the boat is passing it at an angle. Fenn records the note under navigation, not geography.

ACT THREE

EXT. FERRY DECK - MORNING

The ferry passes Gull Island at a safe distance. The island remains visible. Fenn updates the chart, writes the tide time, and replaces the torn overlay with a fresh clear sheet. The harbor master confirms the route. The passenger asks if the ferry can stop on the island. Ora says there is no dock.

PASSENGER: Then the map is not the only thing missing.

LUZ: There is a beach.

ORA: It is underwater again.

The captain orders the ferry back to the marked channel. Fenn records the island's position in the logbook and draws a tiny picture of it.

FINAL IMAGE: The map shows the island in blue ink while the passenger holds a tide chart over the window.
