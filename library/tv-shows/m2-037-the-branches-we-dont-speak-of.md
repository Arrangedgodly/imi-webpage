# The Branches We Don't Speak Of

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: serialized daytime soap operas, family secrets, and cliffhanger reveals*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-002*

Premise: At the Fig Tree Estate, two estranged siblings reunite to settle their late aunt's orchard, only to discover the inheritance includes a branch everyone assumed was decorative.

Cast: Sella, a dramatic macaque; Ruvan, her gibbon brother; Aunt Mela, a capuchin estate keeper; Nomi, a mandrill attorney; and Cress, a baboon groundskeeper.

## Episode: "The Branch in the Envelope"

### Cold open

INT. FIG TREE ESTATE, SUNSET

[Sella opens a long envelope. A dried leaf falls out. Ruvan watches from the doorway.]

SELLA: You came.

RUVAN: I was told the tree was in danger.

SELLA: The tree is always in danger when you arrive.

MELA: I found Aunt Piri's final note.

NOMI: It says the orchard is divided between you.

SELLA: Between us?

RUVAN: Aunt Piri knew we had not spoken in nine seasons.

SELLA: She also knew we never agreed about the south branch.

CRESS: The south branch is not in the orchard.

[Everyone turns toward a small potted tree.]

SELLA: What is that?

CRESS: The south branch.

[The potted tree sheds one leaf.]

RUVAN: It is dying.

MELA: It is dramatic.

### Act One

INT. ESTATE PARLOR

[The family sits around a table. Nomi unrolls a map. The south branch's pot is placed on a stool.]

NOMI: Aunt Piri's note says, "The orchard belongs to the two who know how to share."

SELLA: That is not a legal instruction.

NOMI: It is also written on the back of the property deed.

RUVAN: Aunt Piri always wrote on both sides.

MELA: She hated blank space.

CRESS: She once wrote a recipe on a fence.

SELLA: What does the fence have to do with the orchard?

CRESS: It has the recipe.

RUVAN: We can divide the trees equally.

SELLA: The east fig is worth more.

RUVAN: It is smaller.

SELLA: It produces sweeter figs.

RUVAN: It produces fewer figs.

SELLA: Quality over quantity.

RUVAN: That is easy to say when you want the sweet tree.

NOMI: We could appraise each tree.

MELA: The appraiser is Aunt Piri's old friend.

SELLA: Then the appraisal will be sentimental.

CRESS: He measures trunks by hugging them.

RUVAN: That's not an appraisal.

NOMI: It is a method.

[The potted south branch leans. Everyone reaches to keep it upright.]

SELLA: We are all holding it.

RUVAN: Because it is tipping.

MELA: This is the first time you have cooperated since the harvest festival.

SELLA: Don't make it sentimental.

### Act Two

EXT. ORCHARD, MORNING

[The family walks between trees. Cress carries the pot. Ruvan and Sella each hold one side of a measuring rope.]

CRESS: The rope is tangled.

SELLA: Give it to me.

RUVAN: You are pulling the wrong end.

SELLA: There is no wrong end.

RUVAN: The rope has two ends. One is attached to the pot.

CRESS: I tied it there so the branch would travel safely.

SELLA: We are measuring the fig tree.

MELA: You are measuring your patience.

NOMI: We can use the boundary markers.

[The markers form a zigzag through the orchard.]

RUVAN: Why does the line turn around the pond?

CRESS: Aunt Piri moved the line after the pond joined the garden club.

SELLA: Ponds cannot join clubs.

CRESS: This one paid its dues with lilies.

RUVAN: The line makes no sense.

NOMI: The deed says it follows the natural features.

SELLA: The pond is not natural. Aunt Piri built it.

MELA: She built it for frogs.

RUVAN: Then the frogs should have a vote.

[They hear a splash.]

CRESS: The frogs are present.

SELLA: We are not letting frogs decide the estate.

MELA: They have been here longer than the attorney.

NOMI: That is true.

[The potted branch begins to tilt again. Sella and Ruvan catch it together.]

RUVAN: We should plant it in the ground.

SELLA: In whose ground?

RUVAN: The center.

SELLA: The center isn't on the deed.

NOMI: We could add a shared plot.

SELLA: I thought you said it was divided.

NOMI: I can amend a family tree without a court filing.

### Act Three

INT. ESTATE PARLOR, EVENING

[Nomi places a new page beside Aunt Piri's note. Sella and Ruvan stand by the window.]

NOMI: The shared plot belongs to both of you, and the south branch will be planted there.

SELLA: We need to take care of it.

RUVAN: Together.

MELA: Aunt Piri would be pleased.

SELLA: Aunt Piri would say we took too long.

CRESS: She wrote that in the margin.

RUVAN: She knew we would argue.

NOMI: The note also says you must share the first harvest.

SELLA: How many figs will it produce?

CRESS: One, if it survives.

SELLA: We can split one fig.

RUVAN: Or plant a second branch.

MELA: There are cuttings in the shed.

SELLA: Why didn't you say that earlier?

MELA: You were busy fighting about the first branch.

RUVAN: I will take one cutting.

SELLA: I will take the other.

NOMI: That sounds like the orchard is divided again.

SELLA: No. We each plant one side of the shared plot.

RUVAN: We can meet in the middle.

CRESS: The middle is where the frog pond begins.

MELA: The frogs can supervise.

[The branch is set on the table. Sella and Ruvan each hold it while Cress adds soil to the pot.]

SELLA: I never wanted to lose the orchard.

RUVAN: Neither did I.

SELLA: I thought you left because you didn't care.

RUVAN: I left because every time I came back, we fought.

SELLA: We are fighting now.

RUVAN: We are also holding a tree together.

### Tag

EXT. SHARED PLOT, NIGHT

[The family plants the branch. A frog sits on the boundary marker.]

CRESS: The frog has the center.

NOMI: Then the frog gets the first fig.

SELLA: If there is one.

[The frog croaks. Ruvan and Sella exchange a look.]

[The frog settles on the marker beside the newly planted branch.]
