# The Detective Reads the Wrong Clock

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: police procedurals built around rigid timestamp evidence and detectives delivering dramatic deductions from clocks*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: DETECTIVE MARA, a macaque; OFFICER JUNE, a gibbon; CLOCKMAKER PELL, an orangutan; MUSEUM DIRECTOR, a baboon; GUARD OTTO, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. TOWN MUSEUM - NIGHT

A silver lantern sits in a glass case. The wall clock reads 9:13. Detective Mara enters with Officer June.

MARA: The lantern was seen at 9:13.

JUNE: The guard saw the clock.

MARA: That's the same thing.

The clock strikes nine. The lantern's reflection disappears from the glass.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

INT. MUSEUM GALLERY - NIGHT

The museum director says the lantern went missing during the broadcast of the history program. The display case is locked, the alarm did not sound, and the gallery camera shows no visitor entering. Guard Otto insists the lantern was there at 9:13 because he checked the clock during his patrol.

DIRECTOR: Everyone has a time.

MARA: Then we have a case.

JUNE: Or a clock.

Mara questions the clockmaker, who has been repairing the museum's large grandfather clock. Pell says the wall clock has a sponsor logo and was set to match the television control room's time.

PELL: It gains seven minutes every hour.

MARA: That is a very fast clock.

PELL: It is a very slow clock that is running fast.

June checks the broadcast schedule. At 9:13 on the wall clock, the program's host is still introducing the lantern. In the actual control-room log, the segment aired at 9:02. The lantern appears on camera at 9:04, then the lights dim for a commercial.

MARA: Someone moved it in two minutes.

JUNE: Or the camera looked away.

The director says the case was locked the entire time. Mara taps the glass and notices a narrow gap under the frame. The lantern's velvet stand is empty, but no glass is broken. Otto shows the patrol sheet: he recorded the case check at 9:13, then walked to the west stair.

OTTO: I looked through the glass. The lantern was shining.

PELL: That was the wall sconce behind it.

The team turns off the gallery lights. The silver lantern's reflection vanishes; the sconce remains bright. Mara looks at the case. There is no lantern inside.

ACT TWO

INT. SECURITY ROOM - NIGHT

June plays the broadcast footage frame by frame. During the commercial, the camera shows the display case reflected in a framed painting. A small hand reaches below the frame, lifts the lantern through the gap, and disappears. The hand wears a striped glove.

MARA: We have a hand.

JUNE: The stagehand wore striped gloves during the broadcast.

They interview the stagehand, who says the lantern was moved to the studio for a close-up and returned through the service hatch. The hatch is hidden behind the case's velvet skirt. The stagehand followed the show's prop list but forgot to sign the return log.

DIRECTOR: It is not stolen?

MARA: It is misplaced with a camera crew.

PELL: That's still a crime against my clock.

June finds the lantern in the studio's prop cabinet, beneath a card marked NOT A REAL LIGHT. The director complains that the show advertised an authentic artifact. The stagehand explains that the lantern's original bulb was replaced with a safe replica for filming. The real lantern never left the locked case.

Mara inspects it. The replica's silver paint matches the sconce reflection. The museum had two lanterns and no inventory label separating them. Otto saw the reflection of the real display, but the camera filmed the replica.

INT. MUSEUM SECURITY ROOM - NIGHT

Mara questions the museum director about the duplicate lantern. The director says the replica was made for a documentary and was supposed to be stored in the studio after filming. The stagehand borrowed it for the close-up but returned the wrong lantern. The real one never left its locked display.

DIRECTOR: The silver finish looked identical on camera.

MARA: It looked identical to a sconce.

JUNE: The actual lantern is copper.

The director insists the label called it silver. Pell opens the maintenance log and finds a note from the conservation team: "Copper lantern polished; appears silver under cold gallery lamps." Mara asks why the replica was silver. The director says the television set used warm lights, so the replica needed a colder finish to match.

MARA: You built a fake to match the lighting, then used it to identify the real one.

DIRECTOR: We built it to look authentic.

JUNE: The camera got fooled before the audience did.

The stagehand appears on a monitor and confirms the replica was left in the prop cabinet. June compares both lanterns side by side. The replica has a plastic handle; the real lantern has an etched maker's mark. The director asks that the case labels be corrected before morning tours. Mara writes "replica" on the prop list and "original" on the conservation card.



ACT THREE

INT. MUSEUM GALLERY - NIGHT

The director labels the real lantern and the replica. Pell resets the wall clock and disconnects it from the studio feed. Mara fills out the report. Officer June puts the striped gloves into the prop bin, not the evidence locker.

MARA: The clock was wrong.

JUNE: The reflection was wrong.

MARA: The inventory was worse.

Otto checks the newly labeled case. The real lantern glows under the proper display light.

FINAL IMAGE: The wall clock ticks accurately while Mara checks the time against her watch before writing it down.
