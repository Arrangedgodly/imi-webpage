# Message from Tomorrow's Tree

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: science fiction anthology convention, especially the time-loop warning that sends a crew chasing its own instructions*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-007*

Premise: A field crew receives a radio message from tomorrow telling them to "turn left at the tree," and spends the day following a voice that keeps getting closer.

Cast: Nara, a gibbon scientist; Pavi, a capuchin radio operator; Kito, a macaque gardener; and Piri, a baboon archivist.

Episode: "The Signal in the Bark"

COLD OPEN

INT. FIELD STATION, MORNING

[SFX: RADIO STATIC, THREE TAPS.]

PAVI: We have a message. "Turn left at the tree. Do not bring the cup."

KITO: Which tree?

Through the window stand six trees, each labeled TREE by Piri's field chart.

NARA: The timestamp says ten-thirty. Our clock says nine-fifty-five.

PIRI: Then the message is thirty-five minutes ahead. A modest future. Probably still taking questions.

Pavi's radio gives another tap. Kito slowly lowers a tin cup away from it.

ACT ONE

EXT. TREE ROW, MORNING

Pavi leads the crew toward the nearest tree. Kito holds the cup behind his back. The signal grows loud, then fades when Nara circles left around the trunk.

PIRI: It is strongest when we face the station.

NARA: A warning from tomorrow that points backward. The universe has developed a filing problem.

Pavi follows the static to a cable looped over a low branch. Nara stops him from yanking it. The cable runs safely to the station antenna, but its loose end taps bark in the breeze.

KITO: There are three taps in the message. The tree is answering.

PIRI: Trees do not use radios.

KITO: This one has a better signal than our station.

Pavi brings the radio close. A faint metallic clink sits under the voice. Kito admits he tested the cup by tapping it against the radio casing while the first test recording was running.

NARA: Nobody touch anything until we know what time it is.

PIRI: It is nine-fifty-eight. I wrote it down, so now it is official.

ACT TWO

INT. FIELD STATION, LATER

Pavi opens the transmission log. The warning is not live; it is a file scheduled to play at ten-thirty. A second file, labeled TEST 2, is also scheduled. The station clock loses seven minutes every hour because its battery is weak.

NARA: We have been treating a slow clock as a portal.

PIRI: It is still the only portal with a sign-in sheet.

Kito removes the cup from the desk. Pavi selects TEST 2 and presses PLAY. The metallic clink disappears, but the message now ends, "Bring the cup."

KITO: It has changed its mind.

NARA: Or we have changed the sound test.

They trace the recording. Pavi made it that morning while checking the antenna. His first line told the crew not to bring the cup because the clink overwhelmed the microphone. The second recording, made after he moved the cup, told them to bring it for comparison. The log displayed the scheduled playback time; Piri had read it as the time of transmission.

PAVI: I made the future sound like me.

PIRI: You do have a very recognizable future.

Nara asks Pavi to cancel both scheduled files. He presses CANCEL, but the radio continues to count down. A banner on its tiny screen reads: SCHEDULE LOCKED UNTIL PLAYBACK. The station's ancient recorder has one rule: a scheduled test cannot be erased while it is armed.

ACT TWO-B

EXT. TREE ROW, 10:29

The crew gathers beside the cable. Piri holds the log, Nara watches the clock, and Kito sets the cup on a stump far from the radio. Pavi stands with one hand over the recorder's stop switch.

At ten-thirty, the radio crackles.

[SFX: THREE TAPS.]

RECORDED PAVI: Turn left at the tree. Do not bring the cup.

Pavi reaches for STOP. The cable slips from the branch and the receiver goes quiet.

NARA: Don't pull it. That cable is keeping our whole signal alive.

Kito uses his long fingers to lift the slack loop clear of the bark. Piri points out that the antenna's loose guide has a manual release. Pavi climbs the station steps, turns the guide, and frees the cable without damaging the tree.

The receiver comes back on. The scheduled file finishes. Pavi switches to the live monitor. The crew hears its own voices arriving through the station speaker several seconds late, each one commenting on what the crew just did.

PIRI: My voice says, "Do not trust the voice."

PAVI: Do you trust the voice?

PIRI: I distrust the filing system.

ACT THREE

INT. FIELD STATION, EVENING

Pavi unplugs the weak clock and sets the recorder to live mode. He saves one test message: "This is a test. Stay where you are." Nara asks him to check the delay before broadcasting. He sends it once; it returns immediately.

Kito stands perfectly still, one foot lifted.

NARA: It says stay where you are.

KITO: I am respecting the science.

He lowers his foot. Piri deletes the two old scheduled files. The display reads NO FUTURE EVENTS.

PAVI: We solved the time loop.

PIRI: We solved a calendar with poor supervision.

TAG

Piri pockets his archive player and accidentally presses its button. It gives one soft click. He has kept a soundbite of the first test recording.

RECORDED PAVI: Do not bring the cup.

All four glance at the stump. The cup remains there, untouched.

KITO: Tomorrow agrees with us.

[SFX: RADIO TONE, THEME MUSIC.]
