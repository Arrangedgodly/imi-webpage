# Level Headed

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: home-renovation reality television*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A renovation crew has one weekend to make an old treehouse usable for a troop of young monkeys, while the camera team keeps asking for dramatic reveals that the structure cannot safely support.

Cast: Jojo, a careful capuchin carpenter; Fern, a macaque designer who loves bold paint and hates ladders; Miri, a gibbon electrician with a habit of testing every wire twice; Uncle Tock, a slow loris homeowner who wants a quiet place to read; and Pipkin, a squirrel monkey child who spots structural problems by climbing them.

## Episode: "The Room with No Floor"

### Cold open

EXT. OLD FIG TREE, MORNING

The hosts stand under the treehouse as a crew member pulls away a cloth. The reveal is a clean new staircase that ends six feet above the ground.

HOST: We wanted the family to have a dramatic first step.

Uncle Tock peers up at it.

TOCK: Where is the rest?

Pipkin climbs the trunk, tests the first rung, and points to a branch that has cracked through the landing. A small piece of floor drops onto the host's hard hat. He thanks the camera for capturing the moment.

### Act one

The production promised a reading room by Sunday. Tock wants a quiet chair for his field guides. His aunt built the treehouse from salvaged boards, adding rooms whenever cousins visited. It now has three doors and no uninterrupted floor.

Jojo begins with a measuring tape. Fern proposes a glass corner that would let Tock watch the pond. Miri explains that the glass would add weight to a branch already under strain. The host asks for a bigger problem shot. Jojo obliges by lifting a loose plank, revealing a wasp nest. The crew steps backward in the same direction and bumps into the ladder.

The crew asks Tock to imagine a quiet morning in the room. The doorway opens onto a wall. Miri points out that the wall holds the roof.

### Act two

Fern finds photos showing a door on every side. Each opened onto a branch that moved at a different angle. They remove the rigid panel and plan a light walkway anchored to the trunk.

 Fern paints a small patch of board in the show's bright turquoise. It looks cheerful and makes the room seem even more crooked. Pipkin takes the sample can, climbs outside, and marks the wobble on the trunk each time the wind changes. His lines reveal that one of the main branches is settling lower throughout the day.

Jojo stops work. The new room cannot safely hang from that branch. Tock asks how much can remain. Miri says the old reading nook on the trunk side is sound. They can repair that space and build a separate ground-level platform below for visits and storage.

The host says the audience expects a full transformation. Tock answers that the audience can visit when it is finished. He will not invite children into a room just because it looks good on camera.


JOJO: The landing moves.
FERN: It has a lively feel.
TOCK: I prefer still floors.
MIRI: The branch is settling.
HOST: Can we get a reveal?
PIPKIN: It revealed the crack.
JOJO: We need a brace.
FERN: Will it show?
TOCK: Better than falling.
HOST: Could it be invisible?
MIRI: The load cannot.
JOJO: Nor should the brace.
FERN: What about glass?
MIRI: Glass is heavy.
TOCK: So is gravity.
HOST: The family wants drama.
TOCK: I want a chair.
PIPKIN: The door opens backward.
JOJO: The whole room does.
FERN: I can repaint it.
MIRI: Paint won't hold weight.
FERN: I know that.
HOST: We need a before shot.
PIPKIN: It is still before.
TOCK: The floor has no floor.
HOST: That's a great line.
TOCK: It is a fact.
JOJO: We can save the nook.
MIRI: And unload the branch.
FERN: We lose the window.

MIRI: Not through that limb.
PIPKIN: The branch says no.

TOCK: It has a point.

TOCK: The book room fits.

JOJO: After we make it safe.

### Act three

The crew removes the heavy glass and the rigid panel. Jojo replaces the cracked landing with a small platform fastened to the trunk, where the wood is healthy. Miri rewires a reading lamp and routes the cable away from a spot where rainwater collects. Fern paints the door turquoise, then lets Tock choose a quieter green for the walls.

Pipkin makes a paper model of the safe walkway. They test the shape on the ground. The bridge curves around the settling branch and joins the platform at the trunk. Every support rests on sound wood.

By Sunday, Tock's reading nook has a chair, shelves, and a view of the pond. He opens a field guide and finds a pressed leaf his aunt used as a bookmark. The host asks for a reaction. Tock says, "I can hear the wind without listening for a crack."

The crew moves the salvageable boards to the ground-level platform. Tock says the children can use it for drawing or snacks, but he wants an adult to check it before anyone climbs. Pipkin writes that rule on a board and hangs it beside the ladder.

### Tag

A week later, the crew returns for a final visit. Tock reads by the window. The host asks for a shot of him taking the stairs. Tock points to the ladder. The host declines.

Pipkin has painted the missing staircase onto a sheet of cardboard. It leans against the trunk and has a tiny painted sign that reads "Imaginary access only."

FERN: At least the paint is level.

Tock turns a page. Everyone stays quiet long enough to hear the leaves move.
