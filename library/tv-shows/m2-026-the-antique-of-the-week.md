# The Antique of the Week

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: antiques appraisal program*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A traveling appraiser visits a community hall where neighbors bring old objects, only to find that the most valuable item is the one nobody thought to bring.

Cast: Olli, a careful macaque appraiser; Bea, a capuchin host; Mr. Brindle, a baboon with a cracked teapot; Nessa, a gibbon with an old brass bell; and Sam, a squirrel monkey caretaker who owns the hall's original key.

## Episode: "The Bell and the Teapot"

### Cold open

INT. COMMUNITY HALL, DAY

Bea presents a brass bell. Nessa rings it. Nothing happens. Mr. Brindle taps his cracked teapot. It rings louder than the bell.

OLLI: I will need to appraise the sound as well as the object.

Sam enters and says the bell belongs on the wall. The mounting hook is empty.

### Act one

The show records at the village hall. Nessa says the bell came from the first schoolhouse. Brindle claims his teapot belonged to an explorer. Olli asks for proof.

Sam opens it with a worn key marked "Hall, 1912." Olli finds the same foundry mark as the bell.

The bell lacks its clapper. The teapot bears a regional pottery stamp, not the explorer's mark.

### Act two

The historian confirms the bell ended each school day. The village blacksmith made both the hall key and its hook.

Olli appraises the bell modestly because it is common brass and missing its clapper. Nessa says she hoped it would be worth enough to repair the hall roof. The appraiser explains that an object's sale price and community value are different measures. The bell matters because generations recognized its sound.

The crew asks Sam to show how the old key works. It fits the hall's storage cabinet, which has not been opened in years. Inside are a set of school attendance books and the bell's clapper, wrapped in cloth. The clapper has a date and the initials of the first caretaker. Sam recognizes the handwriting as his grandmother's.


[Olli places the bell on a felt pad.]
OLLI: It is brass, not gold.
NESSA: It rang for school.
BEA: Can it ring now?
NESSA: The clapper is missing.
MR. BRINDLE: My teapot rings.
OLLI: It is cracked.
BRINDLE: It has a clear note.
SAM: The hook is empty.
BEA: What hung there?
SAM: This bell.
OLLI: Do you have proof?
SAM: I have the key.
OLLI: A key proves a door.
SAM: It opens the cabinet.
NESSA: Try it.
SAM: The lock sticks.
BRINDLE: Add oil?
SAM: Not yet.
OLLI: Turn it gently.
[SFX: CABINET OPENS.]
BEA: What's inside?
SAM: Attendance books.
NESSA: And a wrapped bundle.
OLLI: That may be the clapper.
SAM: My grandmother wrapped it.
BRINDLE: Does it fit?
OLLI: It fits the bell.
BEA: What is the price?
OLLI: Modest at auction.
NESSA: And here?
OLLI: Important to the village.
SAM: It stays in the hall.
BRINDLE: My teapot stays home.
BEA: The roof still needs fixing.
SAM: We'll hold a fundraiser.
NESSA: The bell can invite people.
OLLI: If you hang it safely.
SAM: The hook is repaired.
BEA: Ring it once.
[SFX: A CLEAR, SMALL BELL NOTE.]

### Act three

The historian suggests returning the clapper to the bell. Nessa asks whether the bell should be sold to raise money for repairs. Sam says the hall belongs to the village, and the bell should stay. Olli agrees that keeping it does not make it less valuable. He writes a replacement appraisal focused on provenance, condition, and local importance, while explaining that its market price remains low.

The caretaker repairs the hook. The crew hangs the bell above the hall entrance. Nessa rings it once. A soft clear note carries into the kitchen, where volunteers are making tea. Mr. Brindle's teapot is displayed beside a photograph of his aunt, who made it. He says he will keep it at home.

The hall committee decides to hold a roof fundraiser. They will show the bell and photographs, but will not sell either object. Bea asks Olli to summarize the appraisal. He says the bell's value is modest at auction, substantial in the village, and impossible to state in one number. Bea asks him to give a number anyway. He says the committee should insure it for replacement cost, then consult a local broker.

### Tag

The show wraps. Sam locks the hall. He uses the key, then rings the bell once to test it.

A baker carrying tea pauses outside.

SAM: We are closed.

BAKER: I heard the bell.

Sam looks at the bell, then lets the baker in for a cup.


### Additional moment

After filming, Sam brings out a photograph of the hall's first winter gathering. The bell hangs above a table of soup bowls, and the key is visible on his grandmother's belt. Nessa recognizes her own mother in the back row. She asks whether the picture can go beside the bell. The historian agrees to make a copy for the committee. Olli notes the photographer's name and the date on the back, then explains that a clear record will help future visitors understand the objects. Bea thanks the residents for showing the history behind ordinary things. Sam locks the cabinet with the old key and puts it back on his ring. The hall is quiet again, except for the tea kettle.
