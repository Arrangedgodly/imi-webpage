# The Weather Channel Stages a Rainy Sunrise

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: weather-channel conventions, manufactured storm graphics, and meteorologists coping with a rain rig that wets the actual forecast board*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-020*

Cast: Anchor Odo, an orangutan; meteorologist Sela, a macaque; field reporter Vex, a baboon; producer Nari, a gibbon; camera operator Pip, a capuchin; studio manager Rumi, a tortoise.

COLD OPEN

INT. WEATHER STUDIO - DAWN

A blue sunrise graphic brightens behind the anchor. Water drips from the edge of the screen.

ODO: We have rain in the studio.

SFX: A light drip hits the desk.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The weather channel is filming a sunrise special with a dramatic cloud wall and a small rain curtain behind the anchor. Sela's real forecast calls for clear skies. Producer Nari says the set's rain effect is supposed to stop before the live segment. Vex reports from the outdoor roof garden, where no rain is falling.

SELA: The actual forecast is dry until evening.

ODO: The screen behind me is wet.

NARI: The rain curtain is a visual effect. It is supposed to fall into a tray.

PIP: The tray is full. Water is running behind the sunrise panel.

VEX: From the roof, I can confirm the sky is clear. The studio is the only place raining.

ODO: We could call it a hyperlocal shower.

SELA: We should call it a set leak.

The crew stops the effect and unplugs the pump. Pip lifts the edge of the screen and finds a loose drain tube. Nari wants to maintain the sunrise shot. Sela refuses to issue a forecast while the water is near an electrical panel.

SELA: We will wait for the studio manager to check the floor.

NARI: The live window starts in three minutes.

ODO: The forecast can begin after the room is safe.

ACT TWO

Studio manager Rumi confirms the power strip is dry and moves it away from the water. Pip drains the tray into a bucket. The tube is reattached. Residual water drains from the overhead rig onto the panel; during the initial effect, the small tray could not contain the pump's flow. Nari asks whether they can lower the flow. Sela explains the effect will look like mist instead of rain.

NARI: Can we keep the clouds and lose the rain?

PIP: The pump can stay off. The cloud wall is a projected image, not water.

VEX: The roof garden is still dry. I can stay on the live link.

ODO: We'll show the cloud graphic while Sela gives the clear forecast.

NARI: The channel promised viewers a sunrise storm.

SELA: It promised a sunrise segment. A storm would be a forecast claim.

Vex holds a paper wind streamer in the roof garden. It hangs still. He shifts it near the roof fan, and the streamer flutters. Odo begins to describe high winds; Sela stops him and asks Vex to show the garden without the fan.

VEX: The wind speed is zero out here. The fan is a studio prop.

ODO: The picture made it look gusty.

SELA: That's why we report the measured conditions, not the background effect.

With Rumi watching the dry panel, the crew reconnects the pump for one controlled test, with the tray removed and its outlet draining into a bucket. The tube drains properly after Nari clears a kink. They stop the pump and disconnect it again. Pip wipes the screen with a dry cloth. A damp spot remains beneath the panel, so the studio manager checks it before allowing the cameras back.

PIP: The floor is dry. The strip is dry. The power panel is clear.

NARI: We can begin the live forecast.

Before the live segment, Pip places a lockout tag on the rain-pump switch and tapes the now-disconnected tube into a collection bag. Nari adds a note to the production checklist: the effect cannot return until a larger catch tray is installed and tested at the designated effects bench. The crew runs the sunrise panel without water, checks beneath the desk, and confirms that the floor and cable ramp are dry.

PIP: Pump is disconnected, tube is bagged, and the desk is dry.

SELA: Then the screen can stay as a picture. We do not need simulated rain to report real weather.

ACT THREE

Odo delivers a live forecast without the rain curtain. Sela reports clear sunrise, light winds, and a chance of evening showers. Vex shows the dry garden, then closes the public link without pretending the roof is in a storm. The production uses the repaired sunrise panel as a static background while the pump remains switched off.

ODO: The only rain we've seen this morning was in the studio.

SELA: And it is not expected to continue.

NARI: We will use the visual rain effect only after the tray is enlarged and tested at the designated effects bench.

The private crew talkback remains open after the public link closes. Vex hears Pip packing away the test bucket.

VEX: Does the bucket go in the forecast?

SELA: Only if it starts raining outside.

PIP: The test is over. I have emptied and dried the bucket, then stored it beside the tagged rig for the next effects-bench test.

FINAL IMAGE: The sunrise graphic glows over a dry desk; the pump is tagged and disconnected, the tube is bagged, and the emptied test bucket is stored beside the rig.
