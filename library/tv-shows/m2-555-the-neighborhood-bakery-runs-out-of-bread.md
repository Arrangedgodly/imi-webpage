# The Neighborhood Bakery Runs Out of Bread

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: workplace sitcoms, small-business emergencies, and staff who turn an empty bread rack into a management crisis*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: OWNER MIRA, a macaque; BAKER TOLL, an orangutan; CASHIER, a gibbon; DELIVERY DRIVER, a baboon; CUSTOMER, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. BAKERY - MORNING

The front door opens. The bread rack is empty. A customer looks at it.

CUSTOMER: Do you have bread?

MIRA: We are a bakery.

TOLL: We are out of bread.

The cashier flips the sign to OPEN. It falls off the door.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

Mira calls the delivery driver. The truck is delayed behind a parade. Toll says he can bake a fresh batch, but the oven needs thirty minutes and the breakfast rush has already arrived. The cashier offers pastries instead. The customer says they came for a loaf.

CASHIER: We have croissants.

CUSTOMER: That is a different shape.

Mira asks Toll what is in the oven. Toll has a tray of dough that did not rise. He says the yeast was replaced by baking powder. The cashier reads the supply label and finds that the flour delivery is scheduled for tomorrow. Mira says they have flour. The delivery driver confirms that he left it at the back door yesterday.

ACT TWO

INT. BAKERY KITCHEN - MORNING

The flour is stacked behind a tall box of napkins. Toll reaches for it, but the box tips. Mira and the cashier catch it. The flour bag is intact, but its label reads CAKE MIX. Toll opens it and identifies it by smell. He says it is bread flour; the distributor reused a bag.

MIRA: We need to bake now.

TOLL: We need yeast.

The cashier finds a small starter jar in the refrigerator, labeled KEEP ALIVE. Toll asks whether it is for bread or a science project. The owner says it was from last week's sourdough demonstration. Toll uses it to start a small batch. Mira explains to customers that the bread will be ready at nine. The customer asks whether they can wait. The cashier hands out number cards.

CASHIER: You are number one.

CUSTOMER: I am the only one.

[More customers enter and take numbers. By the time bread is ready, the waiting line has reached the window. The delivery driver returns with flour and asks where to unload. Mira points to the empty rack, now filled with fresh loaves. The driver says the second shipment is for a cafe across the street.]

DELIVERY DRIVER: Which bakery is this?

MIRA: The one with bread.

INT. BAKERY KITCHEN - LATER

Toll checks the starter jar. It bubbles, but only on one side. He rotates it and sees that the refrigerator shelf is not level. The starter has settled. The cashier asks whether it is still alive. Toll stirs it and watches the bubbles rise.

TOLL: It is awake.

MIRA: Can it make bread?

TOLL: It can try.

The oven timer rings. A tray of yesterday's rolls warms through. Mira offers them as samples while the new dough rises. The customer tastes one and asks whether the loaves will be ready before the bus leaves. The cashier checks the number cards. There are twelve customers waiting; number one is now number seven because a family joined the line under one ticket.

CASHIER: The queue has multiplied.

MIRA: Count the people, not the cards.

The delivery driver unloads flour at the back door. Toll asks for yeast, and the driver finds an unopened packet in the truck. Mira checks the label and date. It is fresh. Toll divides the dough into smaller loaves so the oven can bake them in two batches. The first batch comes out golden. The second is still rising. The cashier writes two pickup times on the board and gives customers the choice to wait or return.

CUSTOMER: I will take the warm roll now and the loaf later.

MIRA: That is a sensible order.

The customer circles a time on the pickup card. The queue shortens without anyone leaving empty-handed.


Mira marks the warm rolls as samples, not full loaves. Toll writes the next baking time on the oven board, and the cashier stops reusing the first customer's number card.

ACT THREE

The shop sells its fresh loaves and gives each customer a number for the next batch. The customer buys one loaf and a croissant, saying they have learned to appreciate shapes. Toll writes YEAST on the starter jar. Mira replaces the sign with one taped securely to the door.

CASHIER: We are still open.

TOLL: Now we are also a bakery.

FINAL IMAGE: A single loaf sits in the front window while the cashier calls number one, who is still waiting outside.
