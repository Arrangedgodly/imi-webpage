# Shelf Life

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: retail mockumentary*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: Staff at a tiny neighborhood hardware shop try to handle a rush of customers before the owner changes the store layout based on a survey filled out by one person.

Cast: Della, a pragmatic macaque owner; Finch, a gibbon clerk with long arms and poor ladder judgment; Rue, a capuchin cashier who remembers every customer; Mel, a shy langur stocker; and the unseen documentary crew, whose camera keeps appearing in reflective paint cans.

## Episode: "Aisle Three Has Moved"

### Cold open

INT. HARDWARE SHOP, MORNING

Della unveils a new layout map. Finch squints at it.

FINCH: Where is aisle three?

DELLA: It is now aisle six.

RUE: And what was aisle six?

Della checks the map. It says "customer journey." A customer enters and immediately asks where the screws are. Everyone points in a different direction.

### Act one

The shop has moved its shelves to make the store feel more modern. Della says a survey showed customers like open sight lines. Rue asks how many people took the survey. Della says one. The respondent did not leave a name, but wrote "more aisle" in the comments section.

Finch has moved the screws near the paint so customers can build a shelf and then paint it. Mel has moved the paint near the register because she does not want to carry heavy cans across the shop. Rue has placed the register where the light is good. Della walks through the aisles and cannot find the broom.

A customer asks for a hinge. Finch brings a doorstop, then a hinge, then a bag of screws. The customer thanks him and says the doorstop will be useful too. Della calls it a successful cross-sell. Rue says the customer asked for a hinge and received a small collection.

### Act two

The documentary crew interviews staff about the new layout. Finch says it is easy to see everything, including the camera operator reflected in the paint cans. Mel says she likes having the heavy stock near the back because the handcart can turn there. Rue says the old layout was easier for regulars, but the new one has brought in customers who like wandering.

Della asks each employee to name one improvement. The answers conflict. Finch wants a better ladder. Mel wants a second handcart. Rue wants a sign showing where common items moved. Della says signs make a shop feel confusing. A customer asks for a sign. Della points to the blank wall.

A sudden delivery arrives. The driver has stacked boxes according to the original shelf plan. There is no room for the new paint display. The staff begin to move cans. Finch climbs a ladder and reaches for a heavy bucket. Mel tells him to come down and use the handcart. He says the camera makes the ladder look less tall. Mel asks the camera operator if that is true. The operator steps back.


[A customer wheels a handcart into the crowded aisle.]
RUE: What are you looking for?
CUSTOMER: Screws for a shelf.
FINCH: They moved aisle three.
DELLA: Aisle three is aisle six.
CUSTOMER: What was six?
MEL: Paint.
CUSTOMER: Where is paint now?
FINCH: Beside the screws.
RUE: We can walk you there.
CUSTOMER: I can see it.
DELLA: That's the new layout.
RUE: The cart cannot turn.
MEL: I'll move the cans.
DELLA: The survey asked for space.
RUE: One person answered it.
DELLA: They wrote "more aisle."
CUSTOMER: They meant this aisle.
FINCH: We can measure it.
MEL: I brought the tape.
DELLA: Why is it in paint?
MEL: It was next to screws.
RUE: Let's move the heavy stock.
FINCH: The ladder is too short.
DELLA: Don't climb higher.
FINCH: I'm only reaching.
MEL: Use the handcart.
FINCH: I can carry one can.
MEL: The cart carries six.
CUSTOMER: Where are the hinges?
FINCH: Here, beside the doorstops.
RUE: Is that a cross-sell?
DELLA: It is a shelf.
CUSTOMER: I'll take the hinge.
FINCH: And the doorstop?
CUSTOMER: I own a door.
RUE: An honest sale.
DELLA: Put up the signs.
MEL: The signs are in aisle six.

### Act three

Della gathers the staff and asks them to restore the old layout. Rue points out the store can keep the wider center aisle while returning common hardware to its old shelf. Mel suggests moving heavy items along the back wall. Finch asks for a fixed ladder with a safety rail. Della agrees and orders one from a local supplier.

They work together to move the screws, hinges, paint, and hand tools. Rue makes clear signs with simple arrows and the aisle numbers customers know. Della puts a small display near the register for useful add-ons, but labels it "extras" rather than pretending it is the main reason people came in.

The next customer finds screws without asking. The following customer finds a broom, which had been under the camera tripod. Della watches him pay and nods toward the crew. The layout is not perfect, but regulars can navigate it and the staff can restock safely.

Della pins the survey beside the staff schedule. She writes "one response" at the top. Rue says the anonymous customer may return. Finch says if they do, he will ask where they want the screws. Mel puts the handcart near the delivery door. The crew captures Della ringing up a small bag of nails, then waits while she helps an elderly customer carry a bucket to the car.

### Tag

A new survey arrives. It has one answer: "More signs."

Della hangs the survey beside the first one. Rue adds an arrow pointing to the sign wall.

FINCH: The customer has a journey.

Della asks him to stop narrating the store.
