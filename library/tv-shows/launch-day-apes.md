# Launch Day Apes

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: space exploration drama convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: A small research crew reaches a moon whose only known resource is a field of rocks that keep rolling back into their survey grid.

Cast: Commander Sela Bonobo, a patient pilot who hates grand speeches; Dr. Venn, a mandrill geologist with sensitive hearing; Rook, a young langur mechanic who labels every screw; Anik, a macaque botanist responsible for the ship's food garden; Mission Control's Tamsin, whose calm voice hides a very full inbox.

## Episode: "The Moving Survey"

### Cold open

EXT. MOON OF PELL, DAY

A survey flag stands alone in a grey plain. Sela and Venn approach in suits. Venn kneels to photograph a fist-sized stone. The stone rolls three centimeters, directly over the flag's shadow.

VENN: Did you touch it?

SELA: I am holding a camera.

The stone rolls back to its first mark. Venn's recorder emits a small, offended beep.

### Act one

The crew has six hours to map a ridge before the ship's orbit takes them behind the moon. Mission Control wants a clean mineral reading for the first public transmission. Tamsin says investors enjoy a simple image. Sela says rocks rarely organize themselves for photographs.

Venn plants a grid of stakes. Every marker shifts by morning. He suspects magnetic dust. Rook checks the rover's wheel bearings and finds no interference. Anik studies the terrain. The stones all lie at the same shallow angle, even those too heavy for the wind to move.

Back aboard, the garden's water line has frozen. Anik uses warm waste heat from the radio stack to thaw it, but the computer warns that the crew has moved one degree outside the prescribed temperature range. Tamsin asks them to preserve the garden for a later crew. Anik says the later crew will not eat a warning light.

Sela sends a message to Control proposing a delay. Tamsin asks for a reason that fits in the mission update. Sela says the reason is the moon is behaving strangely. Tamsin types. The transmitted version reads: "Terrain conditions remain under review."

### Act two

Venn spends the afternoon logging the rocks. Each time he returns to a previous marker, the stone has moved, but the tracks point downhill, toward a shallow bowl. The moon's surface is not shifting at random. Small quakes loosen the loose stones, and the weak gravity lets them roll farther than expected. Their careful grid has been placed across an old rockfall path.

He wants to move the survey north. Control objects because the new area was not in the approved map. Sela reminds Tamsin that the map was made from orbit, where the field looked flat. Tamsin says she has a supervisor asking why the crew cannot follow the marked boxes. Sela says the boxes are floating above the ground on a screen.

Rook and Anik try to secure the rover with a drill anchor. The drill bounces off a buried sheet of glassy rock. Venn says the surface layer formed when an ancient lava flow cooled. It will support a rover if they spread the weight, but a deep anchor will crack it. The simplest route out runs over the thinnest section.

Anik has another idea. The frozen water line can feed small steam bursts through a spare hose, loosening the dust around the stones without disturbing the crust. Rook calls it a heated broom. Venn dislikes the name, then uses it in his notes.

### Act three

The crew clears a narrow track with short pulses of warm vapor. Stones slide into the shallow bowl and stop. They mark a safe path to the ridge and collect samples from the exposed rock. The mineral reading is not the one Control requested. Venn finds evidence of a porous layer that could hold water under the surface.

Tamsin requests a staged shot of Sela holding the sample against the stars. Sela holds it up, then lowers it to check for a crack. The camera catches her face instead of the sample.

SELA: That's the part worth sending.

Control receives the live field report. Tamsin's supervisor asks why the planned grid changed. Tamsin replies that the first plan assumed the rocks would stay put. She includes the new map and the timing of the little quakes. The data draws a quiet minute from the room, then her supervisor asks for the map in the morning packet.

The crew returns to orbit with the garden alive and the sample sealed. Venn says the moon did not move the rocks. The crew picked a place where the rocks were already going. Sela tells him that distinction belongs in the report.

### Tag

A rock rolls across the empty survey grid and settles over the camera tripod. Mission Control's public feed shows a close-up of grey stone.

Tamsin watches the feed from Earth. She changes the caption from "The Moon's First Greeting" to "Camera Placement Test, Unplanned." Her supervisor asks if the original caption was better.

TAMSIN: Only if the rock had signed a release.
