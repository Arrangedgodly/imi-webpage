# The Meteor Shower Missed Its Cue

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: live astronomy broadcasts, countdown graphics, telescope close-ups, and hosts who promise a sky event at an exact minute*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-014*

Logline: A planetarium broadcast promises a meteor shower at nine, but the host's countdown graphic keeps triggering the ceiling stars before anything crosses the sky.

Cast: Presenter Omi, a macaque; astronomer Vali, a gibbon; camera operator Koro, a capuchin; technician Piri, a baboon; audience guest Tavi, an orangutan.

COLD OPEN

INT. PLANETARIUM - NIGHT

The ceiling lights flash. A streak crosses the dome.

OMI: The shower has begun.

VALI: That was the opening graphic.

PIRI: We are still forty minutes from the forecast peak.

ACT ONE

Omi introduces the live sky watch. Vali explains the meteor shower may be visible during a broad window, not at a precise minute. The screen shows a countdown to nine o'clock. Piri says the automatic star display is linked to the countdown.

OMI: Can we turn off the stars until then?

PIRI: The stars are on. The streak graphic is what keeps firing.

Koro points the telescope camera at the actual night sky through the open dome slit. It shows clouds. Tavi asks whether the clouds will clear. Vali says they may, but the program cannot promise it. Omi asks for an indoor segment about the meteor shower's path.

ACT TWO

Vali uses a globe and a small lamp to show how Earth's rotation affects what viewers can see. Omi asks the camera to show the globe. Koro frames the lamp and loses the globe. Piri moves the lamp so its cord no longer crosses the platform. It shines on the ceiling, activating the star graphic again.

VALI: The studio lamp is not a meteor.

OMI: It is a light in the sky.

VALI: It is a light in the dome.

Piri disables the automatic sequence. The dome goes dark. The audience waits. Omi asks Tavi if he has seen a meteor before. He says once, while taking the bus home. Omi asks him to describe it. Tavi says it was a brief streak, and then the bus passed under a streetlight.

TAVI: I thought there might be another one.

KORO: We have streetlights outside too.

ACT TWO-B

The clouds thin. Vali sees a small point moving across the camera view. Koro zooms in. The point becomes a blinking light. Piri checks its path and identifies it as a satellite. Omi asks if the show can call it a precursor. Vali says it is an artificial satellite, not a meteor.

OMI: It is still something crossing the sky.

VALI: We can describe what it is.

Tavi asks why the satellite blinks. Koro says its rotating panels reflect sunlight. Vali explains briefly, then returns the camera to a wider view. The countdown graphic reaches zero. The dome begins to flash again. Piri cuts power to the graphic before it can create a fake streak.

PIRI: The clock reached nine. The shower did not receive the cue.

The audience laughs. Vali points to the real sky. One meteor crosses the camera view, faint and fast. Koro stays wide. Omi starts to announce it but waits until it has passed.

VALI: That one was a meteor.

TAVI: I saw it.

ACT TWO-B - VIEWER QUESTION

A child in the audience asks whether a meteor could hit the planetarium roof. Omi looks at the dome and asks Piri to show the roof. The camera points up; the ceiling star effect wakes and projects a large cartoon crater directly above the host.

OMI: I am now standing in the impact zone.

VALI: That is a projector. The dome is a roof.

TAVI: The roof has excellent graphics.

Piri cuts the effect and turns on the house lights. Vali shows the audience a small meteorite fragment behind glass, explaining that it is a specimen, not a prediction. Omi tries to move the case to the center mark, but the camera cable stops her. Koro asks her to leave it on the table. She does, then notices the audience view now includes the fire exit sign.

OMI: At least the exit is real.

VALI: We should keep it visible.

They return to the telescope feed. Tavi checks the clouds against the dome display; the real view has no animated streaks. Omi asks if the show can announce a meteor when one appears. Vali says only after verifying the footage. Koro sets a wide frame and locks it off.

ACT THREE

The crew shows the short recording once. Vali confirms the time and explains that the shower can continue after the broadcast. Omi stops the countdown and removes the claim that the peak would occur at nine. Koro keeps the telescope on a wide section of sky instead of magnifying a single blinking point.

OMI: We have one confirmed meteor, one satellite, and a dome that is no longer making either.

PIRI: The dome is a ceiling.

OMI: A helpful ceiling.

Vali invites viewers to keep watching the sky from a dark, safe place. The control room leaves the camera running without graphics. Another faint streak appears near the edge of frame; the team logs its time and does not announce until Vali checks the footage.

TAG

The credits start. The countdown screen displays "NEXT EVENT: TOMORROW." Piri turns it off before the dome can flash.

FINAL IMAGE: The telescope camera holds the real night sky, empty for a moment, while the audience waits without a timer.
