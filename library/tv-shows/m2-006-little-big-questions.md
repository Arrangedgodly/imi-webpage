# Little Big Questions

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: children's educational program*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A curious host and a troop of young helpers set out to explain why shadows change, while their well-meaning puppet keeps giving answers that sound certain and are completely wrong.

Cast: Zaza, a lively marmoset host; Professor Pebble, a cloth puppet with a removable felt hat; Rue, a thoughtful young macaque; twins Tiko and Taka, energetic squirrel monkeys; and Aunt Luma, a patient langur caretaker who likes questions better than quizzes.

## Episode: "Where Did the Shadow Go?"

### Cold open

EXT. PLAYGROUND, MORNING

Professor Pebble casts a huge shadow against the wall. Zaza steps between him and the sun. The shadow disappears.

PEBBLE: I have been erased by science!

Zaza moves aside. The shadow returns, wearing a felt hat that is still on Pebble's head.

ZAZA: Or maybe a shadow is something light makes when it meets an object.

Pebble turns to the wall and bows. The shadow bows too.

### Act one

Rue asks why the tree's shadow changes length between morning and noon. Pebble says the sun walks around it. Aunt Luma asks if the sun has feet. Pebble checks his pockets.

Zaza draws a sun, a tree, and a shadow on a board. The children arrange blocks around a lamp. Rue moves it and notices the shadow changes sides.

They mark the tree's shadow at breakfast and lunch. Tiko wants to wait beside the chalk line. Taka says they can play. Pebble says the tree is stretching because it likes the ground.

### Act two

At noon, the children compare the marks. The shadow is short, but it has not vanished. Rue says the sunlight reaches the ground from high overhead, so the tree blocks a smaller patch behind it. Zaza demonstrates with a flashlight and a toy tree. When the light is high, the shadow hugs the base. When the light is low, it stretches across the board.

Pebble insists the sun is still walking. Taka turns the globe on the set. The globe rotates while the lamp stays still. Zaza explains that Earth spins, so the sun appears to move across the sky. Pebble says he also spins when he is confused. The children try turning gently in place and stop before anyone gets dizzy.

Rue wonders whether a shadow has color. The group compares a shadow under the tree to the dim shape beneath a red cloth. The shaded spot looks cooler and darker, but it is still lit by light bouncing from nearby leaves. Zaza says shadows can look different depending on what light reaches them. Pebble paints his own shadow purple, then realizes paint only changes the paper.

A cloud passes. The tree's shadow becomes faint. Tiko says the shadow went away. Rue points to the soft outline on the grass. The cloud is blocking some direct sunlight, so the edges blur. Aunt Luma asks the children to look at their own hands. Their shadows are pale under the cloud, then sharpen as it moves on.


ZAZA: What makes a shadow?
PEBBLE: A tiny ghost.
AUNT LUMA: No ghosts today.
TIKO: The sun is bright.
TAKA: The tree blocks light.
RUE: Then we see shade.
PEBBLE: My shadow is hungry.
ZAZA: Shadows cannot eat.
PEBBLE: It followed my lunch.
AUNT LUMA: You moved it.
PEBBLE: I moved the lunch.
TIKO: The lamp is high.
TAKA: The shadow is small.
RUE: Move the lamp lower.
PEBBLE: Now I am huge.
ZAZA: The puppet is closer.
PEBBLE: Science made me tall.
AUNT LUMA: Your feet stayed put.
TIKO: The cloud is here.
TAKA: The shadow is soft.
RUE: The light got scattered.
PEBBLE: The cloud stole it.
ZAZA: It blocked some sunlight.
PEBBLE: Give it back.
AUNT LUMA: Ask the weather.
TIKO: The cloud moved away.
TAKA: The shadow is sharp.
PEBBLE: I knew it would.
RUE: You said it was stolen.
PEBBLE: I changed my theory.
ZAZA: That's how we learn.
PEBBLE: Do I get a badge?
AUNT LUMA: Make one yourself.
PEBBLE: Mine is enormous.
TIKO: The lamp is close.
TAKA: Your badge looks giant.
PEBBLE: I am a genius.
RUE: You are near the lamp.
PEBBLE: Same thing for now.

### Act three

The children make a shadow theater from a sheet and a lamp. They first put the puppet close to the sheet. Its shape looks small and crisp. Then they move it close to the lamp. The shape grows enormous, so Pebble appears to be a monster with long ears. He frightens himself and runs behind the curtain.

Zaza asks how they can make an enormous shadow without becoming enormous. Taka moves a cardboard moon nearer the lamp. The moon shape expands across the sheet. The children explain that an object blocks light, and its shadow appears on a surface behind it. Moving the object or the light changes the shadow's size.

Rue writes the day's discoveries on the board. The sun looks like it moves because Earth spins. A shadow forms where light is blocked. Clouds soften shadows. A shadow can change size and shape, but it is not a creature following the object. Pebble returns, asking whether he can keep his shadow anyway. Zaza says it is already coming home with him.

The children pack the lamp and leave the chalk marks for the next class to observe. Aunt Luma says questions do not need a prize at the end. Tiko asks for one anyway. Pebble awards everyone a paper badge shaped like a shadow, each cut to a different size.

### Tag

Pebble walks toward the exit and sees his shadow on the wall. He tiptoes. It tiptoes too. He waves. It waves back.

PEBBLE: I knew it was friendly.

Zaza moves the lamp. The shadow stretches across the door.

PEBBLE: And very tall.

The children wave goodbye to the wall before they leave.
