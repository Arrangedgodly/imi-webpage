# The Game After the Game

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: sports documentary*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A film crew follows a veteran macaque cricket captain through the last season of her neighborhood team, where the toughest opponent is a field that floods every spring.

Cast: Captain Daro, a disciplined macaque batter; Fen, a young langur bowler; Tika, a squirrel monkey scorer; Coach Rumi, an orangutan who repairs equipment; and the documentary narrator, who tries to make every practice sound historic.

## Episode: "The Wet Wicket"

### Cold open

EXT. CRICKET FIELD, MORNING

The team jogs onto the field. Daro takes three steps, plants her foot, and slides across the grass. Fen skids into a hay bale. Tika raises the scoreboard.

TIKA: Rain: one. Team: zero.

NARRATOR: For Daro, this could be the season that defines her legacy.

Daro wrings water from her sleeve.

DARO: It is Tuesday.

### Act one

Daro captains the Riverside Rooks for a final season and wants Fen to succeed her. The field's drain has failed before the first match.

Coach Rumi says the old drain is clogged with leaves and clay. The club cannot afford to replace it, but the city will provide gravel if the team clears the channels. Daro worries the work will take time away from practice. Fen says they cannot practice on water. The narrator describes a captain at a crossroads. Tika asks if he can describe the drain instead.

They clear a channel, leave a beetle nest undisturbed, and check the slope. Fen gets the measuring rod.

### Act two

The crew interviews Daro about retirement. She says she is not retiring from cricket, only from making every decision. Fen watches from the boundary and says Daro still calls the field positions during warmups. Daro replies that he has not asked her to stop.

During practice, Fen bowls too quickly on the wet edge. Tika calls out that the run-up is slick. Fen says he can control it. He slips and twists his ankle slightly. Rumi checks him and sends him to rest. Daro is frustrated, but Fen says he heard the warning and ignored it. The narrator calls the injury a turning point. Tika says it is a minor strain and the team has an ice pack.

While Fen rests, Daro asks him to run the practice. He starts with a safety check, moves the cones away from the soft ground, and assigns a slower drill. The players listen. Daro hangs back, then notices that she keeps stepping in to correct the field. She lets Fen make a mistake in the batting order and asks him to explain his choice afterward. He admits he mixed up two players' positions. They fix it together.

The drainage crew opens a second channel, but water still pools near the wicket. Coach Rumi discovers the ground slopes toward the center. They lay the gravel in a shallow trench to guide rain toward the old culvert. It is not a permanent fix, but it should keep the pitch playable until the city can assess the field.

### Act three

The first match arrives with a forecast of scattered rain. The team checks the field and finds no puddles, though the grass is damp. Daro lets Fen choose the opening lineup. He chooses a steady bowler and a quick runner, then names Daro as a reserve batter. She raises an eyebrow. Fen says she still gets a turn if the team needs one.

The match is close. Daro stays on the boundary, calling encouragement but not instructions. Fen's team loses two wickets early. He asks Daro to bat. She steps in, takes a breath, and hits a careful shot into open ground. The team scores enough to tie, then Fen makes the final run. The players cheer, and Daro shakes his hand.

The documentary asks Daro if she feels proud. She says the team made a good decision when the field was bad and another when the game was close. Fen says he is not ready to captain alone, but he can share the job. Daro agrees to stay on as assistant next season. Tika records the match as a draw. The narrator calls it a victory of leadership. Daro says it was a draw.

### Tag

After the cameras leave, Fen practices a slow bowling approach. Daro watches from the bench. He stops before the wet patch and adjusts his stride.

DARO: Better.

The narrator begins to speak. Tika covers the microphone with the scorecard.
