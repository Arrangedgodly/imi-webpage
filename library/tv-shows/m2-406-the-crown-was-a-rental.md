# The Crown Was a Rental

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: televised historical reenactments, authoritative narration, costume close-ups, and experts asked to approve inaccurate scenes*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-014*

Logline: A history program recreates an old orchard ceremony, but the rented crown belongs to a different festival and keeps slipping over the actor's eyes.

Cast: Historian Reni, a macaque; narrator Boro, a baboon; reenactor Tavi, a gibbon; costumer Seli, a capuchin; producer Omi, an orangutan; camera operator Piri, a lemur.

COLD OPEN

INT. REENACTMENT SET - DAY

Tavi stands in a painted orchard holding a basket. The crown slips down over his eyes. Boro narrates in a grave voice.

BORO: The orchard king surveyed his harvest.

RENI: There was no king. Town records say the harvest marshal inspected the orchard.

OMI: Can we keep the crown?

ACT ONE

Reni shows the production team a copy of the town record. The festival elected a harvest marshal, not a king. The marshal wore a ribbon and carried a ledger. Omi says the crown was rented for a more cinematic picture. Seli checks the rental tag: it belongs to a midsummer lantern parade.

SELI: This is the lantern crown.

TAVI: It is too big.

BORO: It is a symbol of authority.

RENI: It is a symbol of the wrong holiday.

The crown slips again. Tavi catches it and places it on a prop apple. Omi asks for a close-up of the apple wearing the crown. Reni takes the ledger and explains the real ceremony.

ACT TWO

Reni describes how the harvest marshal weighed baskets, noted the orchard rows, and marked which trees needed pruning. Boro asks if the account includes a dramatic rivalry. Reni says it includes a disagreement about a damaged scale. Tavi reenacts the argument with the ledger. The scale prop tips, and the apples roll across the floor.

TAVI: The basket was too close to the edge.

SELI: The scale is made of foam.

OMI: Could we get the apples rolling in slow motion?

Reni picks up an apple and checks it. It is a painted prop. Tavi finds one real apple among the props. Seli removes it so no one bites the waxed display fruit. Boro records a voice-over about the scale's importance to fair harvest shares.

BORO: The citizens gathered to witness the weighing.

RENI: Four people witnessed it.

BORO: They gathered in a small room.

ACT TWO-B

Seli makes a simple costume ribbon from fabric in the wardrobe kit. It fits Tavi. Omi asks the camera to film the ribbon in a dramatic breeze. A fan blows it over the ledger, obscuring the only written record. Reni pulls it away and weights the pages with a book.

RENI: We can film the actual record after the reenactment.

OMI: The record does not move.

RENI: That is why it is readable.

Tavi rehearses the scene without the crown. He weighs a basket, writes a number, then notes a split scale. The historian gives him the correct sequence: record the weight first, inspect the damage, then note the repair. Tavi follows it. The scale's needle points backward because the prop is assembled incorrectly.

SELI: The face plate is mounted backward.

Reni steadies the scale while Seli removes the two thumb screws, lifts out the dial, turns it around, and seats the needle on its zero pin. She tightens the screws. Tavi places a marked one-kilo weight on the pan. The pointer lands on one; he removes it and the pointer returns to zero.

TAVI: Now the scale agrees with the weight.

RENI: Film that repair, not the old mistake.

Boro rerecords the narration without calling the marshal a king. Omi asks him to make the correction sound grand. Boro speaks slowly over a wide shot of the small room.

Reni adds a second source: a photograph of the ribbon worn by the harvest marshal. The photo is tiny and cropped. Omi asks Piri to zoom in. The image becomes a blur of red fabric. Reni identifies the small stitched seal and compares it with the copy in the record book. Tavi places the ribbon against the reproduction to show it fits.

BORO: The official entered the room wearing a ribbon.

RENI: A plain ribbon with the town seal.

OMI: It does not sparkle.

SELI: It is not the rental crown.

Omi removes the glitter graphic from the screen and replaces it with a clear caption naming the office. The historian signs off on the wording before the camera starts the final take.

ACT THREE

The reenactment is filmed once with the ribbon, ledger and correctly oriented scale. Tavi records the weight, then demonstrates the repaired dial returning to zero. Reni confirms the details against the town record. Boro narrates that the harvest marshal's work kept the shared count honest. Omi approves the scene. The crew leaves the crown beside the open rental box until the final photograph.

OMI: It did look impressive.

RENI: It was from another festival.

TAVI: The ribbon stayed on.

Seli labels the costume box with its event. The crew packs the apple props separately from the scale. Boro hands Reni the final narration card. She checks the names and dates before the recording light turns on.

TAG

Omi lifts the midsummer crown for the final photograph. It slips over her eyes. Seli takes it off, sets it in the open rental box, and closes the lid once.

FINAL IMAGE: The sealed rental box sits beside the harvest ledger and plain ribbon as the reenactors leave the small room.
