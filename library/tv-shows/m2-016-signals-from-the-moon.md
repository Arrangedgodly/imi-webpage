# Signals from the Moon

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: anthology science-fiction series*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: On a moon station where every crew member has a different way to communicate, a quiet transmission forces them to choose between decoding it quickly and understanding it well.

Cast: Commander Ivo, a cautious macaque; Dr. Pali, a lemur linguist; Teek, a squirrel monkey mechanic; Nera, an orangutan botanist; and the station's old service robot, C-Bean, which speaks only in chimes and printed labels.

## Episode: "The Quiet Frequency"

### Cold open

INT. MOON STATION, NIGHT

The radio crackles once. A row of green lights appears on the console. Teek leaps from his chair.

TEEK: We have a visitor.

Dr. Pali studies the waveform. The signal repeats, then pauses. C-Bean rolls in and prints a label: "DOOR IS OPEN."

Commander Ivo looks at the sealed airlock.

IVO: Which door?

The printer produces another label: "UNKNOWN."

### Act one

The signal repeats every twelve minutes. Teek wants to answer with the station beacon. Pali warns that the sender may not understand it.

Nera has been growing a patch of hardy moss in a sealed greenhouse. She notices the signal's pulse intervals match the grow lamp's cycle. The radio may be picking up interference from the greenhouse. Teek checks the wiring and finds that a damaged cable touches the frame when the station vibrates.

The greenhouse lamp dims with the next transmission. Pali compares the pulses with the water recycler log. The gaps match the pump cycle.

C-Bean prints, "WATER WANTS ATTENTION." Teek says the robot has become poetic. The robot prints, "FILTER 3 BLOCKED."

### Act two

Filter three is clogged with moss fibers. C-Bean prints that its warning light was disconnected because the old alarm was too loud.

Ivo orders a controlled test. Teek swaps the filter and starts the pump. The interference stops. When he shuts it down, one long note returns.

The first transmission predates the filter clog. Pali still cannot rule out another source, so she sends one low-power reply.

Nothing happens. Teek wants to try a louder burst. Ivo refuses because a powerful signal could interfere with other equipment. Nera suggests waiting for the next cycle and listening for a response. They set an alert and return to their work.


[The pump starts. The radio clicks in time.]
IVO: Hold the reply.
TEEK: The signal changed.
PALI: The pump changed.
NERA: Look at the lamp.
TEEK: It dims with the pulse.
IVO: Could it be local?
PALI: Let's test that.
C-BEAN: FILTER THREE BLOCKED.
TEEK: That is not a greeting.
NERA: It is a warning.
IVO: From whom?
PALI: The station itself.
TEEK: Through a bad cable.
C-BEAN: CABLE TOUCHES FRAME.
IVO: Repair the filter first.
PALI: And log the signal.
TEEK: Cable sleeve installed.
NERA: Pump off.
[SFX: RADIO GOES QUIET.]
TEEK: The pulse stopped.
PALI: The noise stopped.
IVO: Are we alone?
PALI: We don't know.
C-BEAN: SOURCE UNKNOWN.
NERA: That is honest.
TEEK: Send a low-power ping.
IVO: One short, one long.
PALI: Then wait.
[SFX: TWO RADIO TONES.]
TEEK: No answer yet.
PALI: Don't increase power.
IVO: Agreed.
C-BEAN: ALARM TOO LOUD.
NERA: The filter light is enough.
TEEK: Someone fixed that.
PALI: We did.
IVO: Log both possibilities.
TEEK: The reply can wait.
NERA: The filter cannot.
C-BEAN: GOOD NIGHT.

### Act three

The next signal comes during the station's night shift. The pattern is new: two short pulses, a long pause, then two short pulses. The crew repeats their reply at low power. The signal answers with the same two short pulses. Pali says it could be a reflection from the satellite. It could also be a distant relay responding to their beacon.

C-Bean prints, "NO PROOF OF A VISITOR." Teek asks whether it wants proof. The robot prints, "I WANT A QUIETER ALARM." The crew laughs, then checks the repaired warning system. The filter light now glows without a siren.

Ivo records the exchange as an unresolved contact, not a confirmed message. Pali adds the raw timing data so a future crew can study it. Nera relocates the moss trays away from the intake. Teek wraps the damaged cable in a protective sleeve and logs the repair. No one claims to know who sent the two pulses.

Before sleep, Pali listens to the recording with the lights off. The pattern sounds almost like a question, but she knows the human ear finds patterns everywhere. She leaves the audio file open for the next shift.

### Tag

C-Bean prints a final strip: "GOOD NIGHT." The crew asks if that is a message from the signal.

C-Bean prints, "FROM ME."

The radio gives two soft pulses. Nobody wakes up.

### Additional scene

Later, Ivo asks the crew to review the station's emergency language card. The card lists alarms but gives no guidance for an unknown signal. Pali adds a note: record the timing, check local equipment, and avoid transmitting at full power until the source is known. Teek suggests adding a picture of the filter light. C-Bean prints the label "GOOD IDEA" and sticks it over the old warning diagram. Nera moves the moss tray another handspan from the intake. The radio stays quiet through the next pump cycle. Pali saves the file with the time, the temperature, and a note that the pattern may have had more than one cause. Ivo leaves the contact field blank rather than entering a guess.
