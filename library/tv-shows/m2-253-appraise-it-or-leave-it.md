# Appraise It or Leave It

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: antiques appraisal television, where owners arrive with everyday objects and expect a fortune*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-009*

Premise: Host Seli and appraiser Pava evaluate a collection of household objects, while their owners reveal that every item is still in daily use.

Cast: Host Seli, capuchin; appraiser Pava, gibbon; guest Odo, baboon; guest Nara, macaque; producer Tavi, lemur.

## Episode: "The Priceless Bowl"

### Cold Open

[STUDIO TABLE. ODO SETS DOWN A WOODEN BOWL.]

SELI: Tell us the history of this bowl.

ODO: I use it for fruit.

PAVA: It has a fine chip on the rim.

ODO: I dropped it yesterday.

[CUT TO TITLE.]

### Act One

[PAVA EXAMINES THE BOWL WITH A MAGNIFYING GLASS.]

PAVA: The grain suggests it was carved by hand.

ODO: I carved it.

PAVA: When?

ODO: Last week.

SELI: Could it be valuable?

ODO: It holds bananas.

Tavi brings out the next item: a wooden spoon with a bent handle. Nara says it belonged to her grandmother. Pava examines it, then asks whether it is still used.

NARA: Every morning.

PAVA: The handle is worn smooth.

NARA: It gets washed.

PAVA: The wear tells a story.

NARA: The story is washing.

Seli turns to the camera and calls it "a vivid portrait of daily life." Nara asks if the spoon can be returned before breakfast.

### Act Two

[THE OBJECTS LINE THE TABLE.]

Odo brings a woven basket. He says it was passed down through three generations. Pava checks the weave and finds one loose strand.

PAVA: The basket is stable, but it needs repair.

ODO: Can I still use it?

PAVA: Yes.

ODO: Then it is valuable to me.

Seli asks Pava to estimate the market price. Pava gives a modest number. Odo is surprised.

ODO: It carried more fruit than that.

Nara brings a flat stone used as a doorstop. Pava says the stone is likely common river rock.

NARA: It has held the door open for years.

PAVA: That is not a market category.

Tavi asks if the show can appraise the studio furniture. Seli says the table is rented. Pava says the chair is older than the bowl but has no maker's mark.

SELI: It may be a hidden treasure.

PAVA: It is a chair.

[PAVA SETS THE STONE DOORSTOP BESIDE AN APPRAISAL CARD.]

She asks Nara how long she has owned the stone. Nara says she found it beside the stream and carried it home because it fit beneath the door.

PAVA: It is a useful shape.

NARA: It was wet when I found it.

PAVA: The surface is smooth from the water.

SELI: Does that make it valuable?

PAVA: It makes it identifiable as a river stone.

Odo places the basket beside it. Pava checks the weave and asks if it can carry a heavier load. Odo fills it with fruit; the loose strand catches on one stem.

ODO: The basket needs repair.

PAVA: The appraisal includes condition.

Odo removes the damaged strand. The basket holds steady. Nara offers to mend it, but Pava says the owner should decide whether to keep it as is or repair it.

### Act Three

[THE GUESTS ARE ASKED TO CHOOSE ONE ITEM TO SELL.]

Odo chooses the basket. Nara chooses the spoon. Both hesitate.

ODO: The basket is needed for tomorrow's harvest.

NARA: The spoon is already in the kitchen.

PAVA: Then neither item is available for sale.

Seli asks whether the show can appraise the viewers' attachment. Pava says there is no scale for that. Odo picks up his bowl and checks the chip. Nara wipes the spoon with a cloth.

Tavi reveals the final item: an old key. Pava identifies it as a key to the studio closet. Seli asks if it is rare.

TAVI: It is the only key.

PAVA: That makes it important, not antique.

Seli asks the owners to place their objects on the "keep" or "sell" side of the table. They all choose keep. The table tips because the heavier side is loaded.

[SFX: TABLE WOBBLES.]

ODO: The table is the most valuable object.

NARA: It is rented.

### Tag

[THE GUESTS TAKE THEIR OBJECTS HOME.]

SELI: We have learned that value depends on use.

PAVA: And condition.

ODO: My bowl is in good condition.

NARA: It has a chip.

ODO: The chip is part of its history.

Tavi carries the table away. The chair remains.

PAVA: That chair is unclaimed.

SELI: It belongs to the studio.

PAVA: Then it is not available.

NARA: The chair belongs to the studio.

SELI: Which makes it a valuable studio chair.

PAVA: We should return to the bowl.

ODO: The bowl is already at home.

[SFX: CHAIR SCRAPES, THEME OUT.]
