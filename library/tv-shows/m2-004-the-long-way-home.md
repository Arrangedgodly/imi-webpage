# The Long Way Home

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: nature documentary*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A patient field crew follows a troop of monkeys as they cross a changing forest, while their narrator keeps trying to turn practical decisions into grand destiny.

Cast: Nuru, a young spider monkey who studies fruit before eating; Bibi, her fearless younger sister; Old Ketu, a scarred baboon who knows the canopy trails; Mara, a field biologist and careful observer; and Vale, the unseen narrator with a velvet voice and a taste for exaggeration.

## Episode: "The Fig That Would Not Fall"

### Cold open

EXT. FIG TREE, PRE-DAWN

A ripe fig hangs from a branch above a shallow stream. Nuru reaches for it. The fruit drops, bounces off a leaf, and lands on a rock. Bibi snatches it and runs.

NARRATOR: In the forest, every meal is a contest against fate.

Bibi returns, places the fig on the ground, and picks off a beetle. She eats the fruit slowly while Nuru waits, offended by the edit.

### Act one

Mara's team has followed the troop for three days. The monkeys usually cross the stream by a fallen trunk, but last night's rain has lifted one end and turned the crossing into a slick ramp. The crew sets up cameras. Vale asks for a wide shot that makes the tree look like a bridge between worlds. Mara asks him not to make the animals look like they understand camera framing.

Ketu tests the trunk with one hand. It rolls under his weight. Nuru tries a low branch instead. She can reach the opposite bank, but Bibi is smaller and cannot make the jump. Ketu turns back toward a longer route through the valley. Nuru shakes her head. The troop's youngest member, Sabi, is carrying a bruised foot and cannot manage the steep descent.

The narrator calls this a test of leadership. Mara records the actual signs: Nuru watches the current, checks the branches for flex, and returns to the same spot. Bibi finds a loop of vine tangled in a limb. She tugs it and makes the branch sway. It looks strong enough for a handhold, but not a full crossing.

### Act two

A pair of birds lands on the opposite bank. One carries a twig toward a nest; the other follows with a strip of bark. The crew films them. Vale describes a partnership forged by instinct. Mara notes that one bird is stealing nesting material from the other and gets chased off.

Nuru watches the birds, then moves upstream. There, a low branch reaches over a gravel bar. It is narrow but stable. Ketu checks it with his weight, then signals the troop to wait. Bibi crosses first and gets to the far side. Nuru follows, carrying a branch that can be laid across a gap in the rocks. The narrator calls her a pioneer. She drops the branch, scrambles back, and brings Sabi's sling of leaves across too.

### Act three

On the far bank, the troop rests beneath a stand of young figs. The fruit is green. Bibi tries one, makes a face, and spits it into the dust. Nuru finds a patch of flowers and drinks water from the cupped petals. Sabi sits with her foot raised while Ketu checks it. Mara keeps the camera far back and records the troop's slow recovery.

The production manager says the episode needs a conclusion. Vale suggests that the troop has completed a heroic migration. Mara points out they have crossed one stream on a route they use every week. They haven't gone anywhere new. The episode is about adapting the route after the old crossing changed.

Nuru returns to the stream and takes a long look at the fallen trunk. She pulls a strip of bark loose and leaves it on the gravel bar. Bibi copies her, adding a feather. The crew cannot know whether the objects mark the crossing or are just play. Mara records both possibilities.

The troop moves toward the grove. Ketu leads Sabi at a steady pace. Nuru stays behind until her sister catches up. The narrator lowers his voice, then admits the camera cannot tell whether anyone is thinking about the fig they left behind.

### Tag

At sunset, the crew films the original fig tree. The fruit from the morning still hangs over the water, now split open. A fish rises and takes a bit of pulp.

NARRATOR: The forest keeps no record of what might have been.

Mara, off camera: It keeps a lot of records. We just miss most of them.

Vale pauses, then leaves her line in. A beetle crosses the lens hood. The camera operator waits for it to pass.
