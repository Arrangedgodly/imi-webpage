# The Troop That Built a Boat

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: family competition television*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: Two monkey families compete to build small boats from safe recycled materials, but a flooded footpath turns their contest into a shared rescue effort.

Cast: Host Nella, a lemur; the Reed family, led by mother Sumi and her twins; the Bark family, led by father Joss and his nephew Piko; Engineer Tam, a capuchin; and Coach Vela, a macaque who keeps the competition kind.

## Episode: "Float Your Own Way"

### Cold open

EXT. POND, DAY

The Reed family launches a boat made from corks and a paper sail. It circles in place. The Bark family's raft moves backward. Host Nella looks at the wind sock.

NELLA: The current has changed.

Tam spots a line of water crossing the footpath. A basket of seedlings is stranded on the far side.

### Act one

The family challenge is to build a small boat that can carry a toy cargo across the pond. The teams have cardboard, corks, string, and sealed plastic bottles. The Reed twins want a fast boat. The Bark family wants a stable one. Coach Vela says the judges will score design, teamwork, and whether each team can explain what they learned.

Rain floods the footpath to the garden, leaving a basket of seedlings across the stream. Nella pauses the contest while Tam checks the hidden ditch.

The Reed twins suggest using their boat to carry the seedlings. Their craft is too small. Joss notices the sealed bottles can support a wider platform. The teams argue briefly over whose materials to use. Coach Vela asks whether a shared boat counts as a competition entry. Nella says the show can change its rules if the safety crew approves.

### Act two

Tam checks the stream with a long pole and marks the shallow edge. The adults work on a wider raft using bottles tied beneath a plank frame. The children sort materials and test how many bottles keep the platform level. The twins disagree about where to place the cargo. Piko offers to sit low in the boat as a counterweight. Tam says no one rides the raft until it has been tested without a person.

The teams make a small weight from sandbags. The raft floats, but leans when the bags sit at one end. Sumi moves the cargo to the center. The platform steadies. The families tie a guide rope to a tree on each bank so the raft cannot drift downstream.

Nella and Tam test it with one empty crate. The raft slides across the shallow stream and reaches the far bank. The gardeners retrieve it and load the seedlings. The families pull the rope together, guiding the basket back. A seedling tips over, but the soil stays in its pot.

The children cheer. The twins admit speed was the wrong goal; Piko says balance mattered more than size.

### Act three

The water recedes by late afternoon. Tam checks the path and confirms the ditch is visible again. The teams return to the pond and finish their toy boat contest. The Reed family's boat now carries two small stones. The Bark raft carries a pinecone without tilting. The judges award the teamwork ribbon to both families.

Nella asks whether the families still want a winner. The children vote for the shared raft. Coach Vela says they can keep the ribbon at the garden shed, where it will remind everyone who helped move the seedlings. The families agree to leave the bottle supports in storage in case the footpath floods again, but they will not use the stream as a regular crossing.

Sumi and Joss thank Tam for checking the water before the children approached. The twins ask if they can build another boat next week. Tam says yes, if they first test it in the pond. Piko suggests a sail. The twins say they can make the sail from an old sheet, but only after asking its owner.

The crew packs up the contest signs. Nella returns the unused plastic bottles to the recycling bin. The families walk to the garden by the now-dry path, carrying the rescued seedlings by hand.
