# The Submarine's Viewport Needs a Curtain

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: submarine suspense dramas, sonar paranoia, and crews who mistake reflections for objects outside the hull*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: CAPTAIN ODO, a baboon; SONAR OFFICER, a macaque; ENGINEER LUMA, an orangutan; NAVIGATOR, a gibbon; MESS COOK, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. SUBMARINE - VIEWPORT - NIGHT

A shadow glides past the window. The sonar officer points.

SONAR OFFICER: Something is following us.

The captain turns off the cabin light. The shadow remains.

ENGINEER LUMA: It's the cook.

The cook waves a ladle from the other side of the glass.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The submarine is running a training patrol. The crew reports a moving silhouette beside the viewport. Sonar shows no contact. The captain orders a silent run. The cook is outside the observation port cleaning the glass with a long brush.

ODO: How did he get outside?

NAVIGATOR: The glass is on the dry observation chamber.

The crew has been looking through a viewport into a sealed display bay, not directly into the ocean. Luma checks the pressure doors. The outer hatch is locked. The cook is in the dry bay, cleaning a smear that makes the reflection look like a large object.

ACT TWO

The captain asks the crew to identify the shadow without opening the outer hatch. The sonar officer uses a lamp. The reflection grows larger. Navigator turns the lamp off; the shadow shrinks. Luma notices the viewport has a removable curtain intended to block internal reflections during nighttime watches. It was packed behind the chart rack.

LUMA: We should have used this before accusing the cook.

ODO: The cook was suspicious.

The cook knocks on the inner hatch. The captain opens it after checking the pressure indicator. The cook reports that the outer pane has a streak of soap. He cannot reach the bottom edge without a longer brush. The crew passes him a pole through the dry chamber hatch, keeping the outer pressure door sealed. He cleans the pane. The strange shadow disappears.

SONAR OFFICER: The object is gone.

COOK: The soap is gone.

The captain asks sonar to scan the water around the vessel. A real contact appears: a school of fish near the hull. The navigator tracks it and confirms it is moving away. The crew does not raise alarm; the captain records it as wildlife and keeps the training route.

INT. SUBMARINE - CONTROL ROOM - NIGHT

The sonar officer reports another contact. The crew lowers the lights and listens. A soft tapping repeats against the hull. The captain asks Luma to check the pressure readings. Every hatch is sealed, the dry observation bay is secure, and the outer viewport is closed.

ODO: Is it the fish?

SONAR OFFICER: It is slower than the fish.

The cook returns from the dry bay carrying the cleaning brush. He says the brush tapped the outer frame when the vessel changed depth. Luma checks the brush's tether and finds it is too long; it can strike the glass whenever the bay shifts. She shortens the tether and secures the handle. The sonar officer scans again. The tapping stops.

The crew still sees a faint shape in the viewport. The captain draws the curtain. The shape disappears. Luma explains that the screen inside the compartment reflects the red instrument lights when the room is dark. The cook's silhouette crossed that reflection and looked like a second vessel.

COOK: I was trying to clean the glass.

ODO: We thought you were following us.

COOK: I was following the streak.

The captain asks the sonar officer to log the fish contact separately from the brush noise. The officer creates two entries and includes the time each one began. Luma tests the curtain track so it does not cover the emergency indicator. The observation port remains sealed throughout the check.



The navigator asks the sonar officer to replay the recording so the crew can compare the fish contact with the tapping. The recording has three tracks: sonar, brush, and the captain's dramatic whisper from the earlier drill. The playback sends the whisper through the galley speaker.

ODO: Contact at two o'clock.

COOK: Dinner is at six.

The crew stops the playback. The sonar officer separates the tracks and mutes the drill microphone. The captain asks for a clean recording of the actual fish contact. The cook returns the brush to its wall clip, where the shortened tether keeps the handle away from the viewport. Luma taps the glass once from inside the bay. No sound reaches the hull sensors. The sonar officer logs the test as an internal knock, not a second creature.

SONAR OFFICER: One fish school, one brush, zero monsters.

ODO: Put the monster in the drill archive.

COOK: Under what category?

ODO: Kitchen equipment.

The cook hangs the ladle beside the brush. The crew resumes the patrol with the curtain open just enough to show the safe inner frame and the real fish moving away on sonar.

ACT THREE

The curtain is installed on the inner frame. The sonar officer labels the reflection procedure. The cook returns to the galley and announces dinner. The captain thanks him for cleaning the pane and for not being a sea monster.

COOK: I have never been a sea monster.

ODO: Not on this shift.

FINAL IMAGE: The curtain covers the dark viewport while the sonar screen shows a harmless school of fish.
