# The Weather Channel's Sunrise Is in Studio B

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: cheerful breakfast weather broadcasts, green-screen forecasts, and producers who schedule sunrise before it happens*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: METEOROLOGIST PALI, a gibbon; ANCHOR, an orangutan; CAMERA OPERATOR, a baboon; PRODUCER, a macaque; INTERN, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. TELEVISION STUDIO - 5:30 A.M.

The anchor greets viewers. Behind her, a sunrise glows over the harbor. The meteorologist looks at the monitor.

ANCHOR: The sun is rising.

INTERN: That is yesterday's footage.

PRODUCER: It is still a sunrise.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The producer wants a live sunrise shot for the breakfast broadcast. The camera operator has the green screen ready, but the actual sky is dark and raining. The meteorologist says sunrise is in forty minutes. The anchor asks whether they can delay the show. The producer says the show begins at six.

PALI: We can show the rain.

PRODUCER: We have a sponsor for sunshine.

The intern checks the video files. The sunrise footage is labeled TUESDAY, but today's is Wednesday. The producer says the harbor looks the same. The camera operator plays the old clip. A boat passes behind the anchor; she mistakes it for the ferry schedule and begins giving directions.

ANCHOR: The boat is on time.

PALI: It is a recording.

The producer asks Pali to forecast with the old video anyway. Pali refuses to say the sky is clear while rain hits the studio windows. The anchor moves to a green-screen map. The map points to the wrong side of town because the digital compass is mirrored.

ACT TWO

The intern finds today's sunrise camera in Studio B. It is pointed at the studio's loading door, where a bright work light is on. The producer has confused the test feed with the harbor camera. The camera operator turns off the light. The image goes dark. The anchor asks if the sun has set.

CAMERA OPERATOR: It was a lamp.

PALI: The sun is still below the horizon.

The producer agrees to use live rain footage until sunrise. The meteorologist gives a forecast for the harbor, then the hills, then the orchard. The anchor asks about the commuter road; Pali says it is wet and recommends extra travel time. The screen graphic labels the road "sunny." The intern corrects it. The producer changes the graphic to "rainy," but the sponsor logo covers the word.

INTERN: The logo is blocking the weather.

PRODUCER: Move the logo.

[The camera operator shifts the map. The sponsor logo appears over the anchor's face. She keeps speaking while the technician slides it aside.]

At 6:10, a pale line appears beyond the studio window. The camera operator switches to the live harbor feed. Pali confirms the sun is rising. The anchor asks whether today's sunrise looks like yesterday's. Pali says the sun is the same; the clouds are not.

INT. STUDIO WEATHER SET - 6:00 A.M.

The live forecast begins. Pali points to the cloud map. The producer asks for a sunny graphic, but the camera operator has switched to the radar layer. A bright green band covers the harbor.

ANCHOR: Is the storm green?

PALI: The map is green. The storm is gray.

The intern changes the layer to precipitation. The sponsor logo appears over the rain bands. The producer asks to shrink it. The logo shrinks, but the map legend vanishes. Pali explains the colors verbally while the camera operator adjusts the layout.

PALI: Dark blue is heavier rain. Pale blue is lighter rain.

ANCHOR: And green?

PALI: That is land.

The producer says the sponsor promised sunshine. Pali replies that the sponsor can sponsor an umbrella. The anchor reads the commute advisory. A viewer calls to ask if the rain will stop before school. Pali says it should ease by eight, then intensify near the hills at ten. The producer asks for a positive sign-off. Pali points to a break in the cloud cover on the west edge of the screen.

PALI: There is your bright spot.

The anchor uses the live camera to show the actual sunrise. The camera operator holds steady while the clouds pass. No sponsor graphic covers the horizon.



The intern saves the corrected layout as LIVE WEATHER and locks the outdated sunrise clip in the archive folder. The anchor repeats the rainy commute advisory once, slowly, while the caption matches the spoken forecast.

ACT THREE

The producer retires the old clip. The intern renames the live feed HARBOR, the loading-door feed STUDIO B, and the old sunrise ARCHIVE. The anchor gives the verified forecast without a sponsor graphic covering it. The show ends as the first sunlight touches the harbor.

ANCHOR: Is the day beginning?

PALI: It began before the broadcast.

FINAL IMAGE: Studio B's work light stays off while the real sunrise appears on the correctly labeled monitor.
