# The Spaceport's Lost Shuttle Has a Parking Ticket

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: science-fiction rescue serials, bureaucratic spaceport rules, and dramatic missions delayed by a parking notice*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: COMMANDER VENN, a baboon; PILOT ONA, a gibbon; TRAFFIC OFFICER, an orangutan; ENGINEER PIP, a macaque; PORT AUTHORITY VOICE, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. SPACEPORT CONTROL - ORBITAL NIGHT

A red alarm flashes: SHUTTLE MISSING. Commander Venn stands at the window, staring into space.

VENN: Launch the rescue beacon.

The port authority voice interrupts.

VOICE: Shuttle 4 has an unpaid parking ticket.

PILOT ONA: The shuttle is in orbit.

VOICE: Then it is parked.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

Venn orders a search of the docking ring. Engineer Pip checks the flight logs. Shuttle 4 left the station three hours ago to inspect a beacon and has not checked in. The traffic officer says it was last seen in Docking Bay C, where its parking permit expired.

TRAFFIC OFFICER: The permit is valid through noon.

VENN: It is midnight.

TRAFFIC OFFICER: On the station clock.

Pip finds a second timestamp in the shuttle transponder. The station's time is twelve hours ahead because the maintenance computer never adjusted after orbit change. Ona says the shuttle could be near the beacon, but the control room cannot send a rescue craft until its departure fee is cleared.

VENN: We are not charging a rescue fee.

TRAFFIC OFFICER: This is not a rescue fee. It is a parking fee.

The authority computer marks the shuttle as "stationary" because its beacon ping has not changed. Pip explains that the signal is bouncing off a relay. Ona offers to fly the rescue route, but the officer needs a launch clearance. The commander signs it. The computer rejects the signature because the pen is not certified for low gravity.

ACT TWO

INT. DOCKING BAY C - LATER

The team searches the empty bay. A tow cart is parked where Shuttle 4 stood. The traffic officer reads the ticket: the shuttle has been cited for occupying a space after departure. Venn points out that an empty dock cannot be occupied. The officer says the system recorded the vehicle at the last known location.

PIP: It is a parking ticket for a memory.

The authority computer shows a blinking dot near the relay beacon. Ona uses the relay's angle to triangulate the shuttle. It is not missing; it is drifting behind the station's far side with a damaged antenna. The crew sends a short pulse. The shuttle answers with a weak signal. Ona prepares to launch.

The port authority voice asks for a payment code. Venn enters the rescue mission number. The system accepts it as a parking citation appeal. The traffic officer reclassifies the ticket as "vehicle absent," but the computer wants a photograph of the empty parking space. The officer takes one with the tow cart in frame.

TRAFFIC OFFICER: There is a cart in the space.

VENN: The cart is not a shuttle.

PIP: The system may need that explained.

Ona launches. She reaches the drifting shuttle, clamps the damaged antenna, and guides it back to the station. The docking computer refuses to grant a bay because the original slot is still marked occupied by the overdue vehicle. Pip manually clears the stale location record after confirming the shuttle's serial number.

INT. SHUTTLE 4 - ORBITAL NIGHT

Ona asks the stranded shuttle pilot to read the instrument panel. The pilot reports that the antenna is loose but the engine is stable. Venn tells Pip to prepare a docking slot. The port authority system rejects it because the shuttle's status is "not present at port."

PIP: It is coming to port.

VOICE: The vehicle must be present before a slot can be assigned.

VENN: We are looking at it.

VOICE: The camera is not a registration device.

The traffic officer finds the shuttle's serial number on the rescue authorization. The database still lists its last dock as occupied. The officer clears the old status, but the system asks for the pilot's signature. The pilot has only a gloved hand available. Ona passes a stylus through the hatch; the pilot signs the emergency form and sends it back.

PILOT: That is a very expensive pencil.

ONA: It is reusable.

The shuttle comes into view beyond the station ring. Ona guides it toward the assigned bay. The traffic officer gives the docking computer a one-time clearance. Venn stands by the window and watches the shuttle's position update from missing to approaching. The commander asks whether the ticket can be canceled before landing.

TRAFFIC OFFICER: The appeal requires proof of arrival.

VENN: How will we prove it?

TRAFFIC OFFICER: It must arrive.



ACT THREE

INT. SPACEPORT CONTROL - ORBITAL NIGHT

Shuttle 4 docks in Bay C. The crew is safe. The traffic officer cancels the ticket, but the authority computer issues a new one for landing in an unreserved space. Venn tells the officer to place the citation in the appeals queue.

ONA: How long does an appeal take?

TRAFFIC OFFICER: It is in orbit.

The port authority voice confirms the new parking permit. Pip labels the relay log with the correct station time.

FINAL IMAGE: The shuttle rests in its assigned bay while a small digital meter counts down its next permitted departure.
