# The Crown Keeps Slipping

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: historical costume dramas, especially court intrigue conducted through elaborate etiquette*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-012*

Premise: A palace drama's succession crisis is triggered by a crown that will not stay on the ruler's head.

Cast: Queen Miri, a macaque; Duke Odo, a baboon; Lady Pava, a gibbon seamstress; Herald Kito, a capuchin; and court naturalist Nara, a lemur.

Episode: "The Tilt of the Crown"

COLD OPEN

INT. THRONE ROOM - MORNING

[MUSIC: A GRAND STRING CHORD, CUT SHORT.]

HERALD KITO: Her Majesty approaches.

The doors open. Queen Miri strides in wearing a gold crown that tilts over one eye. She catches it before it falls.

QUEEN MIRI: No one saw that.

DUKE ODO: I saw it, Your Majesty.

QUEEN MIRI: Then no one will discuss it.

Kito announces the opening of court. The crown slides backward. Miri holds it in place with one hand.

ACT ONE

INT. THRONE ROOM - CONTINUOUS

The duke presents a treaty to sign. Miri reaches for the quill; the crown tips forward and blocks her view.

QUEEN MIRI: The treaty is tilted.

DUKE ODO: The treaty is flat.

LADY PAVA: It is the crown. The inner band is too loose.

Kito gasps as if a scandal has been exposed. Odo says only the royal jeweler may adjust the crown. Pava says the jeweler is in another province. Miri orders the court to proceed while she holds the crown.

NARA: May I examine the fit?

Miri lowers her head. Nara notices the crown has a soft felt lining. A loose thread catches on the queen's fur when she moves.

QUEEN MIRI: It has been loyal for years.

NARA: The lining is worn. Your fur is not a royal mounting.

Odo claims the crown has always been secure. It slips as he lifts it from the cushion.

DUKE ODO: The cushion is unstable.

PAVA: It is the cushion's first day in court.

Kito announces an emergency council to determine whether the crown's tilt is a bad omen. Odo says the law states the monarch must wear the crown at public ceremonies. Miri asks if it says the crown must remain upright.

Kito consults the etiquette book. If a royal object is questioned, the court must bow three times before discussing it. Odo orders a demonstration. Everyone bows; the crown slides forward. They bow again; it slides back.

QUEEN MIRI: Are we honoring the crown or trying to keep it on my head?

DUKE ODO: The ceremony is still in progress.

NARA: The ceremony has proved the lining is loose.

Odo asks if the court should postpone the treaty until the omen passes. Miri says the border dispute will not wait for a hat to sit still. Kito marks the treaty unsigned and announces an emergency recess for the crown.

ACT TWO

INT. COSTUME CHAMBER - LATER

Pava brings the crown to her work table. Odo insists on watching, in case anyone alters the royal insignia. Nara checks the old felt and removes one snagged thread. Pava asks Miri how it feels when she turns quickly.

QUEEN MIRI: Heavy. Hot. Important.

NARA: Those last two are not measurements.

Odo suggests a chain attached to the throne. Miri says she does not want to be tethered to her chair. Kito proposes a dramatic veil to conceal the tilt. Pava says the veil will catch on the same thread.

Miri demonstrates her usual entrance. She rises from the chair, steps forward, and turns toward the court. The crown slips each time she pivots.

NARA: Your crown moves when you move.

QUEEN MIRI: I move when the court watches.

PAVA: Then let us fit it while you do what a ruler does.

They pad the inner band with a narrow strip of fresh felt. Miri wears the crown and walks the room. It stays in place until she turns sharply to glare at Odo.

DUKE ODO: I have not opposed you.

QUEEN MIRI: You have opposed the fit.

Nara asks Miri to groom the crown edge gently with her fingers, as she would settle fur. Miri does. Her hands distribute the felt evenly. The crown steadies.

ACT THREE

INT. THRONE ROOM - AFTERNOON

The court reconvenes for the treaty. Kito announces Miri's entrance with a flourish. She steps through the doors, pauses, and lets the crown settle before walking. It stays upright.

Odo asks whether the court may record the repair. Miri says yes, if the record credits Pava and Nara. Kito asks if this means the omen has been defeated.

NARA: It means the band was loose.

KITO: A less expensive prophecy.

Miri takes up the quill to examine the treaty. Odo reaches to steady the crown. Miri gives him a look. He lowers his paw.

QUEEN MIRI: The court may now discuss the treaty.

DUKE ODO: And the crown?

QUEEN MIRI: The crown is secure. The treaty may not be.

She reads the document, finds a missing boundary clause, and hands it back to Odo for correction. The duke nods and begins writing.

Pava checks the seam one last time. Nara confirms the felt sits flat against the inner band. Miri nods and returns her attention to the treaty.

TAG

Kito announces that the boundary clause has been amended. Miri reads it, then signs without moving the crown.

The crown remains still as Miri turns to face the room.

QUEEN MIRI: I am listening.

Odo adjusts his posture instead.
