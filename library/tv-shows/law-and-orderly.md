# Law and Orderly

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: city procedural convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: At the Department of Lost Things, a pair of inspectors follow misplaced property through a city whose paperwork has more suspects than its streets.

Cast: Inspector Noko, precise and unhurried; Inspector Bree, new to the desk and quick with a question; Clerk Oren, keeper of the evidence shelves; Deputy Marshal Vela, who handles hearings; Mr. Tumble, a street performer with a very memorable hat.

## Episode: "The Case of the Missing Bench"

### Cold open

EXT. CIVIC SQUARE, MORNING

A city worker paints a box around an empty patch of pavement. The sign reads: PUBLIC BENCH, TEMPORARILY REMOVED.

An old macaque arrives with a thermos and asks where he should sit. The worker points to the box.

OLD MACAQUE: That's a picture of where the bench was.

The worker nods. The old macaque sits on the sign.

### Act one

Inspectors Noko and Bree investigate the disappearance. The bench was bolted to the square for thirty years. There are no pry marks, only four clean circles where the bolts used to be. A city notice says it was transferred to Recreation, then to Sanitation, then to an office called Temporary Solutions.

BREE: Is Temporary Solutions a department?

NOKO: It is temporary. The solutions are permanent.

They find the removal request signed by Mr. Tumble, who performs juggling tricks near the square. He says he asked the city to fix a loose plank, not take the whole bench. He has a photo proving it: the bench appears in the background, though only its left arm is visible beneath his hat.

The inspectors visit the storage yard. Clerk Oren has logged 138 benches, none from the square. He points to a row marked "miscellaneous seating," full of stools with one leg missing.

### Act two

Bree interviews the maintenance crew. They say a truck arrived with an order to remove all objects that might be mistaken for sculpture. The bench had been decorated with a knitted scarf by a local elder. Someone reported the scarf as a public art installation. The crew followed the form.

Noko asks why they didn't check with the square's regular users. The foreman says the form had no field for that. It did have six boxes for supervisor initials.

The paper trail leads to Deputy Marshal Vela, who issued the removal order under a rule about unlicensed structures. She says she didn't know it was a bench. The application described it as "a low, horizontal municipal object with social use." She believed that meant a sculpture.

VELA: I am not a sculptor.

BREE: Did you see the photo?

Vela had. The image was filed sideways, so the bench looked like a woolly animal. Noko rotates the print. Vela recognizes it and says the order can be reversed. She asks whether the bench is still in the yard.

Oren remembers a truck taking a "low horizontal object" to the recycling depot.

### Act three

The inspectors arrive at the depot before the metal is crushed. The bench sits beside a broken fountain, scarf intact. The depot supervisor wants a signed release from Temporary Solutions. Nobody knows who works there. Bree follows a dusty directory to a shared office where a single clerk handles temporary forms on Tuesdays.

The clerk says the bench's case was closed once it left the square. Noko asks who closed it. The clerk shows them a checkbox marked "resolved by relocation." The city had counted removing the bench as solving the maintenance request.

Bree gets Vela to sign an order restoring it. Noko gets the crew to carry it back. The old macaque watches, holding the thermos. At the square, workers bolt the bench in place and return the knitted scarf to its maker.

The city worker asks whether to repaint the box.

OLD MACAQUE: Paint it around the bench. Then you'll know where it is.

Oren records the item as "public seating, found in use." The old macaque thanks the inspectors and leaves his thermos on the bench. He returns for it one minute later.

Before they leave, Bree asks the city worker to remove the empty outline. He says that requires an order from Public Markings. Noko finds the form in his folder and fills it out on the hood of the truck. It asks for the bench's current location, previous location, and expected location after the paint crew arrives. The worker studies the form.

WORKER: Where should I put the truck while they paint?

NOKO: In the street.

The worker marks the box labeled "other," then draws a tiny truck in the margin. Oren stamps the form. It is the first stamp Bree has seen that day that does not cause a new case number.

### Tag

The next morning, a new notice appears: PUBLIC BENCH, CONFIRMED PRESENT.

Bree adds a note beneath it: "Please do not transfer to Temporary Solutions."

Noko sits down to read the paper. The bench holds.
