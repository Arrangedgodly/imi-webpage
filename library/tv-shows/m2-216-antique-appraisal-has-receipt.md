# The Antique Appraisal Has a Receipt

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: televised antique appraisals that inflate a household object's value through dramatic guesses about its past*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-008*

Premise: A traveling appraiser is asked to value a dented tea tin, but the owner's careful receipts keep defeating the host's grand provenance theories.

Cast: Host Dera, a gibbon; appraiser Kito, a macaque; owner Vela, a capuchin; and floor producer Omi, a baboon.

Episode: "The Tin with a Story"

COLD OPEN

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - DAY

Vela sets a dented tea tin on the appraisal table. Dera leans toward it. Kito turns it over and checks the bottom.

DERA: This could have belonged to a famous explorer.

VELA: It belonged to my aunt.

KITO: The stamp says it was made twenty years ago.

DERA: A later tin, perhaps used by an explorer's descendant.

VELA: My aunt kept sewing needles in it.

[OMI PUTS A SMALL ENVELOPE ON THE TABLE.]

OMI: The purchase receipt is in here.

DERA: Let's save the reveal.

ACT ONE

Dera asks Vela to describe the tin's history. Vela opens it and shows a paper receipt folded inside.

VELA: My aunt bought it at the hardware store. She used it for needles.

DERA: Did she travel?

VELA: She took the bus to the market.

KITO: There is a maker's mark under the dent.

DERA: Could the dent be a battle scar?

VELA: A saucepan fell off the shelf.

[THE AUDIENCE REACTS WITH A SMALL "OH." DERA LIFTS A HAND.]

DERA: We'll return to that after the break.

KITO: The tin is worth about three shells as a collectible, five if someone likes the design.

DERA: That's less than the audience expects.

OMI: The show promised a surprise value.

ACT TWO

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - LATER

Kito inspects the maker's mark with a magnifier. Omi places a lamp over the table. Dera holds up a card reading "RARE?"

KITO: The mark identifies a local maker.

DERA: A rare local maker?

KITO: They sold thousands of these.

VELA: My aunt bought three. One held buttons.

DERA: Could that one be more valuable?

VELA: It is in my sewing drawer.

[OMI HANDS VELA A CONSENT FORM.]

OMI: We could film the drawer reveal.

VELA: The drawer is at home.

DERA: Is it a dramatic drawer?

VELA: It sticks unless you lift the handle.

KITO: That is ordinary wear.

DERA: A stubborn drawer with a hidden collection. Strong segment.

ACT TWO-B

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - AFTERNOON

Omi rolls in a small filing box. It contains old receipts. Vela sorts them by date while Kito checks the tin.

OMI: We found the receipt for the tin.

DERA: We can present the provenance in a scroll.

VELA: It is one receipt.

KITO: It confirms the price and date.

DERA: Read it slowly.

VELA: "Tea tin, hardware store, four shells."

DERA: Four shells when?

VELA: On the date printed right here.

[KITO POINTS TO THE DATE. OMI HOLDS UP THE CAMERA CARD: "MYSTERY VALUE." ]

KITO: The mystery is solved.

DERA: The value is still uncertain.

KITO: It is a useful tin. Its market value is modest.

VELA: Then I will keep it.

ACT TWO-C

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - LATE AFTERNOON

Omi brings up a photo of Vela's aunt using the tin at a sewing table. Dera asks Vela to hold it beside the tin.

DERA: This gives the object a personal history.

KITO: It does not change the market price.

VELA: I would not sell it.

OMI: We have a viewer poll. Should we value the tin by its age or its use?

DERA: The poll closes after the reveal.

KITO: We have already given the estimate.

[DERA TURNS THE MONITOR. THE POLL SHOWS "KEEP IT" AND "SELL IT." THE VIEWERS HAVE VOTED TO KEEP IT.]

DERA: The audience has made an appraisal.

KITO: They have made a choice for Vela.

VELA: I made that choice before I came here.

DERA: Could we show the tin being used?

VELA: I brought the needles.

[SHE PUTS A NEEDLE THROUGH THE EYE OF A THREAD. THE CAMERA FINALLY GETS A CLEAR SHOT OF THE TIN IN USE.]

ACT THREE

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - FINAL APPRAISAL

Dera faces the camera. Kito puts the tin beside the receipt. Vela fills it with needles.

DERA: The final estimate is three to five shells, depending on the buyer.

VELA: My aunt used it for twenty years.

KITO: That's the value that does not fit on the card.

[OMI TAKES DOWN THE "RARE?" CARD. DERA PUTS A NEW ONE UP: "FOUR SHELLS, ONCE."]

DERA: The receipt has ended the mystery.

VELA: Good. I have a needle to thread.

TAG

Dera lifts the tin's lid. It squeaks.

DERA: A hidden sound feature.

KITO: It needs oil.

[VELA CLOSES THE TIN. THE SQUEAK STOPS.]
