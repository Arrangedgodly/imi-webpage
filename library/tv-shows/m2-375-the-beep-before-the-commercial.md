# The Beep Before the Commercial

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: medical procedurals, diagnostic montages, rival doctors, and cliffhangers timed to commercial breaks*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-013*

Logline: A clinic's dramatic monitor keeps beeping even after the patient has gone home, sending two rival doctors through increasingly cinematic diagnoses.

Cast: Dr. Varo, an orangutan; Dr. Eni, a macaque; patient Kema, a capuchin; nurse Tali, a gibbon; producer Bex, a lemur.

COLD OPEN

INT. CLINIC - NIGHT

A heart monitor beeps. Dr. Varo leans over an empty examination bed.

VARO: The patient is gone.

ENI: The monitor is still on.

TALI: We discharged Kema an hour ago.

BEX (IN EARPIECE): Hold the silence. We are coming back from break.

ACT ONE

Kema enters through the clinic door carrying a bag of oranges. Tali checks the chart and says the patient feels well, has normal breathing, and is here because the clinic called about an unfinished reading from the monitor.

KEMA: I went home. Then you called. Now I am back.

VARO: Your heart rate is steady.

ENI: The monitor is attached to the bed, not Kema.

The monitor beeps. Varo says there is a rhythm. Eni unplugs it. The beeping continues. Tali lifts the receiver from a phone; it stops. Bex signals for the screen to cut to a dramatic close-up of Varo's face.

VARO: We have a mystery.

ENI: We have a phone off the hook.

ACT TWO

Varo asks for tests. Eni refuses until they examine Kema. Tali checks the pulse manually; it is regular. Kema says the oranges are for the front desk. Bex asks for the doctors to consult in the hallway while Kema waits under a blanket.

KEMA: I am cold because you opened the door.

TALI: I can close it.

VARO: The monitor suggests a second rhythm.

ENI: That is the phone's busy signal.

Varo orders the monitor wheeled to the lab. The cord catches on the bed wheel. Tali frees it. The phone starts beeping again when Bex puts it back on the cradle, then stops when the line disconnects.

VARO: The patient is stable, but the signal is not.

ENI: We can say the telephone line has an issue.

BEX: That cannot end an act.

Kema asks whether they can return the oranges and go home. Tali says yes. Varo asks one more question: did Kema hear a beep at home? Kema says the kitchen timer went off while the oranges were baking in a cake. She shows a small timer in the bag. It beeps as she sets it down.

ACT TWO-B

The doctors exchange a look. Bex cues a percussion sting. The timer beeps. Eni picks it up and presses stop. The monitor is silent.

ENI: There is your missing rhythm.

VARO: The timer was in the bag.

KEMA: I brought the oranges. The timer was clipped to the bag.

TALI: We can add it to the property list.

VARO: I want to check the phone receiver once more.

ENI: That is not a patient test.

VARO: It may be a sound source.

The clinic phone rings. Everyone freezes. Tali answers and says the call is for billing. Bex tries to cut to commercial, but the camera remains on the doctors. Eni points directly at the lens.

ENI: We found the beep. We checked Kema. Kema is well and leaving.

BEX: Can you say it with more urgency?

KEMA: I am missing the last bus.

Bex asks for one final scan before the break. Eni says no scan is needed; Kema is well. Varo lifts the unplugged monitor to prove it is silent. Its battery chirps once.

VARO: The machine has another symptom.

TALI: Low battery.

ENI: We can replace the battery after the show.

KEMA: Can I go now?

TALI: Yes.

Varo sets the monitor down. The chirp stops. Bex tries to announce a cliffhanger, but the camera shows Kema leaving through the door. The doctors watch her catch the bus from the window.

ACT THREE

Tali gives Kema the oranges. Varo signs the chart as "stable; no further monitoring indicated." Eni walks Kema to the door and holds it open. The phone stays on its cradle. The monitor remains off.

VARO: I was prepared for a rare arrhythmia.

ENI: It was a kitchen timer.

VARO: The case had several sound cues.

TALI: One was the phone, one was the timer, and one was the monitor itself.

Bex asks them to repeat the final line so the station can place the commercial. Varo refuses to pretend the patient is still in danger. Instead, Eni turns the empty bed around so the monitor faces the wall and puts the orange bag on the front desk.

TAG

As the show signs off, the phone rings again. Tali answers it and hands Varo a bill.

TALI: This is the only alert we cannot turn off.

FINAL IMAGE: The doctors stare at the bill as the monitor stays quiet and the end credits beep once, on purpose.
