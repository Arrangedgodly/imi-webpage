# The People Versus the Pinecone

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: courtroom television and overblown legal argument about a petty neighborhood dispute*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-004*

Premise: A neighborhood court hears a complaint about a pinecone that keeps rolling into the shared footpath. Counsel treat this minor matter as precedent-setting while the witnesses keep demonstrating the problem.

Cast: Judge Ula, a mandrill; claimant Miko, a squirrel monkey; respondent Brin, a baboon; and clerk Fenn, a capuchin.

## Episode: "The Case of the Moving Cone"

### Cold Open

[THE JUDGE'S BENCH IS A LOW STUMP. THE CLERK SETS A PINECONE ON THE EVIDENCE TABLE.]

FENN: The court will hear Miko versus Brin.

MIKO: That pinecone rolls across my path every morning.

BRIN: It is a pinecone. It rolls where the ground slopes.

[THE CONE ROLLS OFF THE TABLE AND STOPS AGAINST THE JUDGE'S FOOT.]

JUDGE ULA: The evidence has entered my personal space.

### Act One

MIKO: I have to step into the mud to get around it.

BRIN: I placed it beside my gate to stop it from rolling downhill.

MIKO: The gate is uphill.

BRIN: The cone has ambition.

JUDGE ULA: Counsel, address the court.

MIKO: I would like the cone moved.

BRIN: I would like the path left as it is.

FENN: Should I record a plea?

JUDGE ULA: Record that both parties have declined to plead against the cone.

[THE CONE ROLLS TOWARD MIKO. MIKO STOPS IT WITH A FOOT.]

MIKO: It has done it again.

BRIN: The court has now witnessed the cone's independent conduct.

### Act Two

[JUDGE ULA CALLS A NEIGHBOR AS WITNESS. THE WITNESS ARRIVES CARRYING A BASKET.]

FENN: Do you swear to tell the truth?

WITNESS: I came to return this basket.

JUDGE ULA: You may do both.

MIKO: The cone rolled into the basket yesterday.

BRIN: That sounds like containment.

MIKO: I picked it up and returned it.

BRIN: That sounds like trespass.

JUDGE ULA: Did the cone cross the property line?

WITNESS: It crossed the path.

FENN: The path is shared.

JUDGE ULA: Then the cone is in the correct jurisdiction.

[THE WITNESS SETS DOWN THE BASKET. THE PINECONE ROLLS INTO IT.]

BRIN: The court has discovered a natural solution.

MIKO: The basket belongs to the witness.

WITNESS: I can lend it until the path is cleared.

JUDGE ULA: The court will not seize the basket.

[THE JUDGE INVITES BOTH PARTIES TO TEST THE PROPOSED REPAIR. FENN TAKES THE CONE BACK TO THE TOP OF THE PATH.]

JUDGE ULA: Place the evidence at its usual location.

MIKO: It starts here, beside Brin's gate.

BRIN: The dip sends it downhill.

FENN: Shall I mark the starting point?

JUDGE ULA: Use chalk. No one is to move the cone by hand.

[FENN MARKS THE SPOT. THE CONE ROLLS DOWN THE PATH AND STOPS AT THE MUDDY DIP.]

MIKO: That is where it makes me step off the path.

BRIN: I thought it was the wind.

JUDGE ULA: The cone has now demonstrated cause and effect.

FENN: Should I call a witness?

JUDGE ULA: We are all witnesses.

[THE WITNESS USES A STICK TO MEASURE THE DIP. THE STICK IS LONGER THAN THE DIP.]

WITNESS: Gravel will fill it.

BRIN: I can bring a small bucket.

MIKO: I can sweep the path after.

JUDGE ULA: The parties have reached an agreement without requiring the cone to testify.

FENN: I prepared questions for the cone.

JUDGE ULA: Ask them at home.

[THE CONE ROLLS AGAIN, THIS TIME HALTING ON A FLAT PATCH.]

FENN: It has complied.

JUDGE ULA: Do not congratulate the evidence.

[THE CLERK OPENS A FOLDER MARKED "PRIOR CONE CASES." IT IS EMPTY.]

FENN: There are no precedents.

JUDGE ULA: Then we will avoid pretending there are.

MIKO: Can we just fix the dip?

JUDGE ULA: That is the most useful testimony so far.

FENN: I will file that under a very short hearing.

### Act Three

[THE JUDGE AND BOTH PARTIES WALK OUT TO THE PATH. THE COURTROOM CREW FOLLOWS.]

JUDGE ULA: The parties shall inspect the slope together.

BRIN: There is a dip here.

MIKO: If we fill it with gravel, the cone will stop.

BRIN: I have some gravel left from the gate.

JUDGE ULA: The court orders a repair, not a winner.

FENN: Should I write that as a ruling?

JUDGE ULA: Write it as a receipt for the gravel.

[BRIN POURS GRAVEL INTO THE DIP. MIKO RAKES IT FLAT. THE CONE ROLLS, SLOWS, AND STOPS AT THE EDGE.]

MIKO: It stayed.

BRIN: The cone has lost its case.

JUDGE ULA: The cone was never a party.

[THE CLERK PLACES A TINY PAPER COLLAR ON THE CONE: "NOT A PARTY TO THIS ACTION." THE JUDGE SIGNS IT.]
