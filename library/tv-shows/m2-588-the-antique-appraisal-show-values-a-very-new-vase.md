# The Antique Appraisal Show Values a Very New Vase

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: antiques television, expert reveals, dramatic valuation cards, and a vase that was made yesterday*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-020*

Cast: Appraiser Niko, a gibbon; host Mira, an orangutan; owner Tavi, a capuchin; producer Luma, a macaque; studio assistant Bram, a mandrill.

COLD OPEN

INT. APPRAISAL SET - DAY

Niko turns a vase over. A wet price sticker clings to its base.

NIKO: This artifact is still drying.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The appraisal show has invited viewers to bring heirlooms. Tavi carries a blue ceramic vase he bought at the market yesterday. Producer Luma wants a hidden-history reveal; host Mira wants the appraisal to be respectful. Appraiser Niko sees a maker's stamp and a price label still attached underneath.

MIRA: Tell us what you know about it.

TAVI: I bought it yesterday because it matched my curtains.

LUMA: Could you say your family has had it for generations?

TAVI: My family has had it for one night.

NIKO: The stamp says the local pottery studio made it this week.

Bram places a small velvet cloth under the vase. Tavi says the cloth is for the display, not part of the item. Luma asks for a close-up of the base. The camera finds the price label, which is upside down and still readable.

MIRA: We will show the whole object, including its label.

LUMA: The label weakens the mystery.

NIKO: It strengthens the provenance.

Tavi explains that he likes the vase because its blue glaze matches his curtains. Niko examines the glaze with a magnifier, checks the base, and asks permission to look inside. The vase is empty. Bram checks whether there is a hidden compartment. Niko says it is a normal vase and should not be struck or turned upside down over the lens.

ACT TWO

Luma suggests that the vase may have been made as a replica of an older design. Niko says that is possible, but the maker's mark identifies a contemporary studio. Tavi says the seller called it "classic." Mira asks what that word meant. Tavi replies that it matched the curtains.

TAVI: I did not buy an ancient object.

MIRA: You bought an object you liked.

NIKO: That is a valid reason to own a vase.

The show prepares a dramatic valuation card. Luma writes an estimated value based on the current studio price, then adds three zeros for suspense. Niko notices before the card goes on camera and removes the extra zeros. Bram holds up the card but has it upside down. The amount now looks like a different amount.

BRAM: I can flip the card.

NIKO: Please do. The value is not a plot twist.

Mira asks Niko to explain the condition. There is one small glaze bubble near the rim, typical of the firing and not a crack. The vase holds water. Tavi does not want to sell it; he wants to know whether it can hold flowers. Niko says yes, after a rinse, and recommends a felt pad so the base does not scratch the table.

LUMA: We could still ask whether he will auction it.

TAVI: I will not.

MIRA: Then we will not pretend he might.

Niko gives the appraisal: the current retail value is thirty coins, the same as Tavi paid. The vase has no hidden treasure and no family history. It has a good glaze, a small bubble, and a useful shape. Luma sighs because there is no shocking reveal.

NIKO: There is a reveal. It is a well-made vase.

ACT THREE

Mira asks Tavi to place the vase beside the curtains at home. Tavi says the studio set's curtains are not his curtains. Bram almost carries the vase toward the wrong drape, then stops when Tavi points to the actual fabric sample he brought.

TAVI: This one. The blue is close enough.

NIKO: The vase is stable on the felt pad.

LUMA: No auction, no inheritance, no secret compartment.

MIRA: A straightforward appraisal.

Tavi chooses to take the vase home rather than leave it for the show's prop department. Niko hands him the care card, and Bram folds the velvet cloth. Luma tries one last suspense sting; Mira asks the orchestra to save it for the next item.

TAVI: I bought it because I liked it yesterday. I like it today.

NIKO: That is a good appraisal result.

The crew records the label and the maker's stamp for the episode notes. The camera gets a wide shot of the vase next to the sample cloth, then cuts before anyone asks whether the curtains are antique.

FINAL IMAGE: Tavi carries the plainly priced vase out of the studio while the valuation card reads THIRTY COINS, NO SECRET COMPARTMENT.
