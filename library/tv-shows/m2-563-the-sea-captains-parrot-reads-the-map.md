# The Sea Captain's Parrot Reads the Map

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: maritime adventure serials, cryptic maps, and captains who ignore a chart because a talking bird has a better route*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: CAPTAIN RHO, a baboon; NAVIGATOR SERA, a gibbon; PARROT NIB, a capuchin monkey performing a parrot; DECKHAND, an orangutan; CARTOGRAPHER, a macaque.

COLD OPEN

INT. SHIP CABIN - DAY

Captain Rho spreads a map across the table. The parrot hops onto it and pecks the corner.

NIB: Turn it over.

RHO: It is already right side up.

SERA: Not for the coast.

The map slides off the table into a cup of tea.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The ship is following an old map to a lighthouse. Navigator Sera says the route is marked from the southern shore, but the captain is reading the northern copy. Nib repeats "turn it over" whenever the chart is opened. Rho believes the bird is giving a secret instruction.

RHO: The bird knows the passage.

SERA: The bird knows the paper is upside down.

The deckhand dries the chart. The ink has blurred, leaving three possible channels. The captain asks Nib to choose. Nib pecks a blue stain near the edge, then steals a cracker from the navigator's pocket.

DECKHAND: We have a course.

SERA: We have a cracker thief.

ACT TWO

The cartographer arrives by launch with a new tide chart. She confirms the lighthouse is on the western point, but the current has shifted a sandbar. The captain asks whether the map is wrong. The cartographer says the map is accurate for low tide. Sera overlays the current chart on top. Nib pecks the transparent layer, then pulls it sideways.

SERA: That is the channel.

RHO: The bird found it.

CARTOGRAPHER: The overlay found it.

The captain instructs the deckhand to steer along the marked passage. A squall pushes the ship toward the old channel. Nib flaps from the map table to the cabin window, where the lighthouse is visible between waves. The parrot calls out the same phrase, "Turn it over." Sera rotates the chart overlay to align the current markers. The new line points away from the rocks.

RHO: We are turning away from the lighthouse.

SERA: We are turning toward the safe water that leads there.

The deckhand adjusts the rudder. The ship clears the sandbar. The captain asks Nib to confirm the route. The bird repeats "cracker" and pecks the dry corner of the chart. The cartographer marks the channel with a fresh pencil line and signs the map.

EXT. SHIP DECK - LATE AFTERNOON

A squall arrives before the ship clears the point. The transparent overlay slides across the map table. Nib hops onto it and pins it with both feet. Sera asks the deckhand to close the hatch so the paper will not blow overboard. The captain orders the ship to slow down. The tide has begun to rise, and the channel will deepen, but the wind is pushing them toward the old sandbar.

RHO: The bird has the overlay.

SERA: The bird has both feet on the overlay.

Nib pecks the edge where the current marker is printed. Sera aligns the chart with the compass, then shows the captain the safe passage. The cartographer confirms it by radio and gives a second bearing. The captain steers away from the shallow water. The deckhand secures the map beneath a clip. Nib tries to pull the cracker from Sera's pocket but finds only a pencil.

NIB: Turn it over.

RHO: The map is right side up.

SERA: The tide layer is upside down.

They rotate only the transparent layer. The channel now matches the water beyond the bow. The captain confirms the route with the harbor master. The cartographer asks them to mark the temporary bearing and date it, so no one mistakes it for a permanent coast chart. The crew reaches calmer water before sunset.



The crew marks the safe channel in pencil on the transparent overlay, not on the original map. Sera checks the new line against both bearings, and the harbor master confirms the ship is clear of the sandbar. Nib receives a cracker only after the paper is clipped shut.

ACT THREE

The ship reaches the lighthouse at low speed. The captain credits Sera and the cartographer for the route. Nib receives a cracker and drops it on the map. The deckhand removes it before it can stain the chart. The captain puts the old map away in a dry case.

RHO: The bird is still the best navigator.

SERA: The bird is the only one who knows where the crackers are.

FINAL IMAGE: The map stays open and right side up while Nib sits on the dry case, guarding the snacks.
