# Hearts on the Veranda

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: daytime soap opera*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: In a seaside boarding house where every resident keeps one secret and three spare keys, a missing family recipe threatens to split two friends over a pie neither of them can bake.

Cast: Maribel, an elegant macaque who runs the Seaglass House; Tavi, her younger capuchin cousin and the boarding house's handyman; Dr. Sol, a serious proboscis monkey who delivers mail when the post cart is late; Aunt Cress, a sharp-eyed colobus who remembers the town's old recipes; and Celia, a ring-tailed lemur singer who has returned after years away.

## Episode: "The Crust Between Us"

### Cold open

INT. SEAGLASS HOUSE KITCHEN, MORNING

Maribel lifts a pie tin from the counter. It is empty except for a handwritten recipe card. A gust from the open window flips the card into the sink, where the ink begins to run.

MARIBEL: The family crust is gone.

Tavi steps in with a wrench and flour on his shoulder. Celia appears in the doorway holding a pie with a perfect golden crust.

CELIA: I thought you said nobody knew the recipe.

The pie collapses in her hands. A single cherry rolls under the stove. Everyone stares at it as if it has delivered a threat.

### Act one

The Seaglass House prepares for its annual supper. Maribel plans to sell it to pay for roof repairs. Celia returns to sing after years away from a quarrel with Maribel.

Aunt Cress wants the family pie as supper's centerpiece. The recipe card is washed out. Celia learned it from Maribel's mother, while Tavi's last crust was tough enough to mend a screen door.

Dr. Sol arrives with a stack of letters. One is addressed to Celia in Maribel's mother's hand, dated the week Celia left. Maribel says she has never seen it. Celia says she wrote twice and got no answer. Maribel asks why she stopped at two. Celia says she was twenty and dramatic. Maribel says she was nineteen and proud. Tavi asks if the pie can wait until after the family argument. Neither woman answers.

### Act two

Celia opens the jar. Inside are paper scraps, each marked with a measurement. She recognizes her mother's habit of writing recipes in pieces because she never wanted the household to depend on one card. Maribel thinks the scraps are a message. Celia says they are probably kitchen notes. The drama pauses while Dr. Sol sorts them by paper age and ink color. He finds that the measurements correspond to a dough recipe, but one number has been crossed out.


MARIBEL: The recipe card is ruined.
CELIA: It is only ink.
AUNT CRESS: It was family ink.
TAVI: The pie is also ruined.
CELIA: I made a sample.
MARIBEL: It collapsed.
CELIA: It was a dramatic entrance.
AUNT CRESS: The crust was raw.
TAVI: The cherries survived.
MARIBEL: We have a letter.
CELIA: From Mother?
MARIBEL: I never sent it.
CELIA: You never answered mine.
MARIBEL: I was proud.
CELIA: I was twenty.
TAVI: I was six.
AUNT CRESS: I was right.
MARIBEL: About what?
AUNT CRESS: Both of you.
CELIA: You knew?
AUNT CRESS: I knew the recipe.
TAVI: What about the house?
MARIBEL: I may sell it.
CELIA: After supper?
MARIBEL: Before the roof caves.
TAVI: I patched the east side.
MARIBEL: With salvaged tile?
TAVI: It still holds.
CELIA: Then let us fix it.
MARIBEL: You just came home.
CELIA: I can stay.
AUNT CRESS: For how long?
CELIA: Until the pie sets.
TAVI: That could take years.
MARIBEL: Don't tempt me.
DR. SOL: The letter has a postmark.
CELIA: It was never mailed.
DR. SOL: Then it is very late.
AUNT CRESS: Warm the oven.
MARIBEL: The recipe is missing.
TAVI: We still have the hands.
CELIA: And the butter.
AUNT CRESS: Keep it cold.
MARIBEL: That's the secret?
AUNT CRESS: That's the crust.

### Act three

Aunt Cress tastes the dough Celia has made from the scraps. It is crumbly and too sweet. She asks who chilled the butter. Nobody did. The missing instruction was not a secret ingredient. Their mother always kept butter in a clay pot cooled by seawater, and the recipe assumed everyone knew to keep it cold.

Tavi fixes the scale and weighs the ingredients. Celia cuts in cold butter, while Maribel adds a pinch of salt. They bicker over the amount, then find the flavor their mother used to make. The pie holds together, plain and a little uneven.

Before the supper, Maribel tells the guests she will postpone selling the house. She asks Tavi to estimate the roof repair and invite the neighbors to help. He agrees, but only if she stops presenting a decision as a favor. Celia asks if she can stay in the spare room while she finds work nearby. Maribel says the room is available, then asks whether Celia is sure she wants a quiet town after years on the road.

Celia answers that she wants to find out. The cousins share the pie on the veranda. It is not as pretty as the sample Celia brought, but it has the taste they remember. Dr. Sol reads the old letter aloud at Celia's request. It ends with an offer to leave the porch light on.

Maribel admits the lamp has been broken for years. Tavi says he can fix it before supper ends. He reaches for his wrench and knocks the cherry under the stove. It rolls out, still shiny.

### Tag

That evening, Celia sings the first verse of an old harbor song. Maribel joins her on the second line, then loses the words and hums instead.

Aunt Cress places the recipe scraps in a tin. She writes "keep cold" on the lid.

Tavi switches on the repaired porch light. It flickers twice, then steadies. Dr. Sol looks up from the doorstep.

DR. SOL: The house has received its reply.

The cousins laugh. The pie, finally, stays on its plate.
