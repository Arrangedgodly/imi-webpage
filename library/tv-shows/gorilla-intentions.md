# Gorilla Intentions

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: prestige crime drama convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: In a port city where every crate carries a story and half of them are lies, Detective Mara Marmoset follows the money behind a missing shipment of IMI's most profitable joke.

Cast: Mara Marmoset, patient major-crimes detective; Detective Puck, her eager partner; Sable Silverback, a freight boss who collects broken clocks; Ada Quill, IMI's harried archivist; Judge Nibs, who has never met a form he could finish in one sitting.

## Episode: "The Joke in Bay Nine"

### Cold open

EXT. HARBOR, DAWN

A cargo crane lowers an empty container onto the pier. The dockworkers wait for the sound of the load landing. It never comes. A single laugh floats out from inside.

One worker opens the doors. The container is empty except for a red punchline card and a small pool of rainwater.

CUT TO TITLE.

### Act one

Detective Mara arrives before sunrise, carrying coffee in a thermos that once belonged to her mother. Detective Puck has taped a yellow line around the container, then another around the first line.

MARA: Why two?

PUCK: One marks the crime scene. The other marks the first one's jurisdiction.

Mara reads the punchline card. It contains the final sentence from an unreleased IMI radio sketch, a line known to six people and the monkey who wrote it. The sketch's archive copy vanished last night.

At headquarters, Ada Quill explains that IMI's catalog is stored in a basement vault whose access log lists everyone, including the cleaning troop and a parrot who only visits to complain about the elevator. The joke has never been published. Ada says it is barely funny.

ADA: Which is how I know someone wanted it.

Mara asks who benefits from a joke's theft. Ada names the usual suspects: rival streamers, a touring comic with a grudge, the licensing team, and an intern who once tried to trademark a sneeze.

### Act two

The container belongs to Sable Silverback, who receives Mara at a warehouse of stopped clocks. He says the shipping manifest is clean. The manifest has a thumbprint over the line for cargo description.

SABLE: Ink covers a mistake. Thumbprints cover a decision.

He claims he was paid to move an empty container and heard nothing inside. Mara notices one clock runs backward. Sable says it is the only one that tells the truth about late fees.

Puck traces the shipment to an IMI loading dock and discovers that the truck weighed less when it left than when it returned. He assumes the joke was smuggled out as a recording. Ada corrects him: the original is a handwritten page, and the paper's watermark can identify it.

They find a watermark fragment in the truck's cab. It matches paper used for invoices. At IMI, Judge Nibs is reviewing a stack of claims from writers whose ideas resemble each other. He says a joke can be stolen, but similarity is a crowded room.

NIBS: Two monkeys can independently fall off the same chair. The question is who billed for the chair.

### Act three

Mara revisits the harbor. She asks the dockworker who found the card whether the laugh came from inside the container or from the crane operator. He says it came from the loudspeaker. The shipping firm had broadcast an old comedy reel to signal a night shift change.

The sound had been planted. The container was a decoy, and the card was bait. The missing script never left the vault. It was checked out under a legal hold, then returned with the wrong folder label.

Ada finds it inside a binder marked "Necessary Silence." The script's margins carry Sable's thumbprint, but he never touched the page. Mara compares it with the freight manifest: both prints came from the same rubber stamp used by the loading clerk.

The clerk confesses. He sold the punchline to a rival show, then copied it onto a card to make the theft look dramatic. He had read the line in a draft email that Ada meant to delete. The script was recovered; the joke itself had already appeared in a rival's promo.

MARA: So who owns it?

ADA: The writer. The company owns the folder.

The clerk is led away. Sable receives a fine for transporting an empty container without declaring it empty. He argues that the container had feelings.

At the station, Puck asks Mara why she let the clerk explain himself before reading the charge. Mara says people tell the truth when they believe the room has stopped listening. Puck points to the ceiling camera. She says the camera has terrible posture and no patience.

Ada returns with the draft email that exposed the joke. She can prove the clerk opened it, but the message also contains a note from the writer asking for a day off. Mara tells her to keep the note out of the report. The writer's private business has nothing to do with the theft. Ada nods, then asks whether the company can still license the promo. The rival has already pulled it. The writer will be paid for the unauthorized use, though the paperwork will take longer than the joke's entire life.

PUCK: Should we tell them the line wasn't funny?

MARA: We tell them what happened. Comedy can defend itself.

### Tag

In the evidence room, Puck reads the stolen punchline aloud. No one laughs. Mara takes a sip from her thermos.

MARA: That's how we know the copy is genuine.

She files the card under "noise." The clock in Sable's warehouse stops at the exact moment the paperwork arrives.
