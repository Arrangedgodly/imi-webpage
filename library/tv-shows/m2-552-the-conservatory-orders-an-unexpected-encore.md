# The Conservatory Orders an Unexpected Encore

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: classical concert broadcasts, audience etiquette, and presenters who treat a polite cough as a demand for another movement*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: CONDUCTOR ELI, a baboon; SOLOIST VERA, a gibbon; PRESENTER, an orangutan; STAGE MANAGER, a macaque; USHER, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. CONSERVATORY CONCERT HALL - NIGHT

A concert ends. The soloist bows. One audience member coughs. The presenter rushes onto the stage.

PRESENTER: The audience demands an encore!

STAGE MANAGER: That was one cough.

The conductor returns to the podium.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The presenter asks Vera to perform another piece. Vera's instrument is already packed in its case. The audience remains seated, waiting to see whether the encore will happen. The usher collects programs at the aisle.

VERA: I have no second piece prepared.

PRESENTER: You must have something.

CONDUCTOR ELI: We have a scheduled silence while the hall clears.

The stage manager points at the broadcast rundown. It lists a four-minute encore, a sponsor mention, and a commercial break, even though no one booked a second piece. The presenter asks Eli to improvise a short passage. Eli flips through the score folder and finds only the first movement.

ELI: I can repeat the final chord.

PRESENTER: A chord is not a piece.

VERA: Then the audience may leave.

The presenter waves to the camera, announcing that the soloist has "chosen a bold new ending." Vera picks up her case and walks toward the exit. The audience stands, thinking the concert is over. The camera follows her down the aisle, and the stage manager tries to keep the microphone cable from catching on the seats.

ACT TWO

INT. BACKSTAGE CORRIDOR - NIGHT

The presenter follows Vera and asks for one more minute. Vera agrees to return only if the encore is labeled as a repeat, not a new composition. Eli asks the stage manager for the original score. The usher brings it from the greenroom but has to walk through the audience, whose members have already begun leaving.

USHER: Please remain seated.

AUDIENCE MEMBER: The concert ended.

USHER: There is an encore.

AUDIENCE MEMBER: I heard a cough.

The stage manager moves the microphone stand and removes the sponsor card from the piano. Vera opens her case, takes out her instrument, and checks the strings. One string has loosened during the first performance. Eli gives her time to tune. The presenter begins a speech about generosity, then is cued to stop before the audience mistakes it for a second piece.

ELI: We will play the short prelude again.

VERA: At the right tempo.

The orchestra returns to the stage. Vera performs the prelude, this time with the repaired string. Eli keeps the chord from stretching into another movement. The audience listens, then applauds. The presenter bows before the musicians, then tries to introduce the sponsor.

STAGE MANAGER: The program is over.

INT. CONCERT HALL - NIGHT

The stage manager asks the orchestra whether a short encore can be performed with the lights half dimmed. The conductor studies the score. Vera says the loose string is repaired, but the instrument needs one more tuning. The presenter asks the usher to keep the audience seated while they tune. The usher says half the audience is already in the lobby.

PRESENTER: Invite them back.

USHER: They think we have finished.

The presenter makes a public announcement that the encore is a brief repeat, not a new work. Some audience members return; others keep walking. The stage manager asks whether the camera should hold on the empty seats. The conductor says to frame the musicians, not the missing people. Vera tests the repaired string. It holds. Eli gives the opening cue.

The orchestra starts. A late audience member walks across the aisle, searching for a seat. The stage manager stops the camera and asks for a reset. Vera objects that the performance was already underway. The usher escorts the late arrival to a side seat without crossing the camera line. Eli starts again, then stops when the presenter begins whispering into the microphone.

ELI: We can hear you.

PRESENTER: I was introducing the piece.

VERA: It is the same piece.

The presenter steps away from the microphone. The orchestra performs the short prelude once, without interruption. The audience applauds. The stage manager signals the broadcast to end before another encore is requested.



ACT THREE

The presenter announces the encore was a repeat of the prelude and thanks the audience for staying. The usher opens the side doors. Vera packs her instrument after the final bow. Eli closes the score folder and removes the unbooked four-minute segment from the broadcast rundown.

PRESENTER: Was that an encore?

VERA: It was the piece we played.

FINAL IMAGE: The concert hall empties while the program still lists an encore that lasted less time than the cough.
