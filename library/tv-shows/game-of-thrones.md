# Game of Groans

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: fantasy political saga convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: Seven monkey houses contest the Throne of Vines while the kingdom's real emergency, a widening hole in the royal budget, creeps toward winter.

Cast: Queen Cora Macaque, ruler who knows every debt by name; Prince Orlo, heir with a talent for speeches; Lady Fig, treasurer from the northern groves; General Knuckle, commander of the palace guard; Pip, a page who hears things because adults forget he can read.

## Episode: "A Small Matter of Succession"

### Cold open

EXT. NORTH WALL, NIGHT

Two sentries watch a frost-covered shape climb over the wall. It is a royal accountant, carrying a ledger and a lantern.

SENTRY ONE: The dead are walking.

ACCOUNTANT: The accounts are late.

He collapses. A sheet of red ink slips from his paw. The sentries look south, where the palace windows glow with a feast.

### Act one

In the palace, Queen Cora hosts a succession supper. She has invited each house to bring its candidate, its grievance, and one dish that does not require a treaty to serve. The long table holds ten empty chairs and a bowl of olives.

Prince Orlo enters in ceremonial armor with a scroll of his virtues. Cora asks him to sit down before he lists them. Lady Fig arrives from the northern groves with a ledger strapped to her back. General Knuckle brings no candidate, only three guards and a map of the wall.

QUEEN CORA: We are discussing who inherits the kingdom.

LADY FIG: We should discuss whether there will be a kingdom to inherit.

The room quiets. Fig says the royal reserve can fund either the spring grain shipment or the palace's annual tournament. Orlo suggests a smaller tournament. Fig asks how small. Orlo says the champions could fight on a table.

Pip is sent to fetch a new quill. He comes back with the page from the northern wall. The ledger shows that three houses have been charging the crown for the same bridge repair, although the bridge was washed away two years ago.

### Act two

Each house blames another. The House of Red Howl says it paid for the bridge. House Mottled says it supplied stone. House Longtail says it sent a crew, though the crew's travel claim places them in a seaside tavern for six weeks.

General Knuckle offers to arrest everyone. Cora refuses. She wants the bridge rebuilt before winter, and the grain shipment protected from the weather. Knuckle points out that the wall guards have no cloaks because the tournament budget absorbed the wool fund.

Orlo stands and offers his inheritance to pay for the grain. Lady Fig asks him to define inheritance. He means the silver crown on his head. The crown is tin, borrowed from the theater company for the supper.

ORLO: My willingness is the valuable part.

FIG: Then we'll spend that.

Pip notices the three bridge claims were approved with the same pawmark. It belongs to Queen Cora's late husband, whose seal remains on a drawer in the royal office. The current clerk has been copying it, convinced that the queen would rather have a bridge on paper than another crisis at supper.

### Act three

Cora summons the clerk. He admits the forgery and explains he moved the money to keep the northern road open after the bridge fell. He thought he could repair it quietly, but the coin went to pay for emergency rope, guard wages, and a small statue of the king that the town council insisted was necessary for morale.

Cora orders the statue sold and the forged claims struck. She assigns each house a share of the bridge work, paid in labor rather than coin. Knuckle will guard the grain road. Orlo must carry supplies with the work crews, without the tin crown. Fig will publish the accounts where every village can see them.

Orlo asks whether this means he is still the heir.

QUEEN CORA: It means you have work tomorrow.

The houses accept, each trying to claim it was their idea. Cora makes them sign the same sheet. Pip holds the inkpot while they argue over whose name goes first.

Fig asks for a second vote, this time on whether the palace should keep charging villages a winter tax while the bridge is closed. Orlo says the tax was meant to buy road salt. Knuckle produces the salt ledger. The last shipment went to the palace kitchens, where it was used to preserve a statue-shaped block of butter for the feast.

CORA: I approved that?

PIP: There are three signatures.

The queen looks at the page, then at her own hand. She crosses out the tax. The houses must refund what they collected or bring salt north themselves. No one volunteers. Orlo folds his sleeves and says he will start with the pantry. Knuckle assigns him two guards, who immediately ask if the prince knows how to carry a sack.

ORLO: I know how to carry a title.

FIG: That is lighter.

### Tag

At dawn, the first wagon of grain rolls north. Orlo walks beside it in plain clothes, carrying the tin crown in a sack.

He asks Pip if the crown is worth anything.

PIP: The theater wants it back.

ORLO: That is a kind of value.

Behind them, the palace treasurer pins the new budget to the gate. The page is already damp with sleet.
