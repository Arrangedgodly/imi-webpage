# The Mountain Lodge's Fireplace Is a Prop

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: cozy mountain mysteries, rustic hospitality dramas, and lodges whose most comforting feature is unconnected to heat*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: LODGE OWNER NARA, an orangutan; DETECTIVE FENN, a capuchin; GUEST LUMA, a baboon; MAINTENANCE TECH, a gibbon; COOK, a macaque.

COLD OPEN

INT. MOUNTAIN LODGE - NIGHT

A blizzard rattles the windows. Guests gather around a glowing fireplace. Luma holds out her hands.

LUMA: It is beautiful.

FENN: It is not warm.

The flames are a looped video playing on a screen inside the stone hearth.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

Nara says the lodge's heating system has stopped. The fireplace is decorative and the central boiler is making a clicking sound. Fenn, who is visiting to investigate a missing key, suspects someone has sabotaged the heat. The maintenance tech says the boiler is old but safe; its pressure valve needs a replacement part.

FENN: Who had access to the boiler room?

NARA: Everyone with a key.

LUMA: The key is missing.

The cook says the key was hanging beside the pantry. The detective checks the hook and finds a square of clean wall where the key used to be. Nara says the staff use a spare. The tech says the spare is locked in the office. The office door is frozen shut from a draft.

ACT TWO

Fenn interviews the guests. Luma says she saw a shadow near the fireplace. The cook says it was the television loop changing scenes. The lodge owner says the loop has three settings: roaring fire, gentle fire, and "embers for breakfast." Fenn asks whether anyone could have hidden the boiler key inside the decorative hearth. The tech checks but finds only a power cable and a remote control.

The key turns up in the sugar jar. The cook says he put it there so the cleaning crew would not mistake it for a spoon. The detective asks why the heat failed at the same time. The tech opens the boiler panel and finds the pressure valve was shut, not broken. Someone turned it to the off position while moving a box of firewood.

LUMA: The room was cold because the real heat was off.

FENN: And the fake fire was on.

NARA: It is a useful decoration.

The tech opens the valve and checks the pressure. The boiler starts warming the radiators. The guests move their chairs closer to an actual radiator. Fenn finds the "missing key" was never stolen; the kitchen staff hid it. He asks Nara to label the sugar jar.

INT. BOILER ROOM - NIGHT

The tech asks Nara to stay by the stairwell while the valve is checked. Fenn tests the pressure gauge and confirms the boiler is safe to restart. Luma holds the flashlight, but the decorative fireplace remote is in her other hand. She clicks it by accident; the screen flames brighten.

LUMA: The fake fire is warmer on camera.

FENN: The real radiator is warming the room.

The tech opens the valve a quarter turn. The pipe knocks once, then settles. The cook checks the kitchen radiator and reports that it is beginning to heat. Nara moves the guests away from the boiler room and closes the service door. Fenn asks the cook who put the key in the sugar jar. The cook says he was carrying it while filling the jar and set it down when the telephone rang. He forgot to retrieve it.

COOK: I thought it was the cinnamon tin.

NARA: It is labeled KEY.

COOK: The label was under the spoon.

The lodge owner places the key on the proper hook and checks the spare. Fenn records the explanation and asks Luma whether she saw a suspicious person. She says she saw only her own reflection in the dark screen. The detective notes the fireplace's power cord and the boiler's valve position separately. Nothing was sabotaged; the storm, the cold room, and the flickering screen had made ordinary delays look like a mystery.



[The guests return to the lobby. Nara switches off the decorative flames and asks the cook to bring hot tea instead. The radiators begin to warm the chairs. Fenn closes his notebook and checks that the key is back on the labeled hook.]

FENN: No theft, no sabotage.

LUMA: Just a prop.

NARA: And a boiler valve.

COOK: The tea is real.


ACT THREE

The lodge heats up. The owner turns off the fireplace screen and serves dinner. The cook returns the key to a locked hook in the office. Fenn writes in his notebook that the case involved a misplaced key, a shut valve, and one fake fire.

LUMA: Can the fireplace stay on?

NARA: It does not use heat.

FINAL IMAGE: Guests sit beside the warm radiator while the decorative flames flicker in the dark hearth.
