# The Winter Carnival Loses Its Snow

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: holiday event specials, live competitions, and organizers who keep a snow festival running after the snow melts*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: EVENT HOST, a baboon; RACE CAPTAIN, an orangutan; WEATHER REPORTER, a gibbon; VENDOR, a capuchin; VOLUNTEER, a macaque.

COLD OPEN

EXT. CARNIVAL FIELD - MORNING

A snowman stands on a patch of grass. The host lifts a banner reading WINTER SPRINT.

HOST: The race begins in ten minutes.

RACE CAPTAIN: The snow is gone.

HOST: The schedule is not.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The carnival organizers scramble to keep the event on time. The weather reporter shows last night's forecast, which predicted flurries. Today's live forecast says warm sun. The volunteer tries to roll a snowball, but it becomes mud. The race captain asks if the runners can use the grassy track instead.

RACE CAPTAIN: It is safe enough for a walk.

HOST: The event is a sprint.

VENDOR: The hot chocolate is still hot.

The host proposes replacing the snow race with a sled demonstration. The vendor's sled is still in the storage tent, and the tent door is stuck behind the food truck. The volunteer tries to pull the door. It slides open, but a stack of plastic skis falls out.

ACT TWO

The weather reporter is asked to stand beside the snowman and say the snow is holding. The snowman loses an arm. The host calls it a dramatic thaw. The race captain moves the runners to a dry field and tests the ground. The volunteer places cones and checks there are no slippery patches.

WEATHER REPORTER: It is warm, dry, and not a snow forecast.

HOST: Can you say that more festively?

WEATHER REPORTER: It is warm, dry, and the snow has gone home.

The event team changes the race to a relay using bright scarves instead of snowballs. The vendor sells the unused snowball tickets as hot chocolate vouchers. The host announces a "winter victory lap." The runners begin. One runner turns too early and loops around the food truck. The race captain stops the relay, resets the marker, and has everyone restart from the same line.

RACE CAPTAIN: We need one course.

VOLUNTEER: We have one cone left.

The volunteer puts it at the finish. The weather reporter holds the map while the host reads the new rules. The runners carry scarves through the course and hand them off at each marker. The team finishes without anyone slipping. The audience cheers at the finish, then asks where the snowman went. It has melted except for its carrot nose, which the vendor has put beside the hot chocolate.

EXT. CARNIVAL COURSE - AFTERNOON

The race captain walks the alternate route with a measuring wheel. One section is muddy, so the host suggests moving the finish line beside the food truck. The captain declines; the course must remain visible to the judges. The volunteer places a cone at each turn and asks the runners to test the ground in their ordinary shoes.

RACE CAPTAIN: No one sprints until we know where the mud is.

The host asks for a dramatic practice run. The first runner slips but stays upright. The captain stops the rehearsal and moves one cone away from the slick patch. The weather reporter explains that the day's warm air melted the snow faster than expected, but the field is stable away from the shaded edge.

WEATHER REPORTER: The course is dry after the second marker.

HOST: Can you say it with suspense?

WEATHER REPORTER: The second marker is dry.

The volunteer redraws the course on the program board. The host adds a rule that each runner must carry the scarf through the marked lane and hand it to the next runner. The race captain demonstrates a handoff. The scarf drops, so the team practices one more time. The second handoff works. The audience asks if the snowman's carrot is the relay baton. The vendor says it is a garnish and keeps it behind the counter.

The host announces the dry course, the weather conditions, and the revised event name: Winter Relay. The runners line up on equal marks. No snow is required to begin.



The host repeats the rules before the start. The race captain checks every cone and confirms the muddy section is out of bounds.

ACT THREE

The host awards the relay ribbon. The race captain records the course as a dry-weather alternate for future years. The reporter provides an accurate forecast for the afternoon. The vendor serves the remaining hot chocolate.

HOST: The snow is gone, but the carnival continues.

RACE CAPTAIN: The snowman is not part of the relay.

FINAL IMAGE: The carrot nose sits on the winner's ribbon while the runners carry the scarves back to storage.
