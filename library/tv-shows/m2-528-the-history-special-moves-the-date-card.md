# The History Special Moves the Date Card

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: history documentaries that let a date graphic drift onto the wrong event*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-018*

Cast: host Suma, a lemur; historian Daro, an orangutan; editor Vali, a macaque; producer Pera, a capuchin; archive technician Ralo, a gibbon.

COLD OPEN

INT. HISTORY STUDIO - DAY

A date card reads 1840. Daro holds a photograph from 1910.

SUMA: This image changed the course of history.

DARO: It was taken seventy years later.

ACT ONE

INT. STUDIO - CONTINUOUS

Pera says the timeline graphic is already animated. Vali has attached the date card to the next clip. The studio monitor shows a train, then a photograph of a bridge. Suma narrates the train's arrival as the bridge appears. Daro says the train did not exist in that year.

Pera asks him to keep the narration broad. Daro refuses. Ralo checks the archive labels. One box has two dates written on a flap. The first is the photograph's date; the second is the date it was cataloged.

ACT TWO

INT. ARCHIVE ROOM - LATER

Ralo finds the original print and its catalog sheet. The image is from 1910; the event described beside it happened in 1840. Vali asks which date the graphic should show. Daro says both, with labels. Pera says two dates will confuse viewers. Suma asks the historian to explain the difference on camera.

[The timeline animation begins early. The card 1840 slides over the photograph before Daro speaks. Vali stops the playback. The card continues moving on a loop.]

Ralo disconnects the animation feed. The date card disappears, but so does the title. Pera asks for a handwritten card. Daro writes the event date and the photograph date on separate lines. Suma reads them in order. Vali places each date beside the correct image. The archive technician checks the captions.

INT. STUDIO - MOMENTS LATER

The corrected sequence plays. First comes the event description, then a text card stating the photograph was taken later. The next image appears with its own date. Suma does not call the photo contemporary. Pera says it feels less dramatic. Daro says it is accurate.

INT. ARCHIVE ROOM - LATER

Ralo checks the next pair of date cards before they go to the edit. One is a catalog entry, the other a historical event. Vali places them on separate stands. Pera asks if the order can be shortened. Daro says the source needs to remain visible. Suma reads the event date, pauses, then introduces the later photograph. The graphic waits until each sentence ends before it appears.

A second card begins to slide across the screen. Vali stops the animation. It belongs to the following segment about a different bridge. Ralo checks the sleeve number and returns it to the right box. Pera asks for a smooth transition. Suma says the transition can happen after the dates are correct. The corrected clip plays once without the stray card. Suma asks Daro to verify the caption against the archive sleeve. He confirms both dates before they record the final narration. Vali saves the sequence under its event name, not the photograph's catalog date. The box closes with both records attached. Pera leaves the dates on screen. Suma reads the card once without shifting it. Ralo returns the sleeve. Pera verifies it. Correctly. The archive record matches.

ACT THREE

INT. HISTORY STUDIO - AFTERNOON

Suma introduces the sequence again. Vali has locked the date labels to their matching clips. Daro explains what happened in 1840, then identifies the later photograph as evidence of how the site looked decades afterward. Ralo holds the catalog sheet at the edge of the frame. The audience can read both dates.

Pera asks for the final animation. Vali declines to run the timeline; the labels will appear one at a time. The first date stays until Daro finishes. Then the photograph date appears. The train image follows only after its own caption. Nothing slides across the wrong event.

Suma asks whether a transition can still be dramatic. Daro turns over the catalog sheet. The reverse holds a small note from the photographer. Vali shows it after the dates, not before. The note describes the bridge repairs. Suma reads it aloud. Pera asks the graphics desk to add the repair date as a third card. Ralo checks the record and confirms it. The production team waits for the historian's cue before displaying it.

TAG

INT. EDITING ROOM - EVENING

Vali saves the corrected sequence. Pera removes the looping animation from the rundown. Ralo returns the print to its dated sleeve.

FINAL IMAGE: Three separate date cards sit beside their matching documents, with no timeline arrow crossing between them.
