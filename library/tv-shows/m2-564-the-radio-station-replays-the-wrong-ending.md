# The Radio Station Replays the Wrong Ending

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: live-to-tape broadcasts, audio drama cliffhangers, and control rooms that replay the ending before the episode begins*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: PRODUCER LEX, a gibbon; HOST RUMA, an orangutan; EDITOR PIP, a capuchin; ACTOR MOSS, a baboon; INTERN, a macaque.

COLD OPEN

INT. STATION CONTROL ROOM - NIGHT

The host is recording an audio drama. The actor delivers the final line: "The door was never locked." The producer checks the rundown.

PRODUCER LEX: We are recording the ending.

INTERN: We have not started the episode.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The station is making a mystery episode for broadcast at eight. The editor has recorded the last scene first, because the actor had to leave early. The producer asks the host to begin from the top. The monitor plays the final line again.

HOST RUMA: The door was never locked.

ACTOR MOSS: I am not reading.

EDITOR PIP: The monitor is.

The intern labels the files. The opening scene is named FINAL, and the finale is named OPENING. Pip says the labels were reversed when the files were transferred. Lex asks whether the audio itself is in the correct order. Pip queues the first scene. It begins with a detective entering a locked room, then immediately reveals that the door was never locked.

ACT TWO

The cast records the episode in order. Moss plays the detective. Ruma reads the witness. Lex watches the scene list. The second scene ends with a suspicious footstep, but the cue sheet says "play final door line." The monitor begins the ending again.

MOSS: I have not found the room yet.

PIP: The file says you have.

The intern checks the cue sheet against the script. The finale's audio file was placed in the auto-play folder, where it plays after every scene. Lex stops the playback. Pip moves the file to the final-scene folder and renames it with a number. The cast resumes. They reach the locked-room scene. The detective checks the handle; it turns easily. Moss improvises the line, "The door was never locked."

HOST: That is the ending.

PRODUCER: It is also the clue.

The show editor asks whether to keep the earlier recorded version. It has a clear delivery but the door sound is missing. They decide to use the new take and place a proper door latch sound afterward. Pip creates one sound effect by closing the studio booth door once, then labels the file DOOR LATCH, not ENDING.

INT. RECORDING BOOTH - NIGHT

The editor plays the episode from the opening. The detective enters the room, the witness gives a clue, and the suspect denies seeing the key. Moss waits for his line, then hears the ending from the control-room monitor.

MONITOR: The door was never locked.

MOSS: There it is again.

PIP: The ending file is in the right folder now.

Lex checks the playback routing. The monitor speaker is set to the station's live return channel, where a previous rehearsal remains in its buffer. Pip clears the buffer and plays the recording again. This time the scene continues to the detective's discovery. The host says the line at the correct moment. The actor tests the door latch sound at half volume; it fits the scene without drowning out the dialogue.

HOST: Now the door opens once.

PRODUCER: And the story ends once.

The intern prints a cue sheet with the scenes numbered in broadcast order. The editor clips it beside the console. Lex asks Moss to record a clean sign-off. Moss says, "The mystery is solved; please return the key." The host replies that the key is still in the lock. They perform the line again with the key removed, then the technician records the correct closing sound and labels it FINAL DOOR.



The station manager asks whether the episode can still air at eight. Lex says the corrected timeline is four minutes shorter because the repeated ending has been removed. The host fills the gap with the show's regular introduction, not a second mystery. The intern updates the schedule and checks that the final file is the one linked to the transmitter.

STATION MANAGER: Is the door line at the end?

PIP: Only at the end.

MOSS: That is where doors belong.

The producer listens to the final thirty seconds without speaking over it. The door latch sounds once. Moss delivers the closing line. The host signs off, and the control-room monitor goes dark.

ACT THREE

The control room assembles the episode in order: opening, investigation, discovery, finale. The producer plays the whole timeline once. The ending appears only at the end. The host signs off. The intern moves the auto-play file out of the opening playlist.

INTERN: No more door line.

MOSS: Until the next episode.

FINAL IMAGE: The monitor displays the episode's final scene while the control-room door stays open.
