# The Robot's Performance Review Has a Pulse

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: corporate workplace dramas, automated performance metrics, and managers who mistake a living operator for a malfunctioning robot*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: MANAGER TALA, a macaque; ROBOT UNIT 8, a baboon in a robot suit; TECH KIP, a capuchin; HR DIRECTOR, an orangutan; INTERN, a gibbon.

COLD OPEN

INT. OFFICE LAB - DAY

Manager Tala reviews a dashboard. Robot Unit 8 stands motionless beside a desk.

TALA: The robot's pulse is too high.

KIP: That is the operator inside it.

TALA: Then the operator needs a performance review.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The company has installed a customer-service robot for the lobby. Unit 8 is played by a baboon performer in a silver suit. The dashboard reports rapid heartbeat, slow response, and a high number of "unclassified emotions." The HR director arrives with a printed review form.

HR: We need to know whether the machine is meeting its targets.

KIP: It is not a machine.

TALA: It has a serial number.

The intern reads the manual. The robot should greet visitors, direct them to the right desk, and never ask personal questions. Unit 8 has greeted one visitor by asking if they are nervous. The visitor was nervous. The robot received a high empathy score and a policy warning.

TALA: Why did it say that?

UNIT 8: The visitor was holding a cactus upside down.

The HR director asks the operator to stay in character while reviewing the performance. Unit 8 says it has been standing for four hours and needs a break. The dashboard marks the statement as a "system interruption."

ACT TWO

The manager runs a role-play. The intern approaches the robot and asks for the legal department. Unit 8 points to the elevator, then asks the intern to secure the cactus. The intern is carrying a plant. The robot's advice is correct, but the dashboard logs it as unsolicited gardening.

KIP: The sensor reads a heartbeat, not a fault.

HR: Can it stop reading the heartbeat?

KIP: Only if we unplug the monitor.

The manager asks Unit 8 to repeat a greeting without empathy. It says, "Welcome. Please proceed to reception." The visitor comes back and asks where the cactus can be placed. Unit 8 points to a stable shelf. The visitor thanks it. The system scores the interaction as positive, then flags the robot for exceeding its approved task by one sentence.

TALA: We need a metric for useful judgment.

HR: There is a blank field.

The manager writes "human oversight required" on the review form. The intern checks the dashboard configuration and finds the robot was linked to the employee wellness sensor in the operator's suit. The pulse warning was never the robot's.

UNIT 8: I would like my break now.

TALA: Approved.

The technician removes the wellness monitor from the suit and links the dashboard to the robot's actual power readout. It displays 82 percent battery and one loose shoulder panel.

INT. HR REVIEW ROOM - DAY

Tala runs a second review using the robot's actual performance logs. The dashboard shows greetings completed, directions given, and one unscheduled cactus warning. The HR director asks whether the operator's heart rate can be removed from the performance chart. Kip unplugs the wellness monitor from the data feed, then watches Unit 8 walk across the room.

TALA: How many steps did it take?

UNIT 8: I do not know. I was walking.

The analyst asks the operator to repeat the route with the suit's shoulder panel secured. Unit 8 approaches the reception desk, greets the intern, and directs them to the legal department. The intern turns the wrong way. Unit 8 corrects the direction without being asked. The dashboard logs two answers for one question.

HR: That is a duplicate response.

KIP: It is a correction.

The HR director proposes measuring accuracy by whether the visitor reaches the correct desk. Tala asks the intern to follow the instructions. The intern arrives at Legal, returns, then asks where the restroom is. Unit 8 answers, but points with the left hand. The training model was configured for a right-handed operator and marks the gesture as wrong.

UNIT 8: I can point with either hand.

TALA: The review will say that.

The director edits the metrics. The operator signs the review as Unit 8's performance partner. The robot's battery is charged, the shoulder panel is fixed, and the human pulse is removed from the machine report.



ACT THREE

The company keeps Unit 8 for lobby directions but changes the review metrics. The operator gets a paid break and a water bottle. The HR director signs the form. Tala asks the robot to say goodbye to the visitor.

UNIT 8: Goodbye. Please secure the cactus.

FINAL IMAGE: The robot's battery meter holds steady while its operator drinks water behind the reception desk.
