# A Phrase Is Not a Branch

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: language-learning television that over-literalizes idioms, inserts recap songs, and quizzes viewers before teaching the answer*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-005*

Premise: Host Vela wants to teach a new idiom each day. Teacher Mako wants the audience to learn how expressions are used, while producer Puck keeps building literal props for each phrase.

Cast: Vela, a lemur host; Mako, a gorilla language teacher; Puck, a capuchin producer; and Seli, a macaque student.

## Episode: "Don't Let the Cat Out of the Bag"

### Cold Open

[CLASSROOM SET. PUCK PLACES A TOY CAT INSIDE A CLOTH BAG.]

VELA: Today's phrase is "let the cat out of the bag."

[PUCK OPENS THE BAG. THE TOY CAT FALLS OUT.]

MAKO: That is what the words say. The phrase means to reveal a secret.

SELI: What secret?

[PUCK CHECKS THE BAG. IT IS EMPTY.]

VELA: Is the secret now out?

MAKO: The toy cat is out. The idiom is about information.

[SELI WRITES "SECRET" ON A CARD AND PUTS IT IN AN ENVELOPE.]

SELI: If I tell Puck what is in the envelope, I let the cat out of the bag.

PUCK: Even if there is no cat?

MAKO: Especially if the bag is empty.

PUCK: The cat was the secret.

### Act One

[VELA WRITES THE IDIOM ON A BOARD. MAKO ADDS A DEFINITION.]

VELA: Can our viewers use the phrase in a sentence?

SELI: I let the cat out of the bag when I told my friend about the surprise party.

MAKO: Good. You revealed a secret.

[PUCK BRINGS OUT A SECOND BAG WITH A PAPER BANNER INSIDE.]

PUCK: Is the party in this bag?

MAKO: No. The phrase is not about storing secrets in fabric.

VELA: We have a visual example.

[PUCK OPENS THE BAG. THE BANNER UNFURLS, HITTING THE CAMERA.]

SELI: The surprise party is ruined.

MAKO: The example is complete.

### Act Two

[PUCK SETS A TABLE WITH CARDS SHOWING OTHER IDIOMS.]

VELA: Next phrase: "hold your horses."

[PUCK BRINGS A SMALL TOY HORSE. SELI HOLDS IT.]

SELI: I am holding the horse.

MAKO: It means wait patiently.

[PUCK ADDS THREE MORE HORSES. SELI'S ARMS ARE FULL.]

SELI: How patient should I be?

MAKO: Until someone tells you to begin.

[VELA STARTS A COUNTDOWN. MAKO TURNS IT OFF.]

MAKO: We are teaching the phrase, not racing the student.

[PUCK SHOWS A RECAP CARD: "THE CAT WAS A SECRET. THE HORSES WERE WAITING."]

VELA: Can we review the phrases?

MAKO: We can review what they mean.

[PUCK PLAYS THE RECAP JINGLE. THE TOY HORSES RATTLE ON THE TABLE. SELI CATCHES THEM BEFORE THEY FALL.]

SELI: I am holding the horses, but I am not waiting.

MAKO: Correct. You are preventing a toy pile-up.

SELI: The horse in my left hand is falling.

[PUCK CATCHES IT. THE TOY CAT SLIDES BACK INTO THE BAG.]

[MAKO GIVES THE VIEWERS A SHORT PRACTICE PROMPT.]

MAKO: If you tell someone where the cake is before the party, what did you do?

SELI: I let the cat out of the bag.

PUCK: There was no cat.

SELI: I revealed the surprise.

[THE STUDIO AUDIENCE ANSWERS ALONG. VELA WAITS FOR THEM TO FINISH BEFORE MOVING TO THE NEXT CARD.]

### Act Three

[MAKO ASKS SELI TO CHOOSE A PHRASE AND EXPLAIN IT.]

SELI: "Break the ice" means make people feel more comfortable when they first meet.

VELA: Can we demonstrate?

[PUCK BRINGS OUT A BLOCK OF ICE. SELI PUTS A CUP ON IT.]

SELI: I would rather ask someone their name.

[SELI GREETS PUCK. HE ANSWERS. THE ICE MELTS ONTO THE TABLE.]

MAKO: That is a useful example.

VELA: The viewers can try it with a partner.

[PUCK CLEANS THE WATER. HE PUTS THE TOY ANIMALS AWAY.]

SELI: The cat stays in the bag now?

MAKO: It is a toy. You can put it on the shelf.

[MAKO CHECKS THE NEXT PHRASE CARD. IT READS "ONCE IN A BLUE MOON."]

SELI: Will there be a moon prop?

MAKO: We can describe the phrase first.

[PUCK PUTS THE TOY CAT, HORSES, AND ICE AWAY BEFORE THE NEXT LESSON.]

[THE AUDIENCE PRACTICES THE FINAL PHRASE WITH A PARTNER. PUCK PUTS AWAY THE LAST PROP.]

SELI: No one had to break real ice.

MAKO: That is another useful sentence.

### Tag

[VELA SINGS A SHORT RECAP JINGLE. PUCK HOLDS UP THE WRONG CARD.]

VELA: A phrase can mean more than its words.

SELI: And a cat can mean a toy cat.

[MAKO TURNS THE CARD RIGHT SIDE UP. THE BOARD READS "NEXT: BREAK THE ICE."]

PUCK: Should I bring a hammer?

MAKO: Bring the definition card.

[PUCK PUTS THE HAMMER BACK IN THE PROP BOX AND TAKES A CHALKBOARD ERASER.]
