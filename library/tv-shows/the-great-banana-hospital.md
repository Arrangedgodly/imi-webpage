# The Great Banana Hospital

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: hospital drama convention*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Premise: A crowded neighborhood hospital tries to deliver careful care while its new administrator counts every minute twice.

Cast: Dr. Miko Langur, an observant physician with a long memory for faces; Nurse Dot, a practical squirrel monkey who can find anything in a supply closet; Dr. Bram Howler, a gifted surgeon who talks through nerves; Ellis, a young capuchin porter who knows every hallway shortcut; Administrator Crake, a clipboard-toting macaque who treats waiting rooms like spreadsheets.

## Episode: "The Quiet Room"

### Cold open

INT. SUNRISE WARD, EARLY MORNING

A patient presses the call button. A distant bell rings. Nurse Dot looks up from a chart, stands, and reaches the door before the sound finishes. The corridor is empty. She checks the call display. It says Room 12, but Room 12 is a linen cupboard.

DOT: The cupboard needs water again.

A muffled voice comes from inside.

PATIENT: I need a blanket.

Dot opens the door. Ellis is inside with a cart, trapped beneath a stack of folded sheets. He has answered the call by taking the shortcut through the cupboard.

### Act one

The ward is full, and Administrator Crake has introduced a new rule: each patient may press the call button twice per hour. A third press triggers a form explaining why the first two were insufficient. Crake says the button logs make staff efficiency visible. Miko asks whether the logs show patients waiting. Crake says the system counts button presses, not waits.

In Room 8, Mr. Teal, an elderly spider monkey, has been admitted for dizziness. He says the ceiling seems to tilt whenever the cart rolls by. Dr. Bram suspects an inner-ear problem and wants to order tests. Miko notices Teal keeps turning his head to follow a small paper bird taped above the bed. Teal made it from a menu and asks if anyone has seen its missing wing.

Meanwhile, a child in Room 11 has a fever and is scared of the oxygen mask. Dot sits beside her and lets her place the mask on a stuffed lemur first. The child whispers that the stuffed animal sounds like a bicycle tire. Dot explains the soft hiss, then asks the child to count the little breaths with her.

Crake visits and sees Dot sitting down. He says the ward has a staffing ratio to maintain. Dot points at the child, who has finally stopped gripping the bed rail. Crake checks his clipboard and writes "effective seated care" as if he has discovered a loophole.

### Act two

Bram orders a scan for Teal. Ellis wheels him toward imaging, but the elevator is reserved for supply deliveries after Crake changed the schedule. Ellis tries the service corridor. Teal asks him to stop at each corner because the turn makes the dizziness worse. Bram starts to explain the anatomy. Teal asks for a minute without a lecture.

Miko studies the room and spots that the ceiling tiles have been replaced at different heights after a leak. The paper bird is level with the floor, but the bed itself sits on a broken caster. Whenever the cart moves, the mattress rocks. Teal is not seeing the room tilt. He is feeling the bed shift beneath him.

Miko asks Dot to keep Teal still while she gets a replacement caster. Dot checks the supply room, then the cupboard. Nothing. Ellis remembers a stack of spare wheels in the pediatric playroom, left over from a toy cart repair. He runs for one. Crake catches him in the hall and says equipment cannot cross departments without transfer paperwork.

ELLIS: The wheel is not in a department. It is in a bucket.

Crake looks at the bucket, then at his form. There is no field for bucket.

Bram returns from imaging and sees the bed repair. He admits he missed the simplest cause because he was already preparing for the complicated one. Teal grins and folds a new wing for the paper bird. He says the hospital could use a better map of its hallways.

### Act three

The repair fixes Teal's dizziness. Bram cancels the scan before it begins and asks Teal to explain how the rocking felt. The patient describes the pattern, and Bram writes it down. He says he will start his next exam by asking what the patient notices, not what the chart expects.

The child in Room 11 asks Dot to keep the stuffed animal's mask on for the ride home. Dot promises to ask the child's parent, who is already waiting. The child removes the real mask herself and gives the toy a careful pat. Her fever has dropped, and her breathing is easy.

Crake reviews the shift numbers. He says the ward recorded fewer call presses after Dot explained how to use the button and repaired the bedside bell that had been ringing from the cupboard. He proposes a chart showing the call limit reduced demand.

Miko tells him the quiet came from people getting help. Crake pauses, then writes that down too. He removes the twice-per-hour restriction, but keeps the log for finding broken bells and slow responses. Dot says the log should include how long someone waited. Crake adds a column, then asks her what color makes a delay look less severe.

DOT: Red.

The team wheels the repaired cart down the hall. Teal's paper bird, now with both wings, rides above it. Bram holds the door while Ellis gets through without using the linen cupboard.

### Tag

A bell rings from Room 12. Crake hurries to the linen cupboard. Inside is a patient who has been waiting for a blanket. Crake hands it over and asks how long the call took.

PATIENT: Long enough to meet your clipboard.

Crake looks down. His clipboard has a little paper bird tucked into its clip. He takes the long hallway back to the ward.
