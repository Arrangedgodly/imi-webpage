# The Building Inspector Finds a Window

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: home-renovation reveals, building-code inspections, and owners who cover a window to make a room look larger*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: INSPECTOR KET, an orangutan; OWNER NELL, a macaque; CONTRACTOR, a baboon; DESIGNER, a gibbon; CAMERAPERSON, a capuchin.

COLD OPEN

INT. RENOVATED ROOM - DAY

The owner removes a curtain to reveal a painted wall.

OWNER NELL: Look at all this space.

INSPECTOR KET: Where is the window?

CONTRACTOR: Behind the wall.

The camera zooms in on a mural of a window.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

Ket inspects a renovated apartment for a television reveal. The contractor says the window was removed to create a clean wall. The designer says they covered it with a mural to preserve the room's "visual openness." Ket asks whether the real window is still accessible. The contractor points to a panel behind the mural. It is screwed shut.

KET: A window must open.

OWNER NELL: It opens in the picture.

The cameraperon asks the owner to stand beside the mural and describe the natural light. Nell says the room feels brighter. Ket checks a light meter; it is dark. The contractor turns on a hidden lamp behind the mural, creating a fake glow. The designer says the effect is cinematic. Ket says the room still needs ventilation.

ACT TWO

The team removes the mural panel. Behind it is the original window, covered with insulation. Ket asks the contractor to check the frame. The window opens outward, but a cabinet placed in front prevents it from moving. The owner says the cabinet holds paint samples. The designer wants to keep it because the room appears larger without visible furniture.

DESIGNER: The layout is minimal.

KET: The exit is blocked.

The cameraperon asks for a dramatic reveal. Ket opens the window; the cabinet has to move first. The contractor pushes it aside, but it catches on a rug. The owner lifts one side. The panel swings open. Cool air enters, and a curtain flutters over the painted mural. The camera catches both the real window and its painted imitation.

OWNER NELL: The room has two windows.

KET: One is a picture.

The inspector checks the latch, hinge, and clearance. The window opens fully and locks again. The cabinet is relocated to a wall that does not block the frame. The designer adds a smaller painted window panel across from it, but Ket confirms it is a flat decoration and not presented as an exit.

INT. RENOVATED ROOM - AFTERNOON

Ket asks the contractor to demonstrate the window's full travel. The cabinet is moved to the opposite wall, and the rug is rolled back. The window opens until the latch catches. The contractor says the latch is designed to limit the opening. Ket asks whether there is a separate release for emergency ventilation. The owner finds a small lever concealed by the curtain.

OWNER NELL: That is the part we never used.

Ket tests the lever. The window opens wider, clearing the egress measurement. The contractor checks the hinge bolts and tightens one loose screw. The designer asks whether the smaller painted window can stay. Ket says yes if it remains clearly decorative and does not cover a detector or path marker. The camera operator asks the owner to repeat the reveal with the curtain closed. The window appears painted again.

KET: We already saw the real window.

The owner opens the curtain. Daylight enters. The designer moves the mural panel to an interior wall, where it no longer hides the actual opening. The inspector measures the clearance and records it. The contractor places the cabinet on a marked spot beyond the window swing. The owner agrees to keep that area clear.

CAMERAPERSON: One final shot?

KET: After the inspection, yes.

Nell opens and locks the window once more. Ket signs the report, and the crew films the completed room without faking the light.



The cameraperon asks for one more wide shot of the painted wall. Ket checks that it does not suggest the window is open when it is not. The designer adds a small plaque reading DECORATIVE PANEL. The owner demonstrates the latch for the camera, then closes the window before the next inspection. The contractor leaves the cabinet in the marked position, clear of the frame.

KET: That is a usable room.

NELL: And a brighter one.

The inspector adds a note to the final report that the mural is not the window and the emergency opening works.

ACT THREE

The owner approves the new layout. The cameraperon films daylight entering through the actual window. The contractor removes the hidden lamp. Ket signs off on the inspection after checking the ventilation and clear path. The designer calls the room "open plan." Ket calls it "openable."

FINAL IMAGE: The real window stands open while the painted window stays flat on the opposite wall.
