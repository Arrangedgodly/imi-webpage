# The One More Thing Was a Lamp

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: televised product launches, countdown reveals, minimalist stages, and presenters who promise a surprise after the surprise*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-014*

Logline: A sleek product-launch show unveils a sun-tracking branch awning, but its sensor follows the studio spotlight instead of the sun.

Cast: Presenter Nema, a macaque; inventor Olo, an orangutan; designer Tavi, a gibbon; stage technician Piri, a capuchin; host Velo, a baboon.

COLD OPEN

INT. LAUNCH STAGE - NIGHT

A countdown hits zero. A white cloth lifts from a small awning mounted on a branch-shaped stand. The awning rotates toward the camera spotlight.

NEMA: It follows the sun.

OLO: That is the spotlight.

ACT ONE

Velo introduces BranchShade, an awning prototype designed to turn toward sunlight and provide a small patch of shade. Nema presents a clean animation of the product under a changing sun. Tavi dims the stage; the awning rotates toward the brightest remaining light.

NEMA: It responds to light.

OLO: It responds to the sensor input.

VELO: A more precise description.

Piri points a work lamp at the stand. The awning follows it. Velo asks for the first feature card. The card reads "AUTOMATIC SHADE." Tavi turns it around; the back says "DEMO MODE."

ACT TWO

Olo explains that the sensor was calibrated outdoors, not under stage spots. Nema asks the crew to simulate daylight. Piri moves the work lamp slowly across the stage. The awning tracks it, then turns toward a glowing exit sign.

TAVI: It found the brightest point.

OLO: The exit sign is not the sun.

NEMA: It is still a useful light source.

Velo asks if the product can adapt to a cloudy day. Olo says the current prototype has one sensor and no weather prediction. The animation shows clouds passing over a branch. The awning follows a moving graphic of the sun projected onto the backdrop instead of the real lamp.

PIRI: The screen is brighter than the lamp.

NEMA: Our animation is very bright.

ACT TWO-B

Tavi turns off the backdrop and asks the crew to test the physical sensor in ordinary light. The awning slowly settles toward the brightest window. Piri blocks the window with a large black flag; the awning turns toward the stage work lamp. Nema asks for a countdown before the next movement. Olo shows the sensor readings on a monitor.

OLO: The device measures light. It does not identify the sun.

VELO: Can we call that a feature?

OLO: We can call it the current prototype behavior.

Nema sees a small card at the stage edge and turns it over. It says "ONE MORE THING." She looks toward Velo, who signals for the reveal. Nema lifts the card; beneath it is an ordinary desk lamp. The awning tracks toward it again.

NEMA: Our one more thing is a lamp.

VELO: We already had a lamp.

PIRI: This one has a label.

A question appears on the audience screen: "Can it hold one position?" Olo turns off tracking and tightens the hand wheel. The awning stays aimed over the small bench. Tavi gently nudges the edge; it remains in place. Nema asks whether the lock is automatic. Olo says it is manual and should be checked after moving the stand.

NEMA: What if we leave tracking on?

OLO: Then it follows the strongest light it can detect.

Velo asks Piri to switch off the stage spots. The awning no longer turns toward them. The desk lamp is still on, so it rotates toward that instead. Piri switches the desk lamp off. The prototype stops. The audience laughs at the last exposed light on the stage, a tiny exit indicator.

TAVI: Is that our final light source?

OLO: We should test it outside.

NEMA: After the show.

ACT THREE

The product team removes the "automatic sun tracking" claim from the screen. They replace it with a prototype note explaining that the awning follows light intensity and needs outdoor testing before release. The manual position demonstration has already held steady; the crew records it once for the launch page.

NEMA: The manual setting works.

TAVI: The sensor still needs calibration.

VELO: We have one accurate demonstration.

Olo places the awning above a small bench and checks that it clears the branch. Nema stands underneath. She is in shade. The product team agrees to film the outdoor sensor test after launch day, not during the broadcast.

TAG

The countdown screen appears again. Piri covers the sensor with a card so it will not follow the studio lights during the closing image.

FINAL IMAGE: The awning stays in its manually set position while the desk lamp remains switched off beside the stand.
