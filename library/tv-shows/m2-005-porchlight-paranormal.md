# Porchlight Paranormal

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: paranormal investigation reality television*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: A night crew investigates strange knocks at a disused lighthouse, where the most frightening presence may be a colony of bats and one very loose shutter.

Cast: Tessa, a skeptical macaque sound technician; Bram, a dramatic mandrill host; Yuki, a young capuchin who reads old maintenance ledgers; Goro, a broad-shouldered baboon caretaker; and Auntie Moss, a retired lemur keeper who knows the lighthouse's habits.

## Episode: "The Knock Above the Stairs"

### Cold open

INT. LIGHTHOUSE, NIGHT

The crew stands in the dark. A knock sounds from above. Bram gasps and clutches a silver detector that is clearly a kitchen ladle with a dial glued on.

BRAM: Something wants us to leave.

A second knock. Tessa points the microphone toward the ceiling. The sound repeats, then a bat drops a pebble through a gap in the stairwell. It lands in Bram's hood.

### Act one

The crew enters with a camera, a temperature meter, and spare bulbs. Bram announces an overnight investigation. Goro says the warning bell stopped when he removed its rope. He had a ladder.

Yuki found a note about a damaged shutter and bats in the lantern room. The first knock comes from the landing every eleven minutes. Bram marks the times on a board.

Tessa asks what business a spirit would conduct at 1:11, 1:22, and 1:33. Bram suggests the numbers may be a code. Yuki notices that wind gusts have the same spacing. The tower has two vents that pull air through the stairwell. The gusts lift a loose shutter, which strikes the frame.

The team climbs to check. The shutter is above a narrow stair turn, and the gap is too small for Goro's shoulders. Bram says he will go first. He takes two steps, hears a flutter, and reverses without making a sound. Tessa makes a note: "Host can retreat silently when motivated."

### Act two

Auntie Moss arrives with tea. She used to clean the lens room and warns the team not to shine lights at the bats, which roost there for shelter and insects.

Yuki finds a sketch of the shutter in an old ledger. A repair note says to secure it with a cotton strap, not a nail, because the tower shifts in strong wind. The supply shelf is empty. Goro offers a strip from his spare work apron. Bram asks whether sacrificing a garment is a traditional cleansing ritual. Goro says it is laundry day.

The crew waits for the next gust. Tessa places two microphones, one by the shutter and one by the bell. When the knock comes, the shutter hits the frame. The bell's clapper moves a fraction, but cannot ring without the rope. Bram says the tower is speaking through architecture. Tessa says architecture is speaking through a loose hinge.

The bats stir when the camera operator raises a lamp. Moss steps between the light and the roost, covering the lens. Bram's dramatic speech stops. She asks them to lower the lamp and keep the walkway clear. The crew switches to infrared and films the bats leaving through the vent to hunt.

### Act three

Goro and Tessa strap the shutter. The next gust makes no knock. Yuki records the fix. The bell stays silent, and Moss refuses to restore the rope because it would disturb the bats and neighbors.

The crew finds a different sound: a small bell in the keeper's kitchen, which rings when the wind pushes a loose pane. That pane is beside the shelf where Bram set the metal detector. The detector's dial has been wobbling all night. Tessa shows that the dial is only a compass needle glued to the ladle, and it points toward the iron stove.

Bram thanks Moss for showing them the tower's ordinary life. He admits he arrived hoping to find a ghost and found a maintenance problem with excellent acoustics. Moss says the bats have been here longer than any keeper. If the crew wants to call them spirits, they should at least call them by their proper name.

Yuki closes the ledger with a new note: "Shutter secured. Bats undisturbed. Bell remains silent by request." The crew leaves the tower before dawn. Bram carries the ladle detector himself, careful not to bend its dial.

### Tag

At the parking area, Bram reviews the footage. A bat crossed the lens during his first speech, making it look as if a shadow had grabbed his shoulder.

BRAM: We should use that shot.

TESSA: We should use the whole shot. Then people can see it was a bat.

Bram considers this. He nods. The bat returns, lands on the sign above his head, and drops another pebble into his hood.

BRAM: It has notes.
