# The Garden Show Awards a Topiary

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: home-and-garden competition conventions, dramatic judging reveals, and a shrub that refuses to fit the category*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-020*

Cast: Host Mira, an orangutan; judge Odo, a macaque; gardener Niko, a gibbon; producer Vex, a baboon; groundskeeper Luma, a capuchin; envelope runner Pip, a squirrel monkey (silent); rosemary gardener Rika, a lemur (silent); two additional judges and camera crew (silent).

COLD OPEN

EXT. GARDEN SHOW - DAY

A velvet curtain hides a clipped shrub. The host raises an envelope.

MIRA: The judges have made their decision.

Mira pulls the curtain aside. A hedge wiggles in the wind. Everyone gasps.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The garden show is filming its annual topiary awards. Producer Vex wants a grand reveal with a suspenseful envelope. Judge Odo has asked for the plants to remain watered and shaded during filming. Gardener Niko's entry is shaped like a teapot, but the printed category list says animal, landmark, or abstract form.

VEX: We will reveal the winner after three commercial breaks.

ODO: There is one entry waiting here.

MIRA: Then why are we waiting?

VEX: It still needs suspense.

NIKO: My teapot is drying in the sun. Can we move it under the shade tent?

LUMA: The wheels on the plant stand are locked. I can unlock them after the camera cables are lifted.

MIRA: The show must protect its suspense.

ODO: The show must protect the plant.

Vex sends a runner to fetch the envelope while Niko checks the soil. The topiary is shaped like a teapot but includes a small handle from a climbing vine. The wind lifts the vine, making the shape look like an animal raising a tail.

MIRA: I see the audience reacting.

ODO: The audience is watching the vine move.

VEX: We have a twist.

NIKO: We have a gust.

ACT TWO

The judges inspect the entries. Odo asks whether the teapot meets the categories. Vex wants to call it an abstract animal and keep the award sequence dramatic. Niko says the intended design is a teapot, but the rules don't list household objects. Mira asks the production to show the category sheet rather than invent one after judging.

ODO: The plant can be evaluated for shape, pruning, and health. The category decides its award.

VEX: Could we create a new category live?

MIRA: We would need to announce the rule before judging.

LUMA: The teapot's lower branch is bent against the stand. It needs a careful turn, not a new category.

NIKO: The stand locks are under the saucer tray. Please lift the camera cable first.

The crew raises the cable. Luma unlocks the wheels. Niko rotates the pot stand by one quarter turn, and Luma locks the wheels again. The vine handle settles behind the teapot silhouette. Odo examines the leaves and finds one brown patch where the plant touched the curtain.

ODO: We can trim the damaged tip, but not during the judging.

NIKO: I can do that after the show. It won't change the outline.

VEX: The camera wants a close-up of the brown patch.

MIRA: Show the whole entry. The patch is a care note, not a cliffhanger.

Rika wheels in a second entry late: a simple spiral made from rosemary. The rules allow it only if it was registered before the deadline. Luma checks the list; the spiral is registered under abstract form. Odo tells the panel the new entry can compete in its own category, but it cannot retroactively change the teapot's classification.

VEX: Now we have two entries. Can we have suspense?

ODO: We can have an actual comparison.

MIRA: The envelope lists the winner in each category. Please read the criteria with the result.

Before the reveal, Luma brings forward the ribbon board. The labels are still arranged in last year's order, and the abstract-form ribbon is printed twice.

LUMA: We have one ribbon for the rosemary spiral and one special citation for the teapot. The duplicate label can go back to the printer.

VEX: Could we reveal both envelopes one at a time?

MIRA: No. Read the category and result together, then attach each label to the right plant.

Odo checks the printed ribbon labels against the registration list and sets them in order on the board. Luma reserves a plain clip for the teapot citation, which will attach to the stand rather than the branches. The rosemary spiral is ready for its category ribbon. No one has to cover a leaf with a paper sash.

ACT THREE

Odo awards the rosemary spiral the abstract-form ribbon for even pruning and healthy growth. Luma attaches that ribbon to its pot, then clips the special design citation to the teapot stand. Household objects were not listed, so the teapot does not receive a category prize. Niko is disappointed until Mira invites the audience to choose a category for next year's show before registrations open.

NIKO: Could you add household forms? Then the teapot would be eligible.

ODO: The judging panel can recommend that change, but the organizers decide next season's rules.

VEX: That means we have to wait a year for the reveal.

MIRA: It means the rules come first.

Luma releases the wheel locks, rolls the teapot to the shade tent, and secures its stand. Niko trims the brown leaf tip with clean shears after the camera is off. Odo checks that the already attached ribbon and citation remain visible, then confirms neither plant is sitting in standing water.

VEX: We can end with the envelope.

MIRA: We ended with the result.

FINAL IMAGE: The teapot topiary rests in shade beside the award sheet, whose blank line reads HOUSEHOLD FORM - PROPOSED FOR NEXT YEAR.
