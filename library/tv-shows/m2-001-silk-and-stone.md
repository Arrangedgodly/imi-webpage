# Silk and Stone

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: historical costume drama*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV*

Premise: At a mountain court obsessed with family rank, a clever young archivist tries to save the village's shared water channel before the palace's spring pageant buries the evidence of a bad decision.

Cast: Lady Sela, a ring-tailed lemur archivist with ink-stained cuffs; Lord Rusk, her proud uncle and keeper of the south orchard; Prince Nilo, a young macaque who can imitate any courtier but hates being mistaken for one; Dame Osa, an elderly langur who remembers every renovation; Pella, a palace cook who climbs curtains when she is nervous.

## Episode: "The Canal Beneath the Ballroom"

### Cold open

INT. PALACE BALLROOM, DAWN

A footman pulls back a velvet curtain. Water pours through the ballroom floorboards and runs downhill toward the garden. A court musician lifts his cello onto a chair. Pella climbs the curtain rod with a tray of buns in her teeth.

LORD RUSK: The ballroom is flooding.

DAME OSA: It has always wanted to be a river.

A cracked floor tile rises like a little island. Beneath it, a carved stone channel carries water through the palace. Sela kneels, touches the wet stone, and spots a date cut into the rim. She looks toward the portraits of three dead dukes.

SELA: This was meant to be opened today.

### Act one

At breakfast, Lord Rusk declares that the annual Spring Arrival will proceed. The court has spent six weeks sewing a ceremonial train for Prince Nilo, and the duchy cannot abandon its pageant because of a little water. Dame Osa points out that the water has reached the dais, where the prince is expected to recite his family's virtues. Nilo says he can recite them from a dry chair.

### Act two

In the village, Nilo learns that the dry wells began when palace workers blocked the old channel during construction. He finds a line of small hoof and paw marks where residents have been hauling water from a distant spring. A young macaque has marked each trip on a wall with charcoal. Nilo asks why nobody brought the marks to court. She answers that the last petition was returned because the handwriting did not match the seal.

Back at the palace, Sela and Dame Osa crawl beneath the ballroom with a lantern. Osa remembers a maintenance hatch hidden behind the portrait of Duke Pomp. She pushes the frame. It swings open, revealing a narrow passage lined with old clay pipes. Sela finds a newer brick plug. Its mortar bears the crest of Lord Rusk's orchard estate.

Rusk enters, carrying a dry pair of dancing shoes. He admits he ordered the canal sealed last year. The orchard's new trees needed water, and he believed the village had another source. The palace ledger shows the canal still belonged to the village, but he feared the court would accuse him of stealing if he asked to change the allotment. He expected to reopen it after the harvest. The harvest failed anyway.

Sela says the court will hear the truth at the pageant, where every family is gathered. Rusk says a public confession could cost him his title. Osa says titles are easier to replace than wells.


SELA: The ballroom is flooding.
RUSK: The ballroom is historic.
OSA: So is the water.
NILO: I rehearsed my speech.
SELA: Rehearse a question.
RUSK: The pageant is today.
PELLA: The floor is wet.
RUSK: We noticed.
PELLA: The buns are wet too.
NILO: That seems worse.
OSA: It is the same water.
SELA: The canal is blocked.
RUSK: The canal is old.
SELA: The village is thirsty.
RUSK: The village has wells.
OSA: Empty wells.
RUSK: Who told you?
SELA: Their buckets did.
NILO: Buckets can talk?
SELA: They sound hollow.
RUSK: I hear no sound.
OSA: You have soft shoes.
PELLA: And a dry pantry.
NILO: Can I cancel the train?
RUSK: It took six seamstresses.
SELA: It will drag in water.
PELLA: I can float it.
RUSK: Nobody floats a prince.
NILO: I can walk.
OSA: A royal first.
RUSK: The pageant needs a speech.
SELA: It needs a working canal.
NILO: I can announce the repair.
RUSK: The repair is not ready.
SELA: The truth is ready.
OSA: That is rarely tailored.
PELLA: Neither is the train.
NILO: Then let it drag.
RUSK: That is not tradition.
SELA: Neither is a flooded ballroom.

### Act three

The musicians begin the pageant. Nilo returns in his court robe, carrying a bucket instead of a ceremonial staff. He interrupts the first speech and asks the village children to read their water marks aloud. The charcoal tallies show empty wells and long walks.

Sela presents the canal plans. Rusk confesses he blocked the channel to favor his orchard. The court gasps. He places his signet ring on the table and offers the orchard's next water share to the village until the channel is repaired. He also agrees to pay the masons from his own stores.

The palace mason removes the brick plug. Water surges through the stone groove, then runs into the ballroom's shallow floor trench. Guests follow the channel outside. Pella carries buns overhead. A duck steals one.

The duchess asks whether the pageant can continue. Nilo suggests a procession to the village spring instead. He recites a short family history that begins with the duke who built the canal and ends with the people who kept it clear. He leaves out three ancestors who only collected tolls.

### Tag

The next morning, the ballroom is dry except for one line of water in the carved channel. The court's seamstress pins the prince's train to the ceiling so it will not drag.

Nilo takes a step. The train stays aloft, and a footman below gets a sudden shower.

DAME OSA: The palace has found a new fountain.

Rusk laughs first. Sela writes the date beside the canal plan, then adds, "Opened by everyone who came to see the pageant."
