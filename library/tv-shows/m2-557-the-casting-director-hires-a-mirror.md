# The Casting Director Hires a Mirror

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: prestige casting dramas, talent-search montages, and producers who believe a reflection can replace an actor*
*By: IMI TV Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-019*

Cast: CASTING DIRECTOR LEX, a gibbon; PRODUCER, a baboon; ACTOR MIRA, an orangutan; ASSISTANT, a capuchin; MIRROR TECH, a macaque.

COLD OPEN

INT. CASTING STUDIO - DAY

A tall mirror stands on the audition mark. The producer looks at the reflection.

PRODUCER: The new lead has range.

CASTING DIRECTOR LEX: That is you.

PRODUCER: Then it is affordable.

CUT TO TITLE.

ACT ONE

The producer announces that the series needs a double for Mira, the star actor, who has injured a tail during rehearsal. Mira arrives with a cushion and says she can still perform from the waist up. The producer points to the mirror, calling it a "non-union visual performer." The mirror tech wheels it to the mark.

MIRA: It reflects the person in front of it.

PRODUCER: That is an excellent audition.

Lex asks the mirror to read a dramatic line. The assistant stands behind the camera and holds the script. The mirror reflects the assistant instead. The producer asks for more emotion. The assistant changes expressions. The mirror reproduces every one.

LEX: The character is reacting.

MIRA: The mirror is reflecting.

The producer asks to cast the mirror as the villain. Lex reads opposite it. Whenever Lex moves, the reflected figure moves too. The producer says the timing is impeccable. Mira asks the mirror tech whether it can turn away. The tech explains that the mirror is mounted on wheels, but only the tech can steer it.

ACT TWO

INT. REHEARSAL ROOM - DAY

The director tests a scene in which the heroine confronts her double. Mira speaks to the mirror. Lex plays the double. The mirror reflects Lex's back, not Mira's face. The producer asks whether they can film the reflection and use it later.

MIRA: Then the mirror is on screen but not in the story.

PRODUCER: The audience will understand.

LEX: Which audience?

The assistant tries a close-up. The camera captures its own lens in the mirror, along with the producer holding a snack. The producer says the snack is part of the character's backstory. The director asks Mira to perform the line without looking at the mirror. She does; the scene becomes stronger. Lex steps in as the double, but the camera operator has placed a second mirror behind them, creating an endless corridor of reflections.

MIRA: We have too many doubles.

The mirror tech rolls one panel away and leaves a single reflecting surface. Lex rehearses the villain's reply. Mira changes one line, and Lex responds with an unexpected pause. The director likes the pause. The producer asks if the mirror can repeat it exactly. Lex says the mirror can only repeat what is already there.

INT. CASTING STUDIO - LATER

Lex asks Mira to stand opposite the mirror for one more camera test. The producer wants to know whether the mirror can serve as a stand-in during a lighting reset. The mirror tech turns it toward the set. Every lamp reflects back into the lens.

ASSISTANT: We have four suns.

MIRA: None of them are real.

The director marks the camera angle with tape. Lex steps onto the mark and delivers the villain's line. Mira answers from off-screen. The mirror reflects Lex, the producer, the assistant, and the camera operator, all at once. The producer says the frame feels crowded. The mirror tech rotates the panel ten degrees. The reflection shows only the empty chair and the bright set lamp.

LEX: Now it looks like an audition.

PRODUCER: Can we keep the mirror in the scene?

MIRA: Only if the character knows it's there.

The director rewrites the scene so the heroine uses the mirror to check whether someone is behind her. Lex plays the double in the doorway. Mira looks at the mirror, sees Lex's reflection, and turns. The action reads clearly on camera. The producer approves the take but asks for one close-up of the mirror. The camera operator records the empty chair reflected in it. The assistant labels the shot "chair, not actor."



ACT THREE

INT. CASTING STUDIO - DAY

Lex is cast as the double. Mira performs the scene with Lex, not the mirror. The producer hires the mirror as a set piece and asks for a credit line. The assistant writes "Mirror: itself." The mirror tech moves it off the mark so the crew can clear the room.

PRODUCER: It had star quality.

LEX: It had a bright surface.

FINAL IMAGE: The mirror reflects the empty audition chair while Mira and Lex walk onto set together.
