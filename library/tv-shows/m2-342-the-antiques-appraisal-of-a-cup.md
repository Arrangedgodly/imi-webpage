# The Antiques Appraisal of a Cup

*Format: TV show parody*
*Parody target: antiques appraisal programs that assign grand histories to ordinary household objects*
*By: IMI Television Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-TV-012*

Premise: A neighborhood appraisal show examines an old cup whose owner simply wants to know if it leaks.

Cast: Appraiser Odo, a baboon; host Leni, a gibbon; owner Pava, a capuchin; historian Nara, a macaque.

Episode: "The Cup on the Table"

COLD OPEN

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - DAY

Pava places a chipped cup on a velvet pad.

LENI: Tell us its story.

PAVA: It was on the shelf.

ODO: A humble beginning.

Nara turns the cup over. A mark reads "KITCHEN."

NARA: It belonged to the kitchen.

ACT ONE

Odo puts on gloves and examines the chip. He estimates the cup has been used for years. Leni asks whether it could be a rare ceremonial vessel. Pava says it held water during meetings.

Odo finds a faded blue stripe. Nara says the stripe is paint. Leni asks if it was added by a local artisan. Pava says someone painted it during a craft afternoon.

Odo asks if the cup has been passed down through generations. Pava says it was purchased last spring. The appraisal team falls quiet. Leni says a new object can still carry history.

ACT TWO

Odo checks the bottom for a maker's mark. The mark is hidden under a felt pad. He peels the pad back and finds a price sticker. Leni asks whether the sticker increases or lowers the value.

NARA: It explains the cup was sold at the market.

Pava says she brought the cup because it drips when she pours. Odo fills it with water. It does not leak. Leni asks if the appraisal can continue. Odo pours faster. A thin crack opens under the handle.

Pava catches the water with a cloth. Nara says the cup is not valuable enough to repair. Pava says she still likes it. Odo asks if it has sentimental value. Pava says it reminds her of the meeting where the garden chose its name.

Leni says that is a story worth keeping. Odo revises the appraised price to one cup of tea. Pava says she did not bring it for money.

ACT TWO-B

INT. COMMUNITY KITCHEN - AFTERNOON

Nara places the cup on a shelf labeled "CRAFTS." Pava says it should stay in the kitchen. Odo tests the crack again; a drop forms under the handle. He recommends not using it for liquids.

Leni asks if the cup can still serve a purpose. Pava says it can hold spoons. She sets three wooden spoons inside. The weight does not worsen the crack.

Odo says the cup has found a new role. Leni asks the camera to frame it beside the meeting sign. Pava asks them not to call it a relic.

ACT TWO-C

Nara asks Pava if the cup has a maker's signature. Pava says she made the blue stripe herself, but did not sign it. Odo says that makes it personal rather than rare. Leni asks if a future collector might value it. Odo says the crack will lower its price, and the cup is not old enough to be an antique.

Pava says she does not care about the market price; she needs to know whether it is safe to keep. Nara tests the crack with a dry cloth and sees a tiny bead of water. The cup should not hold drinks, but it can hold spoons. Odo checks whether the wooden spoons will scratch the clay. They are smooth and do not.

Leni asks the crew to describe the cup in plain terms for the inventory. Pava dictates: "A blue-striped kitchen cup, purchased last spring, with a crack under the handle." Odo adds: "Owner wishes to keep it." The appraisal camera zooms out from the velvet pad to show the cup beside the utensil jar.

Leni asks Odo to appraise the spoons the cup now holds. He says they are common wooden spoons, worth a spoonful of tea each. Pava says that is exactly what she needs. Nara checks the cup's new job by taking a spoon out and returning it. The crack stays dry. Odo changes the item card from "drinking vessel" to "utensil holder." Leni asks if the cup can still be displayed. Pava says it can sit on her kitchen shelf where she can reach it. The crew removes the velvet pad and wraps the cup in a towel for the trip home.

ACT THREE

The team records the final appraisal. The cup is common, chipped, and not suitable for drinking, but Pava wants to keep it as a spoon holder. Nara adds a label: "Kitchen cup, now used for utensils." Odo says it has no auction value. Leni says its value is determined by its owner.

PAVA: And the spoons fit.

TAG

Odo looks at the empty velvet pad.

LENI: What is its history?

ODO: It came with the show.
