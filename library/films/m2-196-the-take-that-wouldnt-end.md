# The Take That Wouldn't End

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: auteur filmmaking, endless retakes, symbolic blocking, and a director who mistakes accidents for deliberate choices*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
DIRECTOR VERO, a capuchin
SANA, a macaque actor
BELL, a gibbon camera operator
MOSS, a ring-tailed lemur park keeper

SCENE 1. EXT. CITY PARK - MORNING

A film crew prepares one simple shot: Sana crosses a bridge, looks at the pond, and leaves a red scarf on a bench. Moss has borrowed it from a regular park visitor until four. Director Vero studies the framing. A duck swims through the shot.

VERO: Cut. The duck changes the meaning.

SANA: It's in the pond.

VERO: It enters the scene with no motivation.

Bell adjusts the camera. The duck exits frame. A squirrel runs across the bridge, carrying a nut.

VERO: Cut. The squirrel's arc is too decisive.

MOSS: The animals use the park.

VERO: Not during my film.

SCENE 2. EXT. BRIDGE - LATER

Sana performs the walk. She looks at the pond, places the scarf on the bench, and leaves. Vero asks for another take with more hesitation.

SANA: Should I hesitate before the bridge or after I see the water?

VERO: Between those feelings.

Sana walks halfway, stops, and looks at the pond for nearly a minute. The crew waits. A cloud covers the sun. Vero calls it the scene's emotional weather.

BELL: The exposure just changed.

VERO: Keep rolling.

SCENE 3. EXT. BENCH - AFTERNOON

The scarf keeps moving. Wind lifts it; Sana catches it. A duck grabs the edge and tugs. Vero says the scarf represents an unfinished goodbye. Moss says the bench is needed by an elderly visitor.

MOSS: The bench reservation ends at four.

VERO: Our scene takes place at four.

MOSS: The park reservation ends at four. The visitor who uses this bench will be here then.

Bell stops the camera. Sana resets the scarf on the bench for the last take. Bell rolls again. A gust pulls it across the boards and into the pond. She retrieves it with a long branch. The duck paddles after it, catches a loose end, and drags the wet scarf beneath the bench.

VERO: Keep rolling. This is the first honest thing that has happened all day.

SANA: It is wet, and the duck has it.

VERO: A complex relationship.

MOSS: It is a scarf. I have a clean towel in the park shed.

SCENE 4. INT. EDITING ROOM - NIGHT

Vero reviews the footage. Vero asks Bell to build the film from three different takes: duck, squirrel, then scarf rescue. Bell lays the clips side by side. The shadows jump, the scarf changes from dry to wet, and the duck reverses direction between cuts.

BELL: This is not one walk. It is three attempts pretending to be one continuous shot.

VERO: The audience will feel the passage of time.

SANA: They will see the scarf dry itself between shots.

MOSS: The visitor came back at four. You asked her to wait while you tried to give the duck a note.

SCENE 4A. INT. EDITING ROOM - LATER

Vero watches only the last continuous take. Sana places the scarf on the bench; wind sends it into the pond; she retrieves it; the duck tugs it beneath the seat. Moss collects the scarf, dries it in the clean towel and exits the frame. The duck pecks at the empty bench. Bell stops the camera. Moss returned the scarf to its owner afterward.

VERO: The scarf is the character.

SANA: It is also the visitor's.

BELL: We have her in none of the footage.

MOSS: I asked if she wanted to wear it in another shot. She said she preferred to wear it home.

Vero cuts the stitched version. Instead, he keeps the single take. He tries to freeze the last frame before Moss enters, but the motionless scarf looks like a catalogue photograph. He restores Moss and removes a credit naming himself "Scarf Choreographer."

SCENE 5. EXT. PARK - DAY

At a small screening, the audience watches the one take: Sana retrieves the scarf, the duck drags it under the bench, and Moss dries it and carries it off. The audience laughs when Vero's off-camera voice begs the duck to look bereaved. Bell has kept the location sound.

VERO: I wanted a film about letting go.

SANA: You kept asking us to do it again.

VERO: That was my directing voice. It should have been removed.

MOSS: You wanted the single honest take. That's the sound of it.

Vero asks Bell to mute the last frame. Bell does. The silent duck pecks the bench. Vero nods, then holds up a card requesting a separate credit for "Duck Silence." Sana takes his pen before he can write it.

FINAL IMAGE: In the finished film, the duck pecks once at the bare bench, then turns toward the pond as the screen cuts to black.
