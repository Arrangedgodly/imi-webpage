# Room Tone for Three Hours

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: arthouse minimalist cinema, long takes, silence, and audience interpretation*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A minimalist filmmaker spends one afternoon filming a monkey waiting for a bus, but the subject's practical errands keep puncturing the crew's solemn theories about stillness.

Cast: Oona, a macaque waiting for the bus; Director Vale, an avant-garde peacock; Kit, a sound recordist; and a bus driver who never appears in focus.

SCENE 1. EXT. BUS STOP, AFTERNOON

A static shot holds on Oona seated beneath a timetable. The shot continues. A leaf crosses the frame. Oona checks the time. The leaf returns. Vale whispers to Kit that the leaf represents time.

Oona stands and throws the leaf into a nearby bin. She sits again.

SCENE 2. EXT. BUS STOP, CONTINUOUS

A long silence. Kit records the hum of traffic. Vale asks Oona to look toward the horizon. Oona looks at the road, checking for the bus. Vale calls this a meditation on distance.

Oona asks if the bus is late. Vale says he does not know. Kit checks the transit app. It says delayed.

SCENE 3. EXT. BUS STOP, LATER

The camera holds on Oona's shoes. One lace is untied. Vale asks her not to fix it, since the image suggests vulnerability. Oona ties the lace. Kit says the microphone caught a good knot sound.

Vale asks Oona what the waiting means. She says she has groceries at home. A bag is visible beside the bench. Vale says the bag changes the entire film.

SCENE 4. EXT. BUS STOP, SUNSET

The bus arrives, but it is full. Oona remains seated. The driver waves and drives on. Vale says the refusal to board may represent resistance. Oona says she needs the next bus because this one skips her stop.

The crew waits through another long shot. Kit's microphone battery fades. Vale asks if the silence can continue. Oona shares a snack with Kit. The wrapper crackles loudly.

SCENE 5. EXT. BUS STOP, NIGHT

The bus finally stops. Oona boards with her groceries. Vale asks her to leave the bag for the final shot. She takes it with her. The crew packs up. Vale says the film is about absence. Kit says it's about a late bus.

SCENE 5A. EXT. BUS STOP, CONTINUOUS

Vale asks the crew to remain after Oona leaves. Kit records room tone. A bus passes without stopping.

VALE: Hold the empty frame.

KIT: The bus was full.

VALE: Hold the absence.

KIT: It left exhaust.

VALE: That is also absence.

KIT: My battery is low.

VALE: Batteries have endings.

KIT: They have percentages.

VALE: Don't show the percentage.

KIT: The screen shows it.

VALE: Turn the screen away.

KIT: The microphone is picking you up.

VALE: Is that bad?

KIT: It's the loudest sound.

VALE: Can we call it silence?

KIT: The audience can hear.

VALE: They can interpret.

KIT: I need a charger.

VALE: Does the charger interrupt?

KIT: It charges.

VALE: Keep filming.

KIT: It's going dark.

VALE: The image or the day?

KIT: Both, soon.

VALE: Stay with the bench.

KIT: It's a public bench.

VALE: Exactly.

KIT: A dog is approaching.

VALE: Is it symbolic?

KIT: It needs water.

VALE: Do we have water?

KIT: Yes.

VALE: Give the dog water.

KIT: That changes the shot.

VALE: Then change the shot.

KIT: We have no time.

VALE: Time is the subject.

KIT: The dog is leaving.

VALE: Follow it.

KIT: You said bench.

VALE: I have evolved.

Kit pans away. The dog is gone. Vale asks if the empty frame can stand for the dog. Kit says no, and switches off the camera.

SCENE 5B. INT. EDITING ROOM, NEXT DAY

Vale and Kit review the footage. The screen shows a bench, a bus, a leaf, and a shoe.

VALE: The shoe suggests departure.

KIT: It was Oona's shoe.

VALE: Is there a difference?

KIT: She wore it.

VALE: The leaf returned.

KIT: It blew back.

VALE: Return is a theme.

KIT: It was wind.

VALE: Wind is a theme.

KIT: The bus was late.

VALE: Delay is a theme.

KIT: The app showed that.

VALE: We need a title.

KIT: "Waiting for the Bus."

VALE: Too literal.

KIT: "Bus Stop."

VALE: Too short.

KIT: "Room Tone."

VALE: Too broad.

KIT: "Three Hours."

VALE: Too accurate.

KIT: Accuracy costs nothing.

VALE: It costs mystery.

KIT: Mystery costs time.

VALE: Time is the subject.

KIT: That was already the subject.

VALE: Then it is consistent.

KIT: The runtime is nine minutes.

VALE: We filmed three hours.

KIT: The camera ran out.

VALE: Perhaps the battery is the protagonist.

KIT: The battery has left.

VALE: A strong ending.

KIT: It shut off.

VALE: Keep that.

KIT: I did.

They sit in silence. Vale watches the blank screen.

VALE: How long is this shot?

KIT: The projector is off.

VALE: Excellent.

FINAL IMAGE: The static camera records an empty bench. A new passenger sits down and asks the camera when the bus comes. The camera does not answer. The passenger checks their phone and leaves before the frame can explain them.
