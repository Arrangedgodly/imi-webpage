# The House with Two Reflections

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: psychological thrillers, unreliable reflections, ominous mirror shots, and a protagonist who believes every double is evidence*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
ESKA, a capuchin mirror cleaner
TOV, a macaque house guest
MIRA, a ring-tailed lemur photographer
THE CARETAKER, a gibbon

SCENE 1. INT. OLD GLASS HOUSE - NIGHT

Eska cleans a mirrored hallway in an old estate. Each polished panel reflects a different doorway. Tov enters, sees himself in the first mirror, and waves. The reflection waves back a second late.

TOV: This house is watching me.

ESKA: It is a mirror.

TOV: The mirror is watching me.

A lamp flickers. Mira, a photographer, takes a flash picture. The hallway fills with reflections of the flash, appearing as a row of silent visitors.

SCENE 2. INT. MIRRORED HALL - LATER

Tov maps the reflections on a paper plan. He marks every apparent visitor with an X. Eska points out that the Xs line up with the lamps. Tov insists one reflection is missing: a small figure in a blue cap.

MIRA: That is your cap.

TOV: My cap is on my head.

Mira takes it off. The small figure disappears. Tov writes "cap is a suspect" on the map.

SCENE 3. INT. CONSERVATORY ROOM - MIDNIGHT

The caretaker leads them into a room with a two-way mirror. The mirrored wall reflects the room but not the corridor outside. Tov sees a second caretaker standing behind him. He turns; no one is there.

CARETAKER: This glass was installed to watch the plants.

TOV: Why do the plants need watching?

CARETAKER: They lean toward the light.

A plant has grown in front of the mirror, blocking half the reflection. As Eska moves it, the second caretaker appears and vanishes. Mira spots a narrow seam behind the glass.

SCENE 4. INT. SERVICE PASSAGE - CONTINUOUS

The group opens the mirrored panel. Behind it is a narrow passage used by the old household staff. A second mirror at the far end creates the delayed wave Tov noticed. The "visitor" in the blue cap was a reflection from the coat stand.

TOV: The house has an entire corridor inside itself.

ESKA: The corridor is behind the wall.

TOV: That is what a house would say.

Mira photographs the passage. Her flash fires and makes the two mirrors reflect back and forth, creating an endless line of tiny caretakers.

SCENE 5. INT. MIRRORED HALL - DAWN

The caretaker opens the passage for inspection. Eska cleans the two-way glass. Tov stands in front of it, waiting for the delayed reflection. This time it moves at the same time. He waves; it waves. Mira turns off the lights, but a faint reflection remains from a window.

TOV: One last figure.

Eska closes the curtain. The figure disappears.

SCENE 6. INT. FRONT PARLOR - MORNING

The house's owner arrives to see the mirror passage. The caretaker explains that it was built for staff to move quietly between rooms. Tov asks to keep his map. Mira takes a photograph of the three of them, with the cap placed on the table.

SCENE 7. INT. FRONT PARLOR - AFTERNOON

The caretaker leads a small group of visitors through the old glass house. Tov offers to explain the mirrored hallway. He stands beside the coat rack and points out how a hat can appear to belong to a second visitor.

VISITOR: Is the house haunted?

TOV: It is reflective.

ESKA: It is also drafty.

A gust moves the curtain. The reflection shows a visitor walking backward. Tov turns, sees the real visitor still standing, and closes the curtain before the guests can invent a second explanation. Mira takes a photograph of the empty hall without using flash.

SCENE 8. INT. SERVICE PASSAGE - LATER

Eska oils the hinges and cleans the narrow mirrors. The caretaker opens the passage for staff again, placing a small lamp at each end. Tov maps the actual route on one side and the reflections on the other. The two drawings match except for his cap, which appears in four places. Eska adjusts one panel; the delayed wave disappears and the passage is visible from both ends.

TOV: The cap is still suspicious.

MIRA: It is only a cap.

TOV: That is exactly what it wants us to think.

The visitors laugh and take turns testing their reflections. The caretaker gives Tov the old tour map, now marked with the actual passage. He adds a small drawing of the cap in the corner, then labels it "personal property, not a suspect." Eska asks him to lead the next group; he agrees, provided the mirrors are kept in pairs.

FINAL IMAGE: The empty mirror shows only the cap. Tov reaches in, retrieves it, and checks whether the reflection waits before following.
