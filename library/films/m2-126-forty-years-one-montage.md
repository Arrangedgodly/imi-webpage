# Forty Years in One Montage

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: biopic montages that compress decades into a few symbolic props and claim every life change happened during one song*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A biopic director tries to portray a lifetime of bridge repairs with one wrench, one gray wig, and a swelling piano cue.

Cast: Lela, a macaque director; Oru, a gorilla bridge inspector; Fenn, a lemur actor; and Puck, a capuchin editor.

SCENE 1. INT. SOUNDSTAGE, MORNING

Fenn stands beside a model bridge holding a wrench. Lela tells him the scene covers forty years of Oru's work. Puck rolls a piano track from the speakers. Fenn tightens one bolt. A card appears: "TWENTY YEARS LATER." He has added a gray wig.

FENN: Do I change tools?

LELA: The wrench represents persistence.

ORU: I used several tools.

Lela says the biopic needs a single visual idea. Oru brings in a box of inspection logs. The logs show repairs, floods, painted railings, and one year when the bridge was closed for geese. Puck asks which pages should appear in the montage. Lela selects the oldest and newest, then places them on the wrong sides of the model.

SCENE 2. INT. SOUNDSTAGE, LATER

The montage begins. Fenn tightens the same bolt. The piano rises. Puck flashes date cards: 1982, 1994, 2006, 2022. The model bridge does not change. Fenn removes the wig, then puts it back on to show the next decade. The piano repeats the same four notes.

LELA: More life experience.

FENN: Should I look older?

ORU: You could read the inspection log.

Fenn opens the log. Puck projects the pages on the backdrop. The date cards move faster than the camera can focus. A page lists a repair to the east railing. The prop bridge's west railing is still loose.

ORU: That repair happened on the other side.

Fenn walks around the model and tightens the matching bolt. The music swells. Puck advances to the next card, but it reads "TWO WEEKS LATER." Lela asks him to make it thirty years. Puck changes the card to "THIRTY YEARS LATER." Fenn changes the wig and repeats the same bolt. Oru turns a page in the log.

ORU: In that year I rebuilt the north support after a flood.

LELA: Can the wrench imply a flood?

Oru lifts a small model boat from the prop box. It has a label from a different production. Lela asks Fenn to carry it across the model bridge. The boat is wider than the bridge and knocks off the rail. Puck pauses the piano.

FENN: Does the bridge survive?

ORU: Not in this version.

Lela calls for a reset. Oru asks to use the actual repair notes instead of props.

SCENE 3. EXT. RIVER BRIDGE, AFTERNOON

Oru takes the crew to the real bridge. Fenn watches him inspect the joints. Oru checks each connection and records what needs work. No music plays. Puck holds the camera steady while a bicycle crosses.

LELA: Could you pause at the center and look back at your past?

ORU: I am checking the railing.

The railing is loose. Oru marks it with chalk and calls the maintenance crew. Fenn asks if he should tighten it with the symbolic wrench. Oru hands him the correct wrench and shows him how to brace the bolt. Fenn leans too hard; the wrench slips, and he sits down in the grass.

ORU: Now you know why I record the work.

Puck films the crew placing a cone by the repair. Lela asks for a golden-hour version. Oru says the bridge must stay closed until the rail is secured. They wait for the maintenance crew. Oru shows Fenn how to read the chalk marks. Each mark records a location, date, and repair needed. Fenn asks which one represents the greatest turning point.

ORU: The day the inspector found the loose support.

FENN: Was there music?

ORU: There was a radio in the truck.

A worker arrives and tightens the joint. Oru checks it again, then signs the log. Lela asks if the signature can conclude the film. Oru says the next inspection is scheduled for spring. Puck records the date instead of cueing the piano.

SCENE 4. EXT. RIVER BRIDGE, SUNSET

The rail is fixed. Oru signs the inspection log. Fenn returns the wrench and removes his gray wig. Lela watches the crew cross the bridge one at a time. The sound is footsteps, not piano.

LELA: We have no montage.

ORU: You have the log.

Puck films the dates in order. The screen shows the real pages, including the year the bridge closed for geese. Lela leaves that page in the cut.

FINAL IMAGE: The model bridge remains on the soundstage with a loose rail. A note beside it reads "Repair before filming."
