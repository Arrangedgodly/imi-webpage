# The Curator in the Gorilla Suit

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: body-swap comedies, mistaken-identity montages, and characters who resolve every problem by switching back*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A museum curator and a gorilla-costumed tour guide swap jobs for a day after a prop mishap, discovering that each role has a very different kind of audience.

Cast: Mara, a lemur curator; Goss, a gorilla tour guide; Nix, a macaque technician; Pip, a capuchin visitor; and a museum mannequin dressed as an explorer.

SCENE 1. INT. MUSEUM GALLERY, MORNING

Mara prepares an exhibit label. Goss rehearses a lively tour in an explorer costume. A loose prop compass rolls across the floor. Goss steps on it; Mara catches the falling hat. They collide beside a display mirror. When they stand, each is wearing the other's badge and jacket.

MARA: Why is my voice booming?

GOSS: Why am I whispering about pottery?

Nix says the costume collars are tangled and hands them each a badge. The badges are also swapped. They decide to trade jobs until lunch so visitors are not confused.

SCENE 2. INT. EXHIBIT HALL, LATER

Mara leads the tour. She reads every label in full, including accession numbers. The group trails behind. Goss tries curating the pottery display but gives each bowl a dramatic backstory. He places a plain bowl on a pedestal and calls it the vessel of destiny.

PIP: Is it for sale?

GOSS: It carries the silence of a century.

MARA: It carries pencils.

She removes the pencils and checks the inventory. A bowl is missing. Goss, still in the curator role, starts a search. He asks every visitor to describe the last time they saw it. Pip says it was in his hands, then points to the gift shop where he returned it.

SCENE 3. INT. GIFT SHOP, AFTERNOON

Mara attempts a curatorial explanation for a display of souvenir magnets. She cites their manufacturing date and packaging code. Goss tells her to let visitors touch one. A magnet sticks to her badge. She asks if that is a conservation concern. Nix removes it with a plastic card. The tour group applauds the successful recovery.

Mara tries to lead the tour outside, but the group follows Goss, who is now explaining the gift-shop postcards as if they were rare artifacts. Nix finds the missing bowl behind the postcard rack. It was used to hold pens. Mara asks Goss to return it to the pottery case. He hesitates because the bowl's dramatic display is drawing a crowd.

GOSS: They finally see the vessel.

MARA: They see the pens.

He returns the bowl and writes a new label: PENCIL HOLDER, TEMPORARY. It is accurate and still gets a laugh.

SCENE 4. INT. STAFF ROOM, LATER

Goss asks Mara to teach him how to write a useful label. She gives him a card with three lines: object, use, source. He fills it out for a broom. The result says "broom, sweeps floors, janitor closet." He asks if it needs a legend. Mara says only if the broom has one. He adds a note that it was used in the morning cleanup and returns it to its hook.

The costume collar twists again while they remove the jackets. Nix checks the fabric tag and finds the badges were attached to the wrong costumes. Mara and Goss return to their regular clothes. They compare notes: Mara learned to pause during tours; Goss learned to check the inventory before inventing a legend.

A visitor asks if the mannequin was based on a real explorer. Mara looks up the catalog number. Goss starts to answer with a grand tale, then stops and asks Nix to verify the label first. It is a mannequin from a department-store window.

SCENE 5. INT. MUSEUM GALLERY, EVENING

The director approves the new labels and asks Goss to check one last exhibit. He starts to describe the mannequin as a famous navigator, then reads its department-store history card before speaking.

The exhibit closes. Mara updates the labels. Goss returns to guide practice and keeps the stories brief. They agree to add one scheduled storytelling tour and one quiet tour, letting visitors choose. Pip returns the postcard and asks if the museum can keep his pencil drawing of the bowl. Mara tags it as a visitor sketch, not an artifact.

Mara takes off the tour jacket and hangs it on the proper hook.

The visitor sketch is placed in a portfolio with its date and the artist name, as requested.

The tour closes with the facts intact.

FINAL IMAGE: Goss leads the last tour past the pencil-holder bowl. A visitor asks if it is the vessel of destiny. He says, "Only if you need a pencil," and hands one over.
