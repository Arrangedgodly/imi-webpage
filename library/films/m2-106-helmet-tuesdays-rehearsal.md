# The Helmet from Tuesday's Rehearsal

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: historical epics, fake artifacts treated as sacred, and authenticity debates settled by louder speeches*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A film crew preparing a medieval market discovers its most revered helmet is a modern rehearsal prop, and the set's historian wants to keep it in the battle anyway.

Cast: Sena, a lemur prop master; Odo, a gorilla actor; Marra, a macaque historian; and Puck, a capuchin assistant director.

SCENE 1. INT. PROP TENT, MORNING

Odo lifts a bronze-colored helmet from a velvet cloth. Puck asks for a solemn close-up. Sena checks the inventory tag.

SENA: This is rubber.

ODO: It has the weight of a dynasty.

MARRA: It weighs two hundred grams.

The helmet was made Tuesday for rehearsal. Odo says he already filmed a speech about it being passed through seven generations. Marra asks which generation used the velcro chin strap. Puck calls for a dramatic silence. Sena peels off the rehearsal label, and it sticks to Odo's glove.

SCENE 2. EXT. MEDIEVAL MARKET SET, LATER

Marra checks the prop ledger. The real helmet is in a locked exhibit case across the museum. It cannot be removed for filming.

ODO: Can we film the case?

MARRA: Yes, behind the barrier.

ODO: Can I touch it?

MARRA: No.

Puck asks the extras to kneel before the case. A school group arrives and asks where the restroom is. Sena guides them to the corridor. Odo asks if they can say the helmet was hidden during a siege. Marra opens the catalog entry and reads its actual use: a ceremonial display, not battle gear. Odo asks if ceremony can involve a battle. Marra closes the book. Odo says the story requires a relic. Sena offers a replica from the approved case. It has a visible seam and weighs less than the rubber one.

ODO: The audience will notice the seam.

SENA: The audience will notice if the actor drops a metal helmet.

Puck asks the extras to form a circle around Odo. They do, but block the food stall and fire lane. Marra moves them to the marked set line and shows Odo the exhibit label. The real helmet dates to a different century than the film. Odo says that makes the story more mysterious.

SCENE 3. EXT. MARKET SET, AFTERNOON

The camera rolls. Odo raises the replica and delivers his speech about an ancient dynasty. The helmet slips over his eyes. He turns the wrong way and walks into a cloth market stall. The extras hold their positions as instructed, creating a perfect circle around him and the collapsed stall.

Sena checks the helmet's fit. The chin strap is too loose. Marra points to the inventory note requiring a padded liner. Puck wants to keep the take because the fall is funny. Odo says the scene is supposed to be solemn. Marra asks whether a king would wear a helmet that obscures his vision. Odo takes it off.

ODO: The king does not fall.

SENA: The actor did.

MARRA: And the helmet covered his eyes.

Sena checks the chin strap. The buckle has slipped under Odo's collar. She adjusts the liner and asks him to turn left, then right. The helmet stays in place. Puck tests the angle without wind or smoke. Odo asks if the scene still looks important. Marra says it looks like a person wearing a hat.

With the liner secured, Odo tries the speech again. This time the helmet stays in place. Puck asks for another take with a wind machine. Sena says the market cloth is already unstable. Puck directs the extras to hold the awnings while Odo gives the line. One awning still flaps, revealing a modern fire extinguisher. Marra moves it just outside frame but keeps the safety lane clear.

SCENE 4. INT. PROP TENT, EVENING

Odo watches the footage. He prefers the take where he walks into the stall. Marra says the audience may think the helmet made him dizzy. Odo says it did. Sena puts the rubber rehearsal helmet in the returned-props bin and the replica in its labeled box.

Puck asks if the film can credit the real museum artifact. Marra agrees, provided the caption says it was not used on set. Odo gives his speech again without the helmet. He says the object matters because people preserved it. Marra says that line can stay. Puck asks to film the real case from the public side of the barrier. Marra approves the shot. The caption names the museum and notes the replica was used. Odo asks whether the artifact will be credited before him. Sena says it has been here longer than the cast. Odo signs the prop return form.

FINAL IMAGE: The replica rests on a labeled foam stand. Odo returns the rubber helmet to Tuesday's rehearsal box, where the velcro strap clings to the inventory tag.
