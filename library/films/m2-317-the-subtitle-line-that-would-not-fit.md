# The Subtitle Line That Would Not Fit

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: subtitle localization that compresses nuance until every character seems to be arguing about punctuation*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-011*

CAST
LOCALIZATION DIRECTOR NIMA, a lemur
SUBTITLE EDITOR ODO, a baboon
ACTOR PALLA, a gibbon
ACTOR LUMA, a capuchin
DISTRIBUTOR VEX, a macaque

SCENE 1. INT. POSTPRODUCTION SUITE - MORNING

A monitor shows a moonlit dock. Palla whispers a long ceremonial farewell in the film's invented island dialect. Subtitle editor Odo types: "Goodbye." Localization director Nima freezes the frame.

NIMA: He just promised to return the borrowed lighthouse.

ODO: There is room for one line.

VEX: Can the lighthouse be shorter?

LUMA: It is the same size in every language.

Vex points to the subtitle safe area. Palla's line lasts nine seconds. Odo says the translation has forty-two words. The screen can fit two lines, each short enough to read before the boat leaves.

ODO: I can make it "I owe you a light."

NIMA: That sounds like he borrowed a lantern.

VEX: A lantern is cheaper to subtitle.

Palla, still in costume, repeats the speech slowly. The sentence grows longer as everyone listens.

SCENE 2. INT. RECORDING BOOTH - LATER

The actors record an alternate shorter line. Luma says, "I'll bring your lighthouse home." The line fits, but now the boatman thinks she owns the building. Odo changes it to "I'll return your lighthouse." The line fits and sounds like the lighthouse is in her handbag.

LUMA: It is bolted to the cliff.

VEX: Then we should show the bolt.

Nima requests a cut to the cliff. The existing shot is a close-up of Palla's emotional face. Vex asks whether a subtitle can make the face look more practical. Odo tests: "I have a receipt." The words fit neatly under Palla's tears.

PALLA: I did not mention a receipt.

ODO: It is a culturally portable word.

Vex enjoys the phrase and asks for it in the trailer. Nima explains that the character's promise is a traditional vow, not a purchase. Vex asks whether the vow can be invoiced. Odo types, "By moonrise, lighthouse returned, no substitutions." The line fills both rows and covers Palla's mouth.

SCENE 3. INT. SCREENING ROOM - AFTERNOON

They preview the timed subtitles. The first line appears late, after Palla has finished speaking. The second blocks the boatman's hands, where he ties a red ribbon to the mooring post to mark the promise. Nima asks Odo to move the subtitle up. It covers the moon. Vex says the moon is not speaking.

NIMA: The moon tells us it is night.

VEX: The subtitle tells us there are words.

Odo shortens the vow again. "I will bring it back." The sentence fits, but the antecedent is unclear. The boatman looks at the ribbon and then at the lighthouse. Luma watches the preview.

LUMA: What is "it"?

ODO: The lighthouse.

PALLA: The ribbon is closer to the subtitle.

Vex asks for a title card reading LIGHTHOUSE. It appears over the next shot, where the boatman lights a lantern. Everyone assumes the lantern is the promised object. The international sales representative sends a message asking if the movie concerns a stolen lamp.

SCENE 4. INT. POSTPRODUCTION SUITE - EVENING

Nima plays the sequence with no subtitle. Palla's voice, the ribbon, and the boatman's look carry the promise. She suggests one concise line: "At dawn, I bring your lighthouse back." Odo lays it over the scene. It fits in two rows and clears the hands and moon.

VEX: But it says dawn. The vow said moonrise.

NIMA: Dawn is when the boat returns.

VEX: Could the moonrise be more convenient?

Odo shifts the line earlier, but Palla is still speaking. The sentence appears before the vow. Nima gives Odo the shot list; he matches the line to the moment when Palla ties the ribbon. Now the words land as the boatman sees the knot. Vex asks to preserve the receipt subtitle as a bonus track. No one answers.

They print the final version. Odo checks the reading speed. The line is brief enough to read, and the actor's mouth remains visible. Vex asks if a subtitle could identify the ribbon as a symbol. Nima points at the red ribbon in the picture. Vex says the audience might miss it, then notices the preview screen has a red ribbon tied around its power cord.

SCENE 5. INT. SALES SCREENING ROOM - NIGHT

A distributor watches the scene with the final subtitle. Palla makes the vow; the line appears at the right moment; the ribbon signals the promise. The distributor nods and writes LIGHTHOUSE ROMANCE on a notepad. Vex smiles at the successful clarity, then sees the subtitle file's character count.

VEX: There is room for more.

NIMA: There is no more line.

VEX: Could it say, "A promise, under moonlight"?

ODO: Then it would cover the ribbon.

The distributor asks for a copy of the clean version. Vex reaches for the keyboard and opens the old file. Nima taps the red ribbon around the power cord. He withdraws his hand. They export the film. The progress bar reaches one hundred percent without the moon being renamed or the lighthouse folded into a suitcase.

FINAL IMAGE: On the monitor, the short vow sits below the moon; Vex's abandoned receipt subtitle remains in a folder labeled LANTERN PROJECT.
