# The Lighthouse Keeper's Borrowed Crown

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: royal succession dramas, ceremonial crowns, mistaken heirs, and proclamations delivered before the paperwork is checked*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
KEEPER LUMA, a lemur
PRINCE VEX, a macaque
ARCHIVIST NARI, an orangutan
MESSENGER PIP, a squirrel monkey
CAPTAIN ODO, a capuchin ferry pilot

SCENE 1. EXT. LIGHTHOUSE - DAWN

A courier arrives at the lighthouse carrying a velvet box. Keeper Luma opens it and finds a gold crown. A note says: "For the rightful head of the island." The island's lighthouse has been maintained by Luma's family for generations. She looks at the crown, then at the foghorn.

LUMA: It does not fit the foghorn.

Pip says Prince Vex is coming from the capital to claim the crown. Luma locks it in the supply cupboard and returns to cleaning the lens. She tests the lighthouse lamp, which has a dusty edge that makes its beam dim. Pip asks whether the crown might be used to reflect the light. Luma says it is a costume prop, not a tool. She sends him for the proper cloth instead.

SCENE 2. INT. LIGHTHOUSE LANTERN ROOM - MORNING

Vex arrives with a small court and a portrait of his ancestor. He says the crown proves his family once ruled the island. Archivist Nari examines the note and finds no seal, date, or official mark. Vex says the crown was hidden for generations. He asks his court to kneel. The court looks to Luma, who is holding a mop and has no idea why they should kneel. Vex steps onto a storage crate to make a proclamation. The crate gives way, and he lands safely in a pile of folded sails.

LUMA: The lighthouse floor is not a throne.

VEX: It could be, with a proper platform.

Nari checks the box while Vex retrieves his sash. The velvet lining has a museum inventory sticker tucked under the hinge. It reads: "Pageant Crown, Shelf B, Theater Wing." Nari photographs it and adds the note to her catalog. Vex says a royal heirloom should have a better label.

Vex says the crown was hidden for generations. Luma points out that the box was delivered this morning.

VEX: The timing is historic.

NARI: The receipt says it was shipped yesterday.

Captain Odo arrives with the ferry manifest. The box was delivered to the lighthouse because the address was smudged; it was intended for the island museum. Vex says the museum is part of his family estate. Nari asks him to prove it.

SCENE 3. INT. LIGHTHOUSE STORAGE ROOM - AFTERNOON

They search the lighthouse logbooks for a record of the crown. Luma finds a sketch of a similar crown on a shipping label used to mark costume rentals. The crown is a prop from the island's annual pageant. Nari reads the catalog history aloud: the theater ordered it as a costume, used it in three pageants, then sent it for repair. The repair shop returned it in an unmarked box. The courier wrote "island light house" on the label because the museum theater sits beside the lighthouse road. The box traveled to the right island and the wrong door. Pip remembers seeing it in the museum theater last summer, worn by the actor playing the first governor.

Vex insists that the actor's role may reflect a real lineage. Nari compares the crown's maker's mark with the museum catalog. It belongs to the theater department. The crown was borrowed for the pageant and never returned because the costume box was sent to the wrong building.

SCENE 4. EXT. MUSEUM COURTYARD - SUNSET

The group carries the crown to the museum. The director recognizes it and signs a return receipt. Vex is embarrassed but says he can still make a royal appearance at the festival. Luma agrees if he helps clean the lighthouse lens before sunset. He removes his ceremonial sash, rolls up his sleeves, and climbs the ladder with a cloth.

The lens turns smoothly. The light sweeps across the harbor. The ferry pilot steers by it and signals that the route is clear. Nari files the crown's return in the catalog. Pip asks whether the crown could be worn for the pageant. The museum director says only with a signed costume checkout.

SCENE 5. INT. MUSEUM THEATER - NIGHT

The pageant begins. Vex plays the lighthouse keeper, not the king. He enters carrying a brass lamp. Luma sits in the audience with the real keeper's logbook. The crown stays in the costume case, properly labeled. Vex delivers his lines without claiming the throne.

After the play, he asks Luma if she will teach him to polish the lens. She says yes, as long as he follows the maintenance list. He bows. The crown tilts in its case but remains behind glass.

FINAL IMAGE: The lighthouse beam passes over the museum window, briefly lighting the crown before turning out toward the sea.
