# The Candlewick Tournament

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: martial-arts tournament films, rival schools, disciplined training montages, and a championship decided by a dropped candle*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
SEN, a macaque staff fighter
MIRI, a capuchin rival
MASTER ODO, a gibbon instructor
JUN, a ring-tailed lemur referee

SCENE 1. INT. CANDLEWICK DOJO - MORNING

Master Odo lights a single candle in the center of the training floor. Sen, a macaque fighter, practices slow staff movements. Miri, a capuchin from a rival school, enters carrying a paper fan.

ODO: A true fighter sees the candle without moving it.

SEN: The door moved it when it opened.

MIRI: Then the door has mastered the first lesson.

Jun, the referee, sets a timer. It is a kitchen timer shaped like a carrot. The carrot rings whenever anyone strikes the floor too hard.

SCENE 2. EXT. DOJO COURTYARD - AFTERNOON

The fighters train through a montage: balance on one foot, sweep leaves, catch falling seeds. Sen's tail knocks over a stack of practice bowls. Miri catches them one by one with her fan. Odo says this demonstrates restraint.

SEN: I was trying not to drop the staff.

MIRI: You dropped six bowls instead.

The rival schools arrive for the tournament. Each wears a different colored sash. Jun asks them to remove loose fabric from the ring. The competitors tie their sashes around their waists so tightly they cannot bow.

SCENE 3. INT. TOURNAMENT HALL - EVENING

The hall is full. A long ceremonial introduction lists every opponent's title and training lineage. Sen's introduction lasts four minutes. Miri's lasts five, mostly because Jun misreads the scroll.

MIRI: I have never trained in a mountain monastery.

JUN: The scroll says "mountain of laundry."

MIRI: That is where our dojo stores the uniforms.

The crowd cheers. The matches begin. Sen wins the first bout by stepping aside from a spinning kick that continues until the opponent leaves the ring.

SCENE 4. INT. TOURNAMENT RING - LATER

Sen and Miri reach the final. They take their positions. The candle flickers in the draft. Odo warns them not to use the dramatic spinning move. Both nod. The gong sounds.

Miri advances with her fan. Sen blocks with the staff. Their movements are quick but controlled. The audience gasps at every near touch. A stagehand crosses the back of the hall and opens a door; the candle blows out.

JUN: The match is paused.

SEN: The flame is out.

MIRI: The door has made its second move.

SCENE 5. INT. TOURNAMENT HALL - CONTINUOUS

The judges bring a new candle. Odo asks them to finish the match without restarting. Sen proposes that both fighters hold their positions while Jun lights the wick. The crowd watches in silence. Jun fumbles with a match; it sticks to his sleeve.

Miri's fan shields the flame. Sen holds the candle steady with the end of his staff. The wick catches. The judges declare that both contestants showed enough discipline to share the championship.

SCENE 6. EXT. DOJO COURTYARD - NIGHT

The fighters return the ceremonial sashes. Sen and Miri bow successfully now that they have loosened the knots. Odo gives them a broom and asks them to clear the leaves from the courtyard. The carrot timer rings as Jun sweeps too vigorously.

SCENE 7. INT. DOJO - NEXT MORNING

Jun reviews the tournament recording. The final match shows Miri shielding the flame and Sen balancing the candle. The judges have written "shared discipline" on the certificate, but the ink has smudged into "shared distance."

JUN: Does distance count?

ODO: It counts if you can explain it.

Sen and Miri practice a bow in front of the camera. The sash knot holds, but their elbows collide. They bow again more slowly, each holding the candle between them. It stays lit until Jun's carrot timer rings and blows it out.

MIRI: The timer is a rival school.

SEN: It has been training all morning.

SCENE 8. EXT. DOJO COURTYARD - AFTERNOON

Sen and Miri meet the other competitors to practice outside the ring. They trade techniques and repair the practice bowls. The rival schools compare their sashes and find several tied in the same impossible knot.

ODO: The tournament taught everyone something.

JUN: It taught the timer to ring.

Miri places the candle in a glass lantern. Sen carries it across the courtyard while the group opens doors ahead of them. The flame stays lit. When they reach the dojo, the carrot timer rings again, and the entire group bows to it. The timer does not bow back; it stays smug.

FINAL IMAGE: The candle burns steadily inside the dojo while the door is held open by a stack of practice bowls.
