# The Jury Saw the Wrong Screen

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom thrillers that save crucial evidence for a theatrical reveal despite obvious presentation equipment mistakes*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-016*

Logline: A jury deliberates over a stolen sculpture case, but the courtroom projector keeps showing the lunch menu instead of the evidence.

Cast: Judge Mela, a macaque; attorney Varo, a gibbon; attorney Sumi, a capuchin; clerk Tavi, a lemur; juror Oru, an orangutan; defendant Niko, a monkey.

SCENE 1 - INT. COURTROOM - MORNING

The clerk tests the projector. A lunch menu appears on the screen. The court is about to hear a case about a missing sculpture from the town gallery.

JUDGE MELA: Clerk, please display the photograph of the sculpture.

TAVI: The remote has two buttons. I pressed the one labeled SCREEN.

VARO: The evidence is ready. The jury must see it before they decide.

SUMI: It is in the file named EXHIBIT B.

Tavi clicks. The menu changes from soup to sandwiches.

JUDGE MELA: That is not Exhibit B.

SCENE 1A - INT. COURTROOM - CONTINUOUS

Varo prepares his opening statement. The projector menu advances to the day's special: banana bread. He points to the screen as though it were a visual aid.

VARO: The item in question was removed from the gallery shelf.

JUDGE MELA: The item on the screen is bread.

VARO: I am establishing the lunch context.

SUMI: The case concerns a sculpture. Please stop using the menu as evidence.

Varo draws a line on the board from the empty shelf to Niko's name. Tavi notices the image is mirrored. The arrow points from Niko's name to the empty shelf, not from the shelf to the defendant.

TAVI: The board is reversed.

VARO: It is a metaphor for uncertainty.

JUDGE MELA: It is a board facing the wrong direction. Turn it around.

Varo rotates the board. The line now points toward the repair room, which Sumi had marked in her notes.

SUMI: The evidence file should show that room.

VARO: I will reserve the reveal until the jury sees it.

SCENE 2 - INT. COURTROOM - LATER

The clerk disconnects and reconnects the cable. The screen goes black. Varo lifts a dramatic finger.

VARO: The gallery photograph will reveal the defendant's movements.

The screen displays a large image of a banana sandwich. Jurors look at Niko. Niko looks at his lunch bag.

NIKO: That is not my sandwich.

SUMI: It is the cafeteria screen. The courtroom and lunchroom are on the same channel.

JUDGE MELA: The jury will ignore the menu.

Oru raises a hand.

ORU: I have seen the sculpture. It is a blue clay heron, not a sandwich.

SCENE 3 - INT. CLERK'S DESK - MOMENTS LATER

Tavi examines the file folder and finds two memory sticks. One is labeled COURT EVIDENCE. The other reads CAFETERIA MENU. They are the same color and have identical caps.

TAVI: I loaded the wrong file.

VARO: We have had the wrong screen for twenty minutes.

SUMI: We should pause until the evidence is ready.

Judge Mela pauses the hearing. The jury leaves the room. Tavi loads the correct file. The first image shows the gallery shelf, empty except for a square dust mark.

SCENE 4 - INT. COURTROOM - AFTERNOON

The jury returns. Varo presents the photograph. Sumi shows a second image of a storage shelf. The sculpture appears there, wrapped in paper. Niko had moved it to the gallery's repair room after a cracked base was found.

NIKO: I moved it for safekeeping. The repair ticket is in the folder.

VARO: The ticket was not included in the first file.

TAVI: It was under the cafeteria menu printout.

JUDGE MELA: The court will admit the repair ticket and the photographs together.

Oru compares the shelf mark and the wrapped sculpture. The jury asks to see the date stamp. Tavi enlarges it without changing the channel. The date shows the move occurred before the sculpture was reported missing.

SCENE 5 - INT. COURTROOM - LATE AFTERNOON

The jury returns a finding: the sculpture was moved for repair, not stolen. The judge closes the case. Niko gives the gallery a copy of the repair ticket. Varo takes down his dramatic presentation board, which has a large arrow pointing at the lunch menu.

VARO: The evidence was clear once the correct screen was on.

SUMI: And once the repair note was included.

TAVI: I will label the memory sticks by content and color.

JUDGE MELA: Label by content. Color is not reliable evidence.

Oru collects his lunch bag. The projector remains on the empty screen, then powers down. The cafeteria menu is moved to a separate display.

FINAL IMAGE: The blue clay heron rests in its repair case beside the court's labeled evidence folder, while the projector remote sits in a drawer marked COURTROOM ONLY.
