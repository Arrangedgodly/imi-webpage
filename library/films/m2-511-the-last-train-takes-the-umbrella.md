# The Last Train Takes the Umbrella

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: ensemble station capers with ticking clocks, crossed luggage, and a final platform reveal*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: NELL, a macaque station porter; BIX, a baboon conductor; ARLO, an orangutan violinist; SERA, a gibbon courier; MOTT, a capuchin clockmaker; INSPECTOR VELL, a mandrill; TRAVELER, a lemur passenger.

INT. CENTRAL STATION - NIGHT

Rain rattles the high windows. The station clock reads 11:40. A brass umbrella stands alone beneath the lost-property sign. Nell watches the crowd stream around it.

NELL: Nobody claims the brass umbrella.

BIX: The last train leaves at midnight. If nobody claims it, it rides with me.

NELL: You say that about every lost thing.

BIX: I have a very full cab.

A violin case rolls beneath the bench. Arlo retrieves it, checks the latch, and notices a blue luggage tag tied to the handle. Nell points out a second, scuffed case under the far end of the same bench, the one Arlo carried in.

ARLO: This is not my case.

NELL: It has your name on it.

ARLO: That is what makes it suspicious. I donated my old case to the station archive last winter. I left the blue tag on it because the archive asked for a name.

Sera hurries in with a sealed envelope. A wet pawprint smears the wax.

SERA: I need the 11:55 platform. A clockmaker is waiting for this.

MOTT enters carrying a small gear assembly wrapped in a blanket.

MOTT: I am the clockmaker. The spring belongs in the tower drive. This gear assembly couples it to the station face through a shaft; it is not a spare clock.

INSPECTOR VELL: Nobody leaves until we know why the station clock has stopped.

The clock clicks. It advances from 11:40 to 11:41.

INSPECTOR VELL: It has begun again.

NELL: Was it stopped?

VELL: It was late.

The inspector has Nell read the rule: lost property travels only after its owner is announced. Bix asks passengers; each denies the umbrella but offers to hold it. Nell refuses.

NELL: The umbrella is not a communal announcement.

BIX: It is an umbrella. It has been around the whole station.

ACT ONE

INT. LOST-PROPERTY ROOM - NIGHT

Nell leads them into a room packed with umbrellas, bags, hats, and one unclaimed boot. Mott opens the violin case. Inside lies a brass clock spring and a note: "Platform Nine. Bring the umbrella."

ARLO: I am a musician. I do not carry clock parts.

MOTT: That spring belongs to my tower clock. Without it, the midnight bell will not ring.

SERA: The envelope says to deliver the spring to the station clockmaker.

NELL: Then why is it inside a violin case?

BIX: Because someone wanted us to ask.

Inspector Vell finds another tag in the case. It reads "Umbrella, platform nine."

VELL: The umbrella is the key.

NELL: An umbrella does not open a clock.

MOTT: Mine does. The handle contains a winding crank.

The brass umbrella is gone.

INT. PLATFORM NINE - NIGHT

They rush through the station. Bix checks the board: the train is delayed two minutes. Sera scans the passengers as a porter wheels a crate past.

SERA: The umbrella was here.

NELL: It walked?

ARLO: Someone took it.

A train arrives. A traveler steps off with the umbrella. Inspector Vell blocks the exit.

VELL: That belongs to the station.

TRAVELER: I found it beside a bench.

NELL: That is exactly where lost things go.

The traveler hands it over. Mott twists the handle; its crank slides out. The spring fails to catch.

MOTT: The spring is reversed.

ARLO: It was packed in a violin case. Everything about tonight is reversed.

SERA: Turn the spring, not the whole clock.

Mott reverses the spring in the exposed gear assembly and seats it. Through the open tower hatch, the linked shaft begins to turn; Nell watches the actual station hands move as the drive catches. The face advances steadily to 11:58, not in a jump.

BIX: The train leaves in two minutes.

ACT TWO

INT. CENTRAL STATION - CONTINUOUS

Inspector Vell studies the envelope and finds a second message beneath the wax: "Ring once when the train is ready." Mott looks toward the platform signal. The train remains at the stop.

MOTT: The clock needs a bell pull.

NELL: The bell rope is in the tower.

BIX: The tower stair is closed for cleaning.

Nell grabs a long umbrella. At the stairwell, a wet-floor sign blocks the door. Bix lifts it; Nell reaches the rope from the lower landing with the umbrella crook.

The bell rings once. Passengers board. Sera gives her sealed envelope to Mott, then climbs aboard the train with her courier satchel. Bix checks the platform and boards last, taking the conductor's cab. He plants one boot on the step and the other remains beside the lost-property bench.

SERA: The courier is aboard.

MOTT: The clock is working.

ARLO: And my violin case?

Nell checks the blue tag again. This is Arlo's donated archive case, as he said; his actual violin case is the scuffed one he retrieved from beneath the bench, with his current luggage label intact.

NELL: We have returned every object to its owner.

BIX: Except the boot.

NELL: The boot belongs to nobody.

BIX: It can ride with me.

The train pulls out with Bix aboard. His spare boot stays by the bench; he had taken it off to dry and deliberately left it for the next lost-property audit. Inspector Vell closes the ledger, then sees the brass umbrella open beside the tower hatch. Its crank is still linked to the clock drive, but the umbrella itself rests safely in a wall bracket.

VELL: Who owns the umbrella?

MOTT: The station. It keeps time and catches drips.

NELL: It has more duties than the conductor.

FINAL IMAGE: The clock strikes midnight. On the platform, Bix's single dry boot waits beside the ledger, tagged: "Conductor's spare. Do not reunite."
