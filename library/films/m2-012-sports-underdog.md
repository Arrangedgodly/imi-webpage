# The League of Almosts

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: sports underdog movies, training montages, and last-minute championship pressure*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A team of slow, short-legged lemurs enters a city relay race after their community park is threatened with closure, but the coach's movie-style training plan keeps getting interrupted by reasonable rest breaks.

Cast: Coach Brindle, a stern but kind tortoise; Tilly, a cautious lemur runner; Zip, a young monkey with great starts and poor stops; Rook, a raven scorekeeper; and the fast-talking Cheetah Club.

SCENE 1. EXT. COMMUNITY PARK, MORNING

The team jogs around a small track. A notice says the park may close unless it hosts a successful fundraiser. Coach Brindle watches them finish one lap.

COACH: We need heart.

TILLY: We also need water.

Zip sprints ahead, misses the turn, and runs into the snack table. Rook records the time. The Cheetah Club runs by in matching uniforms, barely breathing.

SCENE 2. EXT. TRACK, MONTAGE

The coach starts a training montage. Tilly runs stairs, but the stairs lead to a slide, and she reaches the bottom faster than planned. Zip practices baton handoffs with a broom handle. He passes it to a pigeon by mistake. The pigeon flies off with it.

The team trains through rain, then sun, then a brief sprinkler malfunction. Coach Brindle times every lap with an hourglass. The sand jams. He taps it. It empties all at once.

TILLY: Was that my personal best?

COACH: It was your best sand.

SCENE 2A. INT. PARK GYM, EVENING

Coach Brindle gathers the team for a strategy session. He draws a diagram of the relay on a chalkboard. The route has four turns and an exchange zone. Zip studies the diagram and asks whether the race goes clockwise or counterclockwise. The coach flips the board. The chalk lines fall onto the floor.

Tilly suggests they walk the course before running it. The coach agrees. They map each turn, identify the uneven patch, and place cones around a puddle. The Cheetah Club runs past and asks why they are walking. Tilly says they are learning where not to fall.

SCENE 2B. EXT. PARK, NIGHT

The team hangs posters for the fundraiser. A gust blows them down. Zip chases one into a tree. He climbs up, retrieves it, then realizes the poster is stuck to his back. He carries it through town. Neighbors read the message and follow him back to the park.

Aunties bring folding chairs. Children bring hand-painted signs. Rook takes photographs, but every shot catches his own wing. He sets the camera on a bench and gets a clear picture of the whole group.

SCENE 3. INT. PARK OFFICE, DAY

The city asks the team to prove the park is used. They gather photos of children playing, elders walking, and neighbors tending the flower beds. Rook brings the wrong binder, which contains only pictures of his own wings. The team sorts the pages and finds one photo of the park's old relay race.

Coach Brindle says the race is their chance. Tilly points out that the relay is a fundraiser, not an Olympic trial. He agrees, then asks them to practice baton passing anyway.

SCENE 4. EXT. CITY TRACK, RACE DAY

The Cheetah Club is favored. Zip starts too fast and drops the baton. Tilly picks it up and runs steadily. She reaches the exchange zone early and waits for the next runner. The crowd groans. Coach Brindle tells her to trust her pace.

The third runner slips on a ribbon from the Cheetah Club's banner. Rook pauses the race to clear the track. The rival team objects. The official says safety rules apply even in the final lap.

Tilly takes the baton for the last leg. A cheer rises from the park residents. She doesn't sprint. She keeps her breathing even. The Cheetah Club finishes first, but the lemurs finish without dropping the baton again.

SCENE 4A. EXT. FINISH LINE, CONTINUOUS

The Cheetah Club wins the race and expects a trophy. The official instead gives them a volunteer clipboard because they have the most experience managing crowds. They are confused, then begin directing spectators away from the wet track.

Tilly crosses the finish line last. Her team cheers. She raises the baton like a torch. The crowd chants her name, then chants for the Cheetahs who are stacking chairs. The announcer tries to call this an upset. Rook corrects him: it is a successful fundraiser.

SCENE 5. EXT. COMMUNITY PARK, EVENING

The race raises enough money to keep the park open for another year. The city announces repairs to the track. The Cheetah Club returns to help paint the lane lines. Zip asks if they can race again. The coach says after stretching.

SCENE 6. EXT. PARK TRACK, AFTERNOON

TILLY: Is the park saved?

ROOK: For one year.

ZIP: Is that a win?

COACH: It is a season.

TILLY: We finished the race.

ZIP: I found the baton.

ROOK: After dropping it.

ZIP: It was still found.

COACH: We can practice.

TILLY: After water.

ZIP: And stretching.

ROOK: And checking cones.

COACH: You sound like a coach.

ZIP: I can shout.

TILLY: Try a calm voice.

ZIP: Run gently.

ROOK: Too gentle.

ZIP: Jog with purpose.

COACH: Better.

TILLY: What about the Cheetahs?

ROOK: Painting lanes.

ZIP: They run fast.

TILLY: They paint straight.

COACH: Every team has strengths.

ZIP: Can I be fast?

COACH: You can be ready.

ZIP: That's less exciting.

TILLY: But useful.

ROOK: The hourglass broke.

COACH: Again?

ZIP: My lap?

ROOK: The sand's stuck.

TILLY: That's your best time.

ZIP: I did not run.

COACH: The clock is improving.

They laugh and start walking the course together, stopping to fix one cone that leans toward the puddle.

FINAL IMAGE: Coach Brindle sets the hourglass upright. The team jogs one slow lap as the sun sets. Rook records the time, then adds a note: "Everyone finished. No pigeons recruited."
