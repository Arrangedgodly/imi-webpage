# The Crown of Paper Stars

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: royal court dramas, ceremonial succession, secret heirs, and a crown that crinkles whenever anyone tries to look majestic*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
PRINCE PELL, a macaque
AUNT VERA, a gibbon royal archivist
SURI, a capuchin paper artist
CAPTAIN MOSS, a ring-tailed lemur guard

SCENE 1. INT. PALACE HALL - MORNING

A throne room stands ready for a succession ceremony. The royal crown is missing. Captain Moss blocks the doors. Aunt Vera opens the family archive and finds a portrait of a young prince wearing a crown made of paper stars.

MOSS: The crown is gold.

VERA: This is the oldest portrait.

PELL: That looks like me.

SURI: The portrait is three hundred years old.

PELL: I have had a complicated week.

The crown appears behind the portrait, folded from gold paper. When Pell lifts it, it makes a loud crinkle. The court gasps.

SCENE 2. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM - LATER

Vera searches the records for a will. She finds a note: "The crown belongs to the one who can wear it without making a sound." Pell tries to walk majestically. Every step crinkles. Moss tries to hold the crown still, but the paper shifts.

SURI: It is folded paper. It will make a sound.

PELL: Perhaps the sound is ceremonial.

MOSS: Then the ceremony will be very loud.

The courtiers test the crown. One sneezes and causes a cascade of crinkles. Another places it on a cushion, where it makes no sound at all.

SCENE 3. INT. PALACE WORKSHOP - AFTERNOON

Suri examines the crown. The gold paper is made from a discarded royal map. Its folds mark a path through the palace garden to a small greenhouse. Vera realizes the heirloom may be a clue, not a crown.

PELL: The crown is a map?

SURI: It is a map that fits on your head.

They follow the creases across the palace. A folded star points to a greenhouse behind the fountain. Moss opens the gate. The lock is a simple paper puzzle; the court guard tries to use a sword.

SCENE 4. INT. GREENHOUSE - EVENING

Inside the greenhouse is a seedling planted by the first ruler. A plaque says the crown should pass to the person who cares for the palace garden. Pell admits he has never tended a plant. Suri knows how to fold paper into watering spouts. Vera finds an old gardening ledger.

MOSS: The heir must be a gardener?

VERA: The note says care for the garden, not inherit a title.

Pell waters the seedling. The paper crown absorbs a drop and softens. Its stars collapse into a gold map.

SCENE 5. INT. PALACE HALL - NIGHT

The court returns to the ceremony. Pell refuses the crown and proposes that the royal garden be managed by its gardeners. The courtiers object that a crown must be worn. Suri folds the paper map into five stars, each given to a different caretaker.

PELL: The ceremony can continue.

MOSS: Without the crown?

PELL: With five crowns.

The court bows to the gardeners. The paper stars crinkle as each person moves.

SCENE 6. EXT. PALACE GARDEN - DAWN

The seedling is planted in a larger bed. Pell learns to water it without splashing the paper map. Vera records the new tradition in the archive. Captain Moss guards the garden gate, holding a watering can instead of a sword.

SCENE 7. INT. ROYAL ARCHIVE - MORNING

Vera files the new garden agreement. The old royal records list a single heir for every season. She adds the names of the gardeners beside each folded star. A gust from the open window scatters the papers across the room.

PELL: The archive has become the garden.

SURI: The papers are seeds.

Moss tries to stop the pages with his ceremonial sword, but the paper stars flutter around him. The guard uses the sword's flat side to gather them into a neat stack. The court agrees that the gardeners will keep the stars in the greenhouse, where the paper can be replaced after each season.

VERA: The royal record will need a new shelf.

PELL: Use the garden shed.

SCENE 8. INT. PALACE HALL - AFTERNOON

A sudden shower blows through the open windows. The paper stars soften and begin to droop. The courtiers reach for the gold crown, but Pell tells them to move the stars to the greenhouse instead. Suri folds small waxed-paper covers for each one.

MOSS: A crown should not need a raincoat.

PELL: This one is a map and a promise.

The gardeners carry the stars down the hall. One slips from Vera's hand and lands on a royal chair. A courtier bows to the chair before noticing the paper star. Vera records the accidental ceremony as "the first time the chair has been honored."

FINAL IMAGE: Five paper stars hang above the young tree, softly crackling whenever the wind moves them.
