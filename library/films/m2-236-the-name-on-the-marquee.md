# The Name on the Marquee

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: movie-star biopics, glamorous origin-story montages, tearful award speeches, and fame caused by a misspelled theater sign*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
REMI, a capuchin projectionist
LORA, a macaque actor
BASK, a gibbon producer
TIV, a ring-tailed lemur historian

SCENE 1. INT. OLD CINEMA - NIGHT

A crowd waits outside the old cinema. Its marquee reads "LORA THE GREAT." Lora, a macaque actor, stands beneath it, confused. Remi, the capuchin projectionist, says the letters were mixed up by the wind.

LORA: I have never been called great.

BASK: You are being honored.

TIV: The theater is honoring someone named "Lora the Great."

The crowd begins a standing ovation. Lora's career montage starts on a screen: she carries a prop lamp, misses a cue, and waves to a camera crew.

SCENE 2. INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - LATER

Remi discovers an old film reel labeled "Lora's first appearance." It shows Lora as a child actor in a commercial for a coat rack. She stands still while everyone hangs scarves on her arms.

LORA: That was my first film?

REMI: You were very still.

BASK: It was a landmark performance in stillness.

Tiv checks the theater archive. The original marquee was meant to read "LORA: THE GREAT." The colon fell off decades ago, turning a modest retrospective into a grand biography.

SCENE 3. INT. LOBBY - EVENING

Bask insists the award ceremony continue. He gives Lora a speech written by a publicist. It claims she trained on a mountain, learned to roar at storms, and once saved a director from a collapsing set.

LORA: I worked on a small stage above a bakery.

TIV: I have the records.

BASK: Records do not make a montage.

Tiv unrolls a program. It shows Lora's actual training: carrying prop furniture, holding a coat rack, and taking the stairs because the elevator was reserved for scenery.

SCENE 4. INT. CINEMA SCREENING ROOM - NIGHT

Remi plays a reel of Lora's early work. The coat-rack commercial is followed by a silent short in which she keeps holding a door open while the crew carries an enormous moon through it. Every time she tries to leave, another person arrives with scenery.

The audience laughs. Lora watches her younger self wait patiently.

LORA: I thought that scene was about the moon.

REMI: It was about the door.

Tiv reveals that the director had asked Lora to improvise the whole sequence. The film was screened once, then shelved because the moon prop was too large for the theater.

SCENE 5. INT. STAGE - LATER

Bask takes the microphone. He introduces Lora as a legend whose greatest role was helping a crew get a moon through a doorway. He gives the award to Remi, who found the reel, and Tiv, who found the records.

BASK: The story was smaller than our poster.

TIV: It was still a story.

Lora takes the award and asks that the theater restore the colon to the marquee. The letters are too heavy; Remi uses two spare brackets to hold the punctuation in place.

SCENE 6. EXT. OLD CINEMA - NIGHT

The crowd returns to the marquee. It now reads "LORA: THE GREAT." The colon falls down again and lands beside the award. Lora picks it up. Bask asks whether the biography will have a sequel.

LORA: Only if the door opens.

SCENE 7. INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - LATER THAT NIGHT

Tiv searches the archive for the date of Lora's first film. The catalog lists the coat-rack commercial under "furniture," then lists Lora under "scenery." Remi finds the original crew notes: Lora kept the door open because the director's hands were full.

TIV: That was the whole legend.

BASK: It needs a montage.

LORA: It had a door, a moon, and a tired crew. It was enough.

The theater audience files past the booth and asks to see the coat-rack commercial again. Remi rewinds the projector. The image jumps, and the coat rack turns into a blurry mountain for three frames.

SCENE 8. INT. OLD CINEMA LOBBY - AFTER MIDNIGHT

A young actor waits for Lora by the door. They ask how she held the pose through the long take. Lora shows the actor how to brace a foot against the doorstop and keep one hand free for the next visitor.

YOUNG ACTOR: Was it difficult?

LORA: The moon was heavy. The door was lighter.

Bask writes this down for the biography. Tiv crosses out the mountain rescue and writes "held door for crew." Remi places the original reel back in its case, labeled with the correct film title.

FINAL IMAGE: The theater door swings wide as the crew carries the oversized moon prop out, and Lora holds the colon over the marquee until the last person passes.
