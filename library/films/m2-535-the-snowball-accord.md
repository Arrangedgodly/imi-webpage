# The Snowball Accord

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: political summit comedies where rival villages negotiate a treaty during a snow-sculpture contest*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: MAYOR FROST, a baboon; MAYOR THAW, a macaque; ENVOY LILT, a gibbon; ENGINEER PELL, an orangutan; REFEREE NIM, a capuchin; COUNCILOR BRACK, a mandrill.

EXT. BORDER COMMON - MORNING

Two villages meet on a snowy field. A rope marks the border. On one side, a snow bear. On the other, a snow bridge. The mayors stand with clipboards.

FROST: Our snow bear is on our land.

THAW: Its paw crosses the border.

ENVOY LILT: It is a sculpture, not a soldier.

BRACK: The paw is a territorial claim.

The snow bear's paw slides another inch downhill.

REFEREE NIM: The contest allows gravity.

ACT ONE

INT. BORDER TENT - DAY

The mayors meet beside a map. A heater hums. Pell checks the snow bridge's support. Its middle sags.

PELL: The sculpture cannot hold its own weight.

THAW: It is supposed to represent cooperation.

FROST: Then it should cooperate with gravity.

Lilt proposes moving the rope to the center line. Brack refuses, saying the border has not moved in forty years. Nim checks the map and finds the rope is two feet north of the actual marker stone.

NIM: The rope was placed for the contest, not the treaty.

The mayors ask both villages to bring their measuring poles. The poles differ by one foot. Pell aligns them against the marker stone. They match when one is turned upside down.

FROST: Your pole is wrong.

THAW: The numbers are printed from the other end.

They move the rope to the true line. The bear's paw is now on Frost's side; the bridge is on Thaw's. The mayors return to the tent to draft a contest rule.

The two mayors return to their teams and read the agreement aloud. One clause gives each village access to the common road. Another says the snow sculptures must stay within the marked area. Councilor Brack objects that the bear's shadow crosses the line even when its paw does not.

BRACK: The shadow occupies our territory.

NIM: The contest rules do not mention shadows.

FROST: The treaty does.

Thaw asks for the actual boundary marker. Pell clears snow around a stone at the edge of the field. The stone carries two old initials, one from each village. The mayors recognize the shared marker from childhood.

THAW: Our grandparents set it together.

FROST: The rope was never the border.

Brack points to the snow bridge, which has partly melted in the warm tent air. The sculpture will collapse if they leave it on its narrow base. Pell proposes widening the base into a platform that sits fully on the common field.

PELL: The bridge can stand without crossing either village line.

The teams haul snow from the edges and pack it around the sculpture. Both villages work from their own side. The snow bridge grows wider, and the bear's paw is set back inside the line. The referee measures the finished pieces again. The mayors ask the clerk to attach the actual marker's location to the signed agreement.

ACT TWO

A snowplow approaches on the road between the villages. The driver cannot pass while the bridge sculpture blocks the route. Both teams hurry to move the snow bridge, but its center gives way. The two halves slide apart, leaving an opening.

PELL: We can bridge the gap with the bear's base.

FROST: The bear is not a construction part.

THAW: It is a snow base on wheels.

They roll the bear base across the gap and set the bridge halves on top. The snowplow passes. Nim checks that the actual border marker remains visible. The teams pack the snow back around the sculpture and leave a clear path.

Brack asks who won the contest. The referee measures both sculptures. The snow bear's paw is inside the line. The bridge stands, but it now rests on the bear's base.

NIM: Both entries meet the rules.

FROST: There is no joint first prize.

THAW: We can share the shovel.

The mayors sign a short agreement: the common road stays clear, and contest ropes do not mark the political border. Lilt pins the agreement to the tent wall. The heater hums; a small icicle drops onto the signature line.

LILT: We need waterproof ink.

Nim holds the agreement under the tent awning while Pell brings a wax pencil from the tools. The mayors sign again on the dry corner of the page. Brack checks that the marker is still visible and takes down the contest rope.

BRACK: The rope is only for the sculptures.

FROST: The stone is for the border.

Thaw carries the shared shovel back to the common road.

FINAL IMAGE: The villages carry the snowplow's cleared road forward as the snow bear holds up the bridge.
