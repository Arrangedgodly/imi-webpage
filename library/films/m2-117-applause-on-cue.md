# Applause on Cue

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: festival films that instruct audiences how to react, followed by Q&As that turn simple questions into lectures*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A filmmaker screens a quiet movie about a bench and provides applause cues, but the audience has its own response.

Cast: Luma, a macaque filmmaker; Rell, a gorilla moderator; Seli, a lemur projectionist; and Pip, a capuchin audience member.

SCENE 1. INT. SMALL FESTIVAL CINEMA, EVENING

Luma hands the audience a program with tiny symbols: a leaf means reflect, a dot means remain silent, and a gold star means applaud. Pip turns the card over, looking for the legend.

LUMA: Please wait until the gold star.

PIP: Is there one in the film?

Rell introduces Luma's movie, "A Bench at Dusk." It shows a bench. A leaf falls. The screen fades to black. The audience waits. Luma's cue card says to hold silence for twelve seconds. A child coughs at second three. Rell gently offers the child a glass of water. Luma marks the cough in her notes as "unplanned interruption." The child drinks, then returns the glass to the usher without spilling. Luma asks whether that action changed the scene. Pip says it was the most useful thing shown so far.

SCENE 2. INT. CINEMA, CONTINUOUS

The film continues with a close-up of the bench's wooden slat. It stays still for twenty seconds. Pip looks at the program. Seli checks the projector; the image is moving. Rell whispers that the silence is intentional. A guest asks whether the film is paused.

PIP: The leaf moved.

LUMA: That is the only motion in the frame.

PIP: It has more screen time than the bench's owner.

LUMA: The bench has no owner.

Pip looks over the audience. A few people shift in their seats. Luma quietly raises a card marked STILLNESS. Pip shifts back. His chair squeaks, and Rell turns the card around to hide it from the audience.

LUMA: The shot asks you to consider waiting.

PIP: I am waiting for the next shot.

The next shot shows the same bench from the opposite side. A cyclist rolls through the deep background, stops, backs up, and checks a map. Luma leans toward Rell.

LUMA: That was not in the shot list.

RELL: It is a real location.

LUMA: The cyclist changes the frame's emotional temperature.

PIP: He is lost.

The cyclist finally exits in the wrong direction. The audience laughs. Luma raises a card marked CONTEMPLATE. The laughter fades into an awkward cough, and she lowers it. The gold star appears in the corner. The audience applauds. Luma looks pleased, then points to the program: the star was meant for the final card. Rell says the audience anticipated the ending.

SCENE 3. INT. CINEMA LOBBY, AFTER THE SCREENING

Rell moderates a Q&A. He asks the audience to hold questions until after Luma's statement. Luma explains that the bench represents the passage of time, the burden of stillness, and the limits of looking. Pip raises his hand.

PIP: Why did the leaf fall?

LUMA: It was autumn.

Rell asks if there are questions about the camera's relationship to the bench. Pip asks where the bench is. Luma says it is in the town square. Seli says the festival shuttle stops nearby. The audience begins to leave. Rell asks them to remain for one more interpretive question.

Pip asks if the bench is comfortable.

PIP: Have you sat on it?

LUMA: I have not tried it.

PIP: Then how do you know it represents waiting?

LUMA: I framed it waiting.

PIP: The people sitting on it might have other plans.

Luma looks at the empty Q&A chairs and stands. They walk outside to the square. They walk outside to the square. The bench has a loose slat and a sign asking visitors to report repairs. Luma asks the custodian to fix it before anyone sits down.

SCENE 4. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, NIGHT

The custodian tightens the slat. Luma watches without filming. He holds up the wrench and asks whether the audience should applaud. Rell starts to raise the gold-star card, then puts it in his pocket. Pip steadies the bench while the custodian tests the repair.

CUSTODIAN: It is solid now.

LUMA: Do you mind if I record the sound?

CUSTODIAN: It is a wrench.

LUMA: Exactly. Pip reads the notice and says the film could include it. Luma says the film is finished. Rell asks whether this is the ending. Seli says the screening ended twenty minutes ago.

They sit on the next bench, which has no loose slats. Luma asks if they feel the passage of time. Pip checks the bus schedule. Rell says the conversation was productive. The bus arrives.

FINAL IMAGE: The festival program lies on the seat, the gold star facing up. A visitor turns it over to read the bus timetable.
