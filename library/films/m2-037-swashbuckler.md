# The Captain of Half a Ship

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: swashbuckling pirate adventures, mutiny speeches, and implausible treasure maps*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A cautious lemur captain inherits half a pirate ship and must negotiate with the rival captain who owns the other half, while the crew keeps staging mutinies for the ballad.

Cast: Captain Sella, a lemur who reads contracts; Captain Brine, a baboon pirate; Midge, a macaque navigator; Oar, a capuchin deckhand; Wick, an owl lighthouse keeper; and a parrot who repeats only the last word of each sentence.

SCENE 1. EXT. HARBOR, MORNING

Sella steps aboard. A white line divides the deck. Brine stands on one side holding a deed; Sella holds its matching half.

BRINE: The ship is mine.

SELLA: Half is mine.

OAR: Can we mutiny?

SELLA: Against whom?

OAR: Whoever gives the better speech.

The parrot repeats "speech." Midge unfolds a treasure map over the line. The map tears exactly in half. Brine calls it a sign. Sella says it was cheap paper.

SCENE 2. INT. CAPTAIN'S CABIN, DAY

Midge traces the map's route. It ends at a reef, then shows a lighthouse and a note: SPLIT HERE. Brine says they should split the treasure. Sella reads the map's crease and says it means fold the page. The parrot repeats "fold." Oar tries to fold the map while Midge is still holding it, pulling Midge across the table.

Brine calls the crew to mutiny practice. Everyone dons bandanas and gathers behind him. The parrot shouts "mutiny" when Sella asks for a cup. The crew turns toward her. Brine says the timing is wrong and asks them to start again. Oar wants a rehearsal stipend.

SCENE 3. EXT. OPEN WATER, AFTERNOON

Midge uses a sextant to measure the reef. Brine looks through the telescope backward and reports that the lighthouse is moving away. Sella turns it around. The lighthouse is stationary; Brine has been watching a gull. The crew debates whether the gull is a lookout. It drops a clam on the map.

OAR: The bird has made its mark.

SELLA: It has made a mess.

Brine tries to raise a flag. The flag is tied to the sail with a knot Oar cannot undo. Sella pulls the correct end and the flag opens, displaying the ship's old name: HALF SHARE. Brine says it sounds like an inn. The parrot repeats the word inn and looks at the crew's lunch basket.

The ship reaches the reef. Brine orders Oar to drop anchor. Oar asks which side owns the anchor. Sella checks the deed; the chain is shared. The anchor falls, but the ship keeps drifting because the line is tied to Brine's half and the deck line has been painted over.

MIDGE: We are moving.

BRINE: At least we agree on that.

A squall arrives. Brine orders everyone to port. Sella says the port rail belongs to her. Brine points at the clouds. The parrot repeats "clouds" and retreats to the cabin. Midge grabs the wheel. Oar and Brine pull the same rope in opposite directions. The sail folds over the deck and lands across both captains, temporarily uniting their halves.

SCENE 4. EXT. LIGHTHOUSE DOCK, DUSK

The sail drops. The ship drifts into the dock at walking speed. Wick, the lighthouse keeper, stands on the pier holding a clipboard.

WICK: Are you here for the light or the treasure?

BRINE: Both.

SELLA: Directions first.

Wick explains the old map marks a repair chest, not gold. Inside are spare rope, canvas, and a ledger showing the vessel was bought by their grandparents as a shared project. Brine finds a line for unpaid docking fees. Sella sees the amount is smaller than the cost of one dramatic cannon shot.

The parrot lands on the ledger and repeats "fees." Brine pays Wick. Oar asks whether the crew can split the bill. Wick charges them one coin each for using the lighthouse stairs. They pay, then help tighten the loose handrail.

SCENE 5. EXT. HARBOR, NIGHT

The agreement takes three drafts. In the first, both captains steer. In the second, neither pays fees. Midge points out a ship cannot be piloted by two people pulling the wheel in opposite directions. Brine concedes the helm on alternating days. Sella adds that the parrot does not vote on ownership. It repeats ownership and takes Brine's pencil.

Oar asks if the crew can mutiny against the paperwork. Sella gives him a job updating the ship log. He accepts, then writes dramatic mutiny under tomorrow's activities.

Sella and Brine return to port. They write a shared-use agreement: one captain steers, the other reads the charts; either can call a safety stop; both pay docking fees. Oar asks if he may still wear a bandana. Sella says yes, except when tying knots. He ties one around the parrot instead. It repeats "shared" and pecks the knot loose.

FINAL IMAGE: The crew begins a ballad about the treasure chest. The first verse celebrates gold; the second lists rope, canvas, fees, and handrail maintenance. Brine asks for a more heroic rhyme. The parrot squawks "maintenance," and the crew sings it louder.
