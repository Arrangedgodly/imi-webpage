# The Sculpture Is Facing East

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: art-house films that replace clear conflict with cryptic pauses and treat basic directions as profound ambiguity*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A town installs a public sculpture, but the artist, curator, and camera crew spend all day debating its orientation while visitors try to use the plaza.

Cast: Oru, a macaque sculptor; Vale, a lemur curator; Bim, a gorilla installer; and Nella, a capuchin plaza gardener.

SCENE 1. EXT. TOWN PLAZA, DAWN

A metal sculpture shaped like a curled leaf rests on a pallet. Oru watches the crane. Vale asks which direction the sculpture should face. Oru says the piece is in conversation with the horizon. Bim checks the installation plan; it says the leaf tip should face east, away from the walkway.

ORU: East is a suggestion.

BIM: The crane needs a direction.

Nella moves a bench so the crew can establish a safety perimeter. Vale asks whether the bench placement changes the work's meaning. Nella says it changes whether people can pass.

SCENE 2. EXT. TOWN PLAZA, MORNING

The sculpture is lifted and set on its base. Oru asks Bim to rotate it slowly. The crane operator pauses for a gust. Vale films the pause and says it captures uncertainty. Bim checks the wind gauge and asks them to wait until the lift is safe.

Visitors arrive. A child asks what the sculpture is. Oru says it is a leaf. Vale says the question should remain open. Nella points out that the child is asking because the information sign is still in the truck. She sets the sign beside the path. It identifies the metal, the artist, and the work's title: "Leaf, Facing East."

Oru asks if the sign is too literal. Vale suggests mounting it behind the sculpture. Nella says visitors need to read it without crossing the safety rope. They place it at the entrance.

Vale asks the camera operator to film the sign without showing the title. Oru says the title is part of the work. The operator shifts the frame, revealing only the word EAST. Vale suggests that the partial view invites interpretation. Nella asks whether the full sign will be available at the opening. Oru says yes. She adds the sign to the checklist so no one forgets to return it from the truck. The crane operator asks for the final signal; Bim gives it only after the crew confirms the perimeter is clear.

SCENE 3. EXT. TOWN PLAZA, AFTERNOON

Oru and Vale argue about whether the sculpture faces east. The compass app points east; the old town map points north because it is rotated. Bim marks the cardinal directions on the plaza with chalk. Vale films the chalk marks in a long, silent shot.

A passerby follows the chalk arrows and asks where the bus stop is. Nella checks the town map and directs them to the west corner. Vale asks whether the question belongs in the film. Oru says it belongs in the plaza. He moves a chalk mark that accidentally points into the planted bed. The town clerk arrives with the installation permit. It specifies a two-meter clear path and an anchor inspection before opening. Bim measures the route and finds a bench leg intruding into the clearance. Nella moves it. Oru asks whether the bench has become part of the composition. Nella says it has become somewhere to sit.

The wind pushes the sculpture's temporary cover into the walkway. Nella retrieves it and asks the crew to secure the cover. Oru says the movement could be part of the work. Bim says the cover is not the sculpture. He ties it to the base. Vale asks whether the knot is too conclusive.

The installer measures the base and confirms the work is stable. Oru checks the plan again and agrees the leaf tip should face east. He asks for one small adjustment. The crane operator rotates it a few degrees while Bim spots the load. The leaf now clears the path and catches the afternoon light.

SCENE 4. EXT. TOWN PLAZA, SUNSET

The opening begins. Vale gives a speech about interpretation. The audience waits. Nella asks visitors to keep the path open for a stroller. Oru adds that the sculpture's shadow changes through the day, but the plaque gives the direction and materials. A child reads it aloud and recognizes the shape.

Vale tries to ask what the leaf symbolizes. Oru says it can be a leaf, a shelter, or simply a curved piece of steel. Nella points to the bench and the clear route. Visitors sit, walk around the work, and take photographs without stepping into the planted beds.

The camera crew asks Oru to pose beside the sculpture. He stands where the sign can be read and asks Bim to check the anchors one last time. Bim signs the inspection tag.

Vale asks Oru to describe the piece. Oru says the curved steel echoes the leaves around the plaza, but its meaning is open to visitors. Vale waits for a long pause. A child points out that the leaf faces east. Oru confirms it. The child asks if east is where the sun comes up. Nella says it is, and the visitors turn to watch the last light.

FINAL IMAGE: The sculpture faces east. Its shadow reaches the bench. Nella turns the information sign toward the next arriving visitors, and nobody has to guess which way the path goes.
