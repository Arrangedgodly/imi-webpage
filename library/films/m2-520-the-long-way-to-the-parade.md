# The Long Way to the Parade

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: road movies with mismatched companions, a deadline, roadside setbacks, and a final arrival that changes the plan*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: ELI, a capuchin courier; BRAM, a baboon mechanic; JO, a gibbon parade marshal; TOMA, an orangutan puppet builder; RAE, a macaque child marshal.

EXT. COUNTRY ROAD - MORNING

Eli pedals a cargo tricycle carrying a huge parade puppet shaped like a moon. The puppet's face points backward. Bram follows in a tiny repair van.

BRAM: The parade starts at four.

ELI: We will reach the town by three.

BRAM: The tricycle has one gear.

ELI: It has a very brave gear.

The puppet's head swivels and faces the road. A gust catches its silver tassels. Eli stops to tuck them under a strap.

TOMA, on the phone: Keep the head upright. The paper frame bends if it catches wind.

JO, on radio: The main road is closed for bridge repairs. Use the orchard lane.

Eli unfolds a map. The orchard lane loops around a hill.

ELI: That is the long way.

BRAM: We have time if we don't stop.

They look at the loose wheel on the repair van.

BRAM: I did not say that about me.

ACT ONE

EXT. ORCHARD LANE - LATER

The cargo tricycle rolls through the orchard. A low branch snags the puppet's tassels and turns the moon's face to the side. Bram unhooks the fabric. Eli holds the frame.

ELI: We cannot arrive with the moon looking at the hedge.

BRAM: It could be a shy moon.

Eli rotates the head upright. Toma asks over the phone if it is still secure. Bram checks each strap and finds one buckle cracked.

BRAM: We need a replacement.

The van has a toolbox, but its rear door is stuck. Bram pulls. It does not move. Eli gives the handle one firm tug; the door swings open and the toolbox slides toward the road. Rae, riding in the van, catches it.

RAE: That was almost a parade of tools.

Bram fits a strap from the toolbox around the puppet frame. The group continues. At a fork, they see two signs: TOWN, 8 MILES, and TOWN, 3 MILES, CLOSED.

ELI: The shorter way is blocked.

BRAM: The longer way is open.

Rae checks the radio. Jo says the parade route has changed and the starting line moved to the school field, not the square.

JO: Use the field gate behind the library. Do not enter the main square.

Eli looks at the map. The orchard lane leads directly to the field gate if they turn before the creek.

ACT TWO

EXT. CREEK CROSSING - AFTERNOON

The orchard lane ends at a narrow footbridge. A sign says NO WHEELED TRAFFIC. The cargo tricycle cannot cross. Bram tests the boards with one boot; the center plank flexes.

BRAM: We can carry the frame across in pieces.

ELI: The moon head is wider than the bridge.

Rae finds a maintenance ramp folded beside the rail. Bram lays it over the soft plank and checks both ends. The group carries the puppet frame across first, then pushes the empty tricycle over the ramp by hand. The front wheel wobbles but stays on the boards.

RAE: We used the long way to avoid the road closure and found a bridge closure.

JO, on radio: The field gate will stay open until four. Keep moving.

They refit the moon head on the far bank. One tassel hangs into the water. Toma reaches with a pole and lifts it clear before the paper darkens.

EXT. SCHOOL FIELD GATE - AFTERNOON

They reach the gate at 3:40. The puppet's lower pole has slipped from its holder. Toma arrives in a small van with a spare bracket. He and Bram refit the pole while Eli steadies the head.

TOMA: This puppet was meant to be carried by four people.

ELI: It has been carried by two and a tricycle.

JO: The parade is waiting. We have one entry slot left.

Rae checks the straps. The paper moon faces forward. The group lifts the puppet from the tricycle and carries it through the field gate. The band begins the opening tune. The moon's silver tassels shake as the bearers walk.

At the parade line, the marshal calls the puppet team. Jo asks if it can turn at the marker. Bram demonstrates the turn. The frame clears the gate.

JO: You are in. Follow the blue cones.

ELI: We took the long way and got here early enough.

RAE: We also brought a repair van, toolbox, spare bracket, and one missing wheel.

Bram looks back at the van. Its wheel is still attached; the loose part was the hubcap.

BRAM: It was not a wheel.

TOMA: We can use it as a moon.

Toma hangs the hubcap from the puppet's lower frame. It spins in the breeze.

FINAL IMAGE: The parade moon rolls onto the field beneath the evening sky, its small hubcap orbiting below it.
