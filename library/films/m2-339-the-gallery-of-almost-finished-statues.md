# The Gallery of Almost Finished Statues

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: art-world dramas, genius-artist monologues, mysterious unfinished works, and critics who invent meanings before checking the label*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
CURATOR LUMA, a lemur
SCULPTOR ODO, an orangutan
CRITIC PIP, a squirrel monkey
GUARD TAVI, a capuchin
RESTORER NARI, a macaque

SCENE 1. INT. CITY GALLERY - MORNING

A marble figure stands in the center of a room, one arm unfinished. Critic Pip circles it with a notebook. Curator Luma prepares for the opening. Odo, the sculptor, carries a small chisel and a lunch bag.

PIP: The missing hand represents absence.

ODO: It represents a hand I have not carved yet.

Luma says the sculpture is part of a series on ordinary work. Pip insists on describing it as a meditation on lost time. Tavi ropes off the work area and tells the visitors not to touch the marble.

SCENE 2. INT. CONSERVATION ROOM - LATER

Restorer Nari examines a crack in the base. She says the statue is stable but the plinth needs leveling. Odo asks to finish the sculpture before the opening. Nari says dust control is required, and the gallery has already installed the lights. Luma offers a small temporary platform in the side room.

A gallery visitor reads the label and asks whether the statue is missing an arm. Pip answers with a speech about the tension between completion and absence. Odo hears him through the glass and taps the window. The visitor asks if the sculptor is trying to correct the label. Pip says art can hold two readings. Odo writes on a pad: 'The arm is unfinished.' Nari hangs the note beside the wall text.

Pip watches the work through the glass and writes that the artist has retreated from the public. Odo can hear him. He holds up the lunch bag and asks if retreat means lunch break. Pip says that is not the language he would use.

SCENE 3. INT. GALLERY HALL - AFTERNOON

The opening begins. Guests read Pip's wall text, which calls the sculpture "A Hand That Never Arrived." Odo reads it and shakes his head. Luma asks Pip to change the label. He refuses at first and invites her to defend the plain description in a recorded interview. She explains the piece is part of a series about how work leaves marks on the body. Odo joins from the workroom and demonstrates how a glove protects a sculptor's palm. Pip listens, then asks if the glove itself is part of the exhibit. Odo says only if it has a label stating whose hand used it. Pip says the room is already full. Nari begins to level the plinth with a wedge. The statue tilts slightly, and every guest gasps.

TAVI: Please step back.

PIP: The work is moving.

NARI: The floor is uneven.

The wedge settles. The sculpture stands straight. The crowd applauds. Pip writes that the audience has witnessed a transformation. Odo says they witnessed a level platform.

SCENE 4. INT. SIDE ROOM - EVENING

Odo moves his tools to the side room. Visitors follow because the doorway is open. He asks them to stay behind the line while he carves a small wooden maquette, not the marble piece. Pip announces that the artist will reveal the unfinished hand. Odo explains he is shaping a work glove to fit the statue's wrist.

Luma asks why a sculpture about ordinary work should wear a glove. Odo says it shows how hands change through labor. Nari shows how the original plinth's angle made the empty wrist appear broken. With the base level, the unfinished edge looks intentional without needing a dramatic explanation.

Pip looks again at his label. He crosses out "never arrived" and writes "work in progress." Odo approves. Tavi moves the rope farther from the doorway, giving visitors a clear view without letting them crowd the tools. He checks the distance twice, then places a small sign that says PLEASE LEAVE ROOM FOR THE WORK.

SCENE 5. INT. CITY GALLERY - NIGHT

The gallery closes. Odo finishes the wooden glove, which rests beside the marble sculpture. He does not attach it; the exhibit now includes the piece's unfinished state and the process behind it. Pip's revised label explains that the statue is one of a series about work and changing hands.

A visitor returns her coat and asks if the missing hand will ever be finished. Odo says he may carve it next year. Luma says the exhibit can remain open to change. Pip closes his notebook and admits the plain version is clearer. Nari checks the plinth once more. It stays level.

FINAL IMAGE: The gallery light falls across the unfinished wrist and the small wooden glove beside it, both casting separate, complete shadows.
