# The Orchestra Beneath the Stage

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: backstage musical dramas, last-minute casting crises, and climactic songs that reveal what every worker was thinking*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: When a lead singer loses her voice on opening night, a shy capuchin stagehand must conduct the orchestra from beneath a collapsing set while the crew performs the musical's finale without its star.

Cast
- NILO, a capuchin stagehand with nimble hands and a quiet singing voice.
- SERA, a macaque lead singer who has strained her voice.
- VEL, a gibbon conductor who refuses to improvise.
- BOK, a mandrill carpenter who can recognize any creak in the set.
- ODA, a ring-tailed lemur lighting operator whose tail holds the color cue cards.
- MR. PELL, an orangutan producer obsessed with the program's star billing.

## Scene 1: Opening Night

INT. RIVERLIGHT THEATER - EVENING

A painted riverboat waits behind the curtain. The theater is full. Sera tries a high note and produces a squeak. Vel lowers his baton.

SERA: I can sing the first verse. The second might not come out.

PELL: The audience bought tickets for your voice.

BOK: The audience bought tickets for the musical. The boat set is leaning left.

NILO: I can brace the platform.

VEL: We cannot change the score on opening night.

Sera walks to the curtain. Nilo crawls beneath the stage to wedge a board under the set. The orchestra plays its overture while the crew whispers in the dark.

## Scene 2: The Substitution

Backstage, Pell suggests canceling the show. Sera refuses. She says the finale can be sung by the ensemble, but the score assigns every line to her.

SERA: Give the river song to the people who built the boat.

VEL: That changes the arrangement.

ODA: I can change the lighting cues. The colors already match the crew's parts.

BOK: My part is fixing the leaning boat.

NILO: I know the harmonies. I can cue them from below.

PELL: You are a stagehand.

NILO: I am standing under the stage where the sound is loudest.

The curtain rises. Sera performs the first verse gently. As her voice fades, Nilo taps the floorboards in time. The ensemble hears him and joins the chorus. Pell watches the audience lean forward instead of leaving.

## Scene 3: The Set Moves

The painted riverboat begins to roll toward the edge of the platform. Bok recognizes the creak. He signals Nilo, who shifts the wedge under the stage. The movement makes the floorboards thump on the beat.

VEL: Is the stage shaking?

NILO: It is keeping time.

SERA: The river is moving beneath us.

The cast turns the accident into choreography. The crew carries lantern props across the stage while Oda changes the lights from blue to gold. The orchestra follows the footstep rhythm. Pell whispers to Vel that the show is becoming a different show.

PELL: Can we call this the revised version?

VEL: We can call it the version currently happening.

## Scene 4: The Song from Below

Nilo's voice carries through the floor. At first, the audience thinks the boat itself is singing. Sera recognizes his harmony and gives him a cue by tapping the deck with her heel. Nilo answers from under the stage. The ensemble repeats his phrase.

The lyrics are short and original:

ENSEMBLE: Hold the plank and pass the light; the river finds its course tonight.

The song was not in the score, but the orchestra adapts. Nilo crawls to the side of the stage and emerges beside the crew, covered in sawdust. The audience applauds at the sight of the hidden singer.

PELL: We cannot give the stagehand top billing.

SERA: Then bill the whole crew.

Pell looks at the program and crosses out the single-star line.

## Scene 5: The Finale

The boat platform stabilizes. Bok secures the brace from behind the painted wheel. Oda puts a warm spotlight on the crew, not the lead. Vel gives the ensemble a clear downbeat. Sera speaks her final line instead of singing it.

SERA: This boat belongs to everyone who kept it afloat.

The entire cast sings the final chorus. Nilo conducts with a wrench. The orchestra follows him. The set stops creaking. The curtain falls exactly on the last note.

## Scene 6: The Bow

The audience stands. Pell tells Sera she can bow first, but she steps aside and brings the stage crew forward. Bok bows with a plank. Oda bows with color cards still looped over her tail. Nilo receives a small baton from Vel.

VEL: You may conduct the next performance from above the floor.

NILO: I prefer having the floor under me.

PELL: The program can list the ensemble.

SERA: It can list everyone's name.

## Final Image

The crew takes one shared bow before the painted riverboat. A loose board gives one final rhythmic creak, and the cast laughs before the curtain closes.
