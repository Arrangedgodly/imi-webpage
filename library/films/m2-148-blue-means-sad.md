# The Blue Means Sad

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: color grading that assigns emotional meaning to every scene and changes practical objects into continuity problems*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A colorist grades every scene in a small repair shop to signal emotion until the crew can no longer tell whether a warning light is red.

Cast: Hema, a lemur colorist; Boro, a gorilla actor; Jori, a macaque director; and Puck, a capuchin editor.

SCENE 1. INT. COLOR SUITE, MORNING

Hema grades a scene of Boro repairing a wall clock. Jori asks for the opening to feel warm. Hema adds amber to the image. The clock face turns yellow, and the white repair manual becomes cream.

JORI: The room should feel welcoming.

BORO: It is a workshop.

Hema cools the shadows to make the scene feel uncertain. The metal tools turn blue. Puck says the screwdriver now matches the background and disappears. Hema raises the contrast. Boro's dark sleeve merges with the workbench. Jori asks Hema to restore the sleeve without changing the room. Hema masks the sleeve. When Boro reaches for a tool, the mask slips onto the clock face and turns its hands blue.

PUCK: The clock looks like it remembers the past.

BORO: It is set to the wrong time.

Hema repairs the mask. Boro's sleeve returns to the image, but the metal tools remain blue. Jori asks whether that makes them feel dangerous.

SCENE 2. INT. REPAIR SHOP SET, LATER

Jori asks for a red warning light to signal danger. The practical lamp on the workbench is green. Hema shifts the entire image toward red. The lamp now appears red, but so does Boro's face.

BORO: I am not warning anyone.

JORI: It is a mood.

Puck checks the raw footage. The lamp has a green shade and indicates the clock is running. Hema adds a mask around it. The light becomes red while the rest of the room stays neutral. Boro turns it on; the lamp flickers and the mask tracks onto his knuckle.

HEMA: The danger has moved.

Boro lowers his hand. Jori asks for a close-up. The camera shows a red knuckle beside the green lamp. Puck compares it to the raw frame. The knuckle is normal in the original footage. Hema tracks the color mask to the lamp and narrows it. Now only the bulb turns red, but the lamp's cord glows too.

HEMA: The cord is connected to the danger.

BORO: It is connected to the wall.

Jori asks for the red to pulse. Puck says that would imply the lamp is blinking. Hema leaves it steady.

SCENE 3. INT. COLOR SUITE, AFTERNOON

Hema creates three looks: warm for the present, blue for memory, and gray for the moment Boro doubts the repair. Puck lays them side by side. The same mug changes from orange to violet to silver. Boro asks if it is still the same mug.

JORI: It represents time.

BORO: It is the tea I brought in.

Hema returns the mug to its original color. Jori says the memory should look more nostalgic. Puck asks what color nostalgia is. Jori says it is the one that makes the scene feel expensive. Hema adds a golden glow. The clock becomes hard to read.

Boro points to the numbers. They need to remain legible so he can set the correct time. Hema reduces the glow. The image becomes clear. Jori asks if the ending can shift from gray to gold when the clock starts. Hema sets a gradient. The wall changes color while Boro listens. He turns toward the wall.

BORO: Is the paint wet?

Jori asks if the shift can happen more slowly. The gradient covers the entire scene. Hema deletes it and leaves the work light as it was.

SCENE 4. INT. REPAIR SHOP SET, EVENING

The crew watches the final grade. The workshop looks warm but the tools remain visible. The warning lamp is green, and Boro's face retains its ordinary color. Jori asks for one final pass to make the ending more hopeful.

HEMA: What changes?

BORO: The clock works.

Puck plays the scene. Boro turns the hands, listens to the ticking, and puts away the tools. The image needs no extra tint. Jori agrees to keep the practical colors. Hema exports the scene. Puck checks the file on a second monitor and confirms the lamp remains green. Boro turns it off, and the clock keeps ticking. Jori does not ask for another tint.

FINAL IMAGE: The last frame holds the green lamp, a readable clock face, and Boro's tea mug in its actual brown glaze. Jori asks for a still image for the program. Puck captures the ungraded frame, where the room and lamp match the actual set. Hema signs off on the export and closes the color suite.
