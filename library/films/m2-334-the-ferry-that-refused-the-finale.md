# The Ferry That Refused the Finale

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: maritime disaster films, countdowns, heroic engine-room speeches, and captains who must choose between cargo and passengers*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
CAPTAIN MARA, a capuchin ferry pilot
ELI, a macaque deckhand
SULA, a ring-tailed lemur mechanic
TOVI, an orangutan violinist
MAYOR KETT, a gibbon

SCENE 1. EXT. HARBOR - DUSK

A storm gathers over the bay as the ferry MINT LEAF prepares to leave. A warning siren blares. Mayor Kett waves from the pier, holding a ceremonial ribbon for the launch. Captain Mara studies a weather chart.

MARA: We can make the crossing before the squall.

SULA: The engine can make it. The ribbon cannot.

A gust tears the ribbon loose and wraps it around the ferry's propeller. The boat jerks, then stops. The siren continues to wail even after the weather clears for a moment.

SCENE 2. INT. ENGINE ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Sula shuts down the engine. Eli arrives carrying life jackets and a crate of ceremonial oranges. He asks if this is the part where the captain gives a speech. Mara sends him to check the passenger deck. Sula pulls a strand of ribbon from the propeller housing and finds the rest is tangled around an inspection cable.

SULA: We have one snag, not a sinking ship.

ELI: The siren disagrees.

Mara checks the alarm panel. The siren is connected to the mayor's ribbon-cutting switch. Kett had installed it for a dramatic launch and forgot to switch it back. Mara shuts off the siren, but a loud countdown starts on the dashboard: 10 minutes to high tide.

SCENE 3. INT. PASSENGER DECK - EVENING

Before the crossing is cleared, Mara checks every passenger's life vest. The first vest is fastened backward. The second is worn over a raincoat. Eli tries to demonstrate the buckle and accidentally locks himself into a row of seats. Tovi helps him out by playing a triumphant fanfare.

ELI: I was testing the seat restraint.

SULA: The seat passed.

The passengers are asked to remain onboard while the harbor crew checks the cable. One child asks if they will miss the festival. Mara says they can see the fireworks from the pier. The child says the fireworks are still in their box. Kett tries to light one as a signal, but the wind blows it out. He pockets it and offers to help coil the ribbon instead.

 INT. PASSENGER DECK - EVENING

Passengers remain calm, but the film's emergency lighting paints everyone red. Tovi plays a violin to distract a group of children. The melody becomes a heroic theme as Eli announces that the ferry is safely anchored. The emergency screen says 9 minutes, then 8, despite the ferry being stationary.

MARA: The countdown measures the tide, not our chances.

ELI: The screen is making it feel personal.

Kett boards in a raincoat and demands that the ferry depart for the ribbon ceremony. Mara explains the propeller is fouled. Kett suggests backing up with more power. Sula shows him the cable wrapped around the shaft. Kett tries to cut it with ceremonial scissors, which are too small to reach.

SCENE 4. EXT. FERRY DECK - NIGHT

The crew secures the ferry to the pier and uses a long boat hook to retrieve the ribbon. The tide lifts the stern. Eli braces a crate of oranges against the rail. One orange rolls away, and Tovi catches it with the scroll of his violin. The children cheer. Kett calls it a rescue.

Sula frees the cable, then checks the propeller by hand. It turns cleanly. The storm remains far offshore. Mara waits for the harbor master to approve departure. Kett grows impatient and starts the launch speech early, but the ferry is still tied to the dock. His words echo across the empty water.

MARA: We leave when the harbor is clear.

KETT: I am saying the part about courage.

MARA: You can say it from the pier.

SCENE 5. EXT. HARBOR - LATER

The harbor master signals safe passage. Mara starts the engine. The ferry glides from the dock; no one needs to leap across a gap or abandon cargo. The passengers applaud as Tovi plays a lively tune instead of the tragic theme. Kett cuts a second ribbon, this time on shore and well away from the propeller.

Eli asks whether the oranges will be served. Sula says they were cargo for the snack kiosk. He carries the crate below deck. Mara radios the pier to confirm their arrival time, and the dashboard calmly displays the expected crossing.

FINAL IMAGE: The ceremonial ribbon floats harmlessly behind the ferry, while an orange rolls to a stop against the neatly coiled line.
