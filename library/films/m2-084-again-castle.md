# Again, But with a Castle

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: nostalgia-driven reboots, shot-for-shot remakes, and sequels that mistake callbacks for new ideas*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A studio remakes a beloved bridge-rescue adventure, but the original crew keeps asking the production to repair the bridge instead of recreating the old poster.

Cast: Pera, a macaque producer; Obo, a gorilla stunt performer from the first film; Liri, a lemur set designer; and Fan, a capuchin archivist.

SCENE 1. EXT. RIVER BRIDGE SET, MORNING

Pera unveils a banner: THE BRIDGE AGAIN. Obo stands where he stood in the old film, holding a rope. Liri points out that the set bridge is missing two planks. Pera asks whether the gaps can be made larger for a more exciting callback. Liri says the bridge is used by crew walking to the camera platform.

FAN: The original scene had a safe bridge.

PERA: It photographed as dangerous.

Obo sets the rope down and asks to inspect the rail. Pera says they need the shot before lunch. Obo says they need the bridge before anyone crosses.

SCENE 2. EXT. BRIDGE, LATER

Liri replaces the missing planks and tightens the rail. Pera keeps moving the camera to match the old poster angle. Every time, the banner blocks the bridge. Fan compares stills from the original film and discovers the famous "broken rail" was a painted prop beside the set, not a real hazard.

Pera asks Obo to repeat his old stunt: swing over the river and land on the far bank. Obo checks the harness plan and finds the anchor point is not certified. He refuses the stunt until the rigging crew checks it. Pera says the audience expects the exact moment. Obo says the audience never sees a checklist, but he does.

Pera studies the old promotional still and asks Liri to age the set. Liri says she can weather the paint but cannot make loose planks safe. Obo points out that the original hero had a handrail in the scene, even though the poster cropped it away. Fan finds the full frame in the archive and displays it on a tablet. The rail was there all along. Pera tells the art crew to build it back into the shot.

The camera operator asks whether the stunt needs a second angle. Obo walks the route on the ground and marks where the landing mat should be. Pera says the old movie used a close-up and a cutaway. Fan points out that the close-up hid the performer using the bridge rail. Obo asks for a rehearsal with the rail visible. Pera agrees, but asks that the rehearsal be called a callback.

The rigging crew arrives and tests the anchor. It is safe only from a different position. Liri moves the landing mat and places a bright mark on the floor. Pera wants the mark hidden. Obo says it must remain visible to the performer. Fan suggests matching the old scene's emotion rather than its camera placement.

SCENE 3. INT. PRODUCTION TENT, AFTERNOON

Fan shows Pera the original film's call sheet. The celebrated bridge sequence took four takes because the first three were blocked by a windblown hat. Obo remembers the hat belonged to the director. Liri notices the new set has no windbreak. She asks the crew to secure the banner before it flies into the rig.

Pera proposes adding the old line about never crossing alone. Obo says he did not say that; the poster did. Fan checks the transcript. The line belongs to a different character in a later scene. Pera has conflated the poster, the film, and a toy package. He asks whether the new film can include the toy's catchphrase. Fan says the toy's catchphrase is not in the movie either.

Pera worries that viewers will not recognize the remake without a repeated phrase. Fan asks what the current scene is about. Liri says the crew is repairing a route used by the town. Obo says the new character is nervous about the height. Pera removes the borrowed catchphrase from the script and writes a new line about asking for a hand. Obo tries it during rehearsal; it sounds like something a person might actually say.

SCENE 4. EXT. BRIDGE, SUNSET

The bridge is repaired, the anchor inspected, and the banner tied down. Obo performs a shorter, safe crossing with the certified harness. The camera captures his focus, not a copy of the old pose. Pera asks for one more take that recreates the poster. Obo asks what the new character wants. Pera looks at the script and discovers the character is crossing to deliver a toolbox, not rescue anyone.

Liri places the toolbox at the far end. Obo walks across, delivers it, and returns to help secure the rail. The camera keeps rolling. Pera watches the footage and sees a complete scene without a callback. Fan writes "new story" on the slate.

The crew screens the take. The stunt is clear, the bridge is visible, and the toolbox arrives exactly where it is needed. Pera removes the oversized poster from the set and replaces it with the actual safety notice. Obo signs the harness log.

The editor asks whether to insert the famous poster pose before the crossing. Pera says to let the action explain the scene. Fan adds a note to the archive that the new film is not a restoration of the old one. Liri puts the retired poster in a sleeve so it can be displayed later with the production records. The crew heads back across the repaired bridge in a single orderly line.

FINAL IMAGE: The old poster is rolled into storage. Its painted broken bridge faces inward, while the repaired set bridge carries the toolbox crew home.
