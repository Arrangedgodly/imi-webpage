# Every Footnote Gets a Close-Up

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: literary adaptations that preserve every sentence and footnote even when the camera cannot show them*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A director adapting a short field manual tries to film every footnote until the camera spends more time on citations than on the story.

Cast: Sena, a lemur director; Oko, a gorilla actor; Miri, a macaque editor; and Puck, a capuchin script supervisor.

SCENE 1. INT. LIBRARY SET, MORNING

Oko sits at a desk holding a manual titled "How to Mend a Folding Chair." The main text has eight steps. The bottom of each page contains a footnote. Sena asks him to read the first step aloud.

OKO: Turn the chair over.

Puck looks at the script. The footnote says, "The author thanks the repair committee." Sena asks for a cutaway to the committee. Miri says the committee is not in the scene. Sena says the adaptation should preserve the book's texture. The crew moves the chair into a meeting room and seats six monkeys around it. One committee member asks why everyone is watching a chair. Sena explains that the book's thank-you is being adapted. The member says she only stapled the manual.

SENA: That deserves a close-up.

MIRI: Of the stapler?

SENA: Of the contribution.

Puck adjusts a lamp to illuminate the stapler. Oko waits with the chair upside down on his lap.

SCENE 2. INT. MEETING ROOM SET, LATER

Oko turns the chair over. Miri holds a close-up on the instruction. Puck points out that the next footnote references a 1972 safety bulletin. Sena asks for the bulletin. Puck finds a photocopy in the archive and lays it beside the chair.

SENA: Can we show the paper?

MIRI: We are making a film about the chair.

SENA: It is part of the source.

Oko reads the citation. The audience cannot see the small print. Puck magnifies it until the letters fill the screen. Sena asks if this is too long. Miri says the shot lasts twenty seconds and they have not yet reached step two. Puck turns the page and finds a correction to the bulletin citation. The original printing used the wrong month. Sena asks to film the correction card. Miri puts it beside the citation, where it covers part of the title.

MIRI: We have two documents and no chair.

Oko lifts the chair into frame. Puck zooms out enough to show all three.

SCENE 3. INT. LIBRARY SET, AFTERNOON

The crew films the second step: tighten the hinge. Oko picks up a screwdriver. Puck checks the footnote: "Use a tool of appropriate size." Sena asks for a close-up of three screwdrivers, followed by a diagram from the appendix. Miri adds the diagram. It is upside down.

OKO: The chair is ready.

SENA: We have not shown the appendix.

Oko tightens the hinge with the correct screwdriver. The chair opens and closes smoothly. Miri asks if the repair can be the end. Puck says the book has six more steps and four acknowledgments. Sena asks if they can compress them into a montage. Puck says the footnotes each have a source note.

Miri cuts between Oko's hands, the chair, and the relevant pages. The montage shows the work instead of reading every sentence. Sena objects that the committee's thank-you is missing. Puck points to the credits, where each committee member is listed. Sena asks if the list can scroll slowly. Miri says the film is already seven minutes longer than the manual's repair demonstration.

OKO: The chair is fixed.

SENA: We should respect the source.

MIRI: The source says to use an appropriate tool.

Oko holds up the screwdriver. The repaired chair supports all six committee members one at a time. Oko turns the chair toward camera and offers the seat to the committee members.

SCENE 4. INT. SCREENING ROOM, EVENING

The group watches the cut. It begins with the chair, shows the repair, and ends with someone sitting safely. The credits list the manual's author and the repair committee. Puck searches the final frame for a remaining footnote. He finds one in the lower margin of the end card.

PUCK: It says "For further reading."

MIRI: The audience is already reading.

Sena lets the card stay for three seconds, then fades to black. Puck asks if they should include the appendix. Miri hands him the actual manual. He opens it, sees it contains a phone number for replacement parts, and decides not to read it aloud. Oko folds the repaired chair and puts it away.

FINAL IMAGE: The manual rests on the chair seat. The screen shows a citation in the credits, small enough to be read without stopping the film.
