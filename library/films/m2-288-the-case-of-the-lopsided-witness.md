# The Case of the Lopsided Witness

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom thrillers, dramatic cross-examinations, surprise evidence, and witnesses whose confident memories change under simple questions*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A capuchin defense attorney must prove that a town's beloved clock was not stolen but temporarily moved for repairs, even as every witness remembers seeing it in a different place.

Cast
- ADVOCATE MIRA, a capuchin lawyer who arranges exhibits by size.
- JUDGE VENN, an orangutan who has little patience for speeches.
- WITNESS TUL, a macaque groundskeeper who saw the clock before dawn.
- WITNESS NERA, a gibbon baker who heard it ring.
- CLERK SAI, a ring-tailed lemur whose tail holds the evidence tags.
- PROSECUTOR BOK, a mandrill who relies on dramatic pauses.

## Scene 1: The Charge

INT. TOWN COURT - MORNING

A large empty plinth sits in the middle of the courtroom. The town's brass clock is missing. Mira represents the repair crew accused of stealing it.

BOK: The clock vanished. The repair crew had tools. Therefore, the crew took it.

MIRA: The clock was heavy. It did not vanish.

JUDGE VENN: Where is it?

MIRA: In the repair shop.

BOK: That is what they want us to believe.

Sai brings in a photograph showing the clock on a cart, but the image is blurry. The judge asks that the case continue until the clock is found.

## Scene 2: The First Witness

Tul testifies that he saw the clock in the square at dawn. Bok asks how certain he is. Tul says he saw the clock's shadow on the ground.

MIRA: Was the sun up?

TUL: No. It was dark.

MIRA: Then what did you see?

TUL: The shadow from the streetlamp.

MIRA: Did you see the clock?

TUL: I saw a shape on the plinth.

BOK: A clock-shaped shape.

TUL: It was the clock's shape.

Mira places a lantern beside the plinth. The shadow belongs to the lamp's brass finial, not the missing clock. The courtroom murmurs.

## Scene 3: The Baker's Ring

Nera says she heard the clock ring at six. Bok claims that proves it was still in the square. Mira asks whether Nera saw the clock.

NERA: I was in my bakery.

MIRA: Could it have been another bell?

NERA: It sounded exactly like the clock.

Mira asks the clerk to ring the court's small brass bell and then a baking timer. Both make similar notes. Nera admits she heard the bell but cannot identify its source.

MIRA: Did you hear the clock strike six, or did you hear a bell at the hour?

NERA: I heard a bell when my oven timer rang.

BOK: The oven timer rang at six?

NERA: I baked the sunrise rolls early. The clock rang in the square at a different time.

MIRA: So your memory combined two sounds.

NERA: Yes. I was certain because I expected the clock.

The judge asks the clerk to write down the testimony as a sequence rather than a conclusion. The baker points to the flour on her sleeve and admits she never left the shop.

BOK: The clock's voice was unmistakable.

JUDGE VENN: It sounded like the court bell.

BOK: The court bell has a similar voice.

## Scene 4: The Surprise Exhibit

Bok unveils a dramatic drawing of a cart leaving town at night. The cart has a tall outline under a blanket.

BOK: The accused moved the clock under cover.

MIRA: The drawing shows the cart going toward the repair shop.

BOK: It could have gone anywhere.

MIRA: The shop is the only road on the map.

Sai brings the original work order. It states the clock was removed to replace a bent bracket. The order is stamped by the judge's office because the square belongs to the court district.

JUDGE VENN: I approved this.

BOK: The stamp may be fake.

JUDGE VENN: It is my stamp.

MIRA: And your initials.

## Scene 5: The Clock Arrives

The courtroom door opens. Two repairers wheel in the clock. One side is slightly lopsided because a temporary brace is still attached. The clock rings once as the cart hits the threshold.

NERA: That is the sound I heard.

TUL: The shadow I saw was the empty plinth.

BOK: The clock is present, but we still need to know whether it was stolen.

MIRA: It was moved under an approved work order, repaired, and returned.

JUDGE VENN: Case dismissed. The clock's ownership is unchanged.

BOK: I request a recess to review the evidence.

JUDGE VENN: You may review it while helping put the clock back.

## Scene 6: The Return

The courtroom clears a path. Bok and Mira push the clock together, arguing about which side is heavier. Sai calls out each turn. The clock reaches its plinth, where the new bracket makes it stand straight.

MIRA: No surprise witness needed.

BOK: I had one ready.

JUDGE VENN: The clock has already testified.

## Final Image

The clock strikes the hour. Every witness looks at it, and for the first time, all of them can see the same thing.
