# A Shadow With Wet Shoes

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: noir detective narration, rain-soaked alleys, and hard-boiled moral certainty*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A hard-boiled gibbon detective hunts the thief who stole a museum's shadow, only to discover the culprit is a sunlamp on wheels and his own narration is ruining every stakeout.

Cast: Marlowe Marmoset, a weary gibbon private eye; Cora, a macaque night custodian with a pocket flashlight; Finch, a pigeon beat cop; Mr. Bristle, a hedgehog museum curator; and a portable sunlamp with a cracked switch.

SCENE 1. INT. MARLOWE'S OFFICE, NIGHT

Rain crawls down the window. MARLOWE sits under a desk lamp, telling the audience about the city's bad habits. A knock comes. CORA enters holding a photograph of a wall where a shadow should be.

CORA: The portrait is still there. The shadow isn't.

MARLOWE: Nothing disappears in this city without leaving a stain.

She shows him the wall. A pale rectangle remains around the portrait. Marlowe puts on his hat and walks into the doorframe. He blames the weather.

SCENE 2. INT. MUSEUM GALLERY, LATER

Marlowe inspects the portrait. His voice-over describes a cold trail. He speaks aloud while Cora takes notes.

MARLOWE: The shadow left in haste. It didn't even take its umbrella.

CORA: That's your coat rack.

Marlowe looks. His hat and coat hang on the stand, casting a large shadow across the wall. Cora moves the stand. The shadow shifts. Marlowe calls it a lead.

Curator BRISTLE explains that the gallery lights were moved for a school tour. He suspects a rival museum. Marlowe declares the case personal because the portrait's subject resembles his aunt.

SCENE 3. EXT. ALLEY BEHIND MUSEUM, NIGHT

Marlowe and Cora stake out a delivery door. Rain falls from a garden hose held by a stagehand who thinks this is a film shoot. Marlowe narrates the rain as if the clouds owe him money.

A figure emerges pushing a trolley. Marlowe leaps from behind a bin and points dramatically. The figure is Finch the pigeon, delivering a crate of night bulbs.

FINCH: You're standing in my puddle.

Cora finds a trail of wheel marks heading toward the service elevator. Marlowe insists on following the footprint of the shadow. He walks into a shallow puddle and leaves wet shoe prints in the wrong direction. Finch gives him a ticket for blocking the delivery lane.

SCENE 4. INT. MUSEUM BASEMENT, NIGHT

The elevator opens onto a storage room. The portable sunlamp sits atop a trolley. Its cracked switch flickers. Each flash throws a different object's shadow across the wall: a broom becomes a gallows, a hat becomes a lurking face, and Marlowe's long coat becomes a suspiciously elegant giraffe.

Cora examines the switch. The lamp has been rolling around since a wheel broke. The moving beam erased the portrait's shadow and painted strange shapes through the halls. No thief entered the building.

MARLOWE: So the culprit is a lamp.

CORA: The culprit is a lamp you ignored while interrogating a coat rack.

Marlowe says he knew that all along. The lamp flashes. His silhouette becomes a tiny rabbit.

SCENE 5. INT. GALLERY, DAWN

The curator wheels the lamp toward repairs. Marlowe writes the report: CASE CLOSED BY SHARP INSTINCT. Cora crosses it out and writes: LAMP ROLLED OFF ITS MARK. Finch delivers a citation for the hose-running stagehand, then another for Marlowe's wet shoes on the polished floor.

Marlowe begins a final voice-over about the lonely detective, but Cora turns off the office microphone. He continues speaking. No one hears him. He follows her out, still narrating to himself.

Marlowe dictates a correction to the report while Cora waits at the door.

MARLOWE: The lamp was a suspect of unusual brightness.

CORA: You mean it was on.

He crosses out the sentence. He adds that the lamp had motive, means, and a rolling wheel. Finch asks whether he is charging it with a crime. Marlowe says the city will decide. Finch points out the lamp has already been wheeled to repair and is not available for arraignment. Marlowe calls this an alibi.

Cora takes the report and files it under Equipment Maintenance, beside the lamp warranty. Marlowe insists the case belongs in Mysteries. The clerk moves it back. They repeat this twice, each time more ceremonially. At last Cora sets the report on top of the filing cabinet, where neither category can reach it.

Finch files the ticket under Marlowe, not under the lamp.

SCENE 6. INT. MARLOWE'S OFFICE, DAWN

MARLOWE: The case is closed.

CORA: By a lamp.

MARLOWE: By me.

FINCH: The lamp's repaired.

MARLOWE: Suspicious timing.

CORA: It was a fuse.

MARLOWE: A convenient fuse.

FINCH: Here's your ticket.

MARLOWE: For whom?

FINCH: Your shoes.

CORA: They're still wet.

MARLOWE: The rain followed me.

FINCH: The hose did.

MARLOWE: Your report says lamp.

CORA: It says pipe.

MARLOWE: Amend the report.

CORA: I did.

FINCH: It says you.

MARLOWE: That's excessive.

CORA: It's accurate.

MARLOWE: Accuracy is expensive.

FINCH: The ticket is five.

MARLOWE: I'll pay later.

CORA: You said that yesterday.

MARLOWE: Different case.

FINCH: Same shoes.

CORA: Same puddle.

MARLOWE: Case reopened.

FINCH: No.

CORA: Office closed.

MARLOWE: The city never sleeps.

FINCH: We do.

MARLOWE: I'll narrate quietly.

CORA: Please don't.

FINCH: Door's there.

MARLOWE: I see it.

He walks into the coat rack. The rack casts a long shadow. Marlowe tips his hat to it.

FINAL IMAGE: Sunlight hits the museum wall. Marlowe's shadow appears beside the portrait, wearing a tiny hat. Finch pins a parking ticket to it. The shadow cannot pay, so Finch leaves the ticket under the door.
