# The Crowdfunded Moon

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: independent films that pause their story to solicit donations and promise backers creative control*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A small crew tries to finish a silent film about a moon crossing the sky while its fundraising video keeps interrupting the shoot.

Cast: Vela, a macaque filmmaker; Toma, a lemur camera operator; Garo, a gorilla actor; and Puck, a capuchin fundraiser.

SCENE 1. EXT. ROOFTOP, NIGHT

Garo stands beneath a painted moon backdrop. Vela directs him to look up as the moon crosses the frame. Toma turns the crank on an old camera. Puck steps in holding a tablet.

PUCK: We are short on the lens rental.

VELA: The moon is moving.

PUCK: Could you ask viewers for support while it moves?

Garo holds his gaze upward. Vela whispers the donation amount. He lowers his eyes.

GARO: How much is the lens?

Toma says the rental is covered until midnight. Puck asks if viewers can vote on the ending. Vela says the film has no dialogue and one scene. Puck offers three possible endings: the moon disappears, the moon stays, or the moon is a lamp. Garo points to the painted backdrop. Puck turns the tablet toward him, showing a live fundraising counter. The number changes when Toma turns the camera crank.

TOMA: It is wired to the battery.

PUCK: Every donation gives us more runtime.

Vela checks the camera. The battery is not connected to the counter. Puck admits he made the graph animate to encourage pledges. The moon continues across the backdrop while they discuss it.

SCENE 2. EXT. ROOFTOP, LATER

The crew films again. Puck records a fundraising message beside the camera. He asks Vela to describe the artistic vision. She says the film is about watching a quiet thing move across a still sky.

PUCK: Can that fit into twenty seconds?

VELA: It took less than that to say.

Puck asks Garo to stand beside a sign listing reward tiers. The lowest tier offers a thank-you note. The highest offers a speaking role. Garo says the film is silent. Puck says the backer can appear holding the lamp.

Toma says the lamp is the moon prop. Puck moves it toward the frame. Toma's camera catches the lamp's cord running under the moon track. The cord stops the backdrop from moving. Garo reaches up to free it, but Vela calls cut before he touches the rig.

VELA: We cannot film a donation perk during the take.

PUCK: The backer wants proof the lamp exists.

TOMA: It exists in the equipment room.

Puck takes the lamp off the roof and photographs it beside the reward list. The painted moon now casts a shadow on the rooftop. Vela asks for the lamp to be moved back. Puck explains that a backer requested a visible reward.

SCENE 3. INT. COMMUNITY MEDIA ROOM, AFTERNOON

Vela screens a rough cut for the backers. The moon crosses the sky. Garo looks up. The shot ends. A donation graphic appears and stays up for thirty seconds. A backer asks if the moon can move slower so they can see their name.

VELA: The moon is on a track.

PUCK: The track can be adjusted.

Toma says the rental ends tonight. The crew has one more take. The backers vote to keep the moon moving at its current speed. They also vote that Garo should hold a thank-you card in the final frame. Vela asks if that is still the film's ending. Puck says it is community input. One backer raises a hand and says the moon should be blue. Another says the title should include their dog. The group votes. The title remains, and the moon remains white. The thank-you card is moved to the credits page.

PUCK: Everyone got a vote.

VELA: And the film kept its scene.

SCENE 4. EXT. ROOFTOP, SUNSET

The crew shoots the final take. The moon moves across the sky. Garo looks up. He does not hold a card. Puck begins to raise the donation graphic, then sees the camera still running and waits. Toma captures the whole movement. The crew watches the take on the small monitor.

PUCK: We need a final ask.

VELA: The film is finished.

GARO: We can thank them after the credits.

The backers applaud. Puck asks them to record short reactions for the campaign page. They say they liked the quiet ending. Vela asks if they can hear the take without music. Toma plays it. The room stays quiet until the moon reaches the edge of frame. Puck puts the thank-you note in the program page. Vela packs the camera before midnight.

FINAL IMAGE: The painted moon is rolled into storage. The donation thermometer remains at half, while the film itself reaches a full stop.
