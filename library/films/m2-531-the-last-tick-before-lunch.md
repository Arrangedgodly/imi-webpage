# The Last Tick Before Lunch

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: talkie-era slapstick homages that borrow silent-comedy machinery, intertitles, and escalating physical gags*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: NILO, a capuchin clockmaker; BARA, a baboon apprentice; ORA, a gibbon caretaker; MISTER BELL, an orangutan clock owner; OFFICER TICK, a macaque inspector.

INT. CLOCK SHOP - MORNING

A tower clock mechanism fills the shop. Its pendulum swings too far, knocking a row of tiny bells. Nilo opens the case. Bara holds a tray of screws.

MISTER BELL: It chimes at noon, then keeps chiming until dinner.

NILO: We will stop it before lunch.

BARA: It is eleven. We have one hour until the noon lunch bell.

The pendulum knocks a screw loose. It rolls across the floor and disappears beneath a cabinet. Nilo lifts the clock case. Bara reaches underneath and pulls out a spool of ribbon instead.

ORA: The clock is pulling its own parts loose.

OFFICER TICK: I am here to inspect the repair, not the ribbon.

[INTERTITLE: THE CLOCK OBJECTS.] The clock chimes once. Officer Tick checks a pocket watch.

TICK: It is eleven.

ACT ONE

INT. CLOCK TOWER - LATER

Nilo and Bara climb the spiral stairs with a tool box. Ora follows with the maintenance log. The clock's hands jump forward as the pendulum swings. A silent-film-style title card appears, then Mister Bell speaks over it: [TITLE: TIME IS MONEY.]

BARA: We need to stop the pendulum.

NILO: Hold the weight.

Bara grasps the counterweight. The pendulum stops, then the clock face spins backward.

ORA: The gears are still turning.

Nilo pulls the brake lever. It is tied to the ribbon spool. The ribbon unwinds down the stairwell.

OFFICER TICK: Do not pull the ribbon.

Bara lets go. The pendulum resumes and knocks the bell hammer aside. It chimes thirteen times.

MISTER BELL: Is the repair finished?

NILO: It is counting.

MISTER BELL: It is counting badly.

Nilo checks the gear train. A small acorn is wedged between two teeth. Ora recognizes it as a squirrel's winter stash from the tower ledge. Bara reaches toward the gear, but the clock ticks and catches the end of his sleeve.

BARA: I am part of the mechanism.

Nilo frees the sleeve with a small hook. Ora holds the pendulum at center. Officer Tick uses a ruler to measure its swing.

TICK: The pendulum is level. The clock is not.

They check the frame and find one foot on a loose tile. Nilo places a folded shim under it. The clock steadies, but the bell hammer is still sideways.

At 11:20, Nilo pulls the labeled brake lever and waits until the pendulum and all gears stop. He removes the bell hammer link and hangs it on a hook marked DISCONNECTED. Only then does the crew turn the gears by hand for a calibration test. The minute hand stops three marks early. Officer Tick checks the pocket watch against the tower face.

TICK: The clock has lost four minutes.

MISTER BELL: It has lost more than that. It has been wrong for a week.

Bara opens the maintenance log. The clock was adjusted yesterday, but the repair note says "move the pendulum one inch." Nilo spots a second note underneath: "do not move the pendulum." The two notes have been stapled together.

BARA: Which instruction did we follow?

ORA: The louder one.

The clock's hands still disagree. Nilo asks Bara to hold the counterweight while he adjusts the case. Bara steps on a loose floorboard; the board springs up and knocks a wrench into the stopped gear case.

NILO: Stop the pendulum.

BARA: I am standing on the floor.

The wrench catches between two cogs. Officer Tick holds the brake down while Nilo removes it with pliers. The gears remain stopped throughout. The hour hand still wobbles, so Ora tightens the case foot and steadies the dial.

MISTER BELL: Can it ring at noon or not?

NILO: It can ring. We are still fixing what it tells the rest of the town.

ACT TWO

At 11:40, the brake is still engaged and every gear has stopped. Nilo uses long tweezers to lift the acorn from between the teeth; Bara catches it in a cloth. Nilo checks the gear teeth, then hand-turns the train one full cycle. Nothing catches. Ora secures the case foot and resets the hour and minute hands against Officer Tick's pocket watch. The checks take nineteen minutes. The hands now agree at 11:59. Only after that calibration does Nilo reconnect the bell hammer link. He releases the brake and watches one quiet minute pass.

MISTER BELL: Will it ring at noon?

NILO: Once.

BARA: At the correct time.

The tower clock shows 11:59. Officer Tick watches both clocks. The shop's pocket watches tick in sync. The tower clock reaches noon and rings once. The crew keeps watching; no extra chime follows.

MISTER BELL: That is the sound I wanted.

Officer Tick checks the tower face against the pocket watch. They agree to the minute. Mister Bell asks if the lunch bell is next; Nilo points out that the tower only marks the hour. Bara hands him the ribbon spool and says the shop has a separate kitchen timer.

BARA: It rings when lunch is ready.

MISTER BELL: I have trusted the wrong clock.

The squirrel appears on the ledge and taps the glass. Bara offers it the acorn. The squirrel takes it and moves off. Officer Tick signs the repair log. The ribbon spool is hung away from the pendulum.

ORA: We finished before lunch.

NILO: The clock did not.

FINAL IMAGE: The second hand crosses noon without wobbling; outside, the squirrel carries its rescued acorn past the shop as the lunch door opens.
