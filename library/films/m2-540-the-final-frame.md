# The Final Frame

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: self-aware filmmaking dramas about a crew losing its last strip of film while the actors keep performing*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: DIRECTOR MOSS, an orangutan; CAMERA OPERATOR JUNE, a macaque; ACTOR VALE, a baboon; SCRIPT SUPERVISOR, a gibbon; RUNNER, a capuchin.

INT. SOUNDSTAGE - NIGHT

A painted forest fills the stage. Director Moss checks the camera. June opens the film can and finds one short strip remaining.

JUNE: Enough for one take.

MOSS: One final scene.

VALE: What is the scene?

SCRIPT SUPERVISOR: The hero leaves the forest through a door, and we hold on the empty stage.

VALE: We are inside a studio.

MOSS: The forest has a door. The studio has a door. The budget has one strip.

The runner props up a leaning tree with a sandbag. A lamp throws a white square across the painted scenery. June refuses to load the film until they know which door the hero will use.

ACT ONE

INT. SOUNDSTAGE - LATER

Moss calls one rough rehearsal without film. Vale crosses the painted forest, reaches the exit marked on the script, and finds a flat wall. The crew has not yet loaded or exposed any stock.

VALE: The path ends at a wall.

MOSS: The forest edge.

SCRIPT SUPERVISOR: The page says the hero walks through a gate, not into the geography.

The runner rotates the flat once. Behind it are folding chairs and a tea kettle, not a gate. June sets a stand-in camera at the chalk mark; the lens sees the wall and the bright lamp square.

JUNE: We have two problems. No usable exit, and the lamp burns the frame.

MOSS: Fix the path before the lighting.

The runner opens the actual stage door to the loading bay. Vale walks from the painted trees through the door and pauses on the threshold. The loading lamp spills directly toward the camera. June turns the reflector to send that light away from the lens. The runner closes the door, clamps the reflector securely, and places a sandbag at the tree base.

SCRIPT SUPERVISOR: Path, light, tree. One rehearsal remains.

Moss asks the crew to reset the forest once. Vale returns to the start mark through the stage door. June adjusts the framing so the door and the full threshold are visible. They perform the real rehearsal without film: Vale crosses, opens the door, steps completely into the loading bay, and clears the frame. June pans just enough to hold the empty doorway.

VALE: You can see me leave?

JUNE: I can see you leave. The audience can see the room you abandoned.

MOSS: That's our ending. Nobody improves it.

The runner crosses first with a white card at lens height. No glare blooms on it. He steps back through the door and gives Vale the exact mark with a strip of tape. The crew checks the actual door path, clamps, lamp, and sandbag. The script supervisor marks the rehearsal complete. June shows the camera gate to Moss; it is clean and ready. She threads the last strip only now. Moss insists every person say their mark aloud, as if the camera could hear confidence. Vale gives his, June gives hers, and the runner whispers his from behind the tree.

ACT TWO

INT. SOUNDSTAGE - CONTINUOUS

Everyone takes position for one recorded take. June starts the camera. Vale crosses the painted forest, passes the braced tree, and opens the real stage door. He steps through and disappears into the loading bay. June holds on the empty stage and open doorway until the film ends.

JUNE: The strip is spent.

MOSS: Did we get the exit?

JUNE: You saw it happen.

The exposed reel is sealed in its can and sent to the laboratory. The crew waits through the overnight processing; nobody projects undeveloped stock. Moss tries to hold a vote on whether the ending will survive development, but June refuses to count hands until the reel exists. The runner sleeps beside the delivery window with the receipt under his hat. The next afternoon, the runner returns from the laboratory with the developed reel. Moss gathers the same crew in the small screening room.

SCRIPT SUPERVISOR: One take, one developed reel, one chance to dislike it.

The projector runs. On screen, Vale crosses the forest, opens the door, exits fully, and leaves the empty stage framed beyond him. The crew watches the image hold, then fade naturally as the strip ends.

VALE: I left the forest.

JUNE: The frame proves it.

MOSS: And there is no second ending after the ending.

The runner starts to say something, then lets the credits roll. Moss leaves the screening room first. Vale follows him through its ordinary door; this time the camera is not running.

FINAL IMAGE: On the screen, the door stands open to an empty loading bay, with no hand left on the knob and no actor visible.
