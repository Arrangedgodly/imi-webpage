# One More Button

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: talent-show audition movies, sob-story packages, and judges' overproduced reaction shots*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A quiet orangutan percussionist enters a televised talent contest, where producers keep interrupting his act to manufacture a triumphant backstory.

Cast: Momo, an orangutan who plays rhythms on found objects; Zizi, a squirrel producer; Judge Flash, a peacock; Judge Reed, a tortoise musician; and Pip, a young macaque stagehand.

SCENE 1. INT. TALENT SHOW BACKSTAGE, NIGHT

Momo waits beside a rack of costume capes. His instrument is a set of ceramic bowls from the community kitchen. Producer Zizi asks for his emotional origin story. Momo explains that he learned rhythm by listening to rain on the roof. Zizi asks for a more tragic version. He says the roof leaked. She orders a close-up of his solemn face.

ZIZI: Can you look toward the light as if remembering?

MOMO: I remember the light bill.

A stagehand offers him a glitter jacket. Momo tries it on; the sleeves cover his hands. Zizi says vulnerability is powerful. Pip rolls the sleeves up and hands him his bowls.

SCENE 2. INT. AUDITION STAGE, CONTINUOUS

The host introduces Momo as a self-taught roof-rhythm survivor. Momo corrects him, but the music sting drowns him out. Judge Flash leans toward the camera and whispers that this could be the night. No performance has begun.

Momo taps one bowl, then another. He builds a bright syncopated pattern using the bowls, a spoon, and a wooden crate. Judge Reed listens carefully. Flash watches the producer's cue light, which changes from green to red whenever he must gasp. He gasps at a bowl wobble.

FLASH: That note changed my life.

REED: It was a bowl slipping.

SCENE 3. INT. CONTROL BOOTH, SAME

Zizi signals for the sad montage. The screen shows Momo standing by a roof gutter, footage filmed that afternoon. A producer asks him to hold a dripping bucket. He refuses because it is a prop from the kitchen and still contains somebody's lunch. The montage instead shows him returning the bucket to its owner.

Zizi cuts to a childhood photo. It is a picture of Momo's old practice bowls. The caption says, HE HAD NOTHING. Pip notices the bowls are clearly visible and covered in blue paint. He turns the caption card around; the other side reads HE HAD FOUR BOWLS.

Momo finishes the first section. The audience claps. Zizi signals for a standing ovation. The audience stands to let a camera dolly pass, then sits. Flash gives a thumbs-up to the dolly operator.

SCENE 4. INT. AUDITION STAGE, LATER

Momo begins again, this time inviting Pip to tap the crate. Their rhythms overlap and become richer. Judge Reed joins with a pencil against his score sheet. Flash tries to join but follows the cue light instead of the beat, clapping whenever it flashes. The lights form an accidental solo for him.

Momo stops. The silence makes Flash's last clap echo. He bows as if the audience applauded him. Reed explains that the best part was the shared rhythm. Zizi asks Momo to repeat the beginning with more hardship. Momo says he would rather play the ending.

MOMO: The roof still leaks. We put a bucket under it.

He plays the rain pattern, then changes it into a cheerful beat as Pip carries the bucket offstage. The audience joins with handclaps. It is the first time the applause follows the music.

SCENE 5. INT. BACKSTAGE HALL, NIGHT

Zizi follows them with a release form for the roof story. Momo asks if the station owns the sound of rain. Zizi says only the footage. Pip takes the form, finds the line about exclusive rights, and asks whether that includes the community kitchen bowls. Zizi says the bowls are not under contract. Momo signs only after Reed adds a note allowing him to perform without the show's logo.

Momo is offered a contract requiring a weekly personal revelation. He asks whether he can tell them about fixing the roof. Zizi considers this insufficiently dramatic. Judge Reed offers to help him record an album using bowls and household objects. Pip asks if the bowls can stay in the community kitchen. Momo agrees; the music can travel without taking the dishes.

Flash signs the contract upside down. Zizi calls it an exclusive. Reed turns it around and points to the signature line. Flash says the camera made it look right.

Momo gives the glitter jacket to Pip, who wears it as a stage curtain and accidentally earns a round of applause while carrying chairs.

FINAL IMAGE: Momo performs in the kitchen, bowls safely on the table. Rain taps the repaired roof. The audience claps along while Zizi, outside, tries to cue a tragic montage and accidentally conducts the beat.
