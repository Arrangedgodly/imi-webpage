# The Last Lane at Lily Pond

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: sports underdog films, grave locker-room speeches, training montages, and climactic matches decided by a tiny rule detail*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
COACH RHEA, a ring-tailed lemur
BIM, a young capuchin
TOL, a slow loris who keeps score
THE REEDS, an opposing team of macaques

SCENE 1. EXT. LILY POND CURLING GREEN - DAWN

A hand-painted sign reads "CITY CURLING FINALS." The curling stones are smooth river rocks. The target circle is a chalk ring around a water lily.

Bim arrives carrying a pebble. Coach Rhea measures it, shakes her head, and places it back in the pond. Bim is the smallest player on the team and the only one whose stone fits neatly in the scoring ring.

A local sports reporter asks Rhea why she chose him. She says he listens to the ice. There is no ice. The match is played on wet clay.

SCENE 2. INT. TEAM SHED - DAY

The team huddles around a chalkboard. Coach Rhea draws a map of the pond. Her arrows become so elaborate they wind around the board and return to the start. Bim studies the diagram, then points to a puddle draining toward the goal.

RHEA: We train for every angle.

BIM: The water is moving the rocks after they stop.

RHEA: That is not an angle. That is a betrayal by the surface.

A montage begins. The team pushes stones across wet clay. Rhea times them with a sundial. Bim watches dew drip from a leaf. The Reeds practice wearing matching warm-up jackets and posing toward a camera between throws. Bim practices releasing a stone, waiting, then checking where it settles after the clay dries.

The scorekeeper, Tol, paints "HUSTLE" on the team's water bottle. Bim adds a second label: "WAIT."

SCENE 3. EXT. COMMUNITY COURT - AFTERNOON

The Reeds arrive in coordinated uniforms, followed by a portable drumline. Coach Rhea asks for a practice lane. The Reeds claim both lanes to keep their jackets clean.

A dispute erupts: the rules allow one sweep of a stone after release. The Reeds sweep before the throw. Rhea protests. The referee rules that sweeping the air is not prohibited, though it has no effect on the stone.

Bim quietly checks the score marker. A loose peg makes the scoring ring slide downhill. He secures it with a forked twig, but a Reed player mistakes the twig for a lucky charm and borrows it. The ring shifts again.

SCENE 4. EXT. LILY POND CURLING GREEN - FINAL

The teams are tied. The last lane is slick with dew. The Reeds sweep their stones in a dramatic synchronized formation, shouting encouragement to each other. Their lead stone glides past the target, circles once in a puddle, and stops outside the ring.

Coach Rhea signals Bim to throw. He pauses. A drop of water travels down a reed leaf and lands beside the target. He waits for the surface to settle. The crowd grows impatient. A commentator whispers that Bim may be frozen by pressure.

Bim releases his rock. It moves slowly, tracking the shallow groove the runoff has made. It reaches the ring, pauses at the edge, then shifts half an inch as the last of the water drains. The scorekeeper bends low.

TOL: It is inside.

The Reeds challenge the call. The referee measures. The rock is inside by the width of a grass blade. The crowd gasps as though a whole stadium has held its breath.

The Reeds' captain points to the borrowed twig, which is still lying beside the ring. The referee checks the official line. The twig is not a marker; it is a twig. The score stands.

SCENE 5. EXT. VICTORY PLATFORM - SUNSET

The team receives a small silver bowl. Coach Rhea begins a speech about courage, sacrifice, and believing when the scoreboard has stopped believing. The speech lasts longer than the match.

Bim sets the bowl on the ground and uses it to catch the runoff. The Reeds' captain watches the water find a new groove. The next lane will be drier if they move the starting stones uphill.

RHEA: We won because he waited.

BIM: We won by one blade of grass.

TOL: The official measure is a blade. The team will remember it as a whole mountain.

RHEA: That is what sports films are for.

RHEA: That is what waiting looks like from the scoreboard.

The teams agree to share the court for practice. The Reeds remove their jackets. Tol updates the scoreboard: "WINNER: BIM. NEXT MATCH: WHEN THE POND DRIES."

FINAL IMAGE: A thin stream runs from the silver bowl and draws a new curling lane across the clay. Bim places a pebble at its starting point; the Reeds line up beside him, all wearing mismatched sleeves.
