# The Last Vote Before Dawn

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: political thrillers with a midnight vote, a missing amendment, and coalition partners arguing over the exact wording*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: SENATOR KELL, a baboon; AIDE RIN, a gibbon; CLERK, a macaque; MAYOR DARA, an orangutan; WHIP SOL, a capuchin; OPPOSITION LEADER, a mandrill.

INT. CITY COUNCIL CHAMBER - NIGHT

A clock reads 11:20 p.m. Council members sit beneath a banner: RIVER BRIDGE VOTE. A thick bill lies on the clerk's desk. One page is missing.

CLERK: The amendment is not in the packet.

WHIP SOL: It was there at dinner.

AIDE RIN: Someone used the page as a coaster.

The clerk lifts a coffee cup. A square dry patch marks the table. Mayor Dara looks at the bill.

DARA: Without the amendment, the bridge budget expires tonight.

OPPOSITION LEADER: We can vote on the original bill.

KELL: The original bill funds the bridge but closes the ferry.

DARA: The ferry is the only route while construction lasts.

Mayor Dara opens the floor for one minute of public comment. A ferry captain calls in on speakerphone, reporting that the last crossing is full. The clerk raises a hand and asks the council to hear the actual timetable before anyone votes.

FERRY CAPTAIN: We can carry the construction crew and residents if the route stays open.

KELL: How long does the crossing take?

FERRY CAPTAIN: Twelve minutes in daylight. Longer after dark.

The opposition leader says the bridge work cannot pause for the ferry. Dara reminds them that the public road is already closed at night. The clerk places the clock where everyone can see it and starts a one-minute timer for the final comments.

CLERK: When this runs out, we vote on the packet we have.

A council member raises a card asking for a postponement. Sol points to the midnight deadline on the bill. No one seconds the motion.

ACT ONE

INT. RECORDS ROOM - NIGHT

Rin and Kell search the chamber's records room. The clerk checks the printer. The amendment was printed at 9:14, but the printer queue shows two copies and one blank page.

RIN: There should be three pages.

KELL: The first copy went to the table.

RIN: The second went to the briefing folder.

The folder is empty. A smear of blue ink marks its cover. Kell follows the smear to the chamber's side door. The opposition leader stands there holding a blue pen.

KELL: Did you take the amendment?

OPPOSITION LEADER: I took the folder. It was left unattended.

RIN: The page is not inside it.

OPPOSITION LEADER: I did not remove it.

The clerk enters carrying a stack of maps. A corner of paper sticks out beneath the river map. The missing amendment is there, folded under the map's legend.

CLERK: Someone used it to cover a coffee ring.

MAYOR DARA: Is the wording readable?

The clerk unfolds the page. The ink is blurred along the bottom line. Rin reads the visible portion aloud: the ferry stays open during bridge construction, with a temporary route funded from the bridge reserve.

WHIP SOL: That is the compromise.

OPPOSITION LEADER: It also moves the vote from bridge reserve to general funds.

KELL: The amendment was changed.

Rin compares it with a saved draft on the council terminal. The two versions differ by one line. The draft keeps the ferry funded without drawing from the general fund.

ACT TWO

At 11:48, the council reconvenes. Kell places the recovered page beside the digital draft. The clerk confirms the digital timestamp predates the coffee ring. Mayor Dara asks both sides to read the line before voting.

DARA: We will vote on the wording everyone can see.

The opposition leader argues that the ferry route must remain open. Sol says the budget cannot absorb a second source of funds. Rin offers a revised sentence: the bridge reserve covers the ferry only until the new bridge opens.

CLERK: That is a new amendment. It needs a public read.

Kell reads the sentence aloud. The members hear it, ask one question about the schedule, then vote. The revised amendment passes by one vote. The original bill passes immediately after.

The clerk reads the result into the record and asks the members to sign the vote sheet. One chair is still empty. A council member arrives at the doorway holding a ferry timetable.

COUNCIL MEMBER: Is the ferry in the amendment?

CLERK: It is funded until the bridge opens.

The member reads the sentence, signs the sheet, and sits. The clerk checks the total one last time before closing the packet.

The clock strikes midnight. The clerk seals the final packet and gives the missing page to Rin for attachment. Mayor Dara calls the ferry operator to confirm service will continue during construction.

OPPOSITION LEADER: The bridge still needs to be built.

KELL: And the ferry still needs to run.

RIN: Both lines are in the signed copy.

The clerk adds a bright tab to the amendment so it cannot be used as a coaster again. The mayor leaves with the signed bill. Sol stacks the chairs.

FINAL IMAGE: The river bridge map lies beneath the sealed vote packet, its page corner marked with a bright orange tab.
