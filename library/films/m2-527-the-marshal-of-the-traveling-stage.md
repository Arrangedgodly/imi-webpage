# The Marshal of the Traveling Stage

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: westerns with a mistaken-identity showdown, a traveling troupe, and a sheriff who mistakes a prop for evidence*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: MARE, a macaque actor; CAL, a baboon stage manager; SEDGE, a gibbon sheriff; TOLL, an orangutan prop maker; FINCH, a capuchin pianist; RUSK, a mandrill rancher.

EXT. DUSTY TOWN STREET - DAY

A small theater wagon rolls into town. Painted scenery rattles behind it. Mare, in a paper marshal's badge, steps down and checks a list.

MARE: One stage, two flats, three chairs, and a fake wanted poster.

CAL: The poster is based on a real person.

Mare looks up. A real poster on the saloon wall shows a bandit named Rusk, wearing the same hat as Mare's costume.

MARE: That is awkward.

Sheriff Sedge approaches, hand near a holster. Mare lifts both hands.

SEDGE: You are under arrest for the Willow Creek robbery.

MARE: I am a traveling actor.

SEDGE: That is what the poster says.

Cal opens the wagon. A painted bank-front falls outward and blocks the street. Finch plays one startled piano chord.

ACT ONE

INT. TOWN HALL - DAY

Sedge questions Mare beside the wagon. Toll brings in the real costume hat and explains that it was copied from a photograph for the show.

TOLL: We use felt. The bandit used felt.

MARE: Most hats do.

Sedge compares the poster, the costume, and Mare's face. They resemble each other only from a distance. Rusk enters wearing the same hat and sees Mare's badge.

RUSK: Why is my face on your poster?

CAL: It is a playbill.

RUSK: It says WANTED.

TOLL: The print shop used the wrong template.

Sedge holds up the poster. The line below Rusk's picture reads WANTED FOR A MATINEE.

SEDGE: That is not a crime.

RUSK: It is if you are trying to nap.

Sedge pins the poster to the town board and asks Mare to stand beside it. Mare's costume hat sits low, shading the eyes. In the poster, Rusk's hat has the same crease, but his coat is dark and Mare's is striped.

SEDGE: The outline is similar. The clothes are not.

MARE: The play has me wearing a marshal badge.

RUSK: The real marshal does not have a paper badge.

Sedge removes the badge and checks its back. It reads PROPERTY OF TOLL'S WAGON. The prop maker takes it and stores it in a labeled box. The sheriff asks Rusk where the saddle went. Rusk points down the street, where his horse stands beside a feed cart wearing the saddle.

RUSK: I borrowed it back from the stable after the horse walked off with it.

SEDGE: That is not a robbery.

CAL: It is now the first scene of the show.

RUSK: It is not.

The pianist tests a comic chord. Rusk gives the pianist a look. Finch stops playing. Sedge tells the troupe to keep the horse outside the hall and the poster away from the real notices.

Rusk asks the troupe to remove the poster. Cal agrees but says the town has already bought tickets. Sedge allows the performance if Rusk stays in the audience and the fake pistols remain unloaded.

EXT. TOWN SQUARE - EVENING

The stage is set. Rusk sits in the front row. Sedge stands beside the wagon. Mare plays the marshal in a scene where the villain robs a paper bank. The paper door sticks. Mare pulls it, and the whole flat begins to tip.

RUSK: The bank is falling!

Toll catches one side. Rusk catches the other. The audience thinks it is part of the play and applauds.

CAL: Hold the wall until the scene ends.

RUSK: You have made me stage crew.

A gust blows the wanted poster across the square. It lands over Sedge's badge. The sheriff reaches for it. Rusk catches the paper before it reaches a lantern.

SEDGE: You just helped us.

RUSK: I was saving my face from burning.

ACT TWO

The show pauses. Cal removes the poster from the lantern and tears away the false WANTED heading. Mare apologizes to Rusk in front of the crowd. Rusk points out that the play made him look like a thief, then admits the falling bank was a convincing stunt.

RUSK: If you print a new poster, spell my name right.

TOLL: We can put your name on the back.

RUSK: I want the front.

Sedge offers a compromise: Rusk can appear in the final scene as the person who stops the bandit. Rusk agrees only if the role has no lines.

The troupe restarts the performance. Mare's paper marshal arrests a cardboard bandit. Rusk steps from the audience and catches the bank flat before it falls. The crowd cheers. After the show, Sedge removes the arrest notice from Mare's coat.

SEDGE: You can leave town.

MARE: We were leaving after the show.

Sedge tears the paper badge from the costume and hands it back. Mare pins it to the stage wagon as a prop, not a credential.

FINAL IMAGE: Rusk watches the theater wagon roll away, wearing the corrected wanted poster as a sunshade.
