# The Crown Is a Coconut

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: historical costume dramas and succession plots when the royal crown is actually a shared shell used for carrying fruit*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-006*

Logline: Two heirs battle for a crown before coronation, until the palace attendant reveals that the object is a coconut bowl from the kitchen.

Cast: Heir Miri, a gibbon; heir Varo, a baboon; attendant Seli, a macaque; and royal chronicler Pavi, a capuchin.

SCENE 1. INT. PALACE HALL, DAWN

A coconut shell sits on a velvet cushion. Miri and Varo stand on opposite sides.

MIRI: By birth, the crown is mine.

VARO: By strength, it should be mine.

Seli enters carrying a tray.

SELI: Who left the fruit bowl here?

The heirs stare at her.

SCENE 2. INT. PALACE LIBRARY, MORNING

Pavi unrolls a family scroll.

PAVI: The crown has passed through seven generations.

Seli turns the scroll over. The back is a kitchen list.

SELI: It says "coconut bowl, return after breakfast."

MIRI: That is a code.

VARO: A royal code.

PAVI: The word "breakfast" appears six times.

SCENE 3. INT. PALACE GARDEN, NOON

The heirs practice their coronation speeches. Miri places the shell on her head. It slips over her eyes.

MIRI: The kingdom is mine.

VARO: You cannot see the kingdom.

Varo tries the shell. It falls over one ear.

VARO: The kingdom is out of balance.

Seli sets fruit into the shell. It holds the fruit.

SELI: It is a bowl.

Pavi writes down the demonstration.

SCENE 4. INT. PALACE HALL, AFTERNOON

A crowd assembles. Pavi announces the coronation.

PAVI: The crown will be given to the rightful heir.

Miri and Varo step forward. Seli takes the shell and carries it toward the kitchen.

MIRI: Where are you going?

SELI: Breakfast is over.

VARO: The ceremony is not.

Seli sets the bowl on a shelf.

SCENE 4A. INT. PALACE ARCHIVE, AFTERNOON

Pavi retrieves an older record from the shelf. Seli reads the label.

SELI: "Coconut bowl, feast service."

Miri and Varo lean over the page.

MIRI: It was never a crown.

PAVI: The court portraits show it above the table.

VARO: The portrait painter may have painted it as a crown.

Seli turns the record. It lists fruit, water, and feast days.

SELI: It was used to serve the whole court.

Miri picks up the shell. It is too wide for her head and too shallow to hold a royal seal.

MIRI: It was made for sharing.

Varo looks at the coronation schedule.

VARO: We can cancel the title ceremony.

Pavi holds up the quill.

PAVI: The announcement is already written.

Seli takes the quill and crosses out "crown." She writes "feast bowl."

SELI: This is what the record says.

The heirs take the shell back to the hall together.

SCENE 4B. INT. PALACE ARCHIVE, CONTINUOUS

Pavi looks through the old portraits. In each one, the coconut shell holds fruit at the edge of the banquet table.

PAVI: The painter placed it near the heirs.

MIRI: So the feast could be shared.

VARO: The portraits were not showing a crown.

Seli compares the archive record with the coronation program.

SELI: We have written the wrong title in every copy.

Pavi takes the program and crosses out "coronation."

PAVI: The ceremony will be a feast.

MIRI: That is more useful.

Varo carries the shell to the kitchen. Seli gathers the corrected programs.

SCENE 5. INT. KITCHEN, CONTINUOUS

The heirs follow. The shell is beside a stack of fruit.

MIRI: The crown belongs to the palace.

SELI: The kitchen is part of the palace.

VARO: Who will rule?

Seli points to a large mirror. Miri and Varo see themselves arguing over the bowl.

PAVI: The history will record a dispute.

MIRI: It should record a decision.

VARO: The bowl can be shared.

Before the feast, Seli retrieves the old kitchen list from the library. The heirs use its grease marks to trace how the shell passed from cook to attendant, not monarch to monarch. Varo tries to make the list sound ceremonial. Miri reads the last instruction aloud: wash, dry, return. Seli takes the bowl to the sink.

SCENE 6. INT. PALACE HALL, SUNSET

The siblings place fruit in the coconut shell and offer it to the crowd.

MIRI: We will rule together.

VARO: We will share the bowl.

Pavi records the proclamation. Seli picks up the shell to return it to the kitchen.

PAVI: The crown is leaving.

SELI: The fruit is finished.

A small monkey takes the shell and wears it on its head. Everyone bows.

VARO: The new heir has arrived.

Seli removes the shell and gives the monkey a fig.

SELI: It is still the kitchen bowl.

The monkey carries the fig away. The siblings follow to help with the feast.
