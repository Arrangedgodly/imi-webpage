# The Planetarium's Last Show

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: science-fiction time-loop adventures with a countdown, a repeated mission briefing, and a practical reset*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: ORA, a gibbon planetarium operator; KEV, a baboon presenter; MICA, a macaque student; LEX, an orangutan technician; DR. NOX, a mandrill astronomer.

INT. PLANETARIUM - NIGHT

The dome fills with stars. A countdown reads 00:07:00 until the building's scheduled power test. Ora stands at the projector. Kev has a pointer shaped like a rocket.

KEV: Tonight we travel beyond the last visible star.

MICA: We are in the city planetarium.

KEV: The dome is convincing.

A star blinks twice, then returns to its earlier position. Dr. Nox checks the console.

DR. NOX: The show has jumped to the opening sequence.

LEX: The projector loop is repeating.

ORA: We have seven minutes before the power test. Can we finish the show once?

KEV: We have done the opening already.

MICA: We have heard it twice.

ACT ONE

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - CONTINUOUS

Lex checks the reel. The paper cue strip has a torn corner. Dr. Nox holds it flat while Ora advances the projector manually. The star field moves forward, then snaps back.

LEX: The cue strip is snagging on the guide.

KEV: That is the time loop.

DR. NOX: It is a paper jam.

Mica finds a second cue strip under the console. It is labeled FINAL SHOW. Kev says the previous operator left it there. Ora places it beside the first strip.

ORA: We need to transfer the final sequence without stopping the projector.

LEX: If we rush it, we will skip the planet reveal.

DR. NOX: We are already on the same three stars.

The countdown shows four minutes. The automatic system restarts the opening. The audience recognizes the same three stars and begins to murmur. Kev freezes with the pointer raised. Mica calls the next line before it appears on the dome.

KEV: We have seen this star before.

MICA: You said it was the last visible star.

KEV: There are more stars behind it.

The projector skips to Saturn, then snaps back to the opening. Someone in the back row laughs. Ora stops the show and asks everyone to remain seated while the crew checks the mechanism. The audience lights come up. A family in the front row passes around the program, comparing the repeated pages.

LEX: The strip is snagging on the guide, but the projector is also reading the splice twice.

ORA: So it repeats the cue and then jumps backward.

DR. NOX: The universe is not looping. Our reel is.

Mica stands and points to a shadow that remains in one spot on the dome while the stars move. The shadow is cast by a loose metal clip near the lens.

MICA: That star has a roof.

Lex turns off the lamp and removes the clip. The shadow disappears. The countdown continues, and the team must finish before the building's automatic test.

Lex checks the projector gate. A small piece of the old cue strip is folded into the wheel. He removes it with tweezers. The star field advances.

ACT TWO

The team loads the final strip. Ora starts the sequence from the moon scene. Kev points the rocket pointer toward Saturn. Dr. Nox narrates the rings while Mica catches the projector's tempo and signals when to advance.

DR. NOX: Saturn's rings are wide, thin, and held in place by gravity.

KEV: This is the part we missed on the loop.

MICA: We saw it once. The projector did not.

The house countdown drops to two minutes. The audience can see it on a small status panel by the exit. Kev trims the next speech to one sentence, but Dr. Nox insists that the planet's rings still need explanation.

DR. NOX: If I skip the rings, the show jumps from moon to Earth with no scale.

KEV: If you explain every ring, the building shuts down before Earth.

ORA: Give the audience one fact and point to the picture.

Dr. Nox turns toward the dome and holds the model ring up to the light. The projector casts a shadow that makes the ring look like a second Saturn. The audience laughs. He rotates it until the shadow lines up with the planet.

DR. NOX: The rings are thin enough to vanish edge-on.

MICA: There. One fact.

The audience follows the model as Kev points the rocket at the planet. Lex keeps one hand on the cue strip and the other on the power-test switch. The stars advance without returning to the opening.

The countdown reaches 00:01:00. Lex watches the power display while Ora continues the show. The final strip reaches its end. The dome shows the moon rising over Earth.

ORA: Full sequence complete.

[The automatic power test starts. The projector shuts off. The dome goes dark, then the emergency lights appear.]

KEV: The universe has ended on schedule.

DR. NOX: The building has performed a test.

LEX: The projector is safe. We can restart it after inspection.

The audience applauds. Ora thanks them for waiting. Mica collects the loose cue strip and puts it in a labeled envelope so the loop does not happen again.

FINAL IMAGE: The dome holds a single emergency-light star while the crew packs the repaired reel.
