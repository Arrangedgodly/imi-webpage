# Showdown at Dry Creek

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: western showdown conventions, standoffs at high noon, and frontier bravado*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: Two rival monkey ranchers prepare for a classic high-noon duel over a creek that has vanished, then discover their dramatic standoff is blocking the only road to the water tank.

Cast: Deputy Fern, a stern owl; Calico, a capuchin rancher with a tiny hat; Boone, a baboon rancher who practices his draw in a mirror; Ada, a tortoise water engineer; and a rooster who announces noon too early.

SCENE 1. EXT. DRY CREEK, MORNING

Calico stands on one side of a dry creek bed. Boone stands on the other. Each wears a polished belt buckle. The rooster crows. Calico checks his watch.

CALICO: It's not noon.

BOONE: The rooster says otherwise.

The rooster crows again, then admits he is practicing. Deputy Fern arrives and tells them to put away their toy pistols. They are squirt guns. The duel is postponed until the water dispute is settled.

SCENE 2. INT. GENERAL STORE, MIDDAY

The ranchers argue over who owns the creek. Ada unrolls a map. The creek changed course after a storm. The old channel is dry, but a new channel runs behind the store. Boone says the map is upside down. Ada rotates it. Boone says it is still suspicious.

Calico buys a length of hose. Boone buys two buckets. They both claim the store has sold them the last of the supplies. The clerk points to a barrel outside full of spare hose.

SCENE 2A. EXT. DRY CREEK, LATER

Calico practices drawing the squirt gun from its holster. The holster catches on his belt. He spins halfway around and points the gun at his own boot. Boone practices too. His toy pistol fires a weak stream that lands at his feet.

A young squirrel watches and asks whether this is the famous showdown. Deputy Fern says it is a rehearsal. The squirrel asks if the winner gets the creek. Fern says the winner gets to help dig the trench. Both ranchers immediately lower their guns.

SCENE 2B. INT. SHERIFF'S OFFICE, AFTERNOON

Fern draws a map of the water route on the wall. Calico and Boone each point to the same place and claim it is their land. Fern asks them to name the boundary. They describe a fence, a rock, and a tree. The creek has moved past all three.

Ada explains that water follows a slope, not a deed. Boone suggests changing the slope. Ada asks if he plans to move the hill. He considers this, then says he knows a cousin with a shovel. Fern tells him the county already owns shovels.

SCENE 2C. EXT. WATER TANK, DUSK

The tank leaks at the seam. The ranchers try to patch it with chewing gum, a saddle blanket, and the town's ceremonial ribbon. None works. Ada uses a clamp and a strip of rubber. Calico holds the tank steady; Boone tightens the bolt.

The tank stops leaking. Both ranchers claim credit. Ada points to the water schedule, which they still have not signed. They each try to sign first, but both pens are dry. Fern hands them one working pencil. They sign together by holding it between them.

SCENE 3. EXT. MAIN STREET, AFTERNOON

The ranchers face off again at high noon. The rooster crows at 11:40. Deputy Fern checks the clock and gives it a stern look. The two ranchers reach for their squirt guns. Ada drags a water tank down the road between them.

The tank catches on a signpost. The hose unspools and wraps around both ranchers. They take one step apart and pull the tank over. Water sprays upward, soaking their hats. Their toy pistols squirt in opposite directions, hitting the rooster.

SCENE 4. EXT. NEW CREEK BED, SUNSET

Ada leads everyone to the new channel. The ranchers build a small diversion trench together. Boone shovels too quickly and fills Calico's boots. Calico retaliates with a careful splash. Deputy Fern stops them, then hands each a shovel.

They finish the trench and direct water toward both ranches. The creek is narrow but flowing. The rooster announces sunset correctly, then asks for a raise.

SCENE 5. INT. GENERAL STORE, EVENING

The ranchers sign a shared water schedule. Boone insists on a dramatic handshake. Calico offers a normal handshake. They compromise with a slow-motion handshake that lasts until Deputy Fern clears her throat.

SCENE 6. EXT. DRY CREEK, NOON

CALICO: Is it noon?

BOONE: The rooster says so.

FERN: The clock says eleven.

ADA: The sun is behind clouds.

CALICO: We can wait.

BOONE: We have waited.

FERN: You arrived early.

BOONE: For dramatic tension.

ADA: The water is flowing.

CALICO: My side too.

BOONE: Mine as well.

FERN: Then what remains?

CALICO: The duel.

ADA: Why?

BOONE: Tradition.

FERN: Who started it?

BOONE: My grandfather.

CALICO: His grandfather lost.

BOONE: That's disputed.

ADA: By whom?

BOONE: My grandfather.

FERN: Put the toys away.

CALICO: They're squirt guns.

FERN: Still toys.

BOONE: One shot?

ADA: At the creek?

BOONE: A ceremonial splash.

FERN: No.

CALICO: We'll splash the buckets.

ADA: Use the water schedule.

BOONE: That's less dramatic.

FERN: It works.

CALICO: We can dramatize it.

BOONE: A bucket at dawn.

ADA: After inspection.

FERN: And not at the rooster.

The rooster crows. Boone puts his squirt gun away and checks the calendar.

CALICO: That was the duel.
BOONE: We both won.
FERN: The creek won.

FINAL IMAGE: The rooster stands beside the creek, wearing a tiny badge. It crows at noon the next day, on time. The ranchers look at their watches, surprised. The rooster points to a calendar. It has been practicing all week.
