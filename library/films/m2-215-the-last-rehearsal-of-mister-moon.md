# The Last Rehearsal of Mister Moon

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: backstage musical dramas, demanding choreographers, show-must-go-on speeches, and a mascot who refuses the final cue*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
FIFI, a lemur dancer
MISTER MOON, a capuchin in a crescent costume
REX, a gibbon choreographer
PALLA, a macaque stage manager

SCENE 1. INT. MUNICIPAL THEATER - AFTERNOON

The troupe rehearses a musical about the changing seasons. Mister Moon, a capuchin in a padded crescent costume, stands on a rolling platform. Rex claps for attention.

REX: At the final cue, the moon rises, smiles, and exits stage left.

MISTER MOON: I cannot see stage left.

PALLA: The crescent blocks your left eye.

REX: Then find it with your heart.

Fifi marks the choreography with chalk. Mister Moon slowly rotates, mistaking the orchestra pit for the exit. Palla catches the platform before it rolls down the steps. Rex writes "more moon confidence" on his score.

SCENE 2. INT. COSTUME ROOM - LATER

The crew tries to add a mirror inside the costume. It fogs immediately. They replace it with a small bell tied to the left sleeve. Mister Moon shakes his arm and hears the cue.

FIFI: When you hear one ring, face the audience. Two rings, leave.

MISTER MOON: What if the bell rings twice while I'm facing away?

PALLA: Then you'll hear it twice.

Rex demonstrates the final bow with a flourish. The moon costume's lower point catches the curtain and carries it across the room. The ensemble applauds because the curtain move looks intentional. Palla marks it in the choreography, then crosses it out when the curtain rod starts to bend.

SCENE 3. INT. STAGE - EVENING

The first rehearsal with lights begins. The moon rises on its platform, turns at the one-ring cue, then exits at the two-ring cue. The curtain follows it again. Rex calls a halt.

REX: The curtain cannot be the star.

FIFI: It got the largest reaction.

MISTER MOON: I can try lifting the point.

He raises the costume's lower point, but the fabric catches the scenery instead. A painted mountain rolls after him. Palla stops it with a sandbag. The ensemble has to hold a mountain-shaped pose while the crew untangles the moon.

SCENE 4. INT. BACKSTAGE - OPENING NIGHT

The moon costume's wheels jam. Palla checks beneath the platform and finds a strand of silver tinsel wrapped around the axle. The orchestra is already playing the overture. Fifi improvises a duet with the stuck moon while Rex signals the crew.

FIFI: The moon seems low tonight.

MISTER MOON: It is a grounded moon.

The audience laughs. Rex lets the line remain and moves the other dancers into a circle around the platform. They perform the season changes in place while Palla frees the wheels. The crescent can now move, but the two-ring bell falls off.

SCENE 5. INT. STAGE - FINALE

Fifi gives one ring by tapping a triangle. Mister Moon faces the audience. The crowd applauds. Rex signals the exit, but the missing bell means the moon hears nothing. Mister Moon stays for an extra bow. The ensemble starts the exit choreography around him, circling faster and faster.

PALLA: We need the moon offstage.

MISTER MOON: I can see the exit now.

He steps out of the crescent. The costume rolls away by itself and exits on cue. The audience cheers for the moon's departure, then cheers again when the capuchin takes a bow. Rex tries to restart the finale, but Fifi holds up the fallen bell and the orchestra gives one last note.

REX: The costume has better timing than we do.

SCENE 6. INT. BACKSTAGE - AFTER THE SHOW

The troupe gathers around the moon costume. Palla finds a note inside the crescent: "Please put me near the window after curtain call." Mister Moon explains that he had been trying to ask for a view of the real moon, but the padded head covered his face.

FIFI: You could have told us.

MISTER MOON: I did. I said I couldn't see.

REX: I thought that was a metaphor.

Palla opens the loading door. The ensemble carries the costume outside and props it safely beside the window. The capuchin takes a deep breath and looks up.

PALLA: Next season, shall we build the moon with a window?

MISTER MOON: Build it with two. I would like to see both the sky and the stage.

REX: We could make that the theme of the show.

FIFI: Or just a window.

Rex adds the note to his score, then circles it several times. Palla removes the circle before he can turn the window into a choreographed finale. The company shares the last of the opening-night oranges while the costume cools by the glass.

FINAL IMAGE: The crescent costume stands beside the theater window, its bell on the sill, while Mister Moon walks home under the real moon, checking over his shoulder for a cue.
