# The Snow Day That Took a Week

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: family holiday movies, surprise reunions, town-wide snow-day montages, and a climax saved by cooperative chores*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
MAYA, a ring-tailed lemur school principal
PIP, a capuchin student
GRAN ETTI, a macaque baker
MR. VALE, a gibbon bus driver

SCENE 1. EXT. KETTLE CITY SCHOOL - MORNING

Snow covers the playground. The school announces a snow day. Students cheer and race outside. Principal Maya tries to lower the flag but the rope is frozen. Pip arrives with a sled made from a cafeteria tray.

PIP: The tray slides better than the old sled.

MAYA: It is not a sled.

Pip demonstrates. He glides to the bottom of the hill, stops safely in a pile of snow, and returns the tray to the cafeteria.

SCENE 2. INT. TOWN BAKERY - DAY

Gran Etti bakes buns for the snow-day crowd. The bus driver, Mr. Vale, arrives with a delivery of flour. His bus is stuck at the curb. The town's only snowplow is at the far end of the road, blocked by a fallen branch.

MAYA: We'll need to clear the road before the buses can run again.

PIP: We can move the branch together.

The town gathers with shovels. A montage shows neighbors clearing sidewalks, pushing the bus, warming mittens on bakery ovens, and making a snow sculpture shaped like the school mascot.

SCENE 2A. INT. BAKERY - AFTERNOON

The power flickers. Gran Etti moves the buns away from the window. The school's snow-day announcement arrives by radio, but the bus route to the far neighborhood is still open.

VALE: I can make one more trip before the road closes.

MAYA: The branch is blocking the north route. We shouldn't send the bus there.

PIP: I know the footpath. We can guide the bus to the south stop.

Maya hesitates. The map is covered in snow and the usual markers are hidden. Pip takes the cafeteria tray and uses it to scrape a narrow line to the safer path. Vale follows slowly, stopping whenever Pip checks the road.

MAYA: No one rides on the tray.

PIP: I'm using it as a shovel.

They reach the south stop and bring three neighbors back to the school shelter. Vale rings the bus bell when they arrive, and the bakery workers answer with a tray tap.

SCENE 3. EXT. TOWN SQUARE - EVENING

The branch is too heavy to lift. Mr. Vale wraps a tow rope around it. Maya warns that the rope could snap. Gran Etti brings a tray of buns and a thermos of cocoa. Everyone stops to eat, then notices the snow is still falling.

PIP: We should break the branch into smaller pieces.

The group finds a hand saw in the school workshop. They cut the branch into manageable sections and stack them along the curb. The bus moves free.

SCENE 3A. INT. TOWN BAKERY - NIGHT

The bakery oven is running on a backup heater. Gran Etti teaches Pip to knead dough while the town waits for the bus route to reopen. Pip presses too hard, and the dough springs back over the table edge.

ETTI: Dough needs a little rest. You cannot rush every part of winter.

PIP: The snow is still falling.

ETTI: Then we have time to make another batch.

Maya arrives with a list of neighbors who need blankets. Instead of assigning each person a name, she lets families choose the blankets they can carry. Vale takes a basket of rolls to the school shelter and returns with an empty tray.

MAYA: We are becoming very good at passing things around.

PIP: The tray has become a town vehicle.

SCENE 4. INT. SCHOOL GYM - NEXT MORNING

The storm continues. The school becomes a shelter for neighbors. Students set up blankets, the bakery brings bread, and the bus driver ferries people from the far road. Maya organizes a warm room and a dry room, then discovers the gym's roof is leaking.

The town uses the snow-day sled tray to carry buckets beneath the drip. Pip guides each tray to the right spot. The leak stops when the snow eases.

SCENE 5. EXT. TOWN SQUARE - ONE WEEK LATER

The snow melts. The school reopens. The town gathers for a delayed winter festival. The snow sculpture has slumped into a lopsided shape that resembles a bus carrying a bread loaf.

MAYA: It was meant to be the school mascot.

PIP: It changed during the thaw.

Gran Etti hands everyone a bun. Mr. Vale rings the bus bell. The students line up to ride to school, but Pip keeps the tray sled for the hill.

MAYA: We are not using the cafeteria tray on school property.

PIP: It's on the hill, not in the cafeteria.

Maya almost scolds him, then notices the tray is clean and the hill is marked safe. She lets him take one more run before class.

FINAL IMAGE: The bus pulls away through the wet streets. The tray sled rests by the school steps, polished from use, while the town's bent snow sculpture melts into the shape of a friendly wave.
