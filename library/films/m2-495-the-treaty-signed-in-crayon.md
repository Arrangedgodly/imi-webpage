# The Treaty Signed in Crayon

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: political thrillers where a ceremonial document becomes the entire fate of diplomacy*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-017*

Logline: Two neighboring monkey kingdoms meet to sign a border treaty, but the only pen available is a crayon borrowed from the children's waiting room.

Cast: envoy Pera, a capuchin; minister Kalo, a macaque; archivist Numa, an orangutan; interpreter Sivi, a lemur; guard Tolo, a gibbon.

SCENE 1 - INT. COUNCIL CHAMBER - DAY

A long table holds two copies of a border agreement. Pera and Kalo sit opposite each other. Numa lays out a fountain pen, but it has dried out. Sivi checks the wording. Tolo guards the door.

PERA: The map has one line between our orchards.

KALO: The line follows the stream.

SIVI: The stream moved last spring.

The ministers argue over whether the boundary follows the old streambed or the current water. Numa asks them to wait while she finds a working pen. A ceremonial bell rings outside. The meeting's photography team enters and asks everyone to hold their signing pose.

SCENE 2 - INT. COUNCIL CHAMBER - LATER

Numa returns with a red crayon from the children's waiting room. Kalo refuses to sign a treaty in red. Pera says the color does not change the agreement. Sivi points out that the crayon is too wide to fit inside the signature boxes.

KALO: I will not put my name on a document that looks like a drawing.

PERA: We have spent four hours drawing the boundary.

Numa cuts the crayon's wrapper off and sharpens it with a small office knife. The point breaks. Tolo tries to catch the crayon and knocks the map onto the floor. The pages slide under the table, where both envoys reach for different copies.

SCENE 3 - INT. COUNCIL CHAMBER - CONTINUOUS

Pera and Kalo compare the map copies. One shows the old streambed; the other shows the current stream. Sivi translates the handwritten note on the back: the boundary is the footpath between the orchards, not the water. Both governments had been using the same path for years.

KALO: We have been negotiating the wrong line.

PERA: We can sign the path.

Numa pins the maps to the table. The crayon rolls toward the edge. Tolo stops it with a gloved hand, leaving a red streak across his palm. The photographers take pictures, interpreting the mark as a dramatic sign of conflict.

Kalo asks for a private recess with Pera. The guards move the photographers outside. The two envoys compare the path on their maps and find that one copy is upside down. Sivi rotates it. The drawn boundary now matches the window view. Numa takes the crayon and traces the path between two orchard symbols. It breaks again. She tapes the pieces together, leaving a small red line on the document. Pera asks whether the line is official. Numa says it is on both copies, so it can be filed.

SCENE 4 - INT. COUNCIL CHAMBER - MOMENTS LATER

Sivi reads the final boundary clause aloud. Pera and Kalo agree to mark the path on both maps and sign the copies. Numa places carbon paper beneath the signatures. The crayon snaps in half. Kalo signs with the shorter piece; Pera uses the longer one. Their names appear in red on both documents.

A photographer asks them to shake hands over the maps. They do. The red crayon mark transfers to both cuffs. Numa notices the duplicated treaty pages have been switched: each envoy holds the other's copy. They trade copies once, checking the signatures.

The photographers return for a final portrait. Pera and Kalo stand on opposite sides of the treaty, holding the map flat. The crayon mark has smudged one sleeve, and each envoy wipes at the wrong cuff. The camera flashes as they trade tissues. The signed copy stays clean. Numa asks them to sign the archive log in pencil; both ministers glance at the red crayon, then choose graphite.

SCENE 5 - INT. COUNCIL CHAMBER - AFTERNOON

The border agreement is signed and archived. The envoys walk to the window overlooking the path. Villagers cross it carrying baskets from both orchards. The diplomats watch and realize the route has always been shared. Tolo offers them soap for the red marks on their cuffs.

PERA: We have made a treaty about a path everyone already uses.

KALO: It is now official.

Numa files the crayon stub in the archive box. Sivi writes a label identifying it as the signing instrument. The photographers ask for one last pose beside the map.

FINAL IMAGE: The treaty hangs between the two map copies, its red signatures matching the path drawn across the border.
