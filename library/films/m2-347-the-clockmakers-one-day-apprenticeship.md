# The Clockmaker's One-Day Apprenticeship

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: apprentice fantasies, magical tests, secret masters, and training montages that solve the wrong problem*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
APPRENTICE PIP, a capuchin
MASTER ODO, an orangutan clockmaker
MIRI, a lemur customer
TAVI, a macaque delivery monkey
THE CUCKOO, a mechanical bird

SCENE 1. INT. CLOCK SHOP - DAWN

Pip arrives for his first day as an apprentice. Master Odo hands him a tiny screwdriver and a list of clocks to wind. A mechanical cuckoo pops from a clock and announces eleven though the wall clock reads six.

PIP: Is it broken?

ODO: It is enthusiastic.

Miri brings in a tower clock's bell mechanism. The bell has stopped before the town's festival. Odo says Pip can assist only after he learns to wind small clocks without overwinding them.

SCENE 2. INT. WORKBENCH - MORNING

Pip winds a mantel clock. It ticks faster and faster until the minute hand spins. Odo stops it and shows him how to ease the key. A training montage begins: Pip winds clocks, cleans gears, and labels springs. The music makes each chore look like a heroic trial. A tiny timer appears in the corner counting down to the festival.

TAVI: The tower bell needs to ring by noon.

ODO: The timer is not helping.

Odo gives Pip a practice clock with a transparent case. He asks him to name the parts before touching them. Pip points to the escape wheel, then asks where it escapes to. Odo shows him how the balance wheel regulates the tick. The explanation becomes a montage: Pip studies diagrams, winds a kitchen timer, and tests a metronome. The metronome begins racing because he has tightened its weight too far.

PIP: I have accelerated time.

ODO: You have moved the weight.

Pip tries to repair a pocket watch without opening it. Odo asks him to identify the problem first. Pip hears a rattle and finds a loose gear. He replaces it with a spring, which launches the watch's cover across the room.

SCENE 3. INT. TOWER MECHANISM ROOM - MIDDAY

Odo and Pip carry the bell mechanism up the tower. A stair is missing, so Tavi brings a ladder. Pip climbs with a tray of screws balanced on his head. The cuckoo clock, accidentally packed with the tools, begins calling from the bag. Its bird pops out and startles Tavi, who nearly drops the ladder.

MIRI: The town is waiting.

PIP: The bird is also waiting.

They fit the mechanism. The bell remains silent. Odo checks the spring tension. It is correct. Pip checks the gear teeth; they line up. The cuckoo clock in the tool bag calls eleven again. Pip hears the same note echo through the tower and notices the clapper is not striking the bell at all. A short length of festival ribbon is wrapped around its hinge.

PIP: The clock is not late. It is tied up.

ODO: A sound diagnosis. Now free it carefully.

Pip holds the clapper steady while Odo unwinds the ribbon. It comes loose without damaging the gear. Pip tests the clapper once more and hears a clear note. Odo congratulates him, but asks Pip to check that the bell can swing freely. Pip steps aside, tests the clapper by hand, and clears the ladder before the bell rings. Its sound shakes dust from the ceiling and lands on their heads.

SCENE 4. EXT. TOWN SQUARE - AFTERNOON

The town gathers for the festival. The bell rings on the hour. Pip has learned to wind the smaller clocks, but Odo asks him to explain how he found the jam. Pip says he listened before pulling. Miri asks if that is the secret of clock repair. Odo says it is one useful habit. The festival band tries to match the bell, misses by half a beat, and starts over with the correct tempo.

Tavi brings a delivery of watch parts. One box is labeled "cuckoo," and a mechanical bird pecks at the string. Pip opens it carefully and finds a replacement bird. He installs it in the clock, whose cuckoo announces the correct hour. The shop's clocks now chime in order, rather than all at once.

SCENE 5. INT. CLOCK SHOP - EVENING

Pip winds the last clock without rushing. The festival timer reaches zero, then stops because it was only set for the workday. Odo gives Pip a small brass key for tomorrow's repairs. Pip asks whether he has passed the apprenticeship test. Odo says there was no test; the clocks needed winding.

PIP: The music made it seem important.

ODO: The clocks are important. The music is optional.

FINAL IMAGE: The cuckoo emerges one minute early, sees Pip watching, and ducks back inside until the correct hour.
