# The Bridge Chorus

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: backstage musicals where a public works crew tries to rehearse while carrying out a noisy bridge repair*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: LEO, a baboon road foreman; SIA, a macaque singer; KETT, an orangutan mechanic; PIP, a capuchin toll collector; MAREN, a gibbon music director.

EXT. RIVER BRIDGE - DAWN

A bridge is closed for a one-day repair. Cones block the road. A work crew sets down tool boxes. MAREN arrives with a music stand.

LEO: The bridge will reopen at five.

MAREN: The chorus will be ready by four.

KETT: The bridge is the chorus.

SIA: We are singing about the repair.

PIP: The toll booth is still open for pedestrians.

A metal plate clanks. The crew tries to repeat the rhythm. Sia sings a note to match it. The plate clanks again, out of time.

SIA: The bridge is in a different key.

ACT ONE

EXT. BRIDGE WORK ZONE - MORNING

Maren sets the choir on the safe side of the cones and asks them to sing the work sequence. The toll collector's bell rings over the first verse. A bicycle messenger waits at the crossing.

MESSENGER: How long until I can cross?

LEO: Five minutes if the plate fits.

MAREN: Four if the chorus keeps time.

The crew refuses to rush. Kett checks each bolt and marks the tightened ones with chalk. Sia holds the plate while Leo drives the first bolt. The crew sings each count, but Pip rings the toll bell whenever a pedestrian approaches. The song becomes a stop-and-start rhythm.

SIA: We need a verse for waiting.

PIP: I can ring on the rests.

MAREN: There are no rests in this measure.

Pip tries a shorter bell tap. It lands between two drill sounds. The choir adjusts the beat and the pedestrians pause safely behind the cones. The bicycle messenger waits without walking into the work area.

The crew lifts a loose plate and replaces the bolts. Maren signals the chorus to begin. A drill starts, drowning out the first verse. Pip rings the toll bell at the same moment.

MAREN: We need one clear beat.

LEO: The drill has a schedule.

PIP: The bell has a queue.

Sia changes the melody to match the drill. The chorus follows, then the wrench falls into a metal bucket with a loud clang.

KETT: That is not in the score.

MAREN: It is now.

The crew lowers the bridge plate. One corner sits too high. Kett checks underneath and finds a pebble wedged at the edge. Sia sings a low note as the crew lifts the plate. Pip retrieves the pebble. They lower the plate again; it fits flush.

LEO: The bridge is level.

SIA: We found the harmony.

The sound engineer sets a microphone near the work zone. The microphone picks up a loud hum from the toll booth's old transformer.

PIP: That hum is not mine.

KETT: The transformer is vibrating against the wall.

They move the microphone and add a rubber pad under the transformer. The hum stops. Maren asks the crew to try the chorus again. This time, the drill stays off and the plate holds.

ACT TWO

At noon, the crew tests the bridge with a small maintenance cart. The wheels cross the repaired plate without a bump. Leo opens the bridge to foot traffic while cars continue to use the marked detour.

MAREN: We have a stage.

LEO: We have a crossing.

The chorus lines up beside the toll booth. Sia leads a song about a bridge that carries people both ways. The first verse gives the pedestrians the right-of-way. The second verse tells the cart driver to wait at the cones. A child starts across during the wrong line, and Pip rings the bell to stop them before the wheels reach the plate.

PIP: That was a safety cue.

MAREN: It was also in rhythm.

The child returns to the marked path and waits for Leo's signal. The crew completes the verse and gives the all-clear before any cart crosses. The crew taps tool handles against the rail in time, then stops when a delivery cart approaches. The cart crosses safely. The song resumes on the far side.

Pip collects no toll from pedestrians. Instead, he rings the bell once for the completed repair. Maren looks at the clock: four o'clock. The rehearsal is over. Leo holds up a sign reading OPEN TO FOOT TRAFFIC.

KETT: We have one hour left.

LEO: Let people use it. The cars can wait until inspection.

The crew rolls the tools to the truck. Sia offers Maren a wrench to conduct the final note. Maren refuses it and raises the music stand instead.

FINAL IMAGE: The bridge carries a line of pedestrians across the river as the chorus echoes from the far bank.
