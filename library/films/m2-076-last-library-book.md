# The Last Library Book

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: post-apocalyptic quest films, solemn narration, and relics treated as more valuable than their readers*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: In a city where every library branch has closed for renovation, a small group crosses town to return the last overdue book before the central library locks its doors.

Cast: Oona, a macaque librarian; Vell, a gorilla courier; Pip, a capuchin reader; Archivist Tern, a tortoise; Narrator, a solemn voice; and a book titled The Practical Guide to Houseplants.

SCENE 1. EXT. EMPTY LIBRARY BRANCH, DAWN

Oona stands outside a shuttered branch while a solemn narrator describes the end of reading. A poster says CLOSED FOR FLOOR REPAIR. Vell carries a book in a waterproof pack. Pip says the book is overdue by one day.

NARRATOR: The knowledge of the old world must be preserved.

OONA: It's a library book. We can return it.

SCENE 2. EXT. CITY STREET, MORNING

The group follows a paper map to the central library. A detour sign redirects them. The narrator calls it a wasteland. Vell asks a crossing guard for directions. The guard points toward the open bridge. Oona asks whether the bridge is safe. The guard says it was inspected yesterday.

The bridge is closed to bicycles, not pedestrians. Vell begins to narrate its dangers as the group walks beside a sign showing a smiling family. A maintenance worker replaces a missing screw on the sign while they wait for a delivery cart to pass. Pip refuses to let the book touch the railing. He holds it above his head and walks into a low branch. Oona takes the pack and adjusts its straps so the book stays dry without obstructing his view.

Pip refuses to leave the book unattended. Earlier, he watered the houseplant pictures with a spray bottle, convinced they were thirsty; now the pages are damp. He brings it across the bridge in both hands. A gust blows the map away; Vell catches it and folds it into a smaller route.

At the bus shelter, a route map shows the central branch is two stops away. Vell taps the posted schedule.

NARRATOR: Across the barren plain, our travelers faced a trial of faith.

OONA: Four minutes. That's the trial.

VELL: What if the bus never comes?

The bus arrives. Vell feeds a commemorative library token into the fare machine. It spits the token into his hand.

NARRATOR: The guardian rejects our offering.

OONA: It rejects souvenir metal.

She pays the ordinary fare. The machine accepts it.

PIP: Was that the sacred password?

OONA: Two leaves and exact change. Sit down.

The narrator opens his mouth. The driver points to a QUIET ZONE sign. He stays silent for the ride.

SCENE 3. INT. CENTRAL LIBRARY, AFTERNOON

The library doors are closing. Archivist Tern waits at the return desk. Oona offers the book. Tern checks the date stamp and says the branch was closed yesterday, so the due date extends automatically. Pip is relieved. The narrator calls this the final test.

PIP: Is there a late fee?

TERN: No. There was a sign.

The book's pages are damp. Tern places it on a drying rack.

VELL: We crossed the city for nothing.

TERN: You returned a borrowed book.

PIP: I watered the pictures before putting it in the waterproof pack.

OONA: So the pack kept the water in.

PIP: The instructions said never let the roots dry out.

TERN: Those roots were printed on page twelve.

The drying rack is already full of cookbooks salvaged from a basement leak. Tern hands Pip a stack of flat blotting sheets. Pip tries to wrap the book in the entire roll. Oona stops his hands and slides one sheet between two damp pages. Vell steadies the cover.

NARRATOR: The keeper begins an ancient preservation rite.

TERN: Read the laminated card.

NARRATOR: "Wait for the paper to settle."

TERN: That's your cue too.

One page has stuck to its neighbor. Tern uses a soft brush and waits instead of peeling it. Pip fidgets, then spots a pressed leaf inside the book. He is about to lift it when Oona asks him to let the paper settle. The leaf crumbles at one edge. Tern puts it in a labeled envelope and records where it was found. The book is not a relic to seize; it is a shared object that needs careful hands.

SCENE 4. INT. LIBRARY REPAIR ROOM, LATER

The archivist examines the damp pages. The book is still readable. Pip offers to fan it. He flaps too hard; a page tears loose. Tern stops him and shows him how to support the spine. Pip helps place the book beneath a gentle fan. The narrator says the relic has survived.

OONA: It's a houseplant guide.

TERN: Someone may need it.

SCENE 5. INT. CENTRAL LIBRARY, EVENING

The book is shelved in the repair section. A notice announces that the branches will reopen when the floors are fixed.

VELL: Should we prepare for another journey?

Oona points to the city bus route.

PIP: Is the bus part of the quest?

OONA: The bus is part of the route.

Tern checks the book's condition and issues a replacement due date for the repair shelf. Pip asks whether they have to carry it back across town. Tern says the library will return it to the branch when the floors are ready. Vell looks disappointed that the expedition has no second leg. Oona offers to help the staff label the repair crates. He agrees, but insists on a map. Tern hands him the building directory. He studies it, turns it right side up, and finds the crate station two doors away.

As they sort the crates, a family asks for a children's book. Pip directs them to the open reading room. Vell starts describing the journey as a heroic return. The child asks if the book is about plants. Pip says yes, and shows her the picture of a sprout. She picks another book and sits down to read. The narrator tries to call this the dawn of a new age. Oona gives him a quiet look, and he lowers his voice.

FINAL IMAGE: The narrator begins a farewell. Oona places a bookmark in the plant guide and leaves a note for the next reader: "Please water the examples, not the book."
