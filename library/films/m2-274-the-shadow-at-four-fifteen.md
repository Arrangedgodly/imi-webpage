# The Shadow at Four-Fifteen

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: film-noir detective mysteries, voice-over narration, dramatic blinds, and clues hidden in plain sight*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: After a theater projection loses its sundial-shaped shadow at 4:15 in daylight, a macaque detective follows the missing reflector bracket through a noir-styled night investigation.

Cast
- DETECTIVE VERA, a macaque whose tail curls around her notebook.
- MOSS, a capuchin park caretaker and a habitual seed sorter.
- LUX, a gibbon theater electrician.
- INSPECTOR RELL, an orangutan who prefers a clean report.
- VISITOR, a young macaque who borrowed the loose bracket to steady a bench.
- THE VOICE, Vera's narration.

## Scene 1: The Empty Hour

EXT. CITY PARK - DUSK

Streetlamps cast long bars across the path. Vera stands beside a stone sundial whose brass reflector mount is empty. Earlier, at 4:15, a theater projection using the dial's long shadow had blinked out in full daylight. The crew is now packing away the screen.

THE VOICE: At four fifteen, the picture lost its shadow. The sun stayed in place, the clock stayed honest, and a caretaker looked like he had been waiting for trouble.

MOSS: I was waiting for the sprinklers to stop.

VERA: Where is the reflector bracket?

MOSS: The shadow vanished at 4:15. The mount was still here then.

VERA: And now?

Moss points to fresh scrapes on the dial's base. Vera notes the lamp angle but rejects it as the cause of the afternoon projection failure.

## Scene 2: The Witness

INT. PARK SHED - NIGHT

Moss pours seeds into three jars by size. Vera asks about the scrape.

MOSS: The theater crew borrowed the big reflector. They said they needed it for a rehearsal.

VERA: A reflector does not remove a shadow.

MOSS: It can put light somewhere else.

THE VOICE: He spoke like a man with nothing to hide, which was suspicious. He also had nothing to hide because the jars were transparent.

Inspector Rell enters and demands a list of missing park property. Moss names the reflector, then points to a storage hook where it should be.

RELL: We will recover it tomorrow.

VERA: The shadow is missing tonight.

Rell leaves. Vera follows the scrape marks out of the shed.

## Scene 3: The Theater

INT. COMMUNITY THEATER - NIGHT

Lux is adjusting a row of stage lights. The reflector stands behind a painted moon.

LUX: I borrowed it to light the rehearsal. It is returned before morning.

VERA: Why does the sundial base have fresh scratches?

LUX: The reflector was mounted there during the day. We used it to send a beam across the park. At 4:15 the bracket slipped, the beam shifted off the screen, and our shadow clock vanished.

VERA: That sounds like a motive.

LUX: It sounds like a rehearsal. The beam slipped when the lamp turned on.

Lux demonstrates: the reflector throws a bright square onto the stage, then onto the wall. The light crosses the painted scenery, but no shadow falls where the sundial should point.

THE VOICE: The room had the usual suspects: wires, curtains, a moon, and one electrician who knew where everything was.

Vera finds a second set of scrapes. They lead to a narrow service corridor, where a lens cap lies beside an open window.

## Scene 4: The Real Theft

EXT. PARK GAZEBO - NIGHT

Vera sets the lens cap on a bench. Its shadow points toward the sundial. She rotates the cap; the shadow shifts. The reflector's missing mounting bracket belongs on the dial's base; the reflector itself is here in the theater.

VERA: Someone took the bracket.

LUX: I thought it was on the reflector.

MOSS: It was not. I found the bolts under the shed shelf.

Rell arrives and accuses Lux of stealing the sundial's shadow for the theater. Vera points out a small trail of brass filings on the path. It ends at the gazebo, where a visitor has used the bracket to secure a loose bench leg.

RELL: The bracket is there?

VERA: It is holding the bench together.

A park visitor, a young macaque, emerges from the dark.

VISITOR: The seat wobbled. I found the metal piece beside the shed.

MOSS: I left it there when I took the reflector down. I should have labeled it.

LUX: I should have checked the mount before borrowing the reflector.

Vera removes the bracket from the bench. The loose leg shifts. The group braces the seat and replaces the bracket after moving the visitor to a stable bench.

## Scene 5: A Measured Ending

INT. PARK OFFICE - LATER

Rell writes the report: reflector borrowed, bracket misplaced, bench repaired, shadow absent because it is night.

RELL: There is no crime.

VERA: There was a missing part and an unstable seat. We found both.

MOSS: I have labeled the shelf.

LUX: The theater can use the reflector again tomorrow, if we sign it out.

RELL: Do not make the form sound sinister.

THE VOICE: At four fifteen, the shadow returned in the morning, as shadows do when the sun arrives. The park clock kept its own schedule.

Vera stands beside the dial as dawn reaches it. The sundial's shadow stretches over the repaired bench.

## Final Image

A beam of sunlight crosses the park path. Vera closes her notebook while the shadow moves one inch. No one follows it.
