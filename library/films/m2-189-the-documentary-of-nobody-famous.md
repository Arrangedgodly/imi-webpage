# The Documentary of Nobody Famous

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: prestige music documentaries, black-and-white rehearsal footage, dramatic origin stories, and experts who discover symbolism in ordinary practice*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
DIRECTOR OMI, a lemur
BAND LEADER KET, a capuchin
DRUMMER RIN, a gibbon
ARCHIVIST SULA, a macaque

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY REC ROOM - EVENING

A camera films a dusty drum kit. The documentary title appears: "THE SOUND THAT CHANGED NOTHING."

OMI (V.O.): Before anyone knew them, the Kettle Street Trio practiced in a room behind the laundromat.

Ket tunes a guitar. Rin taps a rhythm on a plastic bucket. They stop to let a washing machine spin.

KET: We need the room for another hour.

RIN: The laundromat closes in twenty minutes.

OMI (V.O.): Their first obstacle was time.

SULA: The city records show the room was booked for a yoga class.

OMI: We're keeping that for the second act.

SCENE 2. INT. INTERVIEW SET - DAY

Ket sits before a black curtain. A single lamp makes his face look weathered.

OMI: When did the music begin?

KET: After dinner. We found a guitar and a bucket.

OMI: What did the bucket represent?

KET: Water. It was a bucket.

A cutaway shows a close-up of the bucket. Slow piano music plays.

RIN: I tapped it because the drum was in the car.

OMI: Was the car the band's first van?

RIN: It was a borrowed cart.

OMI: We'll call it a van in the trailer.

SCENE 3. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM - AFTERNOON

Sula examines a box of photographs. The oldest image shows Ket and Rin standing beside a sign that reads "OPEN MIC NIGHT."

SULA: The photograph is from last spring.

OMI: The band's formative era.

SULA: It was four months ago.

OMI: A compressed period of artistic upheaval.

The band rehearses. The guitarist misses a chord. Rin stops the song and asks for a break. The director asks them to repeat the mistake for the camera.

KET: I can play the song correctly.

OMI: The mistake reveals vulnerability.

KET: The mistake reveals I need to tune the guitar.

SCENE 3A. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM - LATER

Sula finds a cassette labeled "FIRST HIT." Omi pauses the camera and asks the band to remember how it felt.

KET: We had sold seven tickets. Two were bought by the laundromat staff.

RIN: The song was called "Spin Cycle." We wrote it because the washer covered up my bucket drum.

OMI: Was the washer a metaphor for an industry that could not hear you?

RIN: It was a washing machine.

The cassette plays. The original recording contains a washer spinning louder than the guitar. Omi asks for a clean take. The band listens and discovers the washer's rhythm matches the chorus.

SULA: The sound you tried to remove became the hook.

KET: We left it in because the tape was already full.

OMI: A budget decision becomes an artistic signature.

SCENE 4. INT. FESTIVAL BACKSTAGE - NIGHT

The Trio's first paid show is at a neighborhood festival. The stage manager tells them their slot is four minutes, then cuts it to three when the previous act runs late.

OMI: This is the moment their story turns.

SULA: They have played this song at every open mic.

OMI: It becomes a turning point when the crowd hears it.

The band takes the stage. The audience is mostly waiting for the food cart to open. Ket starts with the right chord. Rin plays the bucket, which has been replaced by a proper drum.

The crowd claps on the wrong beat. Rin adjusts to match them. The band's rhythm changes. Ket smiles and follows. The audience moves with the new groove, turning the chorus into a call-and-response.

SCENE 5. INT. INTERVIEW SET - LATER

Omi asks the band what the song meant.

KET: We needed a song that fit the shorter set.

RIN: We changed the beat to match the crowd.

SULA: That's the whole story.

OMI: We can still add a voice-over.

SCENE 5A. INT. FESTIVAL HALL - LATER

The director asks the band to recreate their first open-mic performance. A volunteer stagehand places the original bucket beside the drum kit. The audience of one camera operator waits in silence.

KET: We played to a real audience last time.

OMI: Then let's invite one.

RIN: The laundromat is closed.

SULA: The festival crew is still here. They can sit by the stage.

The crew gathers on folding chairs. Omi asks them to respond naturally. They clap at the wrong moment again. The band shifts to meet the clap, and the song finds the same new rhythm.

OMI: This is the turning point.

SULA: It's the same song as the first take.

OMI: The audience has changed.

SCENE 6. EXT. FESTIVAL STAGE - NIGHT

The documentary crew watches the band play an encore. The crowd sings the wrong words, but the band continues, laughing. The food cart opens beside the stage. A line of listeners waits for dumplings while tapping the beat. The director films the line from above, trying to frame the crowd as a sold-out arena. The dumpling seller asks the crew to move so customers can reach the counter.

FINAL IMAGE: The old plastic bucket sits beside the proper drum kit. It catches rain from a loose tent seam. Each drop taps the Trio's first rhythm while the camera lingers long enough to make a puddle look historic.
