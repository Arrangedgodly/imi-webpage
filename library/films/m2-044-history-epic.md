# The Battle of Three Picnic Tables

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: historical war epics, grand strategy maps, and speeches before minor disputes*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: Two villages prepare for a legendary battle over a meadow, but their old map is upside down and the opposing army is a neighboring potluck group.

Cast: General Morrow, a baboon village elder; Scout Luma, a langur; Captain Peep, a chicken messenger; Archivist Odo, an orangutan; and Mayor Fern, a macaque from the next village.

SCENE 1. EXT. MEADOW, DAWN

Sunlight falls across three picnic tables. Morrow studies a map. Luma turns it around.

MORROW: The river is still blue.

LUMA: It is behind us.

MORROW: Then the enemy is behind us.

LUMA: The river is behind us. The village is not.

Captain Peep runs off with a message. He returns carrying muffins. Morrow declares them captured provisions. Peep says the bakery gave them to him because he looked hungry.

SCENE 2. INT. VILLAGE HALL, MORNING

Odo opens the archive's treaty book. The meadow is shared on alternating Sundays. Today is Sunday, but the calendar was printed twice with dates shifted by a week. Morrow orders banners raised. The villagers hold tablecloths on poles.

MORROW: We defend the shade!

ODO: The treaty makes no claim on shade.

MORROW: Then we defend the tables.

ODO: The treaty says share the tables.

Morrow asks Odo to find a loophole. Odo reads the clause aloud. Captain Peep delivers a note to Mayor Fern; it is a grocery list. Fern sends it back with a correction: the muffins are for everyone.

SCENE 3. EXT. MEADOW, MIDDAY

Both villages arrive, each carrying chairs and food. Morrow has built a cardboard fortress around one table. Fern asks him to move it so she can sit in the shade. He says the table is strategically important. A gust flattens the fortress over the table.

FERN: Your defense has become a tablecloth.

MORROW: It is a tactical retreat.

Captain Peep tries to deliver the revised schedule but mixes the papers. Morrow receives a note that says bring napkins; Fern receives a diagram of the fortress. Odo asks both sides to stop and compare dates. They discover the duplicate calendar and the shared-use clause.

SCENE 4. EXT. MEADOW, AFTERNOON

Morrow asks Peep to sound the retreat trumpet. Peep has brought a picnic whistle. It makes a small squeak. The villagers retreat three steps, then stop because the food is on the table. Fern says a retreat should include carrying the chairs. Everyone lifts one. The line of chairs moves in a circle and returns to its starting point. Odo records the maneuver as a successful rotation.

MORROW: The archive will call that a victory.

ODO: The archive will call it chair movement.

Morrow and Fern mark the picnic areas with chalk. The chalk line runs through a table leg. They move the table together. The line ends at a tree, where both groups want the same patch of shade. Odo checks the treaty again and finds no rule about trees. The villagers rotate the shaded seats every half hour.

The groups attempt a ceremonial chant. Morrow starts a battle cry; Fern answers with the potluck verse. The lines overlap into a list of dishes. A child joins in and asks when lunch begins. Morrow lowers his banner.

MORROW: At noon.

FERN: It is noon.

They sit. Peep finally delivers the correct message: shared picnic, bring plates. He reads it aloud before handing it over. The villagers applaud his accuracy. Peep bows and drops the muffins.

SCENE 5. EXT. MEADOW, SUNSET

Before signing, Fern asks Morrow to initial the corrected date. He writes his name across the full line. Odo slides the board toward Fern. She adds her signature in a neat square. Peep tries to sign with his beak and leaves a muffin crumb on the ink. Odo files the board after wiping it clean.

Odo writes a new schedule on a board and gets both mayors to sign. The archive keeps one copy; the second is posted at the meadow entrance. Morrow asks if the day will go into the history book. Odo says yes, under shared-use agreements.

MORROW: Can it say we won?

FERN: We got three tables.

MORROW: We won three tables.

The villagers divide the leftovers into containers for next Sunday. The map is set flat under a bowl so it cannot blow away again.

The muffins are shared.

FINAL IMAGE: The two flags hang side by side. A child uses one as a picnic blanket. Morrow begins a victory speech; a muffin lands on his map. He folds it and asks Fern for another plate.
