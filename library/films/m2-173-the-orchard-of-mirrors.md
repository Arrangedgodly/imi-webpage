# The Orchard of Mirrors

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: psychological thrillers that confuse characters with reflections and manufacture suspicion from framing*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-006*

Logline: A pruning crew enters a greenhouse lined with reflective panels and mistakes every glimpse of a colleague for an intruder.

Cast: Luma, a lemur orchard keeper; Tavi, a macaque assistant; Piri, a capuchin inspector; and a troop of fruit bats sleeping in the rafters.

SCENE 1. INT. MIRROR GREENHOUSE, MORNING

Rows of potted citrus trees stand between tall reflective panels. Luma carries a pruning hook. Tavi enters from the far door and appears in six reflections.

LUMA: Someone is behind the trees.

TAVI: It is me. I am here.

Luma moves left. The reflections move too. She crouches. So do the reflections.

PIRI enters with a clipboard and sees Luma pointing her hook at the mirror.

PIRI: Why are you threatening the wall?

LUMA: It copied my stance.

Piri checks the panel with a knuckle. It is glass, not a doorway.

SCENE 2. INT. GREENHOUSE AISLE, LATER

Piri marks each mirror edge with a strip of bright cloth. The crew can now tell the real aisle from the reflections. Tavi picks up a fallen orange. In the mirror, it appears to roll behind him.

TAVI: There is another orange moving.

PIRI: It is the same orange.

Tavi lifts it. The reflection lifts its orange too. Luma studies the floor for a hidden chute and finds only a damp footprint from her own boot.

LUMA: We have an intruder who follows us exactly.

PIRI: That is the reflection.

A bat wakes above them and flutters across the panels. Its reflections seem to multiply into a whole flock. Everyone ducks. The bat lands on a beam and folds its wings.

TAVI: The intruders are hanging upside down.

PIRI: Those are fruit bats. They live here.

Luma lowers her hook and apologizes to the bat. It resumes sleeping.

SCENE 3. INT. GREENHOUSE CENTER, AFTERNOON

The inspector asks them to trim one branch blocking a narrow aisle. The mirror makes the branch look like it crosses the path in both directions. Tavi ties a cloth to its real end. Piri traces the true branch with a finger.

PIRI: Follow the leaf, not the image.

Luma starts to cut, then notices a reflection that seems to show a second worker behind her. She stops and turns. The aisle is empty. She sees her own sleeve move a beat later in the opposite panel.

LUMA: I have found the culprit.

TAVI: Which one?

LUMA: The one with my cuff.

Piri uses a paper screen to cover the side panel. The doubled branch disappears. Luma makes one clean cut. The branch lands in Tavi's basket.

SCENE 4. INT. GREENHOUSE ENTRY, EVENING

The crew clears the walkway. Piri records the inspection: the mirrors are intact, the tree is pruned, and the bat colony remains undisturbed. Luma adds a simple diagram showing which panels face each other. Tavi tapes it by the entrance.

TAVI: Visitors will know where the real path is.

LUMA: Unless the diagram has a reflection.

Piri tilts the paper until the light falls away. The instructions are readable. A bat glides low and lands beside the diagram, upside down.

PIRI: That is not an exit sign.

TAVI: It may be the bat's map.

The bat stretches one wing and knocks the paper loose. It lands face down.

Piri asks Luma to demonstrate the pruning cut once more for the inspection record. Luma explains the angle, then hands the hook to Tavi so he can hold the basket beneath the branch. The bat colony remains asleep while they finish. Piri marks the diagram with the actual door and the actual aisle, not the mirrored versions. The crew checks each cloth marker on the way out.

Tavi pauses by the last panel and waves once to test the sightline. The reflection waves too, but the bright cloth at the edge makes the glass boundary obvious. Luma points out the real door, and Piri records that the marker can be seen from both sides. They leave the hook in its rack and close the greenhouse without startling the bats.

SCENE 5. EXT. GREENHOUSE, SUNSET

The crew steps outside. From outdoors, the mirrored panels show only the orchard and sky. Luma looks back and sees herself leaving, but this time the reflection is expected.

LUMA: It is much easier when I know who I am looking at.

PIRI: And where the panel ends.

Tavi picks up a fallen orange from the real path and places it in the basket. The bat's silhouette passes over the glass. Luma looks up instead of turning toward the reflection.

TAVI: One bat.

PIRI: One bat.

They carry the pruning basket away. In the glass, the three reflections appear to follow, then vanish as the crew rounds the corner.
