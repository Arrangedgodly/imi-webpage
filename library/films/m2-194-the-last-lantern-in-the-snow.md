# The Last Lantern in the Snow

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: survival films, extreme weather montages, heroic resource counting, and a rescue delayed by one avoidable misunderstanding*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
KORI, a gibbon guide
FALA, a capuchin climber
YEN, a macaque radio operator

SCENE 1. EXT. SNOW PASS - DAY

A whiteout blankets the mountain trail. Kori, Fala, and Yen shelter beneath a rock ledge. Their expedition lantern has one hour of fuel. A radio crackles: the rescue crew is searching from the north ridge.

YEN: We should signal from the pass.

KORI: The wind will carry the sound away.

FALA: We can use the lantern as a visual signal when the cloud breaks.

SCENE 2. INT. ROCK SHELTER - LATER

They inventory supplies. One loaf of bread, two apples, a thermos, and three dry socks. Fala insists the socks are for feet. Yen suggests using one as a filter for snow melt. Kori counts each item twice, then realizes the thermos is already empty.

A survival montage follows: they dig a wind wall, mark the trail with bright cloth, ration food, and take turns resting. The lantern flame shrinks. Fala recites a list of mountain facts she learned from a guidebook, only to find the page has blown away.

SCENE 2A. EXT. ROCK SHELTER - AFTERNOON

The wind changes direction. Snow starts drifting across the trail markers. Kori ties the bright cloth lower on the rocks so it does not flap loose. Fala breaks the bread into equal pieces. Yen turns the radio antenna toward the ridge.

A message crackles through: "Search team returning to base. Visibility poor."

FALA: They're going back.

KORI: They're pausing until they can see safely. We should stay sheltered and signal when the cloud lifts.

Yen tries again. Static. Fala asks whether they can walk to the next marker. Kori checks the slope and refuses; the snow hides a drop beyond the ledge.

KORI: We wait here, where we can be found.

They take turns keeping watch. Fala warms the thermos in her coat, though it is empty. Yen laughs, then uses it as a cup to catch meltwater from the shelter edge. The water is clear but too cold to drink until they heat it.

SCENE 3. EXT. SNOW PASS - DUSK

The cloud breaks. Kori raises the lantern. A distant light answers from the north ridge, then vanishes. Yen radios the rescue team but hears only static.

YEN: They're moving south.

KORI: The ridge is north.

YEN: The signal is reversed by the valley.

Fala plants a line of colored cloth toward the safe trail. The wind turns it into a bright spiral. The crew debates whether the rescuers will read it as an arrow or a warning.

SCENE 4. INT. ROCK SHELTER - NIGHT

The lantern flickers out. In darkness, a series of taps sounds from beyond the rock wall. Fala answers with three taps. A pause. Two taps return.

KORI: Rescue code?

YEN: It's our metal thermos rolling in the wind.

The thermos is outside. Kori crawls out and retrieves it. The snow has melted enough to reveal a trail marker. He recognizes the symbol: the rescue station is west, not north. The radio's compass indicator was installed backward.

SCENE 5. EXT. WESTERN SLOPE - DAWN

The trio follows the marked route, conserving warmth and carrying the lantern unlit. They find the rescue team searching the northern ridge. Fala uses the bright cloth to signal from the slope. The rescuers spot them.

The team arrives with a spare radio and hot soup. Yen explains the compass error. The rescue chief confirms the radio indicator was rotated during maintenance. Kori reports the trail markers are intact. Fala asks for a new dry sock.

SCENE 5A. EXT. WESTERN SLOPE - DAWN

The rescue crew approaches with a stretcher and a thermos of soup. They confirm the three climbers are warm enough to walk. Yen hands over the radio with the reversed compass indicator.

RESCUE LEAD: We were searching the north ridge because that was where the radio pointed.

YEN: The indicator was rotated. The signal itself was clear.

KORI: The cloth marks show the route we used. We left the drop-off area untouched.

Fala asks about the thermos. The rescue lead pours hot water into it and gives her a mug. The empty vessel has finally become useful again.

FALA: I carried this all night for nothing.

RESCUE LEAD: You used it to catch water and keep the radio dry. That is not nothing.

They walk together toward the cabin, leaving the rescue team to collect the trail cloth once the wind settles.

SCENE 6. INT. RESCUE CABIN - MORNING

The expedition warms by a stove. The lantern is refilled and lit, now used to illuminate the cabin map. The team updates the compass marker and posts a clear direction sign.

FINAL IMAGE: The lantern burns steady beside the corrected map. Snow falls outside, but the trail to the cabin is marked in bright cloth, pointing west without a single dramatic arrow.
