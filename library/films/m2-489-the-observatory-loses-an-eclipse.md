# The Observatory Loses an Eclipse

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: cosmic suspense films that promise a rare celestial event with countdowns, ominous briefings, and immaculate viewing conditions*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-017*

Logline: An observatory director promises a perfect eclipse to visiting dignitaries, then keeps moving the audience between telescopes as a small cloud crosses the sky.

Cast: director Tavi, a macaque; astronomer Eno, a lemur; guide Miri, a capuchin; mayor Kessa, an orangutan; photographer Sol, a gibbon.

SCENE 1 - INT. OBSERVATORY DOME - DUSK

Tavi unveils a diagram of the eclipse path. Eno checks the telescope and notes that thin clouds are moving west. Miri welcomes the visitors and hands each a viewing card.

TAVI: At exactly nine, the moon will cover the sun.

ENO: This is a lunar eclipse. The Earth will cover the moon.

TAVI: At nine, something will be covered.

The mayor asks whether the event can be seen from the roof. Eno says the courtyard has a clearer view. Tavi refuses to move the guests because the dome is framed for the broadcast. Sol sets up a camera pointed at the screen.

SCENE 2 - EXT. OBSERVATORY COURTYARD - NIGHT

The guests follow Miri outside. Eno sets up a safe indirect viewing screen. Tavi points toward a cloud bank and says the shadow is approaching. The cloud crosses the moon. The guests gasp. Eno explains that the cloud has no relation to the eclipse.

MIRI: The moon is behind that cloud.

TAVI: A dramatic veil.

Kessa asks if they can wait. Tavi starts a countdown from ten. The cloud moves off after the countdown reaches four. Tavi insists the timing was exact.

SCENE 3 - INT. OBSERVATORY CONTROL ROOM - LATER

Eno checks the broadcast camera. Its lens cap is still on. Sol removes it, but the monitor shows a close-up of the dome ceiling because the camera is pointed indoors. Tavi instructs Miri to move the guests back into the dome for the live image. Eno says the courtyard screen is better for safety and visibility.

TAVI: The broadcast needs a picture.

ENO: Then point it at the eclipse.

Sol carries the camera outside. It is connected by a short cable, which stops at the doorway. Tavi asks the crew to extend the cable. Miri brings a battery camera from the education room. Sol frames the moon. The cloud has returned.

A breeze moves the cloud bank. Tavi asks the crowd to count down again, hoping to match the shadow's movement to his cue. Eno says the eclipse does not follow the countdown. The crowd counts anyway. At zero the cloud clears, and the moon's shadow begins crossing. Tavi steps into the telescope's projected circle to point out the event. His silhouette covers the largest part of the image. Miri moves the viewing screen to the side and lets the visitors see the actual shadow. The mayor holds Tavi's sleeve so he cannot drift back into the picture. Tavi whispers a fresh countdown; Eno tells him the moon is not listening. He keeps one hand on the rail and the other on the broadcast cue card.

Tavi asks the mayor to stand beside the telescope for a ceremonial photograph. Sol frames the mayor, the dome, and the cloud all at once. Eno asks him to move the camera away from the sun-facing window. Sol adjusts the tripod and the dome drops out of the shot. Tavi climbs onto a low step to restore the composition. The step wobbles. Kessa holds it steady while he takes the picture, leaving the eclipse entirely out of frame.

SCENE 4 - EXT. OBSERVATORY COURTYARD - CONTINUOUS

The eclipse begins. The moon's shadow crosses its face. The visitors look through the indirect screen. Tavi stands in front of it to narrate the event and blocks half the view. Kessa asks him to move. He sidesteps and enters the camera frame, where his silhouette appears larger than the moon.

TAVI: The darkness has arrived.

ENO: That is your head.

Tavi moves behind the telescope. Sol captures the actual eclipse. The cloud passes again, and Miri hands out the printed viewing cards, whose time is ten minutes late because the printer clock was never reset after daylight change.

SCENE 5 - INT. OBSERVATORY LOBBY - NIGHT

The visitors gather by the exit. The mayor thanks Eno for showing the eclipse safely. Tavi asks Sol whether the broadcast went well. Sol plays the recording: the actual moon is visible for four seconds, then Tavi's shadow fills the screen. Tavi asks to retake it next month. Eno points to the calendar; the next eclipse is years away.

FINAL IMAGE: Tavi pins the blurry broadcast frame beside the precise astronomy chart, with his own silhouette carefully labeled in the corner.
