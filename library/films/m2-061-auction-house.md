# Going Once, Going Twice, Going Home

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: art-auction thrillers, secret bids, and escalating paddle-raise suspense*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A cautious macaque auction clerk tries to sell a town's old clock at a charity auction, but a parrot mistakes every raised hand for a bid and buys nearly the entire room.

Cast: Kiri, a macaque clerk who trusts labels; Finch, a parrot bidder who cannot keep still; Ada, an orangutan appraiser; Tumble, a capuchin porter; a baboon auctioneer who hears bids everywhere; and Mayor Plink, who needs the clock returned before the square meeting.

SCENE 1. INT. AUCTION HALL, MORNING

Kiri pins a card to a clock: TOWN CLOCK, LOANED FOR SALE, RESERVE: TWO HUNDRED. She turns to a row of bidders rehearsing solemn expressions. Finch stretches one wing. The auctioneer strikes the bell.

AUCTIONEER: Lot one, a harbor painting. Do I hear ten?

FINCH: I was only loosening my shoulder.

AUCTIONEER: Twenty from the bird in the back.

Finch is handed the painting. He tries to return it; the auctioneer interprets the gesture as a second bid. Tumble takes it away before the frame tips Finch backward.

KIRI: Please use the paddle for bids.

FINCH: I haven't got a paddle.

Kiri issues him paddle seven and tapes his wing to his side with a strip of paper marked NOT A BID. He considers this considerate customer service.

SCENE 2. INT. AUCTION HALL, LATER

The bidding grows theatrical. Ada examines a silver teapot with a magnifier. Finch raises paddle seven to ask whether it whistles. The auctioneer sells it to him. Ada whispers that the teapot is a painted tin replica. Finch peers inside and finds a tiny whistle.

FINCH: It whistles. I have paid for proof.

A collector raises a paddle. Finch raises his to ask if the collector means him. He buys a rug. Tumble lays the rug under Finch's chair; Finch stands to thank him and buys two lamps. The lamps are placed beside his chair. He is now framed like a portrait of bad decisions.

Mayor Plink slips Kiri a note: the clock must return to the square before the evening meeting. The council's reserve bid is ready, but the auctioneer insists each lot must be presented in order. Kiri points at the clock. The auctioneer unveils a crate of garden gnomes instead.

SCENE 3. INT. AUCTION HALL STORAGE ROOM, AFTERNOON

Kiri, Ada, and Tumble hide every paddle in a cabinet. Finch follows the sound of the auction bell, which Tumble is testing for loose screws. Finch waves at Mayor Plink through the doorway. The auctioneer, visible through the open door, sells a crate of garden tools to Finch.

KIRI: That was a greeting.

AUCTIONEER: Greetings are not listed in the catalog.

ADA: Neither are tools. They're in the catalog under horticultural implements.

Tumble wheels the clock toward the loading door. A wheel squeaks in the same rhythm as the bell. Finch hears it, turns, and raises both wings. The auctioneer announces a matched set of stage curtains. A curtain drops over the clock and traps Mayor Plink inside its drape. Plink's muffled demand to be released is called a bid from the balcony.

Kiri stops the bell with her hand. The room falls silent. Finch asks if the silence is for sale.

SCENE 4. INT. AUCTION HALL, CONTINUOUS

Ada proposes a question card. Bidders write queries and hold them at chest level; only the paddle goes overhead. Finch practices with a card: DOES THE CLOCK TELL TIME? Kiri answers yes. He nods, hands down. The auctioneer starts the final lot.

The council bids two hundred. A collector bids two-fifty. Mayor Plink, still wrapped in a curtain, pokes up one thumb. Kiri holds a hand over the bell before the auctioneer can mistake it for an offer.

MAYOR PLINK: I am approving the clock's return to the town.

The auctioneer asks if that is a bid. Ada says it is a municipal resolution. The auctioneer studies the catalog, finds a section called municipal resolutions, and begins counting down. Kiri shuts the book.

KIRI: The clock goes home.

SCENE 5. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, SUNSET

Tumble and Kiri set the clock on its plinth. Finch arrives carrying one teapot. The lamps, rug, painting, and gnomes are being delivered to the auction hall's resale table; the town has arranged to unwind his accidental purchases. Finch says he will attend the next auction with both wings tucked in. A gust lifts his program. He catches it with a wing.

From inside the hall, the bell rings. The auctioneer announces the sale of the front row. Everyone turns toward Finch. He lowers his wing and backs away from the clock.

FINAL IMAGE: The town clock strikes six. Finch flinches, instinctively raises both wings, and hears the auctioneer shout, "Sold: the evening sky!" The whole square looks up as Finch hides beneath the teapot.
