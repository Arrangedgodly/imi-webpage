# The Day That Rang Twice

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: time-loop comedies, repeated-day montages, small changes with oversized consequences, and heroes who keep trying the same solution*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A capuchin bell keeper relives the same morning until she stops trying to ring the town bell perfectly and listens to the people who depend on it.

Cast
- PELA, a capuchin bell keeper whose tail coils around the rope.
- NIM, a macaque baker who begins each day with a floury greeting.
- OMI, an orangutan clock repairer who hates repeating explanations.
- TESS, a gibbon bus driver who knows every route change.
- KET, a ring-tailed lemur child who carries a weather vane.

## Scene 1: First Ring

INT. TOWN BELL TOWER - DAWN

Pela winds the bell rope and rings at exactly six. The bell sounds a flat note. Down below, the bakery wakes late, the bus misses its turn, and the market opens before its shade cloth is raised. At 6:07, a gust knocks Ket's weather vane onto the street.

PELA: The bell was wrong.

OMI: It was one note.

PELA: It should tell everyone the morning has begun.

OMI: It told them a bell rang.

Pela goes to bed that night. The next morning, the same six o'clock arrives. The bell rings. The weather vane falls. The bakery door sticks. The bus misses the turn.

## Scene 2: The Loop

Pela tests the day. She rings early, rings late, and tries to stop the bell from sounding. Each time, the morning repeats. The same flour puff rises from Nim's bakery window. The same bus takes the wrong lane. The same weather vane falls.

PELA: I have changed the bell.

OMI: The rest of the morning is unchanged.

PELA: I will change the bell more.

She pulls the rope with both hands. It rings so loudly the pigeons lift from the tower and settle on her head. By six fifteen, the vane falls again.

## Scene 3: The Montage

Pela tries a series of increasingly elaborate fixes. She pads the bell clapper with cloth, sets a clockwork alarm, and climbs the tower before dawn. Nim bakes earlier, then later. Tess drives the bus backward around the square. The exact same gust arrives at the exact same moment.

TESS: I cannot reroute the wind.

PELA: You rerouted the bus.

TESS: I put it in reverse.

Nim tapes the bakery door open. The same gust blows flour across the square, covering the tape. Omi removes the bell's clapper to stop it from ringing. The bell still swings and strikes its frame.

OMI: The bell has found another way to be a bell.

On the next loop, Pela does not ring at all. She waits in the square to see what happens. Nim opens the bakery window, the bus passes, and the wind arrives. Ket points at the weather vane just before its bracket bends. Pela catches the moment but not the vane; it lands in a basket of laundry.

PELA: The morning happens even without my bell.

OMI: People still wake up. Some of them wake up because of the bakery.

Pela hears the bus turn late, then watches the driver stop to move a fallen branch. She marks the time and notices the same gust follows the same cloud each loop.

## Scene 4: The Other Problem

On the fifth loop, Pela notices Ket looking up at the weather vane before it falls. She asks why.

KET: The vane catches the gust before it comes around the tower. I use it to know when to bring in the market shade.

PELA: Why didn't you fix the bracket?

KET: I told the repair crew. They said it could wait until Friday.

Omi checks the bracket. It is bent, not broken. The gust makes the vane's weight pull it loose. Tess says the bus misses its turn because the shade cloth blocks the street sign. Nim's door sticks because the same wind swells the wood.

PELA: The bell did not cause any of it.

OMI: It made us all look at the clock instead of the street.

## Scene 5: One Useful Morning

Pela does not touch the bell rope. She goes to the square before dawn and asks the market crew to raise the shade cloth after the bus passes. Tess waits for the sign to be visible. Nim props the bakery door open with a wooden wedge. Omi tightens the vane bracket. Ket signals the gust by turning the vane toward the tower.

At six, the bell rings its same flat note. The town is ready. The bus turns correctly; the bakery opens; the market shade goes up. The vane rattles but stays fixed.

PELA: I did not improve the note.

OMI: You improved the morning.

## Scene 6: The Last Loop

Pela goes to bed, expecting the day to repeat. The next morning arrives, but it is a new day. The bell rings at six. The bakery door opens. The bus follows its route. Ket waves from the square.

Pela checks the bell. Its tone is still flat. She laughs and leaves it alone.

## Final Image

The weather vane points toward the wind. Its bracket holds. The bell rope rests coiled over Pela's tail as she walks downstairs into the morning.
