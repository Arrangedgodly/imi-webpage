# Frost on the Greenhouse Glass

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: environmental-disaster epics, inspirational community speeches, and countdowns to a preventable crisis*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A sudden cold snap threatens a neighborhood greenhouse, but the mayor's disaster plan centers on a dramatic speech while the gardeners need insulation and a working thermostat.

Cast: Imani, an orangutan greenhouse keeper; Mayor Plink, a squirrel; Varo, a macaque plumber; Pip, a capuchin student; and Lute, a tortoise weather observer.

SCENE 1. INT. GREENHOUSE, MORNING

The forecast predicts a hard freeze overnight. Imani checks the thermometer. The heater is off. Mayor Plink arrives with a podium and a banner reading LAST STAND FOR THE LETTUCE.

IMANI: The seedlings need warmth.

PLINK: They need hope.

VARO: They need the heater fixed.

Varo checks the thermostat and finds a loose wire. He turns off the power before repairing it. Plink asks whether this is the countdown moment. Lute says the freeze is not until midnight.

SCENE 2. INT. TOWN HALL, AFTERNOON

The mayor convenes an emergency meeting. He assigns Pip to give an inspirational message to the plants. Pip reads a poem. The plants do not react. Imani hands the volunteers a list: seal the north windows, lay straw mats, and move delicate pots away from the glass.

Plink asks if the list can be made more cinematic. Imani says the list keeps the greenhouse warm. He asks for a montage. The volunteers begin sealing windows while the mayor narrates each roll of tape. They stop listening and finish faster.

Pip wants to help but is handed the wrong end of a long insulation roll. He backs through the doorway, unspooling it across town hall and wrapping the podium like a parcel. Plink calls it a visual metaphor for community. Imani follows the strip and cuts it at the greenhouse door. She shows the volunteers how to overlap each seam so drafts cannot lift the edge. A volunteer asks whether the mayor should still narrate. Imani points to the window that needs sealing. He picks up the tape dispenser.

The town hall's supply closet contains one working extension cable, three ceremonial shovels, and a banner pole. Varo refuses to run a heater from the cable until he checks its rating. Plink tries to declare the shovels emergency equipment. Pip uses one to hold the door while the volunteers carry straw mats through. The actual work takes less time than the debate about naming a task force.

SCENE 3. EXT. GREENHOUSE, EVENING

Wind pushes at the doors. A pane rattles. Varo tightens the frame while Lute measures the temperature. The heater turns on, then off. The sensor is reading the warm ceiling. Imani moves it lower. Plink tries to deliver his speech through the glass, but the condensation fogs the banner.

The wind knocks a stack of empty seed trays into the aisle. Pip catches one, then another, until he is wearing them like a wobbly crown. Imani asks him to stack them away from the heater intake. He does, and discovers the intake was partly blocked by a volunteer's folded tarp. Varo clears it after switching off the unit. The heat resumes. Plink steps inside to help, dragging his banner behind him; it catches on the door closer and repeatedly rolls itself up as he speaks.

Lute reads the outdoor thermometer and reports a faster drop than forecast. Plink reaches for his podium notes, but the volunteers have used them to label the insulated pots. He tries to recover the speech without the pages. Pip asks if the speech includes what to do if the heater fails. Plink admits it does not. Imani points out the backup unit and assigns one volunteer to watch its indicator. The mayor writes the instruction on the back of the banner.

PIP: The plants can't read that.

PLINK: The volunteers can.

IMANI: They are moving the pots.

SCENE 4. INT. GREENHOUSE, NIGHT

The temperature drops. The heater holds steady. One volunteer notices a door left ajar. Plink orders a dramatic rescue team. Imani closes the door and checks the latch. The seedlings remain warm. Varo explains the backup heater will start if the main one fails. The mayor asks how long until sunrise. Lute says six hours.

The mayor offers everyone cocoa. The volunteers take cups and continue checking the windows. Pip gives one to a plant by mistake. Imani moves the cup to the table.

SCENE 5. INT. GREENHOUSE, DAWN

The freeze passes. The plants are safe. The mayor asks the news camera if this counts as an epic survival story. Imani shows the repaired thermostat and the checklist. Plink thanks the volunteers and removes the podium so the morning light can reach the plants.

The camera operator asks who led the response. Plink begins to step forward, sees the volunteers still carrying the mats, and holds the door for them instead. Pip reads the checklist aloud while Imani checks the thermometer. The temperature stayed above the seedlings' safe range all night. Varo demonstrates the repaired sensor to the reporter, who asks if the plants have anything to say. One leaf slowly unfolds in the warmth. Plink starts to translate, but Imani says it is just a leaf. He nods and lets the camera film it without a voice-over.

FINAL IMAGE: The banner is reused as a frost cover. The words LAST STAND FOR THE LETTUCE show through the fabric above a row of thriving seedlings.
