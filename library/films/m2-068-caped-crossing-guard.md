# The Caped Crossing Guard

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: superhero origin films, tragic backstories, and costume reveals before ordinary problems are solved*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A macaque crossing guard gains a dramatic cape after a traffic-light outage, but the neighborhood needs a working signal more than a masked hero.

Cast: Mina, a macaque crossing guard; Volt, a gibbon inventor; Mayor Plink, a squirrel; Koko, a capuchin student; and the mysterious Caped Crossing Guard.

SCENE 1. EXT. SCHOOL CROSSING, MORNING

The light turns green in every direction. Mina blows her whistle and stops traffic. Mayor Plink arrives, certain a supervillain is responsible. Volt examines the control box and finds a squirrel nest inside.

MINA: The wires are chewed.

MAYOR: A villain's nest.

VOLT: A squirrel's nest.

Plink points at the nest. It contains acorns and a tiny stolen bulb. Mina guides the students across by hand while Volt calls the repair crew.

SCENE 2. INT. VOLT'S WORKSHOP, AFTERNOON

Volt shows Mina a wall of prototype costumes. One has a utility belt with six capes, another includes a helmet shaped like a traffic cone. Mina tries the helmet; it blocks her view of the crossing. She switches to a reflective sash and a simple whistle. Volt says a hero needs a symbol. Mina points to the WALK sign.

KOKO: Does the cape have a name?

MINA: Yes. It is called a cape.

Volt tests the grappling hook on a training dummy. It pulls the dummy across the room and knocks over the costume rack. Mina catches a fallen vest and gives it to the dummy.

Volt presents Mina with a cape and a utility belt. He says the town needs a hero. Mina says it needs a new signal. The belt contains a flashlight, whistle, reflective sash, and a grappling hook that attaches to the workshop door.

KOKO: Can you fly?

MINA: No. I can stop traffic.

The cape gets caught in the door. Mina removes it and tests the whistle. The sound is strong enough to make a stack of bolts fall into a neat row. Volt calls it an origin power.

SCENE 3. EXT. SCHOOL CROSSING, NEXT MORNING

A delivery truck blocks the crossing. Mina wears the cape. She tries a dramatic leap onto the hood but lands beside it, safely on the sidewalk. The driver asks if he is in trouble. Mina points to the no-parking sign. He moves the truck. Students applaud.

Mayor Plink asks her to reveal her secret identity. Mina says it is on her badge. The cape hides the badge. She pins it to the outside. Plink says the reveal is underwhelming. Mina says the crossing is clear.

SCENE 4. INT. TRAFFIC CONTROL ROOM, DAY

Plink asks Volt to identify the villain by analyzing the damage. Volt projects a dramatic scan of the squirrel nest. Mina sees a loose wire chewed near the edge. The scan labels it as unknown energy source. Volt removes the label and writes wire damage. Plink asks if they can leave the red warning light on for the press. Mina says it is a warning, not decoration.

Volt and Mina inspect the signal system. The backup battery is dead. Plink insists the villain must be stopped before the lunch rush. Volt removes the battery; the panel begins beeping. Plink assumes the machine is counting down. Mina reads the label: LOW POWER.

Koko brings a charged battery from the school office. Volt installs it. The lights return to normal. The squirrel nest is moved to a nearby tree with help from a wildlife volunteer. Plink asks if this counts as the third act. Mina says it counts as maintenance.

SCENE 5. EXT. SCHOOL CROSSING, AFTERNOON

The repaired signal is tested. Mina presses the walk button and waits for the signal. Plink starts to cross early for the camera. She stops him with one finger. He waits. The students cross safely and cheer when the walk symbol lights.

Mina leads the students across with the repaired signals. Plink prepares to announce the hero's victory. She asks him to hold the sign while Volt tightens the pole. Plink does. A gust lifts the cape over his face, and he accidentally directs traffic in a circle. Mina lifts the cape and repositions him.

Mina takes the cape off and hangs it on the sign post as a visibility marker.

The whistle hangs beside it.

The principal hangs a new schedule by the crossing.

It works on first bell.

FINAL IMAGE: Mina hangs the cape on the crossing sign so drivers can see it. The reflective fabric shines brightly. Plink says it looks heroic. Mina says it looks visible.
