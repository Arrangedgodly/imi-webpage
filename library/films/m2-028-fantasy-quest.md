# The Errand of the Eldest Acorn

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: fantasy quest tropes, prophecy language, and fellowship logistics*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A troop of young monkeys sets out to return an ancient acorn to its sacred oak, but the prophecy is mostly directions written by a squirrel who forgot to include a map legend.

Cast: Luma, a langur chosen by prophecy; Berrit, a squirrel archivist; Dodo, a capuchin who packs too much; Oona, a macaque cartographer; and the guardian owl, Wold.

SCENE 1. INT. OLD LIBRARY, MORNING

Berrit reads from a scroll. It says the eldest acorn must be carried east until the path divides, then placed beneath the tree that remembers.

LUMA: Which tree?

BERRIT: The one that remembers.

OONA: What does it remember?

BERRIT: Trees don't talk.

Dodo packs a frying pan, blanket, three jars, and a small stool. Luma says the quest needs only the acorn. Dodo says the prophecy did not mention weather.

SCENE 2. EXT. FOREST PATH, DAY

The group reaches a fork. A sign points east in two directions. Oona checks her map. The map has no legend. Berrit says it was written by the ancient squirrel cartographers. Oona says the ancient cartographers used the same handwriting as Berrit.

They choose the left path. It leads to a picnic clearing. Dodo unpacks lunch. Luma says they are on a quest. Dodo says a quest can have lunch.

SCENE 3. EXT. STONE BRIDGE, AFTERNOON

The bridge guardian, Wold, asks a riddle: what grows older while staying in one place? Dodo answers "a tree." Wold says correct, then asks why they carry a stool. Dodo says for resting. Wold says the bridge has benches.

The group crosses. The acorn rolls out of its cloth and down the bridge. Luma catches it. Wold says this is a test of destiny. Oona says it is a slope.

SCENE 3A. EXT. FOREST CLEARING, CONTINUOUS

A stone marker bears the words "Follow the oldest path." Berrit declares that the path is ancient. Oona points out it leads to a stream and appears to have been made by snails. Dodo asks whether the snails are wise.

A line of snails crosses the path, each carrying a leaf. The troop waits. Luma says the prophecy did not mention waiting for snails. Wold says every quest requires patience. Dodo sits on the broken stool. It collapses another inch.

Berrit checks the scroll again and finds a footnote hidden in the fold. The "oldest path" is the one the snails use, because they have lived there longer than the trail markers. Oona redraws the route around them. Dodo carries the stool pieces so nobody steps on the snails. The journey advances six feet in twenty minutes, which Berrit records as "substantial progress."

SCENE 4. EXT. ANCIENT OAK, SUNSET

They find a huge oak with carved marks. Berrit matches the marks to the prophecy. Oona points out the tree is full of acorns. Luma asks which is eldest. Berrit admits the scroll's author meant the acorn should be planted, not returned.

They dig a hole. Dodo uses the stool as a spade. The stool breaks. The acorn is planted. Wold appears and declares the prophecy fulfilled.

SCENE 5. EXT. OAK, NEXT MORNING

The group waits for a sign. Nothing happens. They leave a marker and return home. Months later, a sprout appears. Luma and Oona revisit it with watering cans. Berrit writes a corrected map legend.

SCENE 6. EXT. FOREST PATH, RETURN JOURNEY

The group walks home without the acorn. Dodo drags the broken stool.

LUMA: The quest is complete.

OONA: The map is corrected.

BERRIT: The legend is clear.

DODO: The lunch is gone.

WOLD: That is a trial.

DODO: Of hunger.

LUMA: We can stop at the clearing.

OONA: The left fork?

BERRIT: The right fork.

OONA: Which way is east?

BERRIT: Toward the sunrise.

DODO: It is evening.

WOLD: Follow the river.

LUMA: Does it go home?

WOLD: Eventually.

OONA: That is vague.

BERRIT: The prophecy was worse.

DODO: Can the stool rest?

LUMA: It is broken.

DODO: It carried us.

OONA: You carried it.

DODO: We supported each other.

WOLD: The stool is not alive.

DODO: It knows.

BERRIT: Trees remember.

OONA: That was a metaphor.

LUMA: The acorn will grow.

DODO: If it rains.

WOLD: I can summon rain.

OONA: Please don't.

WOLD: I won't.

BERRIT: The new map has a key.

OONA: And labels.

DODO: Does it mark food?

LUMA: It marks the clearing.

DODO: Good enough.

WOLD: The forest path is clear.

OONA: We should still bring a compass.

BERRIT: And a ruler.

DODO: And lunch.

LUMA: No stool.

DODO: We'll see.

They arrive at the clearing. Dodo's pack opens, and one jar of beans rolls to the path. Everyone watches it stop beside the map's new "food" symbol.

FINAL IMAGE: Dodo stands guard beside the tiny sprout, holding the broken stool like a ceremonial staff. Wold asks if he is now a guardian. Dodo says he is waiting for lunch.
