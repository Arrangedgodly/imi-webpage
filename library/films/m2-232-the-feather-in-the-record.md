# The Feather in the Record

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom dramas, forensic evidence close-ups, surprise witnesses, and a judge who wants the case to fit the exhibit labels*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
JUDGE LARK, a gibbon
SOL, a capuchin defense advocate
KIRI, a macaque witness
PIP, a ring-tailed lemur court clerk

SCENE 1. INT. COURTROOM - MORNING

A single blue feather rests beneath a glass dome. The trial concerns who moved the town's parade banner. Judge Lark enters. Pip, the court clerk, places the feather on the evidence table, then places the table beside the witness stand.

JUDGE LARK: The court will hear the matter of the missing banner.

SOL: The banner was found.

JUDGE LARK: Then the court will hear the matter of who moved it.

Kiri, a macaque, takes the stand. Sol asks where she was when the banner disappeared. She says she was near the fountain, counting seeds.

SCENE 2. INT. COURTROOM - LATER

The prosecutor presents a photograph of the feather beside the banner pole. The court gasps. Judge Lark asks the clerk to enlarge the image. Pip moves the photograph closer to the bench.

JUDGE LARK: That is not enlarged.

PIP: It is closer.

Sol points out that blue feathers are common around the park. The judge asks for a blue-feather exhibit. Pip returns with a blue hat, a blue scarf, and one painted pebble.

SCENE 3. INT. EVIDENCE ROOM - AFTERNOON

Sol and Kiri inspect the evidence. The feather has a small stripe of yellow paint. Kiri remembers the banner was moved by the parade crew, who painted the pole that morning. The feather could have caught paint from the pole after the banner was lifted.

KIRI: It was not at the scene when the banner moved.

SOL: It was at the pole later.

Pip enters with the court transcript. He has written every gasp as "evidence noted." Judge Lark calls the court back into session.

SCENE 4. INT. COURTROOM - CONTINUOUS

The prosecution calls a surprise witness: the town's parade drummer. He says he heard a flap, then a thump. The courtroom imagines a dramatic theft. Kiri asks whether the drum was playing at the time.

DRUMMER: I was practicing the parade roll.

JUDGE LARK: Could you repeat the sound?

The drummer performs a long, impressive roll. A blue feather falls from his drum strap. Pip catches it and labels it Exhibit B.

SCENE 5. INT. COURTROOM - LATER

Sol asks the drummer to turn around. A small hook on the drum strap has a matching yellow paint mark. The banner's corner had caught on the hook during rehearsal and was carried across the square. No one stole it. The drummer releases it when the cloth snagged the fountain statue.

The judge examines the evidence dome. The original feather is still inside. Pip has placed Exhibit B beside it, where the two are clearly different sizes.

JUDGE LARK: The court finds the banner moved by rehearsal, not theft.

SCENE 6. INT. COURTROOM - AFTERNOON

The drummer apologizes and offers to repair the banner. Kiri is dismissed. Sol returns the feather to the park. Judge Lark orders the court to stop labeling every feather separately.

PIP: Should I file the order?

JUDGE LARK: File it under common sense.

Pip looks at the shelf. It has no such label.

SCENE 7. EXT. COURTHOUSE STEPS - AFTERNOON

Reporters wait for Sol and Kiri. One asks whether the feather has changed the justice system. Judge Lark exits behind them and answers that the court will continue to consider evidence carefully. Pip brings the evidence dome outside so the reporters can photograph the feather.

SOL: The feather is not the story.

PIP: The camera keeps asking for it.

The drummer arrives with the repaired banner. He hangs it on the courthouse rail. A gust blows it over the feather dome, making the glass look like a tiny parade float. Kiri lifts the banner free and folds it for the next celebration. The reporters photograph the repair instead of the evidence.

JUDGE LARK: The case is closed.

PIP: Should I label the photograph?

JUDGE LARK: Label it "banner."

SCENE 8. INT. COURT CLERK'S OFFICE - EVENING

Pip files the case papers. He places the two feathers in separate envelopes and labels one "Exhibit A" and the other "Exhibit B." The blue hat and painted pebble go into the evidence drawer. He finds an empty folder marked COMMON SENSE.

JUDGE LARK: Do not file the hat there.

PIP: It has been very helpful.

The judge signs the final order. Sol checks the corrected record before leaving. Kiri carries the banner outside, where the parade crew is waiting. The feather remains on the sill, caught under the corner of the open file.

FINAL IMAGE: The blue feather floats out the open courtroom window while the judge watches it go without calling it as a witness.
