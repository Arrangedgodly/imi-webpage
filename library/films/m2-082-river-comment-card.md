# The River Has a Comment Card

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: grand environmental documentaries, ominous narration, and human problems framed as a nature spectacle*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A film crew arrives to document a river's mysterious disappearance, then learns the water has been rerouted through a town's neglected irrigation gate.

Cast: Mara, an orangutan cinematographer; Venn, a macaque narrator; Sol, a beaver waterkeeper; Pip, a capuchin reporter; and the town's volunteer dam crew.

SCENE 1. EXT. DRY RIVERBED, DAWN

Mara frames cracked mud. Venn speaks in a grave whisper about a vanishing waterway. Pip walks into shot carrying a bucket from the town fountain. Sol arrives with a wrench and asks whether they have checked the upstream gate.

VENN: The river has abandoned us.

SOL: It is behind the gate.

Venn asks Sol to repeat that more mysteriously. Sol points uphill. The crew follows the channel to a locked irrigation gate, its schedule board covered in faded notices.

SCENE 2. EXT. IRRIGATION GATE, MORNING

Sol shows them the gate is stuck half-closed. The water has been diverted into a side channel that ends at a field. A volunteer crew arrives with tools, but no one has the key to the control cabinet. Pip reads the posted contact number. The person with the key is at the feed store and can arrive in twenty minutes.

Venn records a narration about a civilization at the brink. Mara lowers the camera when a volunteer asks for help carrying sandbags. The crew stacks them at the channel edge to keep the water from flooding the footpath when the gate opens. Pip photographs the schedule so the town can replace it.

The volunteers disagree about which side channel to close. Sol walks the route with them and traces the water with a stick. One channel feeds the school garden; the other has a broken screen and carries leaves into a neighbor's pond. The crew finds the old gate schedule on a post, where rain has blurred the dates. Pip holds a clear plastic sheet over it so Mara can photograph the remaining marks.

Venn asks the volunteers to stand in a line and look toward the horizon for a scene about shared responsibility. They keep moving sandbags while he waits. Mara films the real work: one volunteer holds a bag while another ties it, and Pip writes down the time the gate last moved. The narrator tries the shot again. This time he holds a sandbag too.

SCENE 3. INT. FEED STORE, LATER

The key holder, a tortoise named Sen, arrives with a box of replacement bolts. Venn asks whether the key has been passed down through generations. Sen says he has had it since last spring. Mara films him unlocking the cabinet. The lock sticks. Sol uses a drop of oil, waits, and turns the key without forcing it.

The control wheel has a painted arrow that is almost worn away. Venn asks the crew to wait for golden-hour light before they adjust it. Sol says the river does not know what golden hour is. The volunteers read the operating sheet and turn the wheel together. Water moves into the main channel. The crew watches for debris and checks the banks.

The first turn releases a trickle, not a dramatic surge. Sen checks the gauge and asks them to wait for the marked safe level. Venn whispers that nature is holding its breath. Sol says the gate moves slowly to avoid washing out the bank. Pip notes the starting gauge reading on the new board. After several minutes, the level rises within the safe range. The volunteers open the gate another notch and record the change.

SCENE 4. EXT. MAIN RIVER, AFTERNOON

The river reaches the dry bend. Pip celebrates, then notices the water is carrying branches toward a footbridge. The crew places a temporary screen upstream and removes the debris with a rake. Mara records the work, including the muddy boots, the bucket, and the volunteers passing tools. Venn's narration keeps trying to turn routine maintenance into an ancient struggle.

VENN: Against the odds, the people answer the call of the current.

SOL: We saw the schedule was wrong.

The town posts an updated gate rotation and assigns two people to inspect the channel every week. Pip asks residents to return comment cards about water levels. Venn asks if they can call them "messages to the river." Sol says they can call them comment cards.

One resident asks whether the card can report a leak. Pip adds a box for that. Another asks where to leave the card; Mara films Sol installing a weatherproof holder beside the schedule. Venn suggests a close-up of a paw placing the note like a message to the wilderness. Sol puts the note in the holder and shuts the lid so rain cannot spoil it.

SCENE 5. EXT. RIVERBANK, SUNSET

The camera crew films the river at normal speed. No one speaks over the water. Mara lets the shot run until a volunteer steps in front of the lens to retrieve a rake. Venn starts a closing narration, then sees Sol handing out the new inspection roster. He reads the roster instead.

FINAL IMAGE: A comment card is clipped to the gate notice: "Water is back. Please keep the footbridge clear." The river passes behind it without dramatic music.
