# The House That Sighed

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: haunted-house comedy tropes, spooky reveals, and creaking-house sound design*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A family of monkeys rents a famously haunted house to save on moving costs, but the ghostly noises turn out to be plumbing, furniture, and one ghost who is tired of being misquoted.

Cast: Etta, a practical macaque renter; Pipkin, her excitable young nephew; Mr. Hush, a ghostly owl who hates loud surprises; Uncle Daro, a gorilla who collects antique chairs; and Mrs. Bell, the landlord.

SCENE 1. INT. OLD HOUSE, NIGHT

A storm rattles the windows. Etta carries a lamp through the hallway. A long moan rises from the basement.

PIPKIN: The house is warning us!

ETTA: The boiler is warning us.

She checks the thermostat. It reads low. Uncle Daro drags a chair upstairs. The chair groans so loudly Pipkin hides behind the lamp. Daro sets the chair down. It gives one final squeak.

PIPKIN: It followed us.

SCENE 2. INT. KITCHEN, MORNING

Pipkin finds muddy footprints leading to the pantry. Etta follows them. They stop at a flour sack. Uncle Daro's shoes are dusted white. He says he came down for a midnight snack. Pipkin asks why he walked backward.

Daro demonstrates. His chair is wedged in the doorway, so he had to shuffle backward while carrying a pie. The pie is missing. A cupboard door swings open by itself. Etta finds the house cat inside, asleep against the hinge.

SCENE 2A. INT. BASEMENT, MIDNIGHT

The boiler gives a hollow clang. Pipkin enters wearing the colander as a helmet and carrying a flashlight backward. Etta turns it around. They watch the pipes vibrate. Mr. Hush floats through the wall, startling them both.

MR. HUSH: I was going to knock, but I don't have hands.

Pipkin asks if ghosts need blankets. Mr. Hush says no, but the basement is cold. Etta gives him a scarf. He wraps it around his shoulders and looks surprised that fabric can comfort him.

A second clang echoes. Uncle Daro appears on the stairs holding the antique chair. He admits the chair's loose leg keeps striking the pipe whenever he moves it. He sets the chair down. The boiler goes quiet. He picks it up again. The boiler clangs. Mr. Hush points at him.

SCENE 2B. INT. PARLOR, NEXT DAY

Mrs. Bell brings a group of prospective renters to tour the house. She has hidden the labels Etta placed by each noise. Pipkin secretly replaces them with larger labels. The group reads BOILER, CAT, and UNCLE DARO. Daro is asleep in the chair. One visitor asks if he is part of the haunting.

Etta says he is part of the furniture. Daro wakes and groans. Everyone runs. He stands, and the chair groans too. They all run faster. Mrs. Bell blames the house, while Mr. Hush follows at a leisurely float, asking whether anyone has seen his scarf.

SCENE 2C. INT. ATTIC, AFTERNOON

Pipkin finds Mr. Hush sorting the old repair requests. The ghost says he has been trying to get the landlord to fix the roof since the house was built. Mrs. Bell reads the dates and is embarrassed. She says she did not know the files existed. The papers are in the trunk she never opened.

Together they list the needed work: roof tiles, boiler, wiring, one chair leg, and a cat door. Mr. Hush adds "quiet hours" for ghosts. Mrs. Bell agrees to the repairs but asks that the house stop scaring the tenants. Mr. Hush says he has been trying to stop the boiler from doing that for decades.

SCENE 3. INT. ATTIC, AFTERNOON

The family hears chains dragging overhead. They climb the stairs carrying a broom, a colander, and a flashlight. The attic door opens slowly. Inside, Mr. Hush floats beside a trunk, dragging a chain attached to his ankle.

MR. HUSH: Could you not bring a broom? I am a ghost, not dust.

Pipkin asks if he will haunt them. Mr. Hush says he is trying to nap. The chain is tangled around a trunk latch. Etta frees it. The trunk opens, revealing old curtains and a stack of unpaid repair requests from the landlord.

SCENE 4. INT. LIVING ROOM, NIGHT

Mrs. Bell arrives to inspect the house. The family explains the sounds. She says the house is old and has quirks. At that moment, a portrait's eyes seem to follow her. Mrs. Bell turns it around. A loose nail lets the portrait swing from side to side.

The fireplace clanks. Etta finds a spoon inside the vent. The lights flicker. Uncle Daro switches on a lamp. Its cord is plugged into a faulty extension. The family fixes it. Mr. Hush quietly moves the extension away from the wall. Mrs. Bell insists that doesn't count as a ghostly act.

SCENE 5. INT. HALLWAY, LATE NIGHT

The family places labels near each noise: BOILER, LOOSE HINGE, CHAIR, CAT. Pipkin adds a label to the attic door: GHOST, PLEASE KNOCK FIRST. Mr. Hush knocks from inside. Pipkin jumps, then laughs.

Mrs. Bell agrees to repair the boiler and replace the extension. Etta asks for the rent reduction promised because of the haunting. Mrs. Bell says the house is not haunted. Mr. Hush floats past carrying the lease. Mrs. Bell lowers the rent.

FINAL IMAGE: Everyone sleeps peacefully. The old house sighs. Etta wakes, checks the boiler, the hinge, the cat, and the chairs. Mr. Hush points to Uncle Daro's stomach, which growls loudly. Etta puts a label on him: MIDNIGHT SNACK.
