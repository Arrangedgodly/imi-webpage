# The Summit Is a Water Tower

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: mountaineering epics that treat a local hill as an unforgiving wilderness and mistake route markers for omens*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-016*

Logline: A group of monkey climbers sets out to conquer the town's highest peak, only to find a marked public trail ending at the water tower.

Cast: Climber Pora, a macaque; guide Nemi, a gibbon; photographer Tavi, a capuchin; ranger Sulo, a lemur; town engineer Vara, an orangutan.

SCENE 1 - EXT. TRAILHEAD - MORNING

Pora tightens a pack strap. Tavi unfolds a topographic map covered with pencil arrows. Nemi points to a low hill beyond the trees.

PORA: The summit is hidden in cloud.

NEMI: That is fog over the reservoir.

SULO: The trail is two miles and well marked. Stay on the path.

TAVI: We have a flag for the summit photo.

Pora removes a folded banner from the pack. It reads CONQUERED.

SCENE 2 - EXT. FOREST TRAIL - LATER

The group walks uphill. Tavi takes a photograph of a root crossing the path. Pora kneels beside it.

PORA: A natural barrier.

NEMI: It is a root. Step over it.

The trail marker points right. Pora studies it, then rotates the map upside down.

TAVI: The map is now upside down.

PORA: North is a state of mind.

Nemi turns the map to match the compass. They continue along the marked route.

SCENE 2A - EXT. FOREST CLEARING - LATE MORNING

Tavi takes out a small altimeter and checks it against the trail sign. The sign lists the elevation and the overlook distance.

TAVI: The summit is three hundred feet above us.

PORA: The clouds make it look higher.

NEMI: The cloud is on the ridge beyond town.

Pora unclips a long climbing rope from the pack. Nemi points to the trail sign, which shows no rope route.

NEMI: We do not need that here.

PORA: A climber must be prepared.

SULO: Please keep the rope coiled. It can catch on the brush.

Tavi photographs a water station. The group fills their bottles and checks the map. Pora takes out the banner, but a gust sends it across the clearing. It catches on a low branch, where the word CONQUERED hangs upside down.

TAVI: The flag is already making a summit statement.

Pora climbs one step onto a rock to retrieve it. The branch bends, and the flag drops directly into his pack. He closes the pack and continues on foot.

PORA: The mountain has accepted my offering.

NEMI: It fell into your backpack.

SCENE 3 - EXT. WATER TOWER ROAD - MIDDAY

The path opens onto a paved service road. A water tower rises above the hill. Pora stares up as if facing a cliff.

PORA: The final wall.

Vara arrives in a maintenance cart with tools. She parks beside the tower gate.

VARA: The access ladder is locked. This is a water utility, not a climbing route.

NEMI: We are following the public trail to the overlook.

VARA: The overlook is on the other side of the tower. The trail continues around the fence.

Tavi frames the water tower in a dramatic shot. Pora plants the flag at the roadside curb, then poses as if he has crossed an ice shelf.

SCENE 4 - EXT. OVERLOOK - MOMENTS LATER

The group reaches the overlook. The town, reservoir and water tower are visible below. A low cloud drifts past the far ridge. Tavi sets up the camera beside a clear trail marker and an open sturdy wooden map stand.

TAVI: The flag is upside down.

PORA: It is a symbol of our uncertain ascent.

NEMI: It is folded backward. Turn it around.

Pora tries to unfold it in the breeze. The banner wraps around the camera tripod. Tavi holds the tripod steady while Nemi untangles the flag.

SULO: The overlook is open to the public until sunset. Please take your gear with you.

SCENE 5 - EXT. TRAILHEAD - AFTERNOON

The climbers descend on the same path. Pora carries the folded banner. Tavi reviews the photographs: the root, the road, the tower and the view. None resembles a dangerous expedition.

PORA: We have reached the summit and returned safely.

NEMI: We followed a public path.

TAVI: The pictures still look good.

Vara gives them a small map of the reservoir trail. It includes distances, water stations and accessible paths.

VARA: The next hike can be longer. No need to climb the tower.

Pora folds the map correctly and places it in the pack. The flag is now right side up.

FINAL IMAGE: The group poses at the trailhead beneath a sign reading WATER TOWER LOOP, 2 MILES, while their upside-down summit flag hangs neatly from a backpack.
