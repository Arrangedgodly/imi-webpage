# The Life of a Very Accurate Clock

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: reverent biopics, lifelong genius montages, and achievements credited to one person instead of a working team*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A filmmaker tries to portray a famous clockmaker as a solitary genius, but the clockmaker's workshop records keep adding apprentices, suppliers, and repairers to the story.

Cast: Runa, a macaque filmmaker; Ivo, an orangutan clockmaker; Dali, a lemur archivist; and Fen, a capuchin apprentice.

SCENE 1. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM, MORNING

Runa reviews a script about Ivo, whose clocks still keep time across the town. The opening shot shows him alone in a dark workshop. Dali gives her the workshop ledger. It lists apprentices, suppliers, and shared repairs.

RUNA: Can we shoot the first gear alone?

DALI: It was installed by the whole shop.

IVO: Fen held the housing while I aligned it.

FEN: You asked me to turn the wheel.

Runa flips to the first workshop page. The entry has four signatures. She circles Ivo's name, then adds the others to her shot list. Nib asks whether the film can still have a hero. Dali says Ivo made the clock. The ledger shows how.

RUNA: The film needs one defining genius.

DALI: The ledger has twelve names.

Ivo examines a clock on the table. Fen winds it. Runa asks him to wait until the camera frames the master. The clock stops when Fen lets go.

IVO: It needs winding.

FEN: Every day.

Runa changes the script title from "The Hand of Time" to "The Clockmaker."

SCENE 2. INT. WORKSHOP, DAY

Runa films a montage of Ivo sketching gears. Fen brings him the wrong pencil. Ivo asks for the one with the softer lead. Runa asks whether the wrong pencil matters to the story. Ivo says it matters to the drawing. Fen brings the right one. Ivo sketches a gear; Fen measures the curve against a brass template.

RUNA: Did you invent that shape?

IVO: The shop tested it.

Dali brings a supplier invoice. Ivo checks the brass batch and rejects it because the teeth are uneven. Runa asks if the refusal came by instinct. Ivo shows her the measurement marks. Fen reads the tolerance while Nib writes it on the slate. Dali brings a supplier invoice. Ivo checks the brass batch and sends it back because the gear teeth are uneven.

RUNA: Can we say you rejected the shipment by instinct?

IVO: I measured it.

Dali shows Runa a repair book with notes in three handwritings. Ivo identifies Fen's entry: "Escapement too tight." Fen says he loosened it after Ivo asked him to listen to the ticking. Runa wants a close-up of Ivo's hand. Fen's hand enters the frame to hold the gear steady.

SCENE 3. INT. TOWN CLOCK ROOM, AFTERNOON

The film crew visits a public clock that has stopped. Ivo opens the panel. Runa asks him to deliver a line about his legacy. He notices a loose pendulum screw and asks Fen for the correct driver. Dali reads the last service note. It says the clock was checked six months ago by the maintenance crew.

Runa starts a dramatic score. Ivo asks her to stop; he needs to hear the gears. Fen holds the lamp. Dali records the screw size. Ivo tightens it, then waits. The clock begins to tick. Runa asks if he wants to claim the repair. Ivo says the maintenance crew kept it running, and he fixed one loose screw.

The apprentice asks how to learn clock repair.

IVO: Start with the repair book.

FEN: I can show you the escapement.

The apprentice leans near the case, but the score covers the ticking. Runa switches off the music. Fen holds the lamp while the apprentice enters a note in the log.

An apprentice asks how to learn clock repair. Ivo points to the repair book. Fen offers to show the apprentice how to check the escapement. Runa films them working together. She asks whether the scene needs a narrator. Dali says the notes explain what they are doing.

SCENE 4. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM, EVENING

Runa screens the cut. The film follows the ledger, the workshop, the rejected brass, and the public-clock repair. Ivo is still the central subject, but the supporting names remain on screen. Runa asks if the story feels too ordinary.

FEN: The clock runs.

DALI: The records say how.

Runa keeps both lines. She adds the names of the apprentices and suppliers to the credits, then asks Ivo to verify the dates. He checks the ledger and corrects one year.

RUNA: Does the correction weaken the story?

DALI: It makes the date right.

IVO: Keep it.

Fen signs the workshop credit. Runa includes the apprentices and suppliers. Ivo points out one missing supplier, and she adds the name before export.

FINAL IMAGE: The town clock ticks above the archive door. In the film's last frame, the camera holds on the many signatures in the repair book.
