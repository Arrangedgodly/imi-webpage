# The Deadline at Lantern Post

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: newsroom thrillers, ticking print deadlines, competing headlines, and a reporter who follows the sound instead of the rumor*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
MIRA, a capuchin reporter
JAX, a macaque photographer
EDDA, a gibbon editor
UNCLE FERN, a ring-tailed lemur bridge keeper

SCENE 1. INT. LANTERN POST NEWSROOM - NIGHT

A wall clock ticks toward the paper's midnight deadline. Editor Edda stands over a headline board. Mira, a capuchin reporter, enters with an empty notebook.

EDDA: The headline is "GLOWING BEAST SEEN BY RIVER."

MIRA: I saw lights near the bridge. I did not see a beast.

EDDA: Then find the beast before the presses start.

Jax, a macaque photographer, loads a camera with flash powder. He has taken a picture of a lantern, but the flash makes it look like a sun.

JAX: It could be a beast.

MIRA: It could be a lantern.

EDDA: Either one sells papers.

SCENE 2. EXT. RIVER WALK - NIGHT

Mira and Jax follow a row of small lights along the river. Jax takes photographs of shadows; each looks more monstrous than the last. Mira notices the lights move in a steady rhythm, stopping whenever a gust blows.

MIRA: A beast wouldn't keep time with the wind.

JAX: Maybe it is a musical beast.

They find Uncle Fern, the bridge keeper, carrying storm lanterns to the far bank. He has set them down to fix a loose plank. Behind him, the lanterns make a long animal-shaped shadow on the wall.

FERN: The bridge is closed until I replace this board.

EDDA, by telephone: Did you find the beast?

MIRA: I found a broken plank.

EDDA: Can it roar?

SCENE 3. INT. NEWSROOM - LATER

Mira returns with the facts. Edda says a broken bridge is not exciting enough for the front page. Jax develops his pictures in a darkroom. One photograph shows an enormous shadow with two glowing eyes.

JAX: This one says beast.

MIRA: The shadow is the lantern rack.

EDDA: The photograph is better than the truth.

Mira looks at the clock. The presses will start in twenty minutes. She draws the bridge, the rack, and the loose plank on the headline board.

MIRA: The lights warned the night walkers not to cross. The shadow is the rack. The real story is that people kept trying to cross anyway.

SCENE 4. EXT. RIVER BRIDGE - NIGHT

Mira and Jax return to photograph the repair. Uncle Fern holds the lanterns while Mira tests the plank. It bends under her weight. Jax spots a family approaching from the far bank and flashes the camera to signal them to wait.

JAX: The flash looks like a beast.

MIRA: Then point it at the safe path.

The family stops. Uncle Fern braces the plank with a spare beam. Mira and Jax help him secure it. The shadow shrinks as they move the lanterns closer to the bridge.

SCENE 5. INT. NEWSROOM - MIDNIGHT

Edda reads Mira's story as the press operator waits. The headline reads "RIVER BRIDGE CLOSED: LANTERNS KEEP WALKERS SAFE." It is less dramatic than the beast headline. The first copies roll out. A reader calls to say the bridge warning kept them from falling.

EDDA: The truth has good timing.

MIRA: It had better timing than our headline.

Jax pins the monster photograph to the wall with a note: "Lantern rack, not beast." Edda asks him to keep the image for a later story about shadows.

SCENE 6. EXT. RIVER BRIDGE - DAWN

The first copies arrive at the riverside kiosk. Uncle Fern reads the article while standing beside the repaired plank. A group of early walkers follows the lanterns instead of the headline. Mira watches from the newsroom window across the street.

FERN: The story says the bridge is closed until the beam sets.

MIRA: The beam is holding the board in place.

FERN: Then the headline could have been clearer.

Jax develops one more photograph. It shows Fern carrying lanterns, with the same enormous shadow behind him. Edda wants to run it as a follow-up, but Mira asks Fern first. He agrees if the caption names the lantern rack.

SCENE 7. INT. NEWSROOM - MORNING

Edda puts the corrected caption under the picture. The press operator carries a stack of papers to the kiosk. Mira writes down the bridge's reopening time and gives it to Jax, who photographs the sign instead of the shadow. Edda pins the updated closing time to the headline board and crosses out the word "beast."

FINAL IMAGE: The first newspaper lands on a kiosk beside the river. Its headline is reflected in the glass, while the lanterns behind it make one last, harmless beast-shaped shadow.
