# The Great Night Train Lift

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: train-heist thrillers, elaborate timing plans, and crews who ignore the station's posted schedule*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A capuchin crew plans to steal a brass station clock during a midnight train stop, but the clock is a loaned prop scheduled for pickup and their timing diagram is wrong.

Cast: Rook, a capuchin planner; Tess, a macaque locksmith; Wicket, a lemur lookout; Mr. Bell, a tortoise station master; and the clock's owner, a baboon theater director.

SCENE 1. INT. ABANDONED PLATFORM, NIGHT

Rook unrolls a plan covered in arrows, measurements, and string. The crew will enter at 11:58, lift the clock, and leave before the train arrives at midnight. Wicket checks the schedule: the train arrives at 11:52.

ROOK: It cannot arrive before midnight.

WICKET: The board says six minutes ago.

Tess asks whether the clock belongs to the station. Rook says it is valuable. Mr. Bell walks up and says it belongs to the theater company; the station borrowed it for a display.

SCENE 2. INT. STATION CLOCK ROOM, CONTINUOUS

Rook unfolds a blueprint showing three routes: roof, tunnel, and employee corridor. Tess says the roof ladder is locked and the tunnel is a ventilation duct. Wicket asks if they could use the front door. Rook refuses because a heist needs a secret entrance. Mr. Bell opens the front door from the inside and points to the loading bay.

ROOK: That is not a secret.

BELL: It is not supposed to be.

Rook asks Tess to pick the lock anyway. She says the display case is open. He has been trying the wrong key on an unlocked latch.

Tess opens the locked display case. The clock is attached to the wall with two safety brackets. Rook has brought suction cups and a velvet bag. Wicket holds the flashlight backward and lights his own face.

TESS: The brackets are part of the exhibit.

ROOK: Then we remove the exhibit.

Mr. Bell hands them a loan form. Rook refuses to sign anything during a heist. The station master explains that the theater's driver is already on the way to collect it.

SCENE 3. EXT. PLATFORM, LATER

A train arrives. The crew hides behind a timetable. The driver steps off and asks where the clock is. Rook says it is not ready. The driver checks his clipboard and says the pickup time is now. The clock's owner arrives wearing a stage manager headset and asks them to use the loading door.

WICKET: We came through the loading door.

OWNER: That's the staff entrance.

The crew starts carrying the clock. Rook whispers for stealth, but the clock chimes every time they tilt it. Passengers turn to watch. Tess asks them to hold the door.

SCENE 4. INT. STATION LOADING BAY, NIGHT

Before lifting the clock, the crew rehearses the route. Wicket calls out each turn; Rook insists they move in silence. The clock chimes at every corner. Passengers begin keeping time with it. Tess asks Rook to stop covering the face; the clock is a timepiece, after all. Mr. Bell checks the loading ramp and wedges it so it cannot roll.

The clock catches on the doorframe. Rook orders everyone to lift on three. They lift on two. The clock tilts, the hands spin, and the bell rings. Mr. Bell reads the removal instructions; they must secure the pendulum first. Tess removes the small pin. The clock moves smoothly.

Rook looks at his elaborate plan and folds it into a protective pad beneath the clock. The crew carries the prop to the theater truck. The owner signs the return receipt. Wicket asks if they still need the velvet bag. The owner uses it to cover the brass face.

SCENE 5. EXT. STATION PLATFORM, DAWN

At dawn, Rook reviews the plan beside the now-empty display. Every red arrow leads to the loading bay. He admits the plan was designed for a theft that never happened. Tess says they still moved the object safely. Wicket asks if that makes it a successful heist. Bell says it makes it a delivery.

The crew leaves empty-handed. Rook asks if the operation was a failure. Tess says no one broke in, damaged anything, or missed the train. Mr. Bell offers them a job moving the station's real timetable board. They accept, but ask for a signed work order.

Mr. Bell offers the crew a receipt for their work. Rook asks whether he can frame it as proof of a successful operation.

FINAL IMAGE: Rook's plan hangs on the station wall as a timetable. The arrows accurately point to the loading bay, the exit, and the nearest coffee cart.
