# The Snack Parade

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: family animation conventions, talking-animal sidekicks, and sentimental life lessons*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A young squirrel and her anxious parent join a neighborhood parade to return a lost lunchbox, but the storybook narration keeps trying to turn simple kindness into a grand lesson.

Cast: Pipkin, a squirrel child who asks direct questions; Mama Moss, a hedgehog parent who overprepares; Tumble, a chatty pigeon sidekick; Grandma Fern, a turtle who runs the parade; and the lunchbox, which has a squeaky latch.

SCENE 1. EXT. MAPLE LANE, MORNING

Pipkin finds a lunchbox under a bench. The narrator describes it as a mysterious treasure. Pipkin opens it. Inside is a sandwich, a note, and a tiny drawing of a dog.

PIPKIN: It belongs to someone.

MAMA MOSS: We'll find them.

TUMBLE: Perhaps the lunchbox has a tragic past.

PIPKIN: It has a sandwich.

Mama packs a repair kit, a compass, spare socks, and three snacks for the search. Pipkin asks why they need socks. Mama says the road may be wet. It is sunny.

SCENE 2. EXT. PARADE ROUTE, LATER

Grandma Fern's parade begins. Floats move slowly down the street. Tumble flies ahead, calling himself the scout. He returns carrying a ribbon and no useful information. Mama follows the map. Pipkin follows the drawing on the note, which shows a dog beside a red wagon.

The narrator announces a heartfelt journey of discovery. Pipkin stops to ask a baker whether he recognizes the drawing. The baker points to a child walking with a dog near the back of the parade.

SCENE 3. EXT. PARADE TURN, MIDDAY

The child is separated from her family. She is a young raccoon named Jo. Pipkin asks if the lunchbox is hers. Jo says it belongs to her little brother, who is riding on the red wagon. The wagon is several floats behind them.

Mama offers the snacks she packed. Jo declines, then accepts one apple slice. Tumble tries to give her the parade ribbon. Jo says the ribbon belongs to Grandma Fern. Tumble returns it and claims the exchange was an official delivery.

SCENE 3A. EXT. PARADE FLOATS, CONTINUOUS

Mama Moss decides the lunchbox should be returned before the next float. She draws a plan on the back of the parade program. Pipkin asks what the arrows mean. Mama says one arrow marks the route and the other marks the emergency snack stop.

Tumble flies to the front of the parade to ask whether anyone has lost a lunchbox. He asks the marching band, the balloon seller, and a dog wearing sunglasses. The dog barks at the lunchbox. Tumble interprets this as a clue and circles the dog twice.

Pipkin notices the child's drawing on the note: the red wagon has a loose wheel and a ribbon tied to the handle. The parade has several red wagons, so they compare wheels. One wagon is carrying flowers. One is carrying a drum. One is carrying a sleepy raccoon child. Pipkin asks each family if they recognize the lunchbox. Mama Moss offers snacks to keep the children patient. By the time she reaches the last wagon, she has fed half the parade.

SCENE 4. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, AFTERNOON

The parade reaches the square. The narrator prepares to say that Pipkin has learned the true meaning of family. Pipkin returns the lunchbox to Jo's brother. The boy hugs the box, not Pipkin. The latch squeaks. The crowd laughs.

Mama Moss realizes she has packed the wrong map. She has brought an old route from last year. Grandma Fern explains the parade changed because the bridge is being repaired. Mama looks at her unused compass, spare socks, and repair kit. The lunchbox needs no repair.

SCENE 4A. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, CONTINUOUS

The storybook narrator announces that the friends have discovered the power of helping. Pipkin says they have discovered Jo's brother is hungry. Mama hands over the sandwich. The boy eats it, then asks if the lunchbox can be returned too.

Tumble wants a photograph of the reunion. He flies to the camera stand and accidentally starts a timer. Everyone rushes into frame, but Mama is still carrying her emergency kit. The picture shows Pipkin, Jo, and the lunchbox, with Mama's compass and spare socks covering the lens.

SCENE 5. EXT. SQUARE, SUNSET

Grandma Fern awards Pipkin a paper medal for helping. Pipkin asks whether it can be used to open the lunchbox. Grandma says no, but the latch is easier if you lift the corner.

FINAL IMAGE: The narrator describes a warm family moment as everyone shares sandwiches on the curb. Tumble takes one bite, gets mustard on his beak, and announces he has learned the importance of napkins. Pipkin hands him one without a speech.
