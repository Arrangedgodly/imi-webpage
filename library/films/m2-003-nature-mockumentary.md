# The Great Laundry Migration

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: nature documentary narration, dramatic wildlife framing, and overconfident field science*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A documentary crew follows a troop's annual migration to the neighborhood laundromat, where a narrator turns mundane clothing habits into epic survival drama until the monkeys stage a field correction.

Cast: NARRATOR, a grand-voiced unseen guide; Dr. Sela, a skeptical macaque field biologist; Puck, a young capuchin who hoards socks; Aunt Vee, a calm elder; and Ren, a camera operator who keeps losing focus to lint.

SCENE 1. EXT. APARTMENT COURTYARD, DAWN

Mist hangs over a row of laundry baskets. The camera creeps toward PUCK, who carries one striped sock in his mouth. The narrator whispers as though announcing a lion hunt.

NARRATOR (V.O.): At first light, the young male claims a rare textile prize.

Dr. SELA steps into frame with a clipboard.

SELA: It's his own sock. He dropped it yesterday.

Puck places the sock on a warm radiator. The narrator calls this a daring act of territorial placement. Aunt Vee gently takes it and folds it into a basket. Puck follows her, then steals the clothespin from her hand.

SCENE 2. INT. LAUNDROMAT, MORNING

The troop enters. The narrator describes a dangerous watering hole. A washing machine gurgles. Ren zooms in on suds. Sela explains the machine is on a delicate cycle.

SELA: Please don't call detergent foam a predator.

Puck spots a dryer turning. He presses his face to the glass and spins in place. The narrator calls it a courtship dance. Puck is trying to see if his striped sock is inside. Aunt Vee taps his shoulder and points to the laundry cart. The sock is folded on top.

A pair of socks tumble out of an open machine. The camera pans dramatically. The narrator announces a rival troop. Sela picks them up. They belong to Ren.

SCENE 3. EXT. CLOTHESLINE LANE, AFTERNOON

The crew follows the group outside. Puck climbs a low tree and hangs his sock from a branch. The narrator calls it a signal to distant kin. Sela says he likes the breeze. A gust blows the sock onto the camera lens.

Ren keeps filming through the fabric. The resulting shot is striped and abstract. The narrator describes a sudden storm. Sela removes the sock from the lens.

SELA: It's cotton. Not weather.

Aunt Vee organizes the laundry by owner. Every monkey retrieves a garment. Puck refuses to give up the clothespin. He uses it to clip a leaf to his tail. The narrator calls this an elaborate disguise. The troop ignores him and walks toward the courtyard.

SCENE 4. INT. COMMUNITY ROOM, EVENING

The footage plays for residents. The narrator's voice fills the room with ominous claims about territorial conflict, courtship, and survival. Onscreen, Puck carries a sock from a basket to a radiator, then returns for the other one.

Sela pauses the film.

SELA: The animal behavior here is called laundry.

She shows Ren's striped lens shot, the dryer close-up, and the tree branch sock. The residents laugh. Ren admits the focus was soft because lint got into the lens. Sela suggests they title the film honestly.

SCENE 5. EXT. COURTYARD, NEXT MORNING

The crew reshoots with Sela narrating. Puck carries a folded towel. Aunt Vee checks the label. The narrator begins to say something dramatic, then stops when Sela looks at him.

SELA: He's bringing a towel to the picnic.

Puck drops it in a puddle, sits on it, and looks pleased. The camera holds. The narrator tries one last grand phrase, but the troop has already moved out of frame.

Ren asks whether the narrator wants another take. The narrator proposes filming Puck at sunset, silhouetted against the apartment block, carrying the sock toward an unknown future. Sela says the sock is clean and the hamper is three steps away.

They set the camera low. Puck approaches, sees the lens, and turns the clothespin toward it like a tiny microphone. He chirps. The narrator calls it a warning to rival troops. Sela says it sounds like a request for a snack. Aunt Vee gives Puck a slice of cucumber. He drops the clothespin and eats it.

Ren captures the shot. The narrator asks for a dramatic close-up. Puck's cheek fills the frame, then the camera loses focus again. Sela checks the lens. A second sock has stuck to it. Ren peels it away and asks whose it is. Everyone looks at their own feet.

SCENE 6. INT. COMMUNITY ROOM, LATER

NARRATOR: The troop gathers near the food.

SELA: It's lunch.

NARRATOR: A ritual meal.

SELA: Sandwiches.

REN: The focus is sharp.

SELA: Good.

NARRATOR: The young one approaches.

SELA: Puck is asking for a napkin.

NARRATOR: A signal?

SELA: A napkin.

PUCK: Please.

NARRATOR: A rare vocalization.

SELA: He said please.

PUCK: And water.

REN: Keep rolling?

SELA: Yes.

NARRATOR: The elder distributes supplies.

SELA: Aunt Vee packed them.

NARRATOR: She provides for the group.

SELA: She made lunch.

PUCK: I dropped mine.

NARRATOR: A loss.

SELA: There are more.

PUCK: I found it.

REN: Under the chair.

NARRATOR: Discovery.

SELA: Pick it up.

PUCK: It's clean.

SELA: It is on the floor.

NARRATOR: The troop hesitates.

SELA: Don't eat it.

PUCK: I won't.

REN: He is eating it.

SELA: Cut the camera.

NARRATOR: The camera remains.

REN: Battery's low.

SELA: Let it end.

The camera dips toward the table. The narrator continues speaking over the blank lens until Ren removes the battery.

FINAL IMAGE: A title card reads THE GREAT LAUNDRY MIGRATION: FOUR MINUTES FROM BASKET TO RADIATOR. A sock slowly slides off the radiator and lands on the narrator's script. The crew leaves it there.
