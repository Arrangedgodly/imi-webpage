# The Strings Know Before the Cast

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: film scores that dictate emotions, foreshadow every small action, and swell over dialogue until no one can hear it*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A composer scores a quiet scene so aggressively that the actors have to wait for the music to tell them what they feel.

Cast: Toma, a macaque composer; Kira, a lemur director; Boro, a gorilla actor; and Puck, a capuchin sound editor.

SCENE 1. INT. SOUNDSTAGE, MORNING

Boro sits at a kitchen table with an envelope. Kira explains that he is waiting for a letter from his sister. The scene is quiet. Toma sits at a keyboard beside the camera.

KIRA: He opens the envelope and reads the note.

TOMA: What is the emotional journey?

BORO: It is a grocery list.

Toma plays a low cello note. Boro looks at the envelope. A choir enters as he reaches for it. Puck raises the music track to cover the rustle of paper.

KIRA: Can we hear the letter?

TOMA: The score says he is afraid to know.

BORO: It says "buy oats."

Toma stops. He asks for a second take with more uncertainty. Boro waits for the music cue before opening the envelope. The cue comes six seconds late. Boro has already read the list and folded it. Toma asks him to unfold it for the musical entrance. Kira tells him to put the page back the way he found it. Boro opens the envelope again and waits.

BORO: Should I feel hopeful?

TOMA: The strings are not ready.

KIRA: He can have an emotion without a cue.

Toma tries a short note. It sounds like a doorbell. Boro looks toward the hallway.

SCENE 2. INT. SOUNDSTAGE, LATER

Toma writes a new score with a separate theme for the table, the envelope, the chair, and the kitchen clock. Puck labels four tracks. The clock theme begins when Boro looks at the time. The envelope theme overlaps it. The chair theme starts when he shifts in his seat.

BORO: I have not stood up yet.

TOMA: The chair anticipates your choice.

Kira asks Boro to deliver his line. He says, "I will call her." The brass section swells over the words. Puck turns down the music. Toma asks if he can keep one horn for emotional support.

KIRA: He is calling about the shopping list.

TOMA: Perhaps the oats are a metaphor.

Boro checks the note. It lists oats, tea, and a replacement kettle. He says he can pick them up after rehearsal. Toma plays a triumphant fanfare. The kitchen clock chimes on the same beat. Puck loops the shot, and the fanfare repeats every time Boro glances at the clock. Kira asks for one version without the clock theme. Toma removes it, leaving a clean view of the hands moving.

BORO: I have rehearsal in ten minutes.

TOMA: The clock is counting down to your decision.

BORO: My decision is to leave on time.

SCENE 3. INT. SOUND MIX ROOM, AFTERNOON

Puck runs the scene with headphones. The music starts before Boro enters. Kira asks for silence until the envelope opens. Toma mutes the orchestra but leaves a single triangle ringing. Puck searches the tracks and finds the triangle is tied to the word "letter."

KIRA: The scene has no letter. It has an envelope.

TOMA: The triangle can represent the possibility of paper.

Puck removes the triangle. The room becomes quiet. Boro opens the envelope, reads the list, and puts it in his pocket. Toma asks whether the audience will feel anything. Kira asks what Boro feels.

BORO: I need to buy oats.

Kira asks him to tell his sister he will stop by. Boro calls her. She answers and asks him to bring tea. Puck records the actual conversation without music. The sister asks whether Boro is still coming by. He says yes. Toma hears her laugh through the speaker and reaches toward the keyboard. Kira shakes her head. Toma lowers his hand and listens.

KIRA: That was the scene.

TOMA: I can write a theme later.

Puck labels the audio "phone call, clean."

SCENE 4. INT. SOUNDSTAGE, EVENING

The final cut plays. Boro opens the envelope. The paper rustles. He reads, smiles at one item, and calls his sister. Toma sits at the keyboard without playing. Puck lets the room tone remain.

TOMA: It feels smaller.

KIRA: It is a small scene.

Boro tells his sister he will bring tea. She reminds him the kettle is broken. He writes it on the list. Toma reaches for the keyboard, then stops when the call ends.

FINAL IMAGE: The end credits list the score as "one soft note, used once." A shopping list lies beside the silent keyboard.
