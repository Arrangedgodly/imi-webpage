# Evidence on the Snack Table

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: crime procedurals that solve every mystery with a dramatic evidence board and a conveniently available expert*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A detective's evidence board grows so crowded that the witnesses must solve the case by finding room to stand.

Cast: Daro, a lemur detective; Kiko, a macaque lab analyst; Misu, a gorilla witness; and Puck, a capuchin clerk.

SCENE 1. INT. PRECINCT BREAK ROOM, MORNING

Daro enters carrying a paper bag. The break room table is covered in photographs, string, and index cards. A half-eaten sandwich sits in the middle. Puck has wrapped string around its crust and connected it to a picture of the station clock.

DARO: Why is lunch on the board?

PUCK: It was in the room when the clue was found.

KIKO: The clue is a wet footprint by the archive door. The sandwich is dry.

Daro asks for a clean surface. Puck slides the board three inches. Every paper shifts and the sandwich lands on a photograph of the archive key. Misu arrives for an interview and cannot find a chair. Daro offers a stool, but its legs are tied to the board to represent "the unstable witness."

MISU: I can stand.

DARO: Please don't. Puck will connect you to something.

SCENE 2. INT. BREAK ROOM, LATER

Kiko places a tray of dust samples on the table. Puck draws a red line from the wet footprint to the coffee machine, then to the sandwich, then to Daro's elbow.

KIKO: The print is water, not coffee.

PUCK: It could have traveled.

Daro makes Puck remove the line. The string catches a card marked "UNKNOWN SUSPECT" and flips it over. The back says "Daro, Tuesday." Daro notices a damp cuff on his own sleeve. He remembers carrying a leaking umbrella through the archive.

DARO: That explains the footprint.

PUCK: It explains one footprint.

KIKO: There is only one.

Puck looks disappointed and pins a second, unrelated footprint from an old case beside it. Kiko measures the tread with a ruler.

KIKO: This one is from a boot. Our print is from a bare foot.

PUCK: The suspect changed shoes.

DARO: The suspect is my umbrella.

Puck takes a picture of the ruler and labels it "expert scale." The ruler slips; the photo makes the print look longer than Daro's arm. Kiko removes the old photo. Puck replaces it with a magnified image of a comma from the witness statement.

Misu reads the statement aloud. She had asked the archive clerk to leave the door open because her hands were full of library books. The clerk did. No key went missing.

MISU: Are we investigating a crime or my punctuation?

DARO: At the moment, your punctuation has a better case file.

SCENE 3. INT. ARCHIVE HALL, AFTERNOON

The team walks to the archive. Puck carries the evidence board on a wheeled stand. It will not fit through the doorway. He removes the wheels; the board tips forward and all the string gathers around Daro's ankles.

Kiko checks the lock. It is intact. Misu points to a stack of return books blocking the door from closing. A note asks staff to move them before the afternoon cart arrives. The clerk left the door open to clear the cart's path.

DARO: So there was no break-in.

PUCK: There was an open door.

KIKO: Open is not the same as broken.

Puck writes "OPEN DOOR" in large letters and searches for a dramatic marker. He finds only a dry-erase pen with no cap. The ink fades halfway through the word. Daro asks him to stop filming the board and help move the books. Puck lifts the wrong stack, revealing a missing library stamp beneath it.

Misu recognizes the stamp. She borrowed it to mark a return slip, then left it on the cart. The cart has already gone to the reading room.

SCENE 4. INT. READING ROOM, EVENING

The team finds the stamp beside a stack of books. Puck asks Misu to stand beside it for scale. Misu reaches for the stamp, and Puck tells her not to touch the evidence. Kiko checks the receipt and confirms the stamp ink matches the return mark.

KIKO: The paperwork solved it before we arrived.

DARO: Good. We can go home.

PUCK: The board says the paperwork is a person of interest.

Daro takes down the card and puts the receipt in the file. Daro checks the return slip; it is signed and dated. The stamp was never stolen. Puck asks if they can still call the discovery a conspiracy. Kiko points to the plain receipt.

DARO: Close the case.

PUCK: Can we at least keep the board?

Misu unties the stool from the evidence board and sits on it. Daro writes the case number on the receipt and files it. Puck coils the red string and labels it EVIDENCE, despite Daro having closed the case.

SCENE 5. INT. PRECINCT BREAK ROOM, NIGHT

The team returns to the break room. Daro puts the filed receipt in the drawer. Kiko wipes the table. Puck drags the red string across the table and pulls the napkin dispenser onto the floor. Daro hands him a dustpan instead of an evidence bag.

FINAL IMAGE: The evidence board stands blank beside the break-room table, where the sandwich has been replaced by a sign reading "Please leave space for lunch."
