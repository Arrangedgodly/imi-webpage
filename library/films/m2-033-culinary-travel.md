# The Last Course Is the Ferry

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: culinary travel films, precious food narration, and hosts who mistake every meal for a life lesson*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A traveling macaque chef tries to serve a village dinner before the last ferry leaves, while a food documentarian keeps stopping the meal to describe its emotional meaning.

Cast: Mara, a macaque cook; Felix, a lemur food host; Anik, a village cook; Bo, a gibbon ferry operator; and Sedge, an elderly turtle who has reserved the last table.

SCENE 1. EXT. BELLWEATHER SQUARE, MORNING

Felix films Mara entering the village with a basket. He asks her to pause by a stone wall and look surprised. She is reading the ferry schedule.

FELIX: What does this place mean to you?

MARA: It means the ferry leaves at six.

Anik meets them with an invitation to cook dinner for the village hall. Felix calls it an unscripted encounter. Anik says the hall booked them last week and hands Mara the kitchen key. Felix keeps filming the key as if it were a relic.

SCENE 2. INT. INN KITCHEN, DAY

Mara and Anik plan a meal using local beans and greens. Felix asks them to describe the ingredients as if greeting old friends. Anik says the beans are dry and need soaking. Mara opens the pantry, finds the beans already soaking, and thanks the previous cook.

FELIX: Can you say that with more wonder?

ANIK: These beans are wet.

Mara chops greens. Felix zooms in on the knife. Anik moves the camera away from the fingers. Felix says the danger makes the cooking intimate. Anik hands him an apron and a pile of onions.

He chops one onion and cries. The camera captures tears. Felix calls it a breakthrough. Mara gives him a towel and asks him to finish the onions before sunset. He tries to narrate the smell; the onion makes him sneeze into the microphone.

SCENE 3. EXT. VILLAGE GARDEN, AFTERNOON

They collect herbs. Felix asks the gardener to explain what the soil remembers. The gardener hands him a shovel. Felix attempts a poetic answer and hits a buried watering pipe. Water sprays his shoes. Mara turns the valve. Anik rescues the herbs from the mud.

FELIX: The earth has spoken.

ANIK: It said you broke the pipe.

The garden's only mint patch is beside the ferry ramp. The ferry bell rings. Mara checks the clock and tells Felix they have two hours to cook. Felix wants a shot of the landscape at golden hour. Anik points to the cloud cover and gives him a basket to carry.

SCENE 4. INT. VILLAGE HALL KITCHEN, EVENING

Anik sets a place for Felix. He asks whether the empty chair is a symbol. Anik says it is a chair and moves it away from the stove. Mara hands Felix a basket of bread. He holds it up for the camera; the rolls slide out and land in the serving basket. The villagers applaud the efficient plating.

The pot simmers. Felix positions a small spoon beside it for a close-up. Sedge arrives early and asks when dinner will be ready. Felix asks what the wait means to him. Sedge says he is hungry and has a ferry ticket.

Mara serves a tasting spoon. Felix describes the broth as a bridge between land and memory. Sedge tastes it and asks for more salt. Mara adjusts the seasoning. Anik sets out bowls while Felix tries to film steam. The camera lens fogs. He wipes it with the towel he used on the onions.

MARA: That towel is not clean.

FELIX: It's been on a journey.

ANIK: It can journey to the laundry.

Sedge checks the ferry board. The last boat departs in twenty minutes. Mara serves the meal promptly. Felix wants one final interview before the first bite. The villagers begin eating without him. The sound of spoons replaces his narration.

SCENE 5. EXT. VILLAGE DOCK, NIGHT

Mara and Anik carry the remaining pot to the dock. Felix rushes after them with his camera and asks whether the dinner changed their lives. Sedge says it changed his evening; he missed the ferry and will sleep at the inn. Bo, the ferry operator, offers to take the empty dishes across on the morning boat.

Felix asks Mara for a closing reflection. She says the meal was good, the ferry schedule was clear, and they should return the borrowed pot. Anik asks if he got that. He checks the camera. The microphone recorded the villagers laughing around the table, but not his narration.

Felix asks Sedge to rate the meal on a scale of one to ten. Sedge says he would rather have seconds than a number.

FINAL IMAGE: Felix finally tastes the stew. He starts a sentence about memory, pauses, and asks for the salt. Mara hands it to him while Bo rings the ferry bell for tomorrow.
