# The Color Grade That Turned Everything Teal

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: color grading trends that impose a fashionable palette regardless of story, props, or skin tone*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-011*

CAST
COLORIST TAVI, a gibbon
DIRECTOR PIP, a capuchin
CINEMATOGRAPHER NIMA, a lemur
ACTOR ODO, a baboon
PRODUCER VEX, a macaque

SCENE 1. INT. COLOR SUITE - MORNING

Colorist Tavi displays a village market scene. Director Pip wants the warm colors of painted awnings and a bowl of ripe persimmons. Producer Vex taps a preset labeled TEAL DESTINY. The market turns blue-green; the persimmons look like small lanterns underwater.

VEX: It feels expensive.

PIP: It feels like the fruit is cold.

NIMA: The scene is set at noon.

TAVI: The shadows are now midnight.

Vex says the palette will unify the film. Odo, the actor, enters carrying a red scarf. The grade turns it dark teal. He asks whether he should still call it red in the dialogue. Vex says the color is metaphorical now.

SCENE 2. INT. MARKET SET - LATER

Nima lights a real version of the market. She adjusts a bounce card so Odo's face stays warm while the awnings remain colorful. Tavi applies the preset. The bounce card turns cyan, and the persimmons lose their red. Pip asks for a reference frame from before grading. Vex says references are how trends get trapped in the past.

ODO: My scarf is not red.

VEX: It remembers red.

NIMA: The character is trying to signal the rescue boat.

VEX: The boat will understand the emotion.

Odo waves the scarf. On the monitor it blends into the sea-colored sky. Tavi attempts to isolate the cloth with a mask. The grade leaves a thin red fringe around Odo's wrist, as if he is wearing a glowing bracelet. Vex likes the effect and asks for a close-up.

SCENE 3. INT. COLOR SUITE - AFTERNOON

Pip tests three looks: natural noon, teal dusk and a split grade that keeps the scarf red. Vex chooses the teal version because it matches the teaser poster. The scene's story depends on Odo finding the correct fruit stand by its red awning. Every stall now looks blue-green.

PIP: Which stand is the red one?

VEX: The one the audience feels is red.

ODO: I am looking at them.

Nima pulls the reference still beside the monitor. The awning is clearly scarlet. Tavi matches the grade to it. Vex says the frame no longer matches the trailer. He asks for a red halo around the correct stand. Tavi adds one; it looks like a warning marker. Pip asks to see the shot without any treatment. The market has texture and contrast again. The audience can identify the stand.

SCENE 4. INT. MARKET SET - EVENING

They shoot the signal scene with the natural grade. Odo waves the red scarf; Nima answers from a boat. Vex asks whether the boat can be teal. Nima says its hull is painted yellow so sailors can see it in fog. Vex asks for a teal reflection in the water. Tavi lowers the saturation slightly, keeping the yellow hull readable and the scarf red.

The image works. Vex asks what makes it cinematic. Pip points out the clear visual information: a red signal, a yellow boat, a market behind them. Vex says clarity is not a look. Odo waves the scarf again, and the fabric snaps in the breeze. Everyone sees the direction of the wind and the signal at once.

VEX: Could it still feel expensive?

TAVI: The lens cost more than the scarf.

Nima laughs. Pip keeps the take. Vex asks for the preset file. Tavi gives it a new name: MARKET AT NOON. Nima compares it against the camera report, and the warm bounce stays on Odo's face while the set keeps its true colors. Vex reaches for the TEAL DESTINY preset once more, then sees how clearly the scarf leads the eye to the boat.

SCENE 5. INT. SCREENING ROOM - NIGHT

The final scene plays. The market looks like noon, the scarf reads red, and the boat appears yellow through the haze. The audience understands the signal without dialogue. Vex watches for comments. A guest says the colors felt vivid. Vex says vivid is a difficult word to sell. Pip tells him to sell the film instead.

After the screening, Vex opens the poster proof. It is still teal, with a tiny red scarf in the corner. He holds it beside the frame. Tavi adjusts the poster's hue until the market looks like the movie. The scarf becomes visible from the back of the room. Vex asks whether they can call the poster a limited palette. Nima answers that it is the same market.

FINAL IMAGE: Vex carries the corrected poster past the fruit bowl; for once, the persimmons look ripe in both places.
