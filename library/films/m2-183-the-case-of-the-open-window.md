# The Case of the Open Window

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom thrillers, surprise witnesses, overruled objections, and a final reveal that changes the meaning of the central evidence*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
JUDGE VALE, a mandrill
ATTORNEY SENA, a capuchin
ATTORNEY GRIT, a macaque
WITNESS MOSS, a ring-tailed lemur
KIT, a squirrel monkey defendant
CLERK, a gibbon

SCENE 1. INT. CIVIC COURTROOM - MORNING

The gallery is full. A ceiling fan turns slowly above the judge's bench. At the defense table, Kit, a squirrel monkey, holds a potted orchid. At the center of the case is a cracked window latch from the Kettle City greenhouse.

GRIT: Your Honor, the accused opened the greenhouse window and let the night wind destroy the prize orchids.

SENA: The latch was already broken. The wind entered through an opening no one knew existed.

JUDGE VALE: The court will hear evidence before it hears another orchid metaphor.

The clerk places the latch in an evidence tray. It rolls to one side and opens the tray's tiny spring catch. Everyone gasps.

SCENE 2. INT. COURTROOM - LATER

Grit calls the greenhouse caretaker. The caretaker testifies that the window was closed at dusk. Sena asks how she knew.

CARETAKER: I pulled the handle until it clicked.

SENA: Did you check the latch?

CARETAKER: I heard a click.

The room murmurs. Judge Vale bangs a gavel. It bounces off a stack of rule books and lands in the clerk's lap.

JUDGE VALE: The court is in recess until the gavel can be recovered.

The clerk returns it. The judge strikes the bench again, this time with a spoon. No one reacts.

SCENE 3. INT. COURTROOM - AFTERNOON

Moss, a ring-tailed lemur who lives above the greenhouse, is called as a witness. He brings a feather, a seed, and a paper map.

GRIT: Did you see the accused open the window?

MOSS: No. I heard it swing in the wind.

GRIT: So you cannot say the window was already open.

SENA: Objection. The witness is being asked to identify a sound as though it were a person.

JUDGE VALE: Sustained. Sounds may be described. They may not be arrested.

Moss demonstrates the sound: a soft squeak followed by a sharp tap. The jury looks toward the ceiling fan. Its loose chain makes the same squeak and tap each time it turns.

SCENE 4. INT. COURTROOM - CONTINUOUS

Sena asks Moss to repeat the sound. He does. The fan's chain swings. A gust from the open courtroom window moves the paper map and the feather in the evidence tray. The feather lands on the latch.

MOSS: That's the draft from the east window.

KIT: The greenhouse latch made that same sound before the storm. I thought it had caught.

SENA: But it had not secured the window.

KIT: I should have tested it. The gardener was coming back to repair it.

GRIT: Then the storm, the loose latch, and a false click all mattered. None of them proves that Kit opened the window.

The jury turns. One courtroom window is ajar. The clerk runs to close it. The window refuses to latch, making the same click heard by the greenhouse caretaker.

GRIT: The court itself has an open window.

JUDGE VALE: The court is not on trial.

SENA: But the latch is identical. The greenhouse window could appear closed while its broken latch left it free to swing.

Grit calls a last-minute witness: the gardener, who enters with the missing latch plate. It was removed during a repair and placed on a bench, where it was mistaken for scrap metal.

SCENE 5. INT. COURTROOM - EVENING

The jury takes the broken latch into a side room. Judge Vale waits. The ceiling fan turns, tapping its loose chain against a light fixture. Grit checks the clock every few seconds. Sena asks the clerk to keep the courtroom window shut. The clerk uses a folded program to wedge it in place.

The jury returns. They have not found that Kit opened the window, and the latch plate confirms the greenhouse was unrepaired before the storm. Kit sets the orchid on the defense table and looks at the leaves.

KIT: I stayed in the greenhouse until dusk. The window was shut when I left.

GRIT: I accept the jury's finding. I was asked to prove who opened the window, and I proved only that it was open later.

SENA: The record can be dramatic without becoming certain.

Judge Vale examines the latch plate against the evidence. The screw holes match. The gardener admits he meant to reinstall it before the storm, but the greenhouse's resident tortoise had crawled beneath the bench and he moved the work to avoid disturbing it.

GRIT: The accused was not responsible for the wind.

SENA: Nor for the repair that was never finished.

JUDGE VALE: The court finds the accused not liable for opening the window. The greenhouse must be repaired before the next storm, and the gardener must stop calling loose hardware "temporary."

The jury applauds. Kit carries the orchid out carefully, keeping it away from the draft. The judge orders the courtroom window closed. The clerk pulls the handle; it clicks but stays open.

JUDGE VALE: We will add one more repair to the order.

Moss places the feather by the latch. The draft pushes it across the bench until it rests against the gavel.

FINAL IMAGE: The gavel, feather, and broken latch sit together in the evidence tray, all held in place by the same thin courtroom draft.
