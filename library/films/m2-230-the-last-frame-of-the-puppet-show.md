# The Last Frame of the Puppet Show

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: stop-motion adventure films, painstaking frame-by-frame movement, handcrafted scenery, and a hero who walks off set between takes*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
KIKO, a capuchin puppet performer
MOSS, a macaque animator
RHEA, a gibbon director
TUP, a ring-tailed lemur sound recordist

SCENE 1. INT. MINIATURE STUDIO - MORNING

A tiny cardboard city sits on a table. Kiko, a capuchin puppet performer, stands in a paper boat. Moss moves the puppet one finger-width at a time while Rhea watches through a camera.

RHEA: One frame. Move the boat a hair.

MOSS: Which hair?

TUP: I have several on the microphone.

The camera clicks. Kiko's puppet has moved a fraction. A small paper moon hangs over the city on a thread. The thread breaks, and the moon rolls across the set.

SCENE 2. INT. MINIATURE STUDIO - LATER

The crew tries to reset the moon. Kiko steps out of the set between takes to drink water. When the camera rolls again, his puppet is gone.

RHEA: Where is the hero?

MOSS: He went to the water table.

RHEA: The city is flooding.

MOSS: It is made of paper.

Tup adds a sound effect of rushing water. The cardboard river begins to sag under a spilled cup. Kiko returns, sees the boat floating in the real puddle, and hops onto a sponge.

SCENE 3. INT. PAPER CITY - AFTERNOON

The crew moves the boat frame by frame across the set. Kiko's puppet visits a paper bridge. The bridge collapses whenever Moss touches it, so the crew builds a new one from popsicle sticks.

RHEA: We need a heroic crossing.

KIKO: I need a bridge that holds.

MOSS: The bridge holds for one frame at a time.

They move the puppet forward. The paper bridge buckles. Kiko places a tiny folded napkin underneath as a support. The bridge stands. The crew shoots twelve frames before Rhea notices the napkin is bright yellow and visible in every shot.

SCENE 4. INT. MINIATURE STUDIO - EVENING

Rhea watches the rough cut. The hero appears to glide across the bridge, then stop to inspect a yellow square. She calls it a symbolic pause. Kiko says it is the napkin that kept the bridge up.

RHEA: The audience may see it as hope.

KIKO: The audience may see it as lunch.

Tup plays a soft drum roll. The crew decides to keep the napkin and give it a close-up. Moss replaces it with a smaller piece, then accidentally moves the hero one whole step forward.

SCENE 5. INT. MINIATURE STUDIO - NIGHT

The final shot requires the paper moon to rise above the city. The crew uses a thread, but the moon swings like a pendulum. Tup records a dramatic wind sound. The moon swings faster, knocking over the tallest building.

RHEA: Cut.

MOSS: The building fell between frames.

KIKO: It fell on the boat.

The crew repairs the city with folded paper. Kiko steadies the moon using both hands while Moss moves it a fraction at a time. The final frame shows the boat passing safely beneath the moon.

SCENE 6. INT. SCREENING ROOM - NEXT DAY

The crew watches the finished short. The hero crosses the bridge, pauses beside a yellow square, and sails under a moon that seems to wobble. The audience laughs at the napkin and applauds the moon. Rhea announces that the prop department deserves a credit.

MOSS: The napkin had the hardest role.

TUP: It held the whole city up.

SCENE 7. INT. SCREENING ROOM - LATER

The audience stays for a short question session. One child asks how the boat really moved. Moss explains the animator shifted it one frame at a time. Kiko demonstrates by moving it too far, and the boat jumps to the recycling bin in a single gesture.

RHEA: That is not the method.

KIKO: It is the method between takes.

Tup plays the recorded wind sound. The cardboard moon begins to sway in the screening room, still attached to its thread. Moss catches it before it hits the projector. Rhea asks the audience to clap for the prop team. The child applauds the napkin, which remains taped to the bridge. Rhea promises to leave it in the next cut. Moss writes "hero" beside the napkin in the prop ledger, and Kiko signs below it with a small boat-shaped mark. The audience asks whether the boat gets its own credit, and Rhea adds a second line for the paper. Kiko bows on the boat's behalf. The child asks for a tiny boat.

FINAL IMAGE: After the screening, Kiko carries the paper boat to the recycling bin, while the moon swings gently above an empty set.
