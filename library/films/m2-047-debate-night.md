# The Candidate With the Quiet Megaphone

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: election-night political dramas, debate zingers, and polling-room suspense*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A gentle lemur candidate refuses her campaign's attack lines and turns a neighborhood debate into a practical microphone repair session.

Cast: Mira, a lemur candidate; Odo, a baboon campaign manager; Tink, a macaque sound technician; Vale, a peacock rival; and voters of Maple Ward.

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY HALL, EVENING

Odo hands Mira a card. It says: MY OPPONENT HAS NO PLAN.

MIRA: Does Vale have a plan?

ODO: That's beside the point.

MIRA: It seems central.

The microphone squeals. Tink traces the cable to a loose plug. Vale strides to the podium and opens with a booming slogan. The audience covers its ears.

VALE: Maple Ward deserves a voice!

TINK: It has one. The speaker is feeding back.

Odo urges Mira to use her prepared zinger. She asks him to read the meeting minutes first. He admits he has not. Tink finds them under the sound board.

SCENE 2. INT. BACKSTAGE, LATER

The minutes show that Vale supported a crosswalk but opposed a curb ramp, pending engineering approval. The approval arrived months ago. Odo tells Mira to accuse Vale of blocking accessibility. Mira wants to ask why he missed the notice.

MIRA: Is the answer in the minutes?

ODO: The answer is on his face.

Tink hands Vale the notice. He reads it and admits he never saw the second page. He offers to call the engineer after the debate. Odo is disappointed; the prepared confrontation has become a task list.

SCENE 3. INT. DEBATE STAGE, CONTINUOUS

The microphone dies. Tink asks everyone to stay seated while she checks the cable. The debate organizer points to the clock. Vale starts to shout his answer from the stage. The audience cannot hear him. Mira steps down and asks the front row to move closer. The voters form a semicircle.

VALE: This was supposed to be a debate.

MIRA: It still is. We can hear each other now.

They take questions from each table. One asks about streetlights. Another asks who will pay for the ramp. A third asks whether the garden bench can face the garden. Vale answers with a slogan, then notices the audience waiting. He gives the actual answer: he does not know the cost and will check with the engineer.

SCENE 4. INT. COMMUNITY HALL, LATER

The organizer calls for closing statements. Odo slides Mira a fresh card with three attack lines. She turns it over and asks Vale to confirm the repair deadline. He calls the engineer on speaker. The engineer says Friday if the weather stays dry. Odo tries to stop the call, but the room hears the answer.

VALE: Friday, weather permitting.

MIRA: Put that on the notice.

Tink writes it on the board. The crowd asks for the streetlight date too. Vale checks, then adds Tuesday. Odo asks if they can announce it with a dramatic pause. The voters want the chair folding to start.

Odo slips Mira a card reading ATTACK THE PLAN. Mira turns it over and writes CHECK THE COST. Tink finds a spare microphone cable in a box under the stage. Vale holds the flashlight while she replaces the damaged connector.

TINK: Can someone test the mic?

ODO: Testing is not on the schedule.

MIRA: Neither is silence.

Vale taps the microphone. It works. The room cheers. Odo asks whether he can call that a campaign win. Tink says it is a repaired cable. The organizer asks both candidates to fold chairs while the votes are counted. One chair sticks in the doorway; Vale and Mira rotate it until it passes through.

SCENE 5. EXT. COMMUNITY HALL, NIGHT

Mira wins by a small margin. She asks the voters to help post the meeting notes and repair dates at the library. Vale calls the engineer and updates the curb-ramp estimate. Odo sorts the attack cards into a recycling bin. Tink plugs the megaphone in and labels it EMERGENCY ONLY.

MIRA: What counts as an emergency?

TINK: When nobody can hear the meeting.

The new council begins its first meeting from the floor rather than behind the podium. Vale brings the working microphone but no longer needs to shout. The voters read the posted streetlight schedule and correct one date together.

Odo folds the last chair and sets it beside the quiet megaphone.

FINAL IMAGE: The megaphone sits unused in its box. Odo tries to deliver a campaign speech to it; it stays silent because it is unplugged. Tink points at the cord. He plugs it in, and it squeals. Everyone chooses the floor again.
