# One Small Step on a Folding Ladder

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: prestige space epics that treat a small staged demonstration as a historic first, relying on solemn voiceover and visibly improvised effects*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-015*

Logline: A neighborhood theater tries to stage the first monkey landing on the moon, but the homemade lunar surface keeps revealing that the crew has not left the rehearsal room.

Cast: Director Pallo, a macaque; astronaut actor Viri, a lemur; effects lead Toma, a capuchin; narrator Dax, a gibbon; stage manager Ena, an orangutan.

SCENE 1 - INT. COMMUNITY THEATER - NIGHT

A gray bedsheet hangs from the ceiling. A painted cardboard moon sits on a folding table. Viri wears a silver cooking apron and a bicycle helmet with a paper antenna.

PALLO: Our film will capture the largest moment in history.

ENA: The audience is arriving in twelve minutes.

TOMA: The moon surface is dry paint. Please do not step on it until it dries.

VIRI: How will I take one small step?

DAX: In my voiceover, I will say, "The mission had begun."

VIRI: I am still on the rehearsal floor.

PALLO: We will cut around the floor.

The camera rolls. Dax speaks in a solemn tone as Viri descends the folding ladder. The ladder wobbles. Ena holds it steady.

DAX: One small step for the crew.

VIRI: I have not reached the moon.

PALLO: Keep going. The camera only sees your boots.

Viri steps onto the cardboard. It buckles with a hollow thump. He freezes. Toma rushes in to prevent the painted surface from tearing.

SCENE 2 - INT. THEATER BACKSTAGE - LATER

The crew reviews the footage. On screen, the ladder is visible in the reflection of a shiny poster board.

TOMA: We can tilt the board away.

ENA: Then the camera sees the fire exit.

PALLO: We can cover the exit with stars.

ENA: We cannot cover an exit sign.

DAX: The narration can explain that the lights are distant galaxies.

VIRI: The exit sign says EXIT.

Pallo scratches his head. The paper antenna falls from Viri's helmet.

PALLO: The antenna has separated from the spacecraft.

TOMA: It was held on with tape.

VIRI: I can hold it while we shoot.

ENA: You need both hands for the ladder.

SCENE 3 - INT. THEATER STAGE - MOMENTS LATER

Toma brings out a small black curtain and a bowl of flour. He sprinkles flour across the cardboard to imitate lunar dust.

TOMA: It will give the boots a visible track.

ENA: The floor is swept after the show. Do not throw flour into the ventilation.

Pallo crouches and blows across the surface. The flour covers the camera lens and the first row of seats.

PALLO: A dust storm.

TOMA: A flour cloud.

VIRI: My visor is fogging. I cannot see the ladder.

Ena takes the helmet off him and wipes the face shield. The paper antenna folds over her shoulder.

SCENE 4 - INT. COMMUNITY THEATER - PRE-SHOW

The audience settles in. Pallo calls everyone to the stage. He has removed the flour and propped the cardboard moon against a crate. Viri climbs the ladder again, this time with a spotter on each side.

PALLO: We will shoot a modest version. No claim of a real landing, no hidden exit, and no voiceover until the step is complete.

DAX: I can narrate the making of the scene.

ENA: After the audience sees the shot.

The camera rolls. Viri reaches the last rung, pauses, then steps down onto a gray mat marked MOON SET. The mat slides an inch, then stops against a sandbag.

VIRI: This is one small step on a folding ladder.

The audience laughs. Dax starts the voiceover, then stops and lets the line sit in silence. Pallo lowers his hand, and the camera keeps recording the quiet after the joke.

SCENE 5 - INT. THEATER LOBBY - LATER

The crew screens the footage on a small monitor. The shot shows the painted moon, the careful ladder and the mat. No flour, no covered exit, no false reflection.

PALLO: It is not a space epic.

VIRI: It is a film about making a space epic in a community theater.

TOMA: The audience liked the mat sliding.

ENA: We will add a sandbag before the next performance.

DAX: My voiceover can introduce the scene honestly: "The crew built a moon from cardboard, cloth and stubbornness."

PALLO: That is the film.

Viri returns the helmet to the prop shelf. The paper antenna falls again. Toma pins it with a clothespin.

TOMA: It is secured for the sequel.

FINAL IMAGE: A hand-lettered poster reads ONE SMALL STEP, followed by a smaller note: LADDER AND SAND BAG ARE THEATRICAL PROPS. The crew's reflection appears beside it in the lobby glass.
