# The Committee of One Hundred Doors

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom dramas, surprise witnesses, and objections used as plot machinery*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A monkey court must decide whether a remodeled treehouse belongs to the neighborhood or its original builder, while both lawyers keep calling witnesses who know nothing about property.

Cast: Judge Boro, a baboon; Kiki, a squirrel prosecutor; Vale, a macaque defense advocate; Nori, a lemur carpenter; and Puck, a capuchin bailiff.

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY COURT, MORNING

Judge Boro enters. Puck calls the case of the disputed treehouse. Kiki says the structure was built on shared branches and must remain public. Vale says Nori built it with her own tools and must keep it. Nori sits between them holding a tape measure.

BORO: Do either of you have a deed?

KIKI: We have a witness.

VALE: We have a witness to the witness.

Boro asks to see the treehouse. Both lawyers object to leaving the courtroom. He orders them to bring evidence instead. Puck puts a plastic model on the table. It has so many doors that it collapses under its own hinges.

SCENE 2. INT. COURTROOM, LATER

Kiki calls a woodpecker as an expert on branches. The bird taps the witness stand until Puck moves it away from the wooden wall. Asked whether the treehouse is safe, the woodpecker says it has never been inside. Kiki tries to establish that the shared tree is public. The woodpecker pecks a hole in the model and exits through it.

Vale calls Nori's neighbor, who says she heard hammering. Vale asks if it was lawful hammering. The neighbor says she was asleep. Kiki objects to the answer as unhelpful. Boro says it is the first reliable testimony so far.

Puck presents the permit. It is a drawing of a tree with a square house and the words "ASK THE NEIGHBORS FIRST." Both lawyers claim the note supports them. Boro asks who signed it. Nori raises her hand and says she drew it before building anything. She asked the neighbors; they agreed to a shared lookout platform. The lawyers interrupt each other with competing interpretations of "shared."

Kiki asks whether the neighbors agreed in writing. Nori points to the back of the drawing, but the court reporter has already filed it face down beneath the model. Puck lifts the model. The roof comes off and reveals a list of names, dates, and the agreed quiet hours. Kiki reads the names aloud; everyone present recognizes at least one. Vale objects that the writing is upside down. Nori turns the page toward the bench. Boro says evidence should be legible before it becomes dramatic.

The court reporter asks whether the list is the agreement or merely a shopping list. Nori identifies a note beside each name describing where the neighbor helped. One borrowed a saw; another carried lumber. Kiki stops asking if the platform is private and asks who maintains it. Nori says the neighbors rotate repairs, but she keeps the tool closet locked because the saws are sharp. Boro directs both lawyers to examine the safety rail before arguing further.

SCENE 3. EXT. TREEHOUSE, AFTERNOON

The court visits the site. Nori shows them the original platform, the public ladder, and a private tool closet. Kiki climbs the public ladder and finds a sign saying PLEASE WIPE YOUR FEET. She asks whether the sign implies ownership. Vale opens the tool closet and finds a dozen borrowed hammers, each labeled with a neighbor's name.

Puck tests the rail while Boro checks the plans. One door opens into the ladder path and blocks it. The lawyers begin objecting to the door. Nori removes the hinge pin, reverses the door, and opens the path. Boro orders the attorneys to help hold the panel while she fixes it. They comply, still arguing about precedent.

Kiki discovers the welcome sign is attached to a railing that wiggles. Vale reaches for a dramatic theory about hidden ownership, but Boro makes him hold the rail while Nori tightens its bracket. Puck marks the repaired spot with chalk. The woodpecker from court returns, lands on the sign, and pecks the loose corner. Kiki asks for the bird to be excluded as a repeat witness. Boro says the bird is a passerby and asks Puck to secure the sign.

SCENE 4. INT. COURTROOM, EVENING

Boro rules that the lookout platform is a neighborhood space, while the tool closet remains Nori's because it stores her equipment. The neighbors may use it by borrowing tools properly. Kiki asks for a separate ruling on the welcome sign. Boro says it welcomes everyone and confers no title.

Vale wants to appeal. Nori hands her the signed neighbor agreement, which both lawyers had overlooked because it was on the back of the model. It already sets a cleaning schedule and limits nighttime noise. Boro adopts it as the court order. Puck stamps the paper. The stamp leaves an ink circle across the tree drawing and a perfect dark moon over the shared platform.

The lawyers ask if there will be a dramatic final statement. Boro says the decision is final and calls the next case, a dispute over who owns the missing courthouse broom. Puck looks at his hand; he has been holding it throughout the hearing.

FINAL IMAGE: The neighborhood gathers on the platform. Nori hangs a small sign beside the ladder: SHARED VIEW, RETURN TOOLS. The lawyers arrive with the broom and a new argument about who gets to sweep first.
