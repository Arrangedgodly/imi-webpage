# The Important Thing Is Just Outside Frame

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: aspect-ratio gimmicks that change the frame to signal memory, dreams, or prestige while cropping out the useful action*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-005*

Logline: A crew keeps widening and narrowing the image to mark emotional shifts until the character's actual work disappears from view.

Cast: Peli, a macaque cinematographer; Oru, a gorilla actor; Vira, a lemur director; and Puck, a capuchin camera assistant.

SCENE 1. INT. REPAIR SHOP SET, MORNING

Oru enters a small repair shop to fix a clock. Vira tells Peli the present-day scenes should be narrow, while memories should be wide. Puck adjusts the frame guides on the camera monitor.

VIRA: The past felt bigger.

ORU: I have one clock to repair.

Peli narrows the frame until Oru's hands are cropped out. Vira asks for a close-up of the face. Oru cannot see the clock. Puck shifts the camera left; now the clock is out of frame.

PELI: We need the face and the clock.

VIRA: The frame is about his loneliness.

Oru reaches for a screwdriver. His hand disappears below the image. The crew hears a tool fall. Puck asks whether the narrow frame can symbolize the dropped screwdriver. Vira asks if the sound can happen offscreen. Oru bends down, and his head disappears below the image.

ORU: I have it.

VIRA: Hold for the memory.

Oru remains crouched while Peli changes the guides. The screwdriver rolls to the base of the camera stand. Puck retrieves it.

SCENE 2. INT. REPAIR SHOP SET, LATER

Vira asks for a memory of Oru learning the trade. The image widens. Puck moves the camera back, revealing the shop wall and a modern fire extinguisher. Vira asks to remove the extinguisher. Puck moves it behind the clock. Oru reaches for it when the tool drawer catches his sleeve.

ORU: It is still there.

Peli widens the frame again to show the drawer. The composition now includes the fire extinguisher, a light stand, and half the camera crew in a mirror. Vira says the memory should feel vast. Puck opens the loading door so the background extends into the alley. A delivery cart passes through the frame.

VIRA: That is not part of the past.

Puck closes the door. The room becomes narrow again. Oru asks whether he can finish repairing the clock. Vira says yes, but she wants the camera to show the memory before the repair. Oru asks which memory. Peli widens the frame. The mirror now shows Oru's younger double, played by a stand-in, adjusting the same clock. Both actors try to reach the same tool at once.

PELI: We need a mark for the handoff.

The stand-in passes the screwdriver to Oru. The frame cuts to the present, but the tool remains in the stand-in's hand. Vira tells him to hold the screwdriver while they test one more ratio.

SCENE 3. INT. REPAIR SHOP SET, AFTERNOON

Peli marks three frame sizes on the monitor. The first is narrow, the second wide, and the third nearly square. Vira says the square image represents a moment when Oru feels trapped. Oru stands next to the clock and turns its hands to the correct time.

ORU: It was six minutes slow.

Puck frames the shot. The clock fits, but Oru's face is cropped. He shifts the camera right. The face appears; the screwdriver drops out of frame. Peli asks Oru to hold both the clock and the tool. He cannot reach them at once.

VIRA: The audience can infer the repair.

ORU: They can see me not repairing it.

Oru places the screwdriver on the table and adjusts the clock. It ticks. Peli holds a steady frame wide enough to show the clock, Oru's hands, and the workbench. Vira watches the monitor. She asks if the shot feels big enough. Peli says the frame contains the work. Vira asks if they can shrink it for one last emotional beat. Oru tests the clock. It ticks once. The square frame crops the clock face.

ORU: We need the numbers.

Peli widens the image again. The numbers return, and the scene ends without a further crop. Peli says it shows the whole task.

SCENE 4. INT. EDITING ROOM, EVENING

The crew reviews the scene. The changing ratios make the memory, present, and imagined future distinct, but the repair only works in the wide frame. Vira asks whether the wide frame can stay for the ending. Peli says yes if the camera remains still. Oru checks the clock. It is on time.

FINAL IMAGE: The final frame holds the clock, the repaired hands, and Oru cleaning the screwdriver, all visible at once. Puck prints a frame guide for the set. Oru places it beside the clock and uses it to keep the repair visible. Vira asks him to repeat the adjustment once, without changing the camera.
