# The Choir at the Waterfall

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: orchestral prestige films that turn a small community performance into a cosmic event*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-006*

Logline: A macaque conductor tries to stage a grand chorus beside a waterfall, but the ensemble keeps losing its notes to the landscape.

Cast: Toma, a macaque conductor; Eri, a gibbon singer; Bolo, a mandrill percussionist; Suni, a capuchin sound recordist; and the chorus of grove residents.

SCENE 1. EXT. WATERFALL CLEARING, DAWN

Toma stands before a line of monkeys. Behind them, the falls roar. He lifts both hands. The chorus opens its mouths. No one can hear anyone.

TOMA: We begin with the soft entrance.

The water crashes. Bolo strikes a hollow log. The note disappears.

BOLO: The waterfall has the bass part.

Toma asks everyone to move uphill. They climb to a ledge where the water is quieter, but the chorus cannot fit. Eri stays on a root below.

ERI: From here I can hear you.

TOMA: And we cannot see you.

Suni raises a recording microphone. A bird lands on it, turning the windscreen sideways.

SUNI: We have acquired a soloist.

SCENE 2. EXT. UPPER LEDGE, MORNING

Toma lays out a map. He marks three places for the chorus: the ledge, a flat rock, and a patch beneath a fig tree.

TOMA: We perform the first verse here, the second there, and the final note at the overlook.

BOLO: Are we moving because of the music or because the map is pretty?

TOMA: Both can be true.

The chorus tries a rising scale while walking. Everyone steps at a different pace. Bolo reaches the overlook alone and finishes the phrase.

BOLO: I have completed the entire symphony.

TOMA: You have completed the walk.

Eri demonstrates a call that rings clearly between the trees. Toma writes it down as a new movement. A troop of macaques answers from the opposite bank.

ERI: They know the ending.

TOMA: They have only heard the beginning.

The troop answers again, this time with a louder call. Toma lowers his baton.

SCENE 3. EXT. FIG TREE, MIDDAY

Suni listens to the test recording. The waterfall fills the sound, but the bird's chirps punctuate each pause.

SUNI: The machine has made the water louder.

TOMA: The machine should be impartial.

SUNI: It is impartial to the loudest thing.

Bolo taps the log gently. The recording catches the tap. He taps the log harder. A nearby fruit drops and lands on the log with a deep thunk.

BOLO: We have a percussion section.

Toma wants to repeat the take. A small monkey has picked up the fruit and rolls it down the hill. It thunks across the rocks, producing a rhythm no one planned. The chorus joins on the beat.

ERI: That is the first time we stayed together.

TOMA: The fruit cannot conduct.

The fruit hits the stream and floats away. The chorus stops.

BOLO: It has left the orchestra.

Toma looks to the ensemble, then removes his baton.

TOMA: Follow the sound you can hear. Do not chase the one we lost.

SCENE 4. EXT. WATERFALL CLEARING, AFTERNOON

The chorus forms a loose semicircle by the water. Toma gives each singer a different place to listen from. Eri stands near the stream, Bolo by the hollow log, and Suni farther up the bank.

TOMA: No one needs to be in the same place to begin together. Watch Eri's hand.

The first note starts. The falls cover it, then ebb in a brief gust. The chorus continues, each voice audible in turn. The bird returns to Suni's microphone and chirps in the rests.

Toma holds back the final note, waiting for the water to quiet. The chorus holds its breath. Bolo's tail brushes the log. A soft thump sounds. The waterfall surges again.

BOLO: The landscape has asked for an encore.

TOMA: The landscape can wait.

They begin the closing phrase. This time the water rises beneath their voices rather than swallowing them. The chorus finishes together.

Toma asks each singer what they heard during the performance. Eri heard the first note travel across the stream. Bolo heard the log answer the final phrase. Suni heard the bird interrupt the silence exactly when the group needed a breath. Toma writes none of this as a score. He marks the next rehearsal at the same hour.

SCENE 5. EXT. CLEARING, SUNSET

Suni plays back the recording. The final chorus is clear for three seconds, then the bird chirps directly into the microphone.

SUNI: The bird has the solo.

Toma considers the playback, then nods.

TOMA: Keep it in the film.

The ensemble packs the log, microphone and map. The waterfall fills the frame as the chorus hums while walking away. Toma starts to lift his baton, notices nobody is watching, and uses it to point out the path.

BOLO: Is that the coda?

TOMA: It is the way home.
