# The Portrait With Two Mustaches

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: family heirloom mysteries, genealogical twists, and solemn ancestral reveals*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A monkey family gathers to identify an old portrait before an estate sale, but every dramatic clue points to a neighbor who once borrowed the frame to keep a storm from ruining his sign-painting business.

Cast: Aunt Lolo, a mandrill family historian; Neri, a macaque appraiser; Tavi, a capuchin cousin; Grandma Suma, an orangutan; and the portrait of a mustached stranger.

SCENE 1. INT. FAMILY ATTIC, MORNING

The portrait is carried downstairs under a sheet. Tavi lifts the sheet with both hands, revealing a painted monkey with a curled mustache. Everyone gasps except Grandma Suma, who is holding the bottom of the frame and asks them to stop leaning it on her foot.

TAVI: That chin is mine.

LOLO: You don't have a chin.

TAVI: Then the painter understood me.

Neri appraises the canvas. It is a decent portrait in a repaired frame, but there is no signature. Aunt Lolo insists it must be a long-lost ancestor because it has been in the attic since anyone can remember. Grandma says it has been in the attic because nobody wanted to carry it downstairs.

SCENE 2. INT. ATTIC, LATER

Neri finds a latch in the frame. The family gathers around it. Lolo turns off the lights and lights a candle. The latch reveals a grocery list, a bus ticket, and a note reading OIL HINGES. Tavi calls the list a coded message. Neri reads it: tea, onions, thread.

TAVI: Those are code words.

GRANDMA SUMA: They are things I bought yesterday.

The bus ticket is dated thirty years earlier and has a punched corner. Lolo says the ancestor traveled secretly. Grandma points out the old bus line ran between the market and the repair shop. Neri notices the ticket is tucked beneath a replacement hinge, meaning the frame has been opened before.

SCENE 3. INT. FAMILY DINING ROOM, AFTERNOON

They compare the portrait to family photographs. Lolo sees her father's brow. Tavi claims the mustache, despite having none. Grandma finds a photograph of herself as a child beside a neighbor, Mr. Sedge, who painted signs at the market. In the photo he wears the same crooked jacket as the portrait's subject.

LOLO: A resemblance isn't proof.

TAVI: My whole face is a resemblance.

Neri checks the back of the canvas and finds a chalk mark from a sign painter's shop. The family argues whether that proves the neighbor painted the portrait or simply repaired the frame. Lolo proposes a solemn reveal at sunset. Grandma asks if she can have dinner first.

SCENE 4. INT. ATTIC, SUNSET

The family removes the backboard carefully. A folded receipt falls out: frame repair, Mr. Sedge, one stormy Tuesday. Beneath it is a small sketch of the same mustache. Grandma remembers Mr. Sedge posed his customers while waiting for paint to dry. The stranger was a market neighbor, not an ancestor. He lent the family the frame during a roof leak; they kept the picture when he moved away.

Lolo is disappointed that the mystery ends with a receipt. Neri says the portrait has historical value to the neighborhood. Tavi asks whether the mustache is still available for inheritance. Grandma says he can inherit the cleaning rag.

Neri prepares a truthful label: UNKNOWN MARKET NEIGHBOR, PAINTED BY MR. SEDGE, KEPT BY THIS FAMILY SINCE THE ROOF LEAK. Lolo adds the year after checking the bus ticket. They agree to display it in the hallway, where it will not sit beneath the attic drip.

SCENE 5. INT. HALLWAY, EVENING

The family hangs the portrait. The hook holds. Tavi stands beside it for a photograph, trying to copy the painted expression. He uses a piece of charcoal to curl a mustache on his face. Grandma wipes the charcoal off before it reaches the portrait. Lolo insists they take one picture with the accurate label visible.

TAVI: Can we call it the family portrait?

NERI: It's a portrait in the family.

GRANDMA SUMA: That's how labels stay honest.

They take the photograph. Tavi asks if he can keep the old grocery list. Grandma hands him a pencil and asks him to write the shopping list for tomorrow. He writes tea, onions, thread, then adds mustache polish. Lolo crosses out the last item.

Tavi puts the grocery list in the kitchen drawer, where it can finally be useful.

FINAL IMAGE: The portrait hangs beneath its plain label. Tavi poses beside it clean-faced. The family camera's timer flashes; everyone looks serious except Grandma, who is holding up the missing charcoal rag like a mustache.
