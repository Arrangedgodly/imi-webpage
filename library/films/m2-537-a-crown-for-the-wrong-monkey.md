# A Crown for the Wrong Monkey

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: royal succession comedies with mistaken identities, ceremonial costumes, and a public coronation interrupted by a practical correction*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: PRINCE PIP, a capuchin; JORA, a baboon tailor; LADY ELI, an orangutan regent; MIM, a macaque courier; HERALD, a gibbon; CAPTAIN BELL, a mandrill guard.

INT. PALACE COSTUME ROOM - MORNING

Prince Pip stands on a stool while Jora adjusts a velvet jacket. A gold crown sits on a hat stand.

PIP: Is it heavy?

JORA: The crown or the jacket?

PIP: Both.

Lady Eli enters with a ledger. She tells Pip the coronation begins at noon. Mim hurries in carrying a sealed box.

MIM: The crown was delivered to the wrong palace.

HERALD: This is the palace.

MIM: The box says East Palace.

Captain Bell checks the label. Their palace is West Palace. The crown on the hat stand is a ceremonial replica.

ELI: Where is the real crown?

MIM: On a cart between the two palaces.

Mim explains that the delivery route changed after a new road opened. The crown box was loaded under the East Palace label because the cart driver used last year's route sheet. Lady Eli asks if the proclamation also went to the wrong palace. Mim checks her satchel and pulls out the sealed scroll.

MIM: I carried the words by hand.

The herald breaks the seal and reads the first line. It names Pip as the new prince, but the final paragraph refers to the old bridge. Pip asks whether he should still promise to keep it open.

ELI: The west bridge is the one people use.

PIP: Then the words need to match the promise.

The clerk opens the proclamation ledger and checks the latest version. It says "west bridge." The herald replaces the old page with a corrected copy, then asks Pip to read it once before the crowd arrives.

HERALD: The crown can be late. The words cannot be wrong.

Mim returns the old route sheet to the driver and pins the new one to the cart. Captain Bell escorts the cart through the correct gate.

ACT ONE

EXT. PALACE COURTYARD - DAY

The coronation crowd gathers. The herald reads the royal proclamation. Pip wears the jacket but not the crown. Lady Eli announces a short delay. Captain Bell sends a runner to intercept the cart.

The cart arrives. Its front wheel catches in a paving seam and the tower of costume hats tilts. Captain Bell steadies the trunk while Jora catches the bird mask. The driver releases the brake and the cart starts to roll downhill. The crowd steps back as Pip and Mim stop it with a wooden bench.

PIP: The crown has nearly rolled into the flower beds.

MIM: It would have been the wrong palace again.

The driver wedges a chock behind the wheel. The cart carries a tower of costume hats, boxes, and one open trunk. The driver says the real crown is in the last box. The crowd watches as each hat is lifted: a gardener's cap, a cook's toque, a guard helmet, and a bird-shaped mask.

PIP: The kingdom has many hats.

JORA: The crown is under the bird mask.

The herald removes the mask. Beneath it is another hat. The cart driver checks the manifest and realizes the boxes were loaded backward. Captain Bell asks the crowd to step back from the cart.

CAPTAIN BELL: Nobody reaches into the trunk.

Mim notices a red ribbon tied around the box at the bottom. She asks Jora to cut the twine. The crown appears, but the velvet lining is caught on the cart latch.

JORA: The crown is not stuck. The cloth is.

Pip steps down from the stool and helps hold the box steady. Jora frees the lining without touching the gold. The crown is intact.

ACT TWO

The herald asks whether they should proceed with the real crown or use the replica. Lady Eli says the crown can wait; the ceremony must remain accurate. She checks the schedule and finds that the oath is first, then the crown. Pip can make the promise without a hat.

HERALD: Do we have the right proclamation?

MIM: I carried it from the East Palace.

Captain Bell checks the seal. It matches the royal mark. The crowd waits as Pip reads the oath. He promises to keep the west bridge open and the market sheltered during the winter. Lady Eli places the crown on his head after the words are complete.

PIP: It is heavier than the jacket.

JORA: That is why the jacket has pockets.

The herald announces the new prince. The crowd cheers. Mim returns the replica crown to the costume room. The cart driver takes the remaining hats to the correct palace.

ELI: Now we can celebrate.

PIP: Can I take off the crown first?

The herald says the portrait is scheduled before lunch. Jora adjusts the crown, then moves the hat stand away from the camera.

FINAL IMAGE: The new prince sits for his portrait while the empty hat stand wears the bird mask.
