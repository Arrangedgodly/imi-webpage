# The Morning in Three Pieces

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: nonlinear mystery films, color-coded timelines, and clues whose meaning depends on watching the last scene first*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A neighborhood cinema editor cuts a missing-kettle mystery into three timelines, then discovers the clues are confusing because the kettle is in the same room.

Cast: Aro, a macaque editor; Seli, a lemur projectionist; Boro, a gorilla caretaker; and Nib, a capuchin usher.

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY CINEMA, NIGHT

A kettle is missing from the staff room. Aro begins a reconstruction film. He labels the morning in three colors: blue before tea, red during the search, and green after the kettle is found. Seli asks why the green sequence begins before the blue one. Aro says the audience will assemble the timeline.

BORO: I moved the kettle.

ARO: That is not how the mystery opens.

Boro says he moved it to keep it away from the projector cables. Aro tells him not to reveal the answer before the opening title. Seli shows them the staff-room counter. There is a clean circular mark where the kettle stood, and a power strip on the opposite side. Boro says he placed the kettle on a dry shelf before the projector was switched on. Aro asks if they can shoot that as a flashback. Seli says it happened once, in the morning, without a flashback.

SCENE 2. INT. PROJECTION BOOTH, LATER

Aro plays the first cut. It shows Boro carrying the kettle, then Seli searching the staff room, then Nib placing a cup on the counter. Aro has reversed the order to build suspense. Seli asks what the red frame means. Aro says it is the moment the audience realizes the kettle is missing.

Seli points out the kettle is visible behind Nib. Aro pauses the frame and crops the kettle out. Boro says the room is small; cropping does not make it disappear. Nib asks if the film is about the kettle or the editing. Aro says both. Seli notices that each color card has a different time printed on it, but the times overlap. Aro says the overlap creates tension. She lays the cards side by side and discovers he used footage from two different days. Nib remembers the second morning because the tea tin had a new label. Aro asks if the label can become a clue. Nib says it marks decaf.

SCENE 3. INT. STAFF ROOM, EVENING

Seli lays out the timeline. Boro moved the kettle before the search. Nib filled it and made tea. Aro edited the footage after the kettle was returned to the shelf. The three events happened in that order. Aro says the green segment should reveal the motive. Boro says he moved it to prevent an electrical spill.

Seli asks why the cable route was under the kettle in the first place. Boro shows them the counter plan. The kettle belongs on the far shelf, away from the projector line. Nib admits he placed it near the cable when making tea. He returns it to the marked spot.

Aro wants to add a flashback showing the kettle being filled. Nib pours water into it, switches it off, and checks the base. Seli says that is a demonstration, not a flashback. Aro says the two are visually similar.

SCENE 4. INT. COMMUNITY CINEMA, NEXT MORNING

The crew watches a new cut in chronological order. It begins with Nib making tea, Boro moving the kettle, Seli looking for it, and the kettle returning to its marked place. Aro adds time cards so no one mistakes the order. The film runs two minutes shorter.

The audience asks why they watched a mystery whose answer appeared in the first scene. Aro explains the edit made a simple event look complicated. Boro asks whether they can now drink the tea. Nib pours a cup. Seli checks that the kettle cord does not cross the projector line.

The crew submits the short to the local festival. The form asks whether the film is fiction or nonfiction. Aro checks both, then crosses out both and writes "Kitchen Incident." Seli tells him the form has no such category. He chooses nonfiction. The programmer asks for a one-sentence description. Aro writes, "A kettle was moved to keep it away from cables." He tries to add a second sentence about memory and guilt. Seli reminds him the form permits one. Boro signs the equipment log, and Nib pours tea into cups set well away from the projector. Aro watches the kettle for a moment, then asks Seli to start the film from the beginning.

FINAL IMAGE: Aro leaves the timeline cards in order on the staff-room counter. The kettle sits on the marked shelf, and no one has to watch the final scene first.
