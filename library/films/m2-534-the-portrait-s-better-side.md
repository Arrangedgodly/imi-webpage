# The Portrait's Better Side

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: romantic period dramas with a formal portrait, misread glances, and a final pose arranged by a fussy painter*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: LORA, a gibbon painter; EMMET, a macaque archivist; LADY RO, an orangutan patron; FINCH, a capuchin page; VOSS, a baboon portrait subject.

INT. GRAND ESTATE GALLERY - AFTERNOON

A portrait painter sets up an easel. Voss stands beside Lady Ro, holding a bouquet. Emmet arrives with a framed portrait from the family archive.

LORA: We need a quiet room and a clear wall.

LADY RO: This is the quiet room.

FINCH: The clock is loud.

EMMET: The archive portrait is not the one you asked for.

Lady Ro turns the frame around. The painting shows a younger Voss standing alone beside an empty chair.

VOSS: That was before the wedding.

LADY RO: You left the chair empty.

VOSS: I was waiting for you.

Emmet looks at Lora. Lora turns away to adjust the easel. Finch quietly moves the bouquet from Voss's left side to his right.

ACT ONE

INT. GALLERY - LATER

Lora begins sketching. Voss keeps turning toward Lady Ro. Lady Ro keeps looking at the empty chair in the old portrait.

LORA: Please hold still.

VOSS: I am holding still.

EMMET: Your eyes are moving.

Lady Ro says the empty chair belonged to her mother, who could not attend the wedding. Voss had left a place for her. The portrait makes it look as if he is waiting for someone else.

VOSS: I should have explained it.

LADY RO: You did. I was looking at the chair.

Emmet places the old portrait on a stand near the new canvas. Lora asks them to stand together again. The bouquet blocks Lady Ro's face. Finch moves it to a side table.

FINCH: The flowers are now part of the background.

VOSS: They have always been part of the family.

[The bouquet tips. Emmet catches it, but one flower falls into the empty chair.]

LADY RO: My mother would have liked that.

Lora starts a new sketch with the chair in the frame. Voss turns toward Lady Ro. She looks directly at him this time.

INT. GALLERY CORRIDOR - MOMENTS LATER

Lady Ro leaves the room with the bouquet. Voss follows, carrying the old portrait carefully by its frame.

VOSS: I thought you wanted to stand beside me.

LADY RO: I wanted you to remember why the chair was empty.

VOSS: I remembered the day. I forgot the reason.

She stops before a window overlooking the garden. Finch catches up with the bouquet and gives it back to her. Lady Ro asks Voss whether he still wants the portrait displayed. He says the old painting makes him look solitary because that was how he posed. She says the new painting should show the room they built together.

LADY RO: Do not pose for the past.

VOSS: Then tell me where to stand.

Lora calls from the gallery. The painter has changed the easel angle and widened the frame. Emmet moves the old portrait onto a low stand. Voss and Lady Ro enter together, no longer carrying separate props.

ACT TWO

The painter asks Emmet to hold the old portrait beside the new one. Lady Ro asks Voss to sit in the empty chair instead of standing beside it. He sits. She stands beside him, placing one hand on the chair back.

VOSS: Is this the right pose?

LADY RO: It is the one I wanted.

Lora paints them with the empty chair included. The old portrait remains on the stand. Emmet notices that the younger Voss's hand rests on the chair, too.

EMMET: He was holding the place.

LADY RO: He was.

Finch moves the bouquet away from the canvas before the petals fall. Lora adds the last blue shadow to the chair. Lady Ro asks for the frame to be hung at the estate entrance, beside the older portrait. Emmet checks that the hooks are secure.

VOSS: The new portrait will show us waiting together.

LORA: It will show you seated.

VOSS: That is more accurate.

Finch tests the wall hooks with an empty frame. One hook tilts. Emmet replaces it with a stronger pair before anyone lifts the finished canvas. Lady Ro watches him test the frame twice, then nods.

LADY RO: Let the picture stay level this time.

Voss sits in the chair while Lady Ro stands beside it. They pose before both portraits as Finch carries the bouquet out of the gallery. Emmet adds the new title card beneath the frame: "The Place We Kept." Lady Ro reads it, then turns the old portrait toward the wall for the night.
FINAL IMAGE: The new portrait shows two figures beside an empty chair, with the old portrait reflected in the glass.
