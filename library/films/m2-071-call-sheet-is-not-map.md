# The Call Sheet Is Not a Map

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: behind-the-scenes documentaries, visionary-director mythology, and production crews who mistake logistics for destiny*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: A documentary crew follows a director through a supposedly chaotic shoot, while a macaque production assistant keeps rescuing the day by reading the call sheet.

Cast: Bex, a macaque production assistant; Director Vale, a lemur filmmaker; Finch, a capuchin camera operator; Nola, a baboon actor; and Rami, a gibbon location manager.

SCENE 1. INT. PRODUCTION TRAILER, DAWN

Director Vale studies a blank wall and calls it the emotional landscape. Finch films him. Bex enters with the call sheet, which lists a courtyard location and a 7:00 start.

VALE: The film must discover its own location.

BEX: The courtyard is booked.

VALE: That's too literal.

Bex points through the window. The courtyard is visible. Vale turns away and tells Finch to capture the moment of uncertainty.

SCENE 2. EXT. PARKING LOT, MORNING

The crew unloads gear in the parking lot. Vale says the open space suggests isolation. Rami arrives with a permit for the courtyard. He says the parking lot is reserved for a delivery truck. Bex checks the call sheet and leads everyone to the correct gate.

NOLA: Are we supposed to look lost?

VALE: Only if the camera finds it.

Nola takes one step in the wrong direction and walks into a stack of light stands. Bex catches them. Finch films the recovery. Vale calls it spontaneous choreography.

SCENE 3. INT. COURTYARD, LATER

The camera battery dies. Vale asks Finch to keep filming the dark screen, saying the absence of image has tension. Finch changes the battery. The next shot opens on a close-up of Bex labeling the spare batteries. Vale calls the label a production motif. Bex asks him to carry the spare kit. He does, holding it away from his costume.

Rami checks the weather notice. Rain is expected at four. Vale asks whether it will arrive on cue. Rami says it has not read the script. The crew moves the prop table under the awning before the first drops fall. Vale watches the rain begin and asks Finch to capture the storm without getting the camera wet.

The set is ready. Vale asks the crew to wait for natural light. A cloud covers the sun. He declares the shadow a thematic interruption. Bex moves the equipment under an awning and places a reflector. The light returns. Vale says the crew has negotiated with the sky.

A prop table is missing a ceramic cup. Bex finds it in the catering tent, where someone used it for tea. Vale says the missing cup is a key symbol. Nola says it is the only clean cup. Bex brings it to the set and checks the prop list.

SCENE 4. INT. COURTYARD, AFTERNOON

A sound technician asks Vale whether the scene needs birdsong. Vale says the courtyard should speak for itself. A real pigeon lands on the prop table and takes a cracker. Nola asks whether the bird is in the script. Vale says it may be an improviser. Bex removes the remaining food and moves the table. The pigeon follows the cracker bag to catering.

NOLA: It has a better call sheet.

VALE: It has a strong instinct for story.

BEX: It has crumbs.

Vale directs a scene in which Nola crosses a doorway and pauses. He asks her to repeat the pause with more history. Nola asks what history. Vale says that is for the audience to infer. Bex points out that the doorway is a fire exit. Rami asks them to keep the passage clear.

Vale blocks the doorway with a camera dolly. Bex redirects the dolly along the marked track. Finch says the new angle looks better. Vale calls it an accidental discovery. The assistant crew carries cables neatly under the tape marks. Vale asks them to leave one cable loose for authenticity. Bex ties it down.

SCENE 5. EXT. COURTYARD, SUNSET

The crew wraps. Vale gives a speech about the day's uncertainty. Bex hands him the production report, showing every location, prop, and safety fix completed on time. Vale says the report doesn't capture the spirit. Finch films him signing it. He asks the camera operator to keep the signature out of frame.

Nola thanks the crew and asks if tomorrow's location is confirmed. Bex checks the call sheet: the greenhouse, with a covered backup if it rains. Vale asks whether they can discover it together. Rami hands him a map.

Finch checks his footage; the clearest shot is Bex pointing everyone to the right location.

FINAL IMAGE: Vale looks at the map, then at the greenhouse across the path. He asks Finch to film him finding it. Bex turns the call sheet over and shows him the printed arrow.
