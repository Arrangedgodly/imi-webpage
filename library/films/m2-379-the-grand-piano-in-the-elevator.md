# The Grand Piano in the Elevator

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: backstage musical comedies, impossible set pieces, rehearsal montages, and performers who move scenery during the overture*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-013*

CAST
PIANIST LUMA, a lemur
STAGE MANAGER VEX, a macaque
CARPENTER ODO, a gibbon
SINGER NARI, an orangutan
RUNNER PIP, a capuchin

SCENE 1. INT. THEATER LOBBY - MORNING

A grand piano is scheduled to arrive for the evening musical. It does not fit in the elevator. Stage manager Vex announces that the show must go on. Carpenter Odo measures the door. Pianist Luma checks the music. Singer Nari asks where she should warm up.

VEX: The piano will rise to the stage.

ODO: The elevator will not widen.

Pip brings a dolly. The piano rolls three feet, then stops against the carpet.

SCENE 2. INT. STAIRWELL - LATER

The crew tries to carry the piano up the stairs. It gets stuck at the turn. Vex calls for a musical montage. The crew pushes in time with the overture, but the piano moves only when Odo counts aloud. Luma asks them to stop before the instrument tips.

Nari warms up in the stairwell. Her note resonates through the building and makes the elevator doors vibrate. Pip finds a service corridor behind the stage that connects to the loading dock. It is wide enough for the piano, but the corridor door is locked.

SCENE 2A. INT. THEATER LOBBY - MIDDAY

The crew checks the piano's dimensions against the loading dock. The instrument fits through the lobby but cannot turn at the staircase. Odo suggests removing the legs; Vex worries the audience will see the piano arrive in pieces. Luma says the audience is there to hear music, not watch furniture travel.

PIP: The elevator opens in the wrong direction.

ODO: The service hall is behind it.

They test the corridor with a padded crate the size of the piano. It clears each turn, but the crate catches on a fire door. Nari holds the door open and reads the safety sign aloud. The crew must not block it during the move. Vex asks whether they can prop it with a drum. Luma says the door has a proper hold-open switch.

SCENE 3. INT. SERVICE CORRIDOR - AFTERNOON

Odo unlocks the corridor. The piano moves through on the dolly, guided by ropes. Vex insists the crew should roll it while the orchestra plays. Luma asks for silence so she can hear whether the piano legs scrape. They move it slowly and stop at each turn.

Pip removes a poster from the wall to make room. It reveals a second door into the orchestra pit. The piano can pass through if its legs are removed. Odo detaches each leg and labels the screws. Vex asks for an intermission to change the set. The crew takes one.

SCENE 3A. INT. SERVICE CORRIDOR - AFTERNOON

The piano reaches the orchestra pit entrance. Odo checks the ramp and finds one caster tilted. He replaces the wheel. Luma tests every key before they continue. A note sticks, but the repair can be made on stage. Nari moves the music stands to clear a path. Vex asks the musicians to rehearse their entrances while the piano is carried. They begin the overture and walk backward toward the stage, then stop when the crew asks them to watch the instrument.

NARI: Music after movement.

PIP: Or we move the music stands first.

They move the stands. The piano passes through without knocking anything over.

SCENE 4. INT. STAGE - EVENING

The piano is assembled on stage. Luma tests the keys. One note sticks. Odo adjusts the key. Nari sings a warm-up scale. The orchestra follows. The overture begins on time, without a moving piano. Vex steps out of the stage manager's way so the audience can see the performers.

During the finale, Pip carries a stool across the stage. He reaches the edge, then finds there is no chair where it belongs. Odo points to the correct mark. Pip places the stool, and Nari finishes the song seated beside the piano.

SCENE 5. INT. THEATER LOBBY - NIGHT

The audience applauds. Vex asks whether the piano can be moved back downstairs. Odo says it can be disassembled after the performance. The crew removes the poster from the corridor door and posts a sign that reads PIANO ROUTE. Odo checks the door swing and clears the route for the next performance. He writes the piano measurements and hallway turn on the service log, then returns the key. Luma writes the key number on the service log.

FINAL IMAGE: The elevator doors close on an empty car as the piano's last note rings from the stage above.
