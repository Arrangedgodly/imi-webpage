# The Life of the Unremarkable Branch

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: prestige biopics that inflate ordinary achievements into awards-season turning points*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-006*

Logline: A narrator tries to give a low branch an award-winning life story, while an arborist distinguishes the living limb from one small twig that must be pruned.

Cast: Narrator Rell, a gibbon; camera operator Pavi, a macaque; arborist Mino, a baboon; and a beetle that does not take direction.

SCENE 1. EXT. GROVE, MORNING

A low branch stretches over a path. Rell speaks as if announcing a royal birth.

RELL (V.O.): For generations, the branch bore the weight of the forest.

MINO: It holds a bird's nest and shade for this path.

Pavi zooms in on marks in the bark.

RELL (V.O.): Scars reveal a history of hardship.

MINO: A beetle made those grooves.

The beetle crawls into view. Pavi follows it with the camera. Rell asks for a slower, more meaningful crawl. The beetle disappears beneath a leaf.

SCENE 2. EXT. GROVE, DAY

A young monkey climbs the branch and sits. Rell cues a swelling string chord. The monkey jumps to a nearby trunk.

RELL (V.O.): The branch carried a young life toward independence.

PAVI: It stayed where it was.

MINO: The branch is alive. Let it grow over the path as long as it stays sound.

Rell asks the monkey to repeat the jump. The monkey climbs down and leaves.

RELL: We lost our lead performer.

PAVI: We did not hire the monkey.

SCENE 3. EXT. GROVE BESIDE FIELD TENT, AFTERNOON

Rell reviews footage. A small twig has broken from the branch during a storm. Mino tests the remaining limb with a gloved hand and checks the arborist guide.

MINO: The limb is solid. This loose twig is the only piece that needs removal.

RELL: Can you call it the branch's final sacrifice?

MINO: No. It is a safe pruning cut.

Pavi sets the camera to normal speed. Rell asks for slow motion. Mino points to the guide's note about making a clean cut. He removes only the loose twig and sets it on a cloth. The main branch remains attached and untouched.

SCENE 4. EXT. GROVE, MOMENTS LATER

A beetle crawls out from beneath the bark on the small cut twig. Pavi moves the lens close. Rell cues another chord.

RELL: An unexpected life emerges from the wound.

MINO: It came from the twig.

The beetle crosses Mino's glove, then steps onto the cloth. Rell waits for a dramatic pause. The beetle walks off the cloth and onto the living branch.

PAVI: It has returned to the same tree.

RELL: A homecoming.

MINO: It is a beetle finding bark.

Pavi frames both the intact limb and the small twig on the cloth. The composition makes the twig look enormous in the foreground.

RELL: The final cut.

PAVI: The camera is too close.

He pulls back. The path, branch, and pruning tools appear in one shot. Rell asks if they can remove the tools from frame. Mino says the tools explain what happened.

SCENE 4A. EXT. GROVE, LATE AFTERNOON

Mino lays the cut twig beside the yard-waste basket. The living limb remains overhead, still shading the path. Rell asks Pavi to capture both in one frame, then asks which one will receive the screen credit.

MINO: The branch is part of the tree. The twig is waste.

RELL: A difficult distinction for the poster.

PAVI: The poster can show the tree.

Rell studies a mockup. The twig has been enlarged until it covers the title. Pavi shrinks it to actual size. Now the branch is visible, and the path beneath it is clear. Rell requests a separate award category for "Best Use of a Supporting Limb." Mino points to the pruning guide and asks him to return the tools.

SCENE 5. EXT. PATH, EVENING

The branch shades the clear path. Mino places the cut twig in a yard-waste basket. Rell proposes a memorial ceremony. Pavi asks what the ceremony would commemorate.

RELL: The twig's brief but meaningful service.

MINO: It was loose. I removed it so it would not fall on someone.

Rell places a paper award beside the basket: "Best Supporting Twig." A gust tips it over. Mino props the award against the basket, where the beetle climbs onto it.

PAVI: The beetle has accepted the award.

RELL: The branch's legacy is secure.

MINO: The branch is still growing.

SCENE 6. EXT. GROVE, NIGHT

The crew packs up. Rell asks for one closing line about resilience. Mino checks the branch once more, confirms it is stable, and turns off the work light.

RELL: A quiet ending, then.

The beetle walks from the paper award to the living limb. Pavi holds the shot. Rell whispers for music. The crew's phone rings with the editor asking for the award category.

MINO: Best twig.

Rell props a printed title card against the bark: "A Branch in the Grove." The beetle crosses the final letter as the screen fades. Rell whispers that the insect is covering his name. Pavi keeps rolling.
