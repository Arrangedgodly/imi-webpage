# The Night of the Rehearsal Raccoons

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: haunted-house horror, ominous sound design, false silhouettes, and investigators who arrive with too many gadgets*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
TESS, a capuchin sound engineer
GARO, a gibbon night custodian
MIRA, an orangutan stage director
BO, a macaque actor
THREE RACCOONS, masked neighborhood visitors

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY THEATER - NIGHT

A dark community theater waits for a rehearsal. A heavy thump echoes from backstage. Tess stops recording. Garo shines a flashlight at a curtain that appears to move by itself.

TESS: Did the building just breathe?

GARO: It has old pipes.

The curtain swells again. A small shadow darts behind it. Garo grips a mop as if it were a spear.

SCENE 2. INT. LOBBY - MOMENTS LATER

Tess sets up a sound recorder, two microphones, and a motion camera. Mira arrives with a stack of cue sheets. She explains the theater is booked for a children's play, and she needs the stage clear by morning. Bo arrives wearing a monster mask from the production. The motion camera tracks him and flashes a red light.

BO: It thinks I'm the monster.

TESS: It thinks you moved.

BO: I was standing still.

TESS: The mask's eyebrows moved.

A scrape echoes from the snack counter. They approach in a line. Bo walks backward so the camera can see his mask. He bumps a folding chair, which closes with a loud snap. Everyone jumps. Mira finds a paper bag on the floor and assumes it is the source of the movement.

SCENE 3. INT. STAGE WINGS - LATER

The group follows scratching sounds toward the stage. Tess plays them through the recorder at high volume. It sounds like claws dragging across stone. Garo opens a storage trunk and finds three costume tails rubbing against the lid as the ventilation fan turns.

MIRA: Those tails are for the woodland scene.

TESS: They sound more threatening in the dark.

A spotlight switches on above them. Bo screams, then realizes it is the rehearsal timer. Its automatic setting turns on at midnight. Mira tests the controls; the light follows her as she crosses the stage. She calls it the ghost of technical theater.

SCENE 4. INT. SNACK COUNTER - NIGHT

Another crash comes from the counter. Tess places the motion camera low to the ground. The footage shows a striped shape with bright eyes reaching into a bowl, then disappearing. Bo calls it a goblin. Garo says the snack counter window has a hole where the latch should be. Mira closes the window and the thumping stops.

A second shadow appears at the back door. Tess raises a handheld light. Three raccoons in borrowed masks stare at them, each holding a paper cup. The animals had slipped into the theater through the broken latch and are now wearing pieces of the play's costumes.

GARO: Those are not actors.

BO: They're in the cast now.

Mira carefully puts a bowl of fruit outside, then opens the door. The raccoons leave one by one, carrying the cups. They drop a mask on the step and vanish into the garden.

SCENE 5. INT. AUDITORIUM - DAWN

Tess reviews the recording. Every terrifying sound has a source: the fan, the timer, the folding chair, and the raccoons. She deletes the fake monster soundtrack she had started composing. Bo removes his mask. Mira updates the play's stage notes to keep the costume tails away from the fan.

Garo repairs the snack counter latch. It clicks shut. The theater is quiet until a single thump comes from the stage. Everyone turns. A sandbag has settled on the floor. Mira labels the moment "normal stage noise" on the cue sheet.

Tess sets the recorder by the stage door and asks Garo to repeat the sounds one at a time. He taps the curtain rail: a soft clank. He rolls the chair: a sharp snap. He turns on the fan: the tails whisper against the trunk. Bo wears the monster mask again, but this time the camera catches his eyebrows moving before he enters the frame.

TESS: The ghost has an excellent call sheet.

MIRA: The play is about woodland animals. They can keep the tails. We will tie them back before the fan starts.

Garo tests the repaired latch by pushing the window. It stays closed. Outside, a raccoon returns, sniffs the door, and trots away when it cannot open it. Tess records the quiet and writes the source list on the cue sheet so tomorrow's crew will not mistake ordinary stage sounds for a new haunting.

The children arrive for rehearsal. One asks if the theater is haunted. Tess answers that it is full of old sounds, which are easier to understand once someone checks them. Bo gives the child the dropped raccoon mask as a prop and warns them not to feed the visitors.

FINAL IMAGE: The motion camera records an empty lobby; its red light reflects in the three raccoons' eyes outside the newly repaired window as they watch the play poster.
