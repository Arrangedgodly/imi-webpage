# The Die Has Sixteen Sides

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: board-game adaptations that turn simple rules into ancient prophecy and tactical warfare*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-003*

Logline: In a cinematic version of a neighborhood board game, players race to the last square while repeatedly stopping to debate what the rules mean.

Cast: Luma, a lemur game designer; Brik, a gorilla champion; Tess, a macaque referee; and Miv, a capuchin who has never played.

SCENE 1. INT. COMMUNITY HALL, NIGHT

A painted map fills the floor. Luma introduces the game Path of the Ancients. Brik enters in a cape and rolls a huge sixteen-sided die. It lands on six. Tess asks everyone to move six spaces. Brik looks for a hidden message in the symbols.

BRIK: The spiral means we must go backward.

TESS: The rules say forward.

Miv steps onto the first square. A light flashes and a speaker announces, "Draw a bridge card." Miv draws one. It says cross the blue line. There is no blue line on the map.

SCENE 2. INT. GAME FLOOR, CONTINUOUS

Luma checks the production notes. The blue line was painted over during set decoration. Tess uses a roll of painter's tape to mark it. Brik says the line should be revealed by a thunderclap. The sound crew plays thunder; the tape stays flat.

The game board's speaker announces a "rising threat." Everyone searches the map for a monster. Luma checks the card deck and finds a paper clip pinning two cards together. The trapped card is a rule explaining that a player must return to the starting square after drawing a storm symbol. Brik says the rule needs a sweeping orchestral score. Miv returns to start while the score plays, one square at a time.

Tess asks who approved the sixteen-sided die. Brik says it represents fate. Luma explains that the printed probability table is for a six-sided die and asks them to use the smaller one. Brik objects that the large die is the only prop visible over his cape. He removes the cape. The die is still oversized, but it no longer blocks the map.

Miv asks what happens if a player lands on a moon symbol. Brik says it means exile. Tess reads the rulebook: the player loses one turn. Brik claims exile is more cinematic. Tess asks him to lose one turn quietly. He sits on a folding chair beside the map and watches the others proceed.

Luma finds three more cards whose instructions require props that are not on set: a key, a boat, and a small bell. The crew searches the prop table. Miv finds the bell attached to Brik's cape. Brik says the cape was part of his champion costume. Luma tells him the rulebook does not list a champion costume. He folds the cape and takes his turn.

The key turns up in a bowl of spare tokens. The boat is a paper marker, but the card says to "launch" it. Miv places it on the blue tape and slides it across. Brik insists it should be carried by a crane. Tess asks whether he is willing to build one during his turn. He declines. Luma notes that the rules work once the players read the verbs as written.

SCENE 3. INT. GAME FLOOR, LATER

The players reach a bridge square. The rules say to answer a riddle. The card asks what can cross a river without touching water. Brik delivers a speech about destiny. Miv answers, "A bridge." The game accepts it. Brik says the riddle should require a sacrifice. Tess asks if he is volunteering his turn. He sits down again.

The next card says trade places with the player farthest behind. Brik and Miv exchange squares. Brik lands on a penalty tile and loses a turn. Miv reaches the mountain square. Luma explains that the board's dramatic geography is decorative; the map still uses the numbered spaces. Brik wants a new rule that lets him challenge the mountain. Tess refuses to invent rules during play.

SCENE 4. INT. GAME FLOOR, FINAL TURN

Miv is two squares from the end. He rolls a three. The rulebook says he must land exactly on the last square. He moves past it, then stops. Brik says the mountain has rejected him. Tess points to the printed rule and lets him take the next turn.

Luma checks the cards for missing props. The key was taped beneath the table, and the boat is a folded paper token. The crew adds the tape line and the bell to the setup list. Brik asks to narrate the next move. Miv rolls a two and reaches the final square. The speaker plays a short victory chime.

After the game, Luma rewrites the setup instructions in large type. Tess tests the revised list while Miv resets the board. Brik asks if the sequel can add a dragon. Luma says a dragon would need a rule, a token, and a storage box. Brik begins sketching one on the margin. Tess circles the storage-box line and hands him a pencil.

There is no explosion, prophecy, or portal. Miv wins and asks whether the prize is another game. Luma offers him the prototype rulebook and asks him to test it for confusing instructions. Tess writes down his notes while Brik practices a smaller, ordinary die roll.

FINAL IMAGE: The crew packs the map into a tube. Brik folds his cape around the tiny bell so it will not be lost again. Miv reads the setup card aloud, and every prop is where the list says it should be.
