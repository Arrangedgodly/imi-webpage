# The Umbrella Makers' Midnight Waltz

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: period romance, ballroom misunderstandings, secret admirers, and a rainstorm timed to the final dance*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
ELLI, a ring-tailed lemur umbrella maker
RAVI, a capuchin dance instructor
COUNTESS NARI, an orangutan festival patron
PIP, a macaque courier
MILO, a gibbon orchestra leader

SCENE 1. INT. UMBRELLA SHOP - EVENING

Elli repairs a blue umbrella beneath a sign advertising the annual Lantern Ball. A note arrives with a silver ribbon: "Meet me at midnight under the clock." Elli assumes it is from Ravi, who has been taking dance lessons. Ravi enters carrying a broken parasol.

RAVI: The handle came loose.

ELLI: So did the note.

Ravi sees the ribbon and blushes. He did not write it. A gust blows the note into the street, and Pip catches it against a puddle with his shoe.

SCENE 2. INT. BALLROOM - NIGHT

Countess Nari opens the Lantern Ball. The orchestra plays a waltz, but the floor has been polished so well that guests slide across it. Milo signals the musicians to slow down. The dancers stop moving and glide another few feet, then form a careful line around the wet patch. Milo lowers the tempo. Ravi teaches Elli a simple turn. She follows, then takes a wrong step to avoid a puddle from the umbrella stand.

RAVI: You're learning quickly.

Milo restarts the tune at a slower tempo, and the dancers no longer slide.

ELLI: I'm keeping my shoes dry.

Pip arrives with a stack of notes that were delivered to the wrong door. One invites the mayor to the clock, another invites the clock to the ball, and the third says "meet me where the music stops." He cannot find the author. Nari asks him to sort by handwriting. All three notes use the same silver ribbon.

SCENE 3. EXT. BALLROOM CLOCK - MIDNIGHT

The guests gather beneath the clock. The clock strikes twelve, but its minute hand is stuck at eleven. Elli notices a loose screw near the clock face. Ravi climbs the stairs to retrieve it while she steadies the ladder. The romantic orchestra swells, then stops when Milo realizes the beat is out of time.

Pip finds the note's author: Countess Nari. She wrote the invitations to gather the festival volunteers for a surprise umbrella repair station. Her handwriting is elegant but nearly identical on every card. She did not mean to invite a secret admirer.

NARI: I was trying to make the instructions sound mysterious.

ELLI: They did.

RAVI: I came because I thought the note was yours.

ELLI: I came because I thought it was yours.

They both laugh. Nari asks them to repair the clock and set up the umbrella station before the rain begins.

SCENE 4. INT. BALLROOM - LATER

The repaired clock strikes correctly. Rain begins outside. Guests open umbrellas indoors, creating a forest of colored fabric. Milo shifts the orchestra to a waltz in three-quarter time. Elli and Ravi dance beneath a blue umbrella, taking turns holding it. When the music ends, neither steps away. They agree to meet again at the shop, this time with no cryptic note.

Pip returns the extra invitations to Nari. She offers him one last card. It reads "Please bring the spare ribbons to the table." Pip checks the handwriting and confirms it is a practical instruction. The volunteers repair umbrellas at the entrance while the party continues. A guest arrives with a broken green umbrella and hands it to Elli. Ravi steadies the frame while she replaces one bent rib. When they open it, the canopy catches a draft from the ballroom door and turns inside out. The guests step back, then laugh as Ravi holds the handle and Elli turns the fabric the right way.

RAVI: The umbrella has taken the lead.

ELLI: It needs someone to follow.

Milo cues the orchestra to a slower waltz. The two repairers dance around the open umbrella, testing its balance as they move. The guests join them only after Nari checks that the floor is dry.

Before the orchestra resumes, rain begins outside the ballroom. Guests reach for the repaired umbrellas. One blue umbrella opens inside out again, so Elli asks Ravi to hold the handle while she reverses the bent rib. He steadies it. They open it together; this time it stays round.

COUNTESS NARI: The repair station is now part of the ball.

MILO: And the waltz has a roof.

The guests take turns carrying the umbrellas to the entrance. Pip gathers the silver ribbons and ties them around each repaired handle so their owners can find them. Elli and Ravi stay beneath the last blue canopy as the clock strikes one.

SCENE 5. EXT. SHOP WINDOW - DAWN

Elli and Ravi walk home beneath the repaired blue umbrella. The rain has stopped, but the pavement remains wet. They pause at the clock tower and check that it is keeping time. It is. The orchestra's last notes drift through an open ballroom window.

FINAL IMAGE: The blue umbrella dries in the shop window, its silver ribbon tied around the handle in a neat bow. The clock outside strikes the hour once.
