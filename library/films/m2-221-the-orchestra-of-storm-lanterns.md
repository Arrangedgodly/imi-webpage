# The Orchestra of Storm Lanterns

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: prestige orchestra dramas, tyrannical conductors, rehearsal montages, and a concert saved by the stage crew*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-008*

CAST
LENA, a macaque percussionist
MAESTRO ORR, a capuchin conductor
VELL, a gibbon stage carpenter
TOMA, a ring-tailed lemur violinist

SCENE 1. INT. GRAND HALL - MORNING

The city orchestra prepares for its annual storm-light concert. Maestro Orr conducts a silent rehearsal, demanding a single perfect note. Lena rolls a timpani onto the stage. Its wheel squeaks on every third turn.

ORR: The hall must hear only the music.

LENA: It can hear my drum.

ORR: Not the wheel.

Vell tests the lighting rig. A storm lantern swings above the orchestra. Toma plays a tuning note, and the lantern flickers in time. Orr orders the crew to stop moving until the sound is perfect.

SCENE 2. INT. REHEARSAL ROOM - AFTERNOON

Orr leads the players through a precision montage: bows rise together, horns point together, and every musician turns a page on the same beat. Lena's page refuses to turn. She licks a finger; the page sticks to her hand.

LENA: I have lost the second movement.

ORR: It is still in your hand.

Lena gives the page to Toma, who uses his tail to hold the corner while playing. The orchestra follows him instead of the conductor for four bars. The sound is lively. Orr calls a stop.

ORR: The music wandered.

TOMA: It found a good seat.

SCENE 3. INT. GRAND HALL - EVENING

A storm knocks out the main lights. The hall's emergency lanterns switch on. The audience waits in darkness while the stage crew checks the rig. The orchestra members hold their instruments in place.

ORR: No one move. We will preserve the arrangement.

LENA: The audience is going to preserve their seats.

Vell enters with a cart of portable lanterns. The wheels squeak in the same three-beat pattern. Lena taps the drum in answer. Toma joins with a low string note. The rest of the orchestra begins playing along, softly, without Orr's cue.

SCENE 4. INT. STAGE WING - CONTINUOUS

Orr tries to regain control. He conducts the lantern crew by mistake, making them raise and lower the lights while the musicians pause. The lanterns swing like a slow dance. The audience applauds.

ORR: They are applauding the lighting.

VELL: The lighting is on the beat.

Orr steps off the podium to quiet the crew. He trips on the squeaking timpani wheel and lands beside Lena's drum. The drum makes a deep, warm note. The hall goes quiet. Orr listens to the echo instead of demanding another take.

ORR: Begin with that sound.

SCENE 5. INT. GRAND HALL - LATER

The orchestra begins the storm piece. Vell's lantern crew creates moving pools of light. Lena's wheel squeak becomes a repeated rhythm; Toma's violin carries the melody. Orr conducts from the floor, keeping time with the audience's gentle clapping.

One lantern swings too far and casts a huge shadow of the conductor on the rear wall. Orr sees it and raises his arms. The shadow becomes a giant bird flapping over the orchestra.

LENA: You have finally conducted a bird.

ORR: It is very responsive.

SCENE 6. INT. GRAND HALL - FINALE

The last passage builds. The storm outside quiets. The audience waits for the famous final note. Orr lifts his baton, then lets Lena finish the rhythm and Toma hold the last tone. The lanterns settle. The hall remains silent for a breath before applause.

Orr bows to the players, the stage crew, and the squeaky cart. Vell puts a drop of oil on the wheel; it no longer squeaks.

ORR: We have lost our percussionist.

LENA: I can make it squeak again.

SCENE 7. INT. STAGE WING - AFTER THE CONCERT

The orchestra packs its instruments. Orr asks Lena to demonstrate the wheel rhythm once more. Vell oils the cart's axle but leaves one corner slightly dry so it still squeaks on cue. The crew auditions the sound against a cymbal, a triangle, and the conductor's shoes.

TOMA: The wheel is the only one that keeps the same tempo.

ORR: It has fewer opinions.

LENA: It has one opinion. It squeaks.

Orr writes the wheel into the score as a percussion part. Vell puts the cart on a padded track for the next concert. The storm-lantern crew requests its own credit on the program, and Orr adds a line for stage carpentry and emergency lighting.

SCENE 8. INT. GRAND HALL - NEXT MORNING

The stage crew rehearses its lantern cue without the orchestra. Vell walks the cart across the stage; the wheel no longer squeaks. Lena arrives with oil and rubs a small amount on the axle, leaving enough friction for the old three-beat sound.

LENA: It is a musical instrument now.

VELL: It is still a cart.

Orr enters carrying a printed program. He has added the crew to the orchestra roster and put the cart under "woodwinds," because its wheel sounds hollow. The musicians meet in the lobby and ask to see the program.

TOMA: The cart has a better credit than the violas.

ORR: The violas kept the lanterns in tune.

FINAL IMAGE: The orchestra walks out beneath the storm lanterns while Vell pushes the quiet cart; the whole company follows, tapping the old rhythm with their feet.
