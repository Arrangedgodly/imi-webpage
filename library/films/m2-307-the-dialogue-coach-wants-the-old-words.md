# The Dialogue Coach Wants the Old Words

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: costume dramas that mistake archaic language for historical accuracy and make every simple exchange incomprehensible*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-011*

CAST
DIALOGUE COACH MIRI, a gibbon
ACTOR LUMA, a lemur
ACTOR ODO, a baboon
DIRECTOR PIP, a capuchin
HISTORIAN VEX, a macaque

SCENE 1. INT. CASTLE SET - MORNING

A stone arch frames a courtyard made of painted boards. Actor Luma carries a wooden key. Dialogue coach Miri holds a stack of cue cards. Director Pip listens from a folding chair.

PIP: You ask the guard to open the gate.

LUMA: Open the gate, please.

MIRI: Too modern.

VEX: People have said please for centuries.

MIRI: The audience expects an older sound.

Miri changes the line to, "Unbar the threshold, keeper of iron."

ODO: That is not a door. That is a title.

Luma tries the new line. Odo opens the small gate in the set; it sticks halfway. He pushes with his shoulder.

ODO: The gate is not convinced. Miri changes the line to "Submit yourself to the hinge," but Odo waits for it to open. Luma taps the latch with the modern key, and it clicks immediately.

Pip asks the crew to loosen the latch. Miri writes "the portal resists her command" on the card.

SCENE 2. INT. CASTLE SET - LATER

The actors rehearse the whole exchange. Luma asks for the gate. Odo refuses because the king has not signed a scroll. Pip says the scene needs urgency. Miri gives Luma a longer version.

LUMA: By order of the moonlit crown, whose ancient right precedes the keep and its...

PIP: We have lost the gate.

Odo holds up a hand.

ODO: Which gate?

The rehearsal stops. Vex consults his historical notes. The set is modeled after a stone watch post, not a royal castle. The line about the crown is wrong. Pip asks Miri to shorten it. She replaces "moonlit crown" with "the crown beneath the moon." Vex says the moon is not visible indoors.

Miri turns to Luma's prop key. It has a modern plastic tag marked GATE B.

MIRI: The tag is inaccurate.

ODO: I am grateful it tells me which gate.

Luma peels it off. The tag leaves a rectangle of adhesive. The key now sticks to the paper scroll when Odo sets it down.

SCENE 3. INT. PROP ROOM - NOON

Vex lays out three samples: a bronze key, an iron key and the actual modern key. The bronze key is decorative and does not fit the gate. The iron one is too large. Luma tries the modern key; it turns the lock.

PIP: We need the right tool, not the right century.

MIRI: The close-up needs a period silhouette.

Odo holds the bronze key beside the lock. It does not fit. He turns it around, tries the other end, then looks at Miri.

ODO: The gate has rejected history.

Vex explains that the set uses a hidden modern latch because the wooden frame cannot hold a real iron lock. Miri asks them to show only the key's outline. Pip suggests the audience already saw the gate open in the previous scene. Miri asks for a title card: "A KEY OF ANCIENT MAKE." Vex says the prop is new. Miri revises it to "A KEY OF UNCERTAIN MAKE."

SCENE 4. INT. CASTLE SET - AFTERNOON

The crew rehearses with the working key. Luma approaches the gate, delivers the short line, and turns the key. It opens. Miri insists on one archaic word.

LUMA: Unbar it.

ODO: It is already unbarred.

Pip asks them to keep the action and use the plain line. Miri listens to the scene. Odo's reply, "I have the key," lands as a dry joke after his extended refusal. She writes no changes.

The first take works until a stagehand rolls a modern toolbox through the rear arch. Vex steps in front of it, shielding the camera, and accidentally appears to be guarding the castle. Pip keeps rolling. Luma finishes the exchange. Odo opens the gate. The toolbox crosses behind them.

PIP: That is the best take.

MIRI: The toolbox breaks the period.

VEX: It is the only thing that opened on time.

SCENE 5. INT. SCREENING ROOM - EVENING

The rough cut plays. The ancient-sounding lines are muffled. The plain lines are clear. The bronze key shines uselessly in close-up. Pip keeps the working key, the short dialogue and the gate opening. Miri watches the audience understand without needing subtitles.

MIRI: The old words were not the history.

VEX: The gate is older than our script.

Luma hands Odo the key. He turns the latch.

FINAL IMAGE: The courtyard gate opens easily while Miri's cue card, covered in crossed-out titles, slips off the prop table.
