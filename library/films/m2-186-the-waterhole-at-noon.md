# The Waterhole at Noon

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: western showdowns, ominous saloon entrances, long stares across a main street, and a duel settled by an unexpected practical rule*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
SUNDOWN, a capuchin marshal
BARK, a mandrill drifter
ELI, a ring-tailed lemur water keeper
TOWN FOLK, macaques and gibbons

SCENE 1. EXT. DRY GULCH - HIGH NOON

A tumble of dust crosses the empty main street. A piano plays inside the saloon, though no one is at the keys. Marshal Sundown steps from the station. Across from him, Bark the drifter waits beneath the only strip of shade.

SUNDOWN: That shade belongs to the town.

BARK: I was standing here first.

SUNDOWN: The town built that awning. The water keeper needs the shade for the queue.

BARK: Then the town can ask politely.

A card appears on the station door: "THE WATERHOLE CLOSES AT NOON."

SCENE 2. INT. SALOON - MOMENTS LATER

The town gathers around a table. Eli, the water keeper, explains that the well is low. Each household gets one bucket at noon. The shade keeps water cool while the line waits.

BARK: I need the shade for my horse.

ELI: You do not have a horse.

BARK: I may acquire one.

SUNDOWN: You're blocking the water queue.

BARK: I am enjoying the western sun.

The town council suggests moving the awning. It is bolted to the saloon. The owner refuses; it is the only thing keeping the piano from overheating.

SCENE 3. EXT. MAIN STREET - NOON

A showdown forms. The townsfolk hide behind barrels. Sundown and Bark stand ten paces apart. The piano begins its dramatic theme. Sundown reaches for his holster. Bark reaches for his belt.

They draw: Sundown holds a measuring tape; Bark holds a silver pocket watch.

The crowd ducks. Nothing happens. Bark checks his watch and says he has waited exactly three minutes under the shade. Sundown unrolls the tape to show that the water queue begins at the pump, not beneath the awning.

BARK: A man can stand anywhere he likes.

SUNDOWN: A man can. A water bucket cannot.

Bark tries to pace out the distance to the pump. The tape keeps unrolling. He walks backward, then forward, then gets wrapped once around the barrel where the townsfolk are hiding. They pull the barrel away, leaving him standing in the open with the tape around his waist.

ELI: That is a long way from the line.

BARK: I was measuring the town.

SUNDOWN: You measured one barrel.

The saloon pianist plays a single suspenseful chord. A loose plank drops from the awning and lands at Bark's feet. He looks up. The shade is not only for the queue; the awning is coming apart.

SUNDOWN: The town ordinance says the awning shade belongs to the person waiting in the water line.

BARK: I can wait in the water line.

ELI: The line starts there.

Bark moves to join it. He steps in front of a macaque with two empty buckets.

SUNDOWN: That is cutting in line.

BARK: I have waited under the shade for an hour.

ELI: You have been standing in one place. That is not the same as waiting for your water.

SCENE 4. EXT. WATER QUEUE - CONTINUOUS

The queue stretches along the street. Bark tries to pass behind the barrels and reappear near the front. The townfolk rotate the barrels to keep the line straight. Each turn returns Bark to the back. The piano continues playing as if this were a duel.

Sundown measures the awning's shadow. It moves one inch every minute. Eli places a water bucket beneath the shaded end. The line shifts as the sun moves; nobody is forced to stand in the heat for long.

BARK: I'll challenge you for the shade.

SUNDOWN: We can settle this by the rule you agreed to: take your place in line.

Bark considers the town, the queue, and the horse he still does not own. He joins the back. The piano stops.

SCENE 5. EXT. MAIN STREET - LATER

The last household fills its bucket. Eli turns the well handle. It produces one final splash, then a single drop. The town stores the remaining water for evening.

Bark's place in line arrives. Eli gives him a cup, not a bucket.

BARK: This is a small drink.

ELI: You waited for a drink. A bucket is for households.

Bark drinks. He steps beneath the awning and holds it up for the next person in line.

SUNDOWN: You can share the shade without owning it.

BARK: Don't make it a speech.

The town repairs the well pump and adds a bench beneath the awning. Bark sits on the bench, still leaving room for the next neighbor. The saloon piano plays a jaunty tune.

FINAL IMAGE: The long shadow of the awning reaches across the empty water line. Bark tips his hat to the shade as it passes from one side of the street to the other.
