# The Letter Before the Train

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: romantic melodramas, missed connections, handwritten letters, and a final reunion delayed by one platform announcement*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
ADA, a capuchin station florist
LEO, a gibbon clock repairer
NELL, a macaque train attendant

SCENE 1. INT. RIVER STATION - MORNING

Ada places a red flower in a paper cone. Leo checks the station clock. They exchange glances, each waiting for the other to speak. An announcement interrupts them: the northbound train will leave in two minutes.

LEO: I have something to tell you.

ADA: I have something to give you.

The train whistle sounds. Leo runs to his platform. Ada slips a folded letter into his coat pocket. The train doors close before she can call his name.

SCENE 2. INT. TRAIN - DAY

Leo discovers the letter. It has no words, only a flower pressed between the pages. He assumes it is a farewell. He writes a reply and hands it to Nell, the train attendant, who promises to deliver it at the next stop.

NELL: You might also ask her what it means.

LEO: It means goodbye.

NELL: It looks like a flower.

The train passes the station. Leo watches the platform slide away in a long, tearful shot.

SCENE 2A. EXT. COUNTRY STATION - DAY

Leo's train stops at a small station for water. He asks the attendant whether the flower on the letter means farewell. The attendant says it means the paper is carrying a flower.

LEO: You have never been in love.

NELL: I have. I asked.

Leo writes a reply, then crosses it out. He tries to describe how he felt when Ada smiled at him. Every version sounds like a timetable. Nell suggests he speak plainly when he returns.

Meanwhile, Ada closes the stall and walks to the platform. She hears a train but sees it is traveling in the opposite direction. She runs after it, then stops when the station attendant calls out that Leo's return train is not until evening.

ADA: I thought the clock was fast.

ATTENDANT: The clock is fast. The schedule is still right.

Ada keeps the flower stall open. She places one red blossom on the counter and saves the rest in water.

SCENE 3. INT. STATION FLORIST - AFTERNOON

Ada waits by the entrance. Nell returns with Leo's letter. It contains a timetable, not a confession. Leo has written "I'll be back at four." Ada reads it as a polite scheduling note.

The station clock is still wrong by five minutes. Ada decides she has missed him. She closes the flower stall. The red flower remains on the counter.

SCENE 3A. INT. STATION FLORIST - LATE AFTERNOON

Ada reads Leo's timetable again. On the back is a note: "I will be back at four. Please wait." It is written in small letters beneath the train times. She thinks it might have been added by Nell as an instruction, not by Leo.

Ada asks Nell when Leo's train arrives. Nell points out that the note is in Leo's handwriting. Ada studies the loops in the letters and recognizes the same slanted line he uses when repairing the clock.

ADA: He wrote this for me.

NELL: Then you know when he will be here.

Ada moves her flower cart to the platform entrance. She starts to wait, but each announcement makes her look toward a different track. Nell marks the correct platform in chalk and tells her the train is delayed by five minutes, not a whole day.

SCENE 4. EXT. STATION PLATFORM - EVENING

Leo's train returns. He hurries through the doors and finds the florist closed. Nell points to the station clock, then to the correct time on her watch. It is only three fifty-five.

Leo leaves the train and runs toward the florist. Ada is loading her cart into a small hand wagon. They meet in the doorway. The wagon rolls away, carrying the flower cones down the platform.

LEO: I thought your letter was goodbye.

ADA: It was a flower. I wanted you to take it with you.

LEO: I thought the timetable was a confession.

ADA: It was a timetable.

They laugh. Nell retrieves the runaway wagon. The flower cones are intact, though each is now in a different order.

SCENE 5. INT. STATION FLORIST - NIGHT

Leo repairs the station clock. Ada gives him another red flower, this time with a handwritten note: "Meet me after the final train."

LEO: I can understand that.

ADA: I wrote the words.

The final train arrives on time. They remain on the platform, watching it leave, then walk back to the florist together.

FINAL IMAGE: The station clock shows the correct time. Beneath it, the flower stall's red blossoms turn toward the open platform doors.
