# The Last Loaf at Snowline Inn

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: prestige cooking dramas, slow-motion ingredient close-ups, culinary rivalry, and a final dish said to carry an entire family history*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A young macaque baker must save her family's mountain inn during a snowstorm, while an exacting food critic waits for the perfect loaf and the oven refuses to hold a steady temperature.

Cast
- TALI, a macaque baker whose hands shape dough faster than her speech.
- GRANDMOTHER NARA, a capuchin innkeeper who guards an old recipe.
- CHEF VEL, a gibbon food critic who describes texture like weather.
- RUK, a ring-tailed lemur kitchen helper who reads the oven gauge.
- SENA, an orangutan traveler stranded by the snow.

## Scene 1: The Review

INT. SNOWLINE INN KITCHEN - EVENING

Snow rattles the windows. A notice says the inn will close unless its winter meal earns a strong review. Tali kneads dough. Chef Vel watches a slow-motion close-up of flour falling across the table.

VEL: The flour enters the room like fresh snowfall.

TALI: It came from the bag.

NARA: The old loaf recipe has kept this inn open for generations.

TALI: The recipe says "warm oven." It does not say a temperature.

RUK: The oven gauge is stuck at cold.

NARA: It always reads cold before it gets hot.

RUK: It reads cold now too.

A gust rattles the flue. The oven flame flickers.

## Scene 2: The Family Recipe

Nara produces a handwritten recipe, stained with butter and flour. Tali compares it with her own notes. The amounts differ. Nara has added honey, while Tali uses dried apples.

NARA: The recipe was written before apples grew on this slope.

TALI: Then it is not the old recipe.

NARA: It is the old idea.

Vel asks each of them to describe the loaf's memory. Nara recalls feeding travelers after a blizzard. Tali remembers baking small rolls for the inn's repair crew. Vel narrates a close-up of a seed topping as though it were a mountain range.

VEL: The seed is an island in a sea of crust.

RUK: It is a pumpkin seed.

## Scene 3: The Oven Crisis

The power flickers. The oven stops heating. The dough continues to rise. Ruk discovers the flue damper is stuck. Tali wants to punch the oven; Nara stops her.

NARA: The oven is not your rival.

TALI: It has been winning.

Ruk uses a long spoon to open the damper. The heat returns unevenly. Vel asks whether this will change the loaf's crust.

TALI: It will change whether we burn it.

Sena, the stranded traveler, offers to move the dining tables closer to the kitchen so the guests can help shape rolls. Tali accepts. The inn guests form a line; each primate shapes a different size, and the smallest rolls bake first.

## Scene 4: The Tasting

The first batch comes out lopsided. Vel tastes one. He pauses dramatically, then chews.

VEL: The crust is loud. The center is soft. The shape is uncertain.

TALI: We call that a loaf.

NARA: The old travelers used to break bread together. They did not wait for a straight edge.

Vel looks at the family recipe, then at the inn's guests. He admits he has been reviewing restaurants as if they are stages, not kitchens.

VEL: I wanted one perfect loaf.

RUK: We have twelve good ones.

Tali serves the rolls to the guests. The room warms. The power returns, but the oven's gauge still reads cold.

## Scene 5: The Final Loaf

Tali combines the family methods: dried apple from her batch, honey from Nara's recipe, and seeds from the pantry. She adjusts the bake by watching the crust, not the faulty gauge. Vel keeps the kitchen clear instead of narrating. Ruk uses a timer. The loaf rises evenly.

The critic tastes it. He says the crust carries the inn's history, then stops himself.

VEL: It tastes like something people can share.

TALI: That is what it is for.

Sena tells them the road has reopened. She offers to spread the word about the inn, but Nara asks her to stay for breakfast first.

## Scene 6: The Review

The next day, Vel writes a review praising the inn's warm room, shared table, and adaptable bread. He gives no score for symmetry. The inn stays open. Tali begins a new recipe book, leaving space for future notes.

NARA: You should write down the oven temperature.

RUK: The gauge does not know.

TALI: We can mark the flame and the time.

## Final Image

A loaf cools on the windowsill as the snow melts from the sign outside. Its crust is irregular, and the inn's guests tear it apart before anyone can photograph it.
