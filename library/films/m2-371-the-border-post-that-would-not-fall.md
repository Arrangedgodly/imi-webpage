# The Border Post That Would Not Fall

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: frontier adventure films, patrol standoffs, dramatic border maps, and heroes who think a fence is a cliffhanger*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-013*

CAST
RANGER LUMA, a lemur
SURVEYOR VEX, a macaque
POST KEEPER NARI, an orangutan
MESSENGER PIP, a capuchin
BEAVER, a local builder

SCENE 1. EXT. FOREST BORDER - MORNING

A wooden marker stands between two park districts. Ranger Luma finds it leaning at an angle. Surveyor Vex declares the border has shifted. Post keeper Nari says the marker is old and the soil is soft. A beaver works nearby, carrying branches.

VEX: The line has moved.

NARI: The post has leaned.

Pip arrives with a map from both districts. The boundary appears to cross a creek, a footpath, and a picnic site.

SCENE 2. INT. RANGER STATION - LATER

Vex spreads the maps across the table. One is printed from an old survey and the other is current. The lines do not match. He traces them with red string, which tangles around his compass. Luma checks the survey date. The older map predates the creek's new channel.

PIP: Is the post in the wrong place?

NARI: The creek moved around it.

Vex proposes a field expedition to restore the boundary. Luma says they first need the official survey markers. Nari points to a drawer labeled MAPS. Vex opens it and finds only folded picnic menus. He pulls the drawer all the way out and discovers the survey folder behind it.

SCENE 2A. INT. RANGER STATION - AFTERNOON

Nari overlays the current survey with the old map. The lines differ because the creek changed course, not because the park boundary moved. She points out a second marker farther north that confirms the boundary. Vex asks if the marker could be a decoy. Luma says the surveyor placed both posts to make the line easy to find. Pip checks the map's scale and sees that the northern marker is a quarter mile away.

PIP: We can walk there before measuring here.

VEX: That delays the dramatic discovery.

NARI: It prevents a mistaken boundary.

They load a compass, stakes, and a level into the ranger truck. Vex packs a flare gun for the expedition. Luma removes it and replaces it with a first-aid kit. The creek is shallow, but the bank is slippery. The crew wears boots and takes the marked path rather than cutting through the brush.

SCENE 3. EXT. FOREST BORDER - AFTERNOON

At the northern survey marker, Nari checks its coordinates against the current map. Vex holds the measuring tape while Pip reads the numbers aloud. The results match the record. They follow the line back to the leaning post and compare its base. Soil has washed away on one side; the boundary has not moved. The beaver's branch dam slowed the creek into a shallow pool beneath the post.

Vex steps toward the bank to measure the waterline. Luma points him back to firm ground. His tape slips from his hand and floats toward the dam. The beaver retrieves it and drops it beside Nari's boot. Pip rewinds the tape slowly so it does not snap back into Vex's face. Vex tries to make the rescue sound like a border negotiation; the beaver leaves before his speech reaches the word "sovereignty." Nari marks the old post's coordinates on the map while Luma asks the ranger team to review the beaver habitat before changing the water flow.

They set a new marker at the surveyed point above the waterline. The beaver drags another branch into its dam, away from the post. Vex announces that the border has been restored. Luma corrects him: they replaced a leaning marker and documented a changed creek. The two park districts agree the shared path remains open.

SCENE 4. EXT. PICNIC SITE - SUNSET

Vex places a dramatic sign beside the new marker. It reads THIS SIDE and THAT SIDE. Nari turns it around and writes the official district names. Pip lays out a picnic blanket directly over the border line. Luma says that is allowed because the site is shared. The beaver approaches and drops a branch beside the post. It has been chewed into a small wedge, perfect for keeping the marker level.

VEX: The border is secure.

NARI: It is visible.

SCENE 5. EXT. FOREST BORDER - NIGHT

The team records the new coordinates and packs the old leaning post away for repair. Vex asks if the beaver should sign the survey. Luma says it has already helped by returning the measuring tape. The crew thanks the animal and leaves the picnic site clean.

FINAL IMAGE: The boundary marker stands upright as the creek curves past it, carrying a leaf from one park district to the other.
