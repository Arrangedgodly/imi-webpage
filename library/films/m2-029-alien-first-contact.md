# The Ambassador Is a Rock

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: first-contact science fiction conventions, translation panic, and protocol rituals*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A monkey observatory team receives a mysterious signal from a distant world, only to discover the "alien ambassador" is a meteorite that keeps triggering their formal greeting protocol.

Cast: Dr. Omi, a macaque astronomer; Captain Bex, a cautious baboon; Wisp, a young lemur translator; Professor Slate, a tortoise linguist; and a meteorite called Visitor.

SCENE 1. INT. OBSERVATORY, NIGHT

The radio telescope picks up a repeating pulse. The team gathers around the console. Wisp translates the pattern as "We are listening." The signal repeats. Captain Bex orders a formal response.

BEX: Welcome, travelers.

OMI: We don't know if it's a message.

SLATE: It could be a natural pulse.

BEX: We should be polite.

Wisp sends a greeting in five languages. The pulse continues unchanged.

SCENE 2. EXT. OBSERVATORY DOME, MORNING

A small meteorite lands near the dome. The telescope's equipment logs a strong signal. The team surrounds the rock with a welcome banner. Bex addresses it. Omi checks the receiver and finds the pulse is coming from a loose antenna cable.

The cable taps the dome in the wind. Wisp says the rhythm resembles the original signal. Slate points out that many things repeat.

BEX: The visitor is silent.

OMI: It is a rock.

BEX: Perhaps reserved.

SCENE 2A. INT. OBSERVATORY CONTROL ROOM, CONTINUOUS

Omi checks the signal against weather patterns. Wisp transcribes the pulse. Bex asks for a reply. Slate proposes waiting for confirmation.

BEX: We should be welcoming.

OMI: We should be accurate.

WISP: The pulse repeats every six seconds.

SLATE: So does the clock.

BEX: Does the clock have a message?

SLATE: It says the hour.

WISP: The signal changes slightly.

OMI: The antenna is loose.

BEX: Or the visitor is nervous.

SLATE: The cable is swaying.

BEX: Or the visitor is waving.

Omi tightens the cable. The signal changes again. Bex steps back from the console and bows. Wisp asks whether the rock should receive the same greeting. Bex says all guests deserve respect. Omi says the rock has no ears. Bex asks how they know.

SCENE 3. INT. OBSERVATORY LAB, AFTERNOON

The team studies the meteorite. It contains unusual minerals. Omi carefully collects a sample. Bex sets out a cup of water and a chair for the "ambassador." The chair is too small for a rock. Wisp suggests a tray. Slate suggests a label.

The antenna cable is tightened. The signal stops. The team panics because they think they have offended the visitor. Wisp asks whether the rock has moved. It has not.

SCENE 3A. INT. LAB, LATER

The team prepares an exchange table. Bex places a name card in front of the meteorite. Wisp writes "Visitor." Slate suggests "Rock." Omi labels it "Iron-rich meteorite, sample pending."

Bex says that sounds unfriendly. Omi says it sounds measurable. The meteorite's small sample rolls off the table and lands in a cup. Bex bows again. Slate retrieves the sample with tweezers.

A message arrives from the school. It includes a photo of the balloon and a request for help finding its missing basket. The team looks at the meteorite, then at the photograph. Omi says the rock is not the balloon. Bex asks if it might be the basket. Wisp says baskets do not contain iron meteorites.

SCENE 4. INT. CONTROL ROOM, EVENING

A new signal arrives from a satellite. It is a recorded greeting from a school in another country. The observatory had been picking up their science project. The students sent a message asking whether anyone had found their balloon.

Bex prepares a reply. Omi sends the coordinates of the meteorite and explains the antenna issue. Wisp translates the answer. The students respond with a drawing of a rock wearing a tiny crown.

SCENE 4A. INT. OBSERVATORY, EVENING

The students join by video call. They explain that their radio project sent regular pings to test the telescope. Bex welcomes them as ambassadors. One student asks if the observatory found their balloon. Omi shares the photo. The students laugh when they see the meteorite wearing a paper crown.

Wisp teaches them how to identify the signal pattern. Slate explains that a real message would need a source and context. Bex still presents the welcome banner. The students ask if they can keep it. He agrees, then realizes it is the observatory's only banner.

SCENE 5. EXT. OBSERVATORY, NIGHT

The team places the meteorite in a display case. They label it "Visitor, natural origin, excellent listener." Bex keeps the welcome banner. Omi says the next unknown signal should be checked before they assemble a committee.

FINAL IMAGE: The wind taps the antenna once. Bex straightens his uniform and raises a hand in greeting. The cable stays quiet. A moth hits the dome, and the whole team bows to it by reflex.
