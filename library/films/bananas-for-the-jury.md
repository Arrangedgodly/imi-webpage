# Bananas for the Jury

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: Courtroom awards dramas and earnest legal procedurals*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Logline: A young orangutan defense lawyer must prove that a famous orchard keeper did not steal his own fruit, while resisting a jury's appetite for a neat villain.

Cast: Judge Wren, a stern robin; Ada Orang, a young orangutan attorney; Mr. Peel, a retired gibbon orchard keeper; Prosecutor Sharp, a fox; Docket, a tortoise bailiff; and Juniper, a macaque witness who can remember every tree she has climbed.

SCENE 1. INT. COUNTY COURTROOM, MORNING

The courtroom is packed. A basket of bananas sits in evidence behind glass. Mr. Peel, a gibbon with old gardening gloves, watches the jury. His fingers twitch as if he is pruning an invisible branch.

SHARP: The defendant took forty crates of fruit from the community orchard.

ADA: The defendant planted the orchard.

JUDGE WREN: Both statements can be true. Continue.

The charge is theft. The orchard board claims Peel harvested fruit after his lease expired. Ada's first trial ended in a hung jury. She has one day before the board sells the land to a snack company.

SCENE 2. INT. ADA'S OFFICE, AFTERNOON

Ada studies a map. Juniper the macaque sits on the window ledge, sorting seed packets by smell. She has climbed every tree in the orchard and remembers the branches better than the paperwork.

JUNIPER: The west row bloomed late.

ADA: That matters?

JUNIPER: The crates were dated before the fruit ripened.

Ada finds a camera timestamp on the evidence receipt. The fruit was photographed two days before its supposed harvest. She hurries back to court, taking Juniper with her. Juniper rides on the back of Ada's chair. Docket the tortoise bailiff takes the stairs one measured step at a time.

SCENE 3. INT. COURTROOM, DAY

Sharp calls the orchard board manager, a polished peacock who says the fruit was removed without permission. He displays a chart with red bars. The jury leans forward.

ADA: Did you inspect the crates?

PEACOCK: I inspected the records.

ADA: Did you inspect the fruit?

PEACOCK: Fruit is a biological matter.

Judge Wren taps her beak. The peacock's chart falls off the easel. Behind it is a photograph showing a crate full of green, unripe bananas. Juniper points to the date sticker.

JUNIPER: Those are from the west row. I saw the blossoms. No bananas yet.

Sharp objects. The board photographed a different shipment and relabeled it. The evidence was not stolen fruit. It was a mislabeled shipment of green plantains meant for a school garden.

SCENE 4. INT. COURTROOM, LATER

The peacock argues that labels are "a flexible business tool." Sharp objects again. Ada calls Mr. Peel. He does not make a grand speech. He lays his gloves on the witness stand and describes each tree, the soil under it, and the children who planted them.

PEEL: I took no fruit. I did water the trees after my lease ended. The board had turned off the tap.

The jury looks at one another. Ada asks if he harvested anything. He says he picked up fallen fruit and left it at the school gate. He kept no money. He admits he broke the board's lock to reach the pump.

ADA: You broke a lock?

PEEL: Yes.

ADA: Why?

PEEL: The trees were thirsty.

His answer is plain, and it lands harder than a speech.

SCENE 5. INT. COURTROOM, EVENING

The jury returns. Docket takes so long to carry the verdict that the court reporter finishes a crossword. The jury finds Peel not guilty of theft. The judge then orders the board to return the orchard keys while the city reviews the lease.

Sharp asks to dismiss the remaining trespass charge. The judge refuses. Peel has admitted to breaking the lock, and the community deserves an honest hearing about why the water was shut off.

JUDGE WREN: A good cause does not make a broken lock disappear.

PEEL: No, Your Honor.

ADA: We'll answer for the lock. And we'll bring the water receipts.

The peacock leaves with his chart folded under one wing. Juniper takes the evidence bananas to the school kitchen, where they are properly labeled and eaten.

SCENE 6. EXT. COMMUNITY ORCHARD, SUNSET

The city opens the gates for a public hearing. Families water the trees. Ada arrives carrying a stack of forms and a watering can. Peel shows her how to prune a branch without cutting the new growth.

ADA: The board wants a longer appeal.

PEEL: Do they have a watering can?

She hands him one. Juniper climbs to the top of a tree and calls down that the west row is finally ripe. The jury foreperson arrives with a pie. Docket the tortoise arrives an hour later with the keys.

The first ripe banana is split into pieces too small for anyone to call a victory meal. Everyone eats anyway. Ada watches the orchard fill with people, not investors.

The camera holds on a new sign at the gate: PICK ONE. PLANT TWO.

Peel adds a third line in pencil: PLEASE WATER THE WEST ROW.

Fade out.
