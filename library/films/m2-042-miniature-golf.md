# The Eighteenth Windmill

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: sports rivalry cinema, underdog comeback edits, and absurd championship stakes*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: Two rival teams compete to save their neighborhood miniature-golf course, while a tournament director invents cinematic rules and the windmill refuses to cooperate.

Cast: Palla, a lemur with a careful putting stroke; Cruz, a capuchin who celebrates early; Coach Bix, a baboon; Judge Pebble, a tortoise referee; and Mr. Whistle, the course manager.

SCENE 1. EXT. MINIATURE GOLF COURSE, MORNING

A sign reads: COURSE MAY CLOSE AFTER TODAY'S TOURNAMENT. Coach Bix gathers his team beside a vending machine.

BIX: We play for the neighborhood.

CRUZ: And the lighthouse trophy.

PALLA: The trophy is plastic.

CRUZ: The neighborhood deserves plastic.

The rival team arrives. The tournament director announces a dramatic single-elimination format. Judge Pebble reads the posted rules: nine holes, lowest score wins. The director asks if they can add a sudden-death finale for television. Pebble says the rules do not mention one.

SCENE 2. EXT. MINIATURE COURSE, LATER

At the bridge hole, Cruz sinks a shot and pumps both fists before the ball reaches the cup. It strikes the bridge rail and rolls back to the tee.

CRUZ: Tactical rebound.

PEBBLE: Stroke two.

The director asks for a replay. Palla takes her shot and sends the ball cleanly under the bridge. The crowd applauds. The director asks her to do it again with more struggle. She points out that the ball is already in the hole.

They reach the loop-de-loop. Cruz hits too hard. His ball circles the loop three times and shoots back out. He bows to the camera; Judge Pebble marks the ball where it stopped. Mr. Whistle examines the course and admits the slope is uneven from last night's rain.

SCENE 3. EXT. WINDMILL HOLE, AFTERNOON

Palla lines up. The windmill blade stops in front of the opening. Mr. Whistle pulls a lever. The blades start, then stop again. He says the motor needs a rest. The director tells the camera operator to zoom in on Palla's determined eyes.

PALLA: Can we repair it?

DIRECTOR: First, a montage.

The teams gather golf balls to weigh down the ramp. Coach Bix stacks them into a tower that topples onto Cruz's scorecard. Palla levels the ramp using a scorecard and a small wedge. Mr. Whistle resets the motor. The windmill spins. Palla takes her shot; the ball passes through the blades and drops into the cup.

The director asks Cruz to miss dramatically for the comeback. Cruz refuses; he wants to make the shot. He taps the ball, and it stops one inch short.

CRUZ: That's close enough for the trailer.

PEBBLE: Not for the score.

SCENE 4. INT. SNACK SHACK, LATE AFTERNOON

The director tries to stage a locker-room speech among the soda machines. Coach Bix asks where the locker room is. The director says the vending machine will do. Bix gives his speech to a row of chips; Cruz buys a bag during the pause.

BIX: We have overcome the windmill.

PALLA: Mr. Whistle repaired the motor.

BIX: We have overcome Mr. Whistle's maintenance schedule.

Pebble asks them to sign the scorecard. The director offers a marker shaped like a trophy. It has no ink. Palla uses an ordinary pencil. The director calls it a humble prop.

The teams compare scores. They are tied. The director unveils a spoon and plastic lighthouse for sudden death. Pebble closes the rulebook.

PEBBLE: No spoon round.

DIRECTOR: Every sports movie has one.

MR. WHISTLE: This is miniature golf.

Palla notices the posted rules specify a shared team putt if the match is tied. The director asks whether the players can pretend not to know. They decline. The crowd votes to play by the sign. Cruz practices a celebration anyway, holding it until the ball lands.

SCENE 5. EXT. EIGHTEENTH HOLE, SUNSET

Before the final putt, the director asks for the teams to stare each other down. Cruz squints so hard that he cannot see the ball. Palla tells him to blink. He does, and the staring contest ends with the players checking the course together.

Palla and Cruz stand at the shared putt. They take turns guiding the ball around a ramp, over a bridge, and through the repaired windmill. It reaches the cup and stops at the edge. Cruz waits. It rolls in. He celebrates only after Pebble confirms the score.

The crowd cheers. Mr. Whistle announces the course will stay open as a community space. The director asks for a trophy shot. The players place the plastic lighthouse beside the real scorecard and invite the volunteers to help repaint the lanes.

The director asks the crowd for a chant. The volunteers chant the hole number and carry paint buckets toward the clubhouse.

FINAL IMAGE: Cruz tries one last early celebration with the empty trophy. Palla turns it upside down; a golf ball drops out and rolls toward the cup. He waits until it stops, then cheers for the ball.
