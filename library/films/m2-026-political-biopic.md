# The Mayor Who Kept the Mic

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: political biopic conventions, heroic memory edits, and campaign retrospective narration*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A campaign filmmaker tries to shape the story of a famous monkey mayor, but every heroic reenactment is interrupted by the citizens who remember the real version.

Cast: Mayor Nella, a macaque who served one term; Director Vale, a peacock documentarian; Bo, a capuchin former aide; Ms. Crumb, a pigeon reporter; and a chorus of residents who bring receipts.

SCENE 1. INT. DOCUMENTARY STUDIO, DAY

Vale records a voice-over beside a framed campaign poster. The poster shows Nella pointing toward a shining skyline. The actual skyline behind her was mostly scaffolding.

VALE: She transformed the city.

NELLA: I approved the sidewalk ramp.

VALE: We can make that more cinematic.

NELLA: It was a ramp.

Vale suggests a dramatic reenactment. Bo wears a sash and walks in slow motion through a hallway. He trips on the cable and knocks over the poster. Vale calls it "the cost of leadership."

SCENE 2. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, AFTERNOON

Nella meets residents for an interview. Vale asks them to describe her greatest achievement. One names the ramp. Another says she fixed the public clock. A third says she moved a bench into the shade.

Vale asks for something grander. Ms. Crumb reads from old meeting minutes. Nella once promised a new fountain but delivered a drinking spout after discovering the fountain would block a wheelchair route.

VALE: Can we call it a bold redesign?

CRUMB: The minutes call it a spout.

SCENE 3. INT. ARCHIVE ROOM, EVENING

Vale searches for archival footage of the campaign. He finds Nella arguing with a broken microphone, helping carry boxes, and waiting for a town hall audience to arrive. The room was empty for forty minutes.

Bo suggests cutting the waiting. Nella says it mattered because the residents had been delayed by a bus detour. Vale says the silence may be hard to sell. Nella says the audience came.

SCENE 4. INT. SCREENING ROOM, NIGHT

The rough cut plays. It includes the slow-motion hallway fall, a dramatic score over the ramp opening, and a close-up of the drinking spout. Residents watch. They laugh at the wrong moments and complain when Vale cuts their questions.

Ms. Crumb asks to see the unedited interview. Vale says there isn't time. Crumb produces her own recording. It shows the crew asking residents to repeat compliments and cut criticism.

Nella asks Vale to include the full town meeting. He says it is eleven hours long. She suggests a five-minute excerpt with the questions left in.

SCENE 4A. INT. EDITING BOOTH, LATER

Vale tries to record a voice-over describing the mayor as a decisive reformer. Behind him, residents are watching the footage and interrupting every grand phrase with a correction. He tries three takes.

VALE: Nella changed the city forever.

CRUMB: She changed one curb cut.

VALE: Nella faced fierce opposition.

RESIDENT: It was a scheduling delay.

VALE: Nella brought hope to the square.

NELLA: I brought a folding table.

Vale stops recording. He asks if the audience can accept an ordinary story. Crumb says they can accept an honest one. Vale rewinds to the meeting footage. The residents explain how Nella listened, changed the plan, and came back with a ramp that worked. Vale removes the dramatic score. The quiet sound of the meeting is clearer, and the audience laughs when the microphone cable trips Bo again.

Bo asks that the next cut include his landing. Nella says it can stay if the crew also shows him carrying the boxes afterward. Bo thinks about this, then agrees. Vale marks the sequence "keep, with bruise."

SCENE 5. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, MORNING

The documentary premieres outdoors. It shows Nella carrying boxes, listening to residents, and losing the microphone cable. The final shot is the new ramp, crowded with people using it. Vale's title card reads "THE MAYOR WHO BUILT A RAMP."

SCENE 6. INT. SCREENING ROOM, CONTINUOUS

The audience asks questions. Vale holds a microphone.

CRUMB: Why cut the bus delay?

VALE: It slowed the story.

NELLA: It delayed the meeting.

BO: We waited.

RESIDENT: We arrived.

VALE: The ramp was small.

NELLA: The curb was high.

CRUMB: It changed the route.

RESIDENT: I use it daily.

VALE: Could we add music?

NELLA: Add the questions.

BO: Keep the cable.

VALE: It fell.

BO: I fell.

CRUMB: You tripped.

BO: The cable tripped.

VALE: The mayor looks heroic.

NELLA: I was carrying boxes.

RESIDENT: I was holding the door.

CRUMB: Who built the ramp?

NELLA: The crew did.

VALE: That's less personal.

NELLA: It was public work.

BO: We have receipts.

VALE: Receipts are not cinematic.

CRUMB: They have dates.

RESIDENT: And totals.

NELLA: Show the minutes.

VALE: All eleven hours?

CRUMB: Five minutes.

BO: With questions.

VALE: Even the criticism?

NELLA: Especially that.

CRUMB: The spout is still crooked.

NELLA: We know.

RESIDENT: The clock works.

BO: Mostly.

VALE: The story has no villain.

CRUMB: It has a ramp.

NELLA: And a meeting.

VALE: We can end there.

CRUMB: We already did.

The audience laughs. Vale writes "RAMP" on the title card, then asks whether it needs a dramatic shadow.

FINAL IMAGE: Nella watches the credits. Vale asks if she wants a more heroic title. She points to the ramp, where Bo is carrying the fallen campaign poster. The poster catches on the railing and becomes a temporary shade canopy.
