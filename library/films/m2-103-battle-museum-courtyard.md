# The Battle of the Museum Courtyard

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: historical battle epics, swelling war-room speeches, and armies that fight over an outdated map*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: Two reenactment groups prepare an epic courtyard battle, then realize their maps describe different versions of the museum grounds.

Cast: Captain Mott, a gorilla; Commander Sela, a lemur; Kavi, a macaque museum guide; and Puck, a capuchin drummer.

SCENE 1. EXT. MUSEUM COURTYARD, MORNING

Two historical reenactment groups face each other across a chalk line. Mott gives a speech about honor. Sela raises a flag. Kavi checks the permit and says the courtyard is open to the public at noon. Puck beats a drum. A tourist walks between the groups and asks where the cafe is.

MOTT: The field belongs to the brave.

KAVI: The field belongs to the museum.

Sela compares the maps. Her copy shows a fountain at the center. Mott's shows a vegetable bed. The fountain was removed last year. Both commanders insist their map is authoritative.

MOTT: My line reaches the fountain.

SELA: The fountain was removed.

MOTT: Perhaps it moved.

KAVI: Fountains do not move. The museum moved it.

Mott lays his map on the ground and places toy figures over the former vegetable bed. Sela moves one figure to the ramp. Mott calls it a flanking move. Kavi asks them to keep the figures off the tactile paving. Puck drums a roll; the echo makes a tourist turn back toward the cafe. Sela asks him to keep the beat inside the marked area.

SCENE 2. INT. MUSEUM MAP ROOM, LATER

Kavi lays out the current grounds plan. The courtyard has a new ramp, a seating area, and a marked fire lane. The reenactors' maps are from different decades. Mott says the old terrain gives the battle authenticity. Sela points out that the old terrain no longer exists.

Puck tests the drum in the map room. It echoes so loudly that a curator asks him to stop. The commanders examine the permit. It allows a demonstration, not a staged battle, and asks that pedestrian paths remain clear. MOTT: A demonstration can still be epic.

KAVI: Then show how the map changed.

Mott asks which date the narrator should describe. Neither map has a date on its cover. Kavi finds archive stamps: 1984 and 2009. Mott says his map is not outdated; it is historical. Sela says that is why the museum should display it, not use it to position people. Puck tries a quiet drumbeat, but the curator asks him to stop until the room tour has passed.

SCENE 3. EXT. MUSEUM COURTYARD, NOON

The public enters. Mott's group sets up near the former vegetable bed. Sela's group lines up by the ramp. A stroller needs the access path. Kavi asks both groups to move their flags. Puck carries the drum to a quieter corner and plays a measured beat.

The commanders begin a mock charge. Their groups stop when they reach the old fountain location, now a bench. Mott orders his line to flank the bench. Sela asks why they are maneuvering around seating. The museum guide points to the current map and traces the clear route.

Mott and Sela agree to a choreographed sequence based on the historical map's revisions: first show the old boundary, then the new ramp, then the current courtyard. They narrate the change rather than pretending the old fountain remains. Visitors watch and ask questions about how the museum expanded.

VISITOR: Why was the ramp added?

KAVI: The museum improved access to the courtyard.

Mott starts a speech about sacrifice. A child points out that his toy soldiers are in the stroller path. He moves them. Sela asks the groups to show the old boundary on a paper plan rather than blocking the real route. They use chalk on the map sheet and compare it with the current courtyard.

SCENE 4. EXT. COURTYARD, AFTERNOON

The demonstration ends on time. Both groups roll up their old maps and attach them to a display board. Kavi adds the current plan and a note explaining the date of each version. Mott asks if the crowd will remember the battle. A child says she will remember the ramp.

Sela suggests using the next rehearsal to check the museum's updated map before setting the route. Puck writes the date on the drum case. Mott thanks the staff for the permit and helps clear the flags.

VISITOR: Will you return next year?

SELA: Yes, with an updated map.

Puck checks the courtyard clock and packs his drum before the event closes. Kavi places both historic maps in sleeves so the old route can be explained without being mistaken for the current one.

FINAL IMAGE: The two commanders stand beside the display, comparing map lines with the real courtyard. The public walks through the open path without having to choose a side.
