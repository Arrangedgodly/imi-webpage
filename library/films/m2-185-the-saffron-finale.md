# The Saffron Finale

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: culinary competition films, tearful childhood recipes, villainous plating reveals, and judges who eat under a ticking clock*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-007*

CAST
CHEF TULA, a capuchin contestant
MARCEL, a gibbon judge
BREE, a squirrel monkey stagehand
CHEF VARN, a macaque rival

SCENE 1. INT. GRAND KITCHEN STUDIO - NIGHT

A giant clock counts down from forty minutes. Twelve chefs begin the final round of the Golden Ladle competition. The challenge: make a dish from one basket of root vegetables, lentils, and saffron.

Tula opens her basket. Under the vegetables is a recipe card stained with tea. She recognizes the handwriting of her grandmother, who taught her to cook by smell because she never trusted clocks.

TULA: I thought I had lost this.

BREE: The producers put it in every basket. They said it would create a personal moment.

Tula turns the card over. It lists no measurements, only "taste as you go." She puts it away.

Across the studio, Varn arranges ingredients by color. He has brought a portable spotlight to make his plating look dramatic.

SCENE 2. INT. GRAND KITCHEN - LATER

The contestants cook. Judges roam between stations asking where the saffron came from, what memory it evokes, and whether the cook can describe a difficult moment in under twelve seconds.

Tula tastes the lentils. They are bland. She adds salt. The broadcast clock cuts to a close-up of her grandmother's recipe card. Varn times his stirring to the ticking soundtrack.

MARCEL: Chef Tula, what does this dish say about your journey?

TULA: It says the lentils need another minute.

The judges exchange a look. The director signals for a more emotional answer. Tula adds a pinch of saffron, then waits for its aroma to open.

Varn's spotlight overheats a bowl of flatbread. Bree turns it off. Varn accuses her of sabotaging his "golden reveal."

BREE: It was smoking.

SCENE 3. INT. GRAND KITCHEN - FINAL TEN MINUTES

A producer delivers a new instruction: the dish must tell a story on the plate. Tula arranges the lentils in a spiral. The director asks her to make the spiral look more like a clock.

TULA: Then it will look like a clock, not food.

Varn stacks vegetables into a tower. One carrot slips. He catches it with a dramatic flourish, then turns the fall into a "deconstructed harvest." The judges applaud the recovery.

Tula hears the clock tick louder. She stops. Instead of racing, she stirs slowly, tasting between turns. The steam carries the saffron scent across the studio. A judge follows it to her station.

The director tries to cut away to Varn's tower, but the camera operator keeps drifting back to Tula's pot. The aroma has reached the gallery. A few crew members lean around the barrier, trying to guess the ingredients.

VARN: If they can smell her dish, they'll expect mine to smell just as strong.

BREE: Your spotlight is heating the saffron tin.

Varn looks. The tin has rolled off the station and is balanced on the edge of the hot lamp base. He lunges, catches it, and tips his vegetable tower. Carrots roll in every direction. The judges' tasting spoons rattle like a bell.

VARN: I meant to create movement.

MARCEL: You have created a clean-up challenge.

Tula turns off her burner, tastes once more, and adjusts the salt. She does not look at the clock. The studio countdown reaches one minute, then ten seconds, then zero. Her lentils are finished before the alarm sounds.

MARCEL: That smells like a kitchen. Not a set.

TULA: It's the saffron blooming. Now the lentils are ready.

Bree brings a plain bowl. Tula serves a small portion with flatbread, no spotlight, no tower.

SCENE 4. INT. JUDGING TABLE - MOMENTS LATER

The chefs present their dishes. Varn unveils his vegetable tower. A carrot rolls off the plate and into the judges' water pitcher. He insists it is a commentary on irrigation.

Tula serves her lentils. The judges taste in silence. The clock reaches zero before they finish chewing.

MARCEL: The dish is warm, balanced, and clear. The recipe is not written down.

TULA: I'll write it after I remember what I did.

MARCEL: You cooked by attention, not by the card.

The judges award Tula the Golden Ladle. Varn's dish earns an honorable mention for irrigation.

SCENE 5. INT. EMPTY STUDIO - LATE NIGHT

The cameras are off. The crew eats the remaining food from mismatched bowls. Bree takes the grandmother's recipe card from the basket and hands it to Tula.

TULA: They wanted a story from the card.

BREE: You gave them one by not following it.

Tula writes the recipe on the back, adding her own measurements. Then she circles the final line: "Taste as you go."

FINAL IMAGE: The Golden Ladle rests in a sink full of dishes. It reflects Tula's smiling face, upside down, as the crew finishes the last bowl.
