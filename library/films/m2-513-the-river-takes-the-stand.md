# The River Takes the Stand

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: courtroom dramas with a symbolic witness, theatrical objections, and a final piece of physical evidence*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: JUDGE SABLE, a mandrill; ADA, a macaque attorney; BRICK, a baboon defense lawyer; ELI, a gibbon surveyor; RILL, an orangutan river keeper; CLERK, a capuchin.

INT. COURTHOUSE - DAY

Rain taps the high windows. A jar of cloudy water sits on the evidence table. Judge Sable enters. Ada rises.

ADA: The town accuses the river of stealing the footbridge.

BRICK: The river is not a person. It cannot be tried.

JUDGE SABLE: Then why did you file a response on its behalf?

BRICK: The water carried my papers away. I found them downstream.

The clerk places a map beside the jar. A red line marks the river bend; a black line marks the bridge.

RILL: The river changed its course after the winter storm. The bridge posts were left on dry ground.

ADA: Did you warn the town before the water rose?

RILL: I posted a flood marker on the bank. The marker moved too.

BRICK: Convenient witness.

RILL: The marker is in the evidence box.

[The clerk opens a waterproof case and lays the bent marker beside the map. Mud covers one end.]

CLERK: It was found downstream, with the bridge plank.

JUDGE SABLE: The court will hear the marker, though it has no oath to take.

ADA: You say the bridge was moved?

RILL: I say the water went around it.

BRICK: An elegant distinction. The town still cannot cross.

Ada asks the clerk to read the complaint. The document says the bridge was "removed by an unknown force." Brick calls the phrase dramatic. Ada says it was written by a committee after the path ended in mud. Judge Sable orders the clerk to display a photograph of the washed-out west bank. The image shows one footing tilted, the other on dry ground.

ADA: Does the town claim the river pulled the whole bridge away?

BRICK: The claim says the river took the crossing.

JUDGE SABLE: Then we will determine what "took" means before we decide who owes the town a bridge.

The clerk pins the photograph beside the map. Rill explains that the river moved around a gravel bar after heavy rain. The old channel is now a shallow side stream, while the new current bends under the surviving bridge span.

ACT ONE

INT. COURTROOM - LATER

Ada calls Eli, the surveyor. Eli unrolls a chart. The end catches on the water jar and pulls it toward the table edge. The clerk catches the jar.

ELI: The river's channel widened by six feet.

BRICK: Is six feet enough to move a bridge?

ELI: The bridge is thirty feet long.

BRICK: Then the river stole less than one fifth of it.

JUDGE SABLE: Objection sustained. Fractions are not a defense.

Ada asks Rill to describe the storm. Rill points to a photograph of the bridge, standing intact before the flood.

RILL: The west bank gave way. The bridge rotated and settled across the old channel.

BRICK: Can the river testify to that?

ADA: The water left a line on the stone.

The clerk displays a stone with a dark water mark. Judge Sable leans closer.

JUDGE SABLE: The mark is higher than the bridge deck.

BRICK: The river is withholding evidence.

RILL: That mark is from the flood peak. The bridge was already gone.

A sudden leak forms beneath the courtroom window. A drop lands in the jar, making a ring.

BRICK: The witness has returned to the scene.

JUDGE SABLE: The witness is outside the building.

ACT TWO

EXT. RIVERBANK - AFTERNOON

The judge moves the hearing to the riverbank. The court follows along a safe path. Ada places two small wooden bridge models on the mud: one across the old channel, one across the new.

ADA: If we move the footings to the new bank, the bridge fits.

BRICK: The town cannot afford a new bridge.

ADA: The beams are intact. We are asking for labor and a new footing, not a second bridge.

BRICK: If we move the support, the crossing could narrow.

ELI: The span can be tested before anyone lifts a beam.

[The judge asks both attorneys to hold one end of the wooden model. The clerk places a small flat stone on the center. It stays level.]

CLERK: The model holds.

BRICK: The model is made of wood.

JUDGE SABLE: So is the bridge.

RILL: The old bridge can be reused. Its center support is sound.

Eli sets the bridge model across the marked points. It fits. Brick tests the span with a flat stone, which holds.

BRICK: I withdraw the theft claim. The river did not take the bridge.

JUDGE SABLE: Then what did?

ELI: The town built it where the river used to be.

The judge orders the town to restore the bridge with the existing beams and keep a clear flood channel. The clerk records the ruling on a waterproof board. Ada and Brick carry the wooden model back to the courthouse.

RILL: The river has no opinion.

BRICK: It has a current.

JUDGE SABLE: The court will not subpoena the current.

The rain stops. The water moves beneath the new bridge site without touching the old bank.

FINAL IMAGE: A drop falls from the courthouse eave into the evidence jar as the town workers lift the first bridge beam.
