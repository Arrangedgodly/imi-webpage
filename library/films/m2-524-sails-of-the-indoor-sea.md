# Sails of the Indoor Sea

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: swashbuckling sea adventures transplanted to a community pool, with a treasure map, mutiny, and a final crossing*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-018*

Cast: CAPTAIN BRINE, a baboon; MIRA, a macaque navigator; OLD FIN, an orangutan deckhand; KIP, a capuchin cabin boy; HARBOR MASTER, a gibbon; ADMIRAL SALT, a mandrill rival.

INT. COMMUNITY POOL - DAY

A pool has been dressed as an indoor sea. Blue fabric hangs over the walls. A cardboard ship floats on a slow-moving lane divider. Captain Brine holds a damp map.

CAPTAIN BRINE: The treasure lies beyond the deep end.

MIRA: The deep end is four feet.

OLD FIN: It is deep enough for a cardboard ship.

KIP: The map says "turn at the ladder."

A lifeguard whistles from the chair. Harbor Master raises a hand.

HARBOR MASTER: No running, no diving from the ship, and no treasure buried in the pool.

Admiral Salt arrives with a much larger cardboard vessel. Its sail blocks the pool clock.

SALT: I claim the treasure first.

BRINE: You do not know the route.

SALT: I have a compass.

Mira checks it. The needle points toward the metal ladder.

MIRA: The compass is attracted to the pool rail.

The Harbor Master lays a map across a dry bench. Mira traces the lane rope and the filter outlet. The slow current moves toward the shallow end, then curls back near the ladder.

MIRA: The sea has a return current.

BRINE: We can sail along it.

HARBOR MASTER: You can move a cardboard boat through the lane after the swimmers are out.

The crew waits while the final swimmers leave. The lifeguard checks the lane and signals them to launch. Brine's ship catches the current but drifts toward Salt's larger vessel. Mira calls a turn. Kip pulls the sail flat, and Old Fin uses a pool noodle as a rudder.

KIP: We have steered with a noodle.

OLD FIN: It is more reliable than the compass.

Salt challenges Brine to a race to the far wall. Harbor Master warns that the pool clock is not a finish line. The captains agree to a slow crossing, keeping both cardboard ships away from the lane rope.

ACT ONE

EXT. POOL DECK - MOMENTS LATER

The captains launch their cardboard ships. Brine's ship drifts slowly. Salt's ship catches a current from the filter jet and races ahead.

KIP: Their ship is faster.

MIRA: The current is pulling it into the lane rope.

Salt's sail tangles in the rope. The ship spins. The lifeguard blows a whistle, and the captains pull both vessels to the deck.

HARBOR MASTER: The lane is closed until the rope is clear.

Salt accuses Brine of sabotage. Mira shows the filter jet on a laminated pool diagram. The current comes from the wall, not from either ship.

MIRA: We can use it if we turn the sail.

Brine rotates the sail. The cardboard ship glides across the pool without touching the lane rope. Salt tries the same maneuver, but their sail folds flat.

Salt tosses a foam ring toward the bow. It lands around the mast and pulls the sail sideways. The ship spins in a slow circle. Brine catches the loose rope, but the Harbor Master orders both crews to stop before the vessels cross into the lesson lane.

SALT: My crew has betrayed me.

OLD FIN: Your sail is wet paper.

The lifeguard retrieves the ring with a long hook. Mira adjusts both ships so the current carries them parallel to the wall. The captains move from rivalry to cooperation long enough to keep the boats from colliding.

SALT: My crew has betrayed me.

OLD FIN: Your sail is wet paper.

The map begins to bleed ink. Kip holds it above the water. A second line appears beneath the first, showing that the treasure is not buried at the deep end. It is taped under the pool's starting block.

KIP: The treasure is at the shallow end.

BRINE: The map was upside down.

ACT TWO

The Harbor Master checks the starting block. A small locked box is taped underneath. The lifeguard approves removing it only after the pool closes. Everyone waits on the deck while the last swimmers leave.

The captain uses the map's brass corner as a flat tool to lift the tape. Inside the box are a set of old pool badges and a note from the center's first swim club.

HARBOR MASTER: This is a local history box, not gold.

SALT: We crossed a sea for badges.

MIRA: We crossed a pool.

Salt asks to keep one badge. Brine says it belongs to the community archive. The Harbor Master agrees to display the box in the lobby. The crews fold both cardboard ships flat and return the lane rope to its hooks.

KIP: We need a new map.

MIRA: We need one that says pool.

The lifeguard tests the filter jet. The current nudges a loose foam ring toward the starting block. Brine catches it and hands it to Salt.

FINAL IMAGE: The two folded cardboard ships rest beside the archive box while the pool clock ticks above the calm water.
