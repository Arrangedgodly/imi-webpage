# The Hat Changes Hands

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: continuity editing, match cuts, and directors who blame props for impossible scene changes*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: A shopkeeper's hat jumps from head to head between takes, and the continuity assistant becomes the only person who knows what the scene is about.

Cast: Mera, a lemur continuity assistant; Oko, a gorilla actor; Fenn, a macaque director; and Puck, a capuchin prop runner.

SCENE 1. INT. COSTUME SHOP SET, MORNING

Oko tries on a tall green hat. Fenn frames a shot of him entering the shop. Puck runs in with a smaller hat.

FENN: We need the hat from the first shot.

PUCK: This is the hat from the first shot.

MERA: That was a blue hat.

Oko checks the script. His character enters without a hat. Fenn says the hat shows his change of status. Mera flips through the continuity photos. The character has already become a shopkeeper; the hat was on the counter.

OKO: Then I enter bareheaded and buy the hat?

FENN: You enter with the hat. The audience understands you have earned it.

MERA: The audience has not seen you earn it.

Fenn asks for a close-up that can imply the missing scene. Oko holds the hat while Puck turns it toward camera. The price tag faces inward. Mera rotates the hat back, then takes a photograph. Puck swaps the hats while she writes the note. She catches him using the reflection in a makeup mirror.

MERA: Blue hat, tag left. Green hat, tag right.

PUCK: What if the hats want privacy?

MERA: They are on a hook in a shop.

Fenn asks Oko to walk past the mirror again, this time noticing his reflection. Oko stops, sees the green hat on his head, and removes it. In the mirror, the hat remains. Puck has put the second hat on the mannequin behind him. Mera photographs the exact mistake for the continuity log.

SCENE 2. INT. COSTUME SHOP SET, LATER

The crew shoots the entrance again. Oko walks in wearing the blue hat. The camera pans to the counter; the green hat sits there. Mera holds up a continuity photo showing both hats on the same hook. The blue hat is on the left; the green hat is on the right. Fenn asks Puck to switch them back for the close-up. Puck switches the hats, then switches the hook too, carrying it to the other wall.

FENN: That is a much bigger change.

PUCK: The hook was unreliable.

Mera measures the distance between the hats with a strip of tape. Oko enters on cue and reaches for the blue hat, but the tape catches his sleeve. He turns, and both hats spin together like a shop window display.

MERA: The hats moved between takes.

FENN: Maybe the hook is unreliable.

Puck ties a string from the hats to the hook. During the next take, Oko bumps the string and the hats swing into the camera frame. Fenn calls it a symbolic transition. Mera asks what the symbol means. Fenn says they will decide in editing.

SCENE 3. INT. COSTUME SHOP SET, AFTERNOON

Mera pins a small blue tag inside the blue hat and a green tag inside the green one. Oko enters with no hat, sees both on the counter, and puts on the blue one. Puck moves the green hat to the wall, where it falls into a basket.

Fenn asks for a dramatic close-up of Oko's face. The hat brim covers his eyes. Oko lifts it. Mera calls cut and records the new position. Fenn wants to continue without resetting. Mera shows him a photo from the previous angle: the hat was on the hook. They restore it, then shoot the line with Oko holding the hat at chest height.

Oko asks what his character wants. Fenn says he is trying to sell the shop. Mera points out that the script has the customer asking for a hat, not the shop. Fenn checks the page and agrees. Oko offers the blue hat to the customer. The customer tries it on; it fits. The scene finally has a beginning, middle, and end.

SCENE 4. INT. EDITING ROOM, EVENING

Mera labels the takes by hat color and position. Fenn reviews the cut. The hats stay where they were placed. Puck asks if the string gag should remain. Fenn says it makes the props look haunted. Mera says it makes the hook look crowded.

The final scene screens at the local cinema. A viewer asks whether the blue hat was important. Oko says it was a hat the customer wanted to buy. Fenn tries to call that a symbol of change. Mera points to the price tag.

FINAL IMAGE: The blue hat sits in the shop window with a clear price tag. The green hat remains in its labeled storage box.
