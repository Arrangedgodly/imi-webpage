# Peaches at Last Call

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: Romantic comedies built around meet-cutes, grand gestures, and rival plans*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

Logline: Two rival fruit-cart owners, a meticulous langur and an impulsive capuchin, fall for each other while trying to save the night market from a developer who plans to replace it with a smoothie kiosk.

Cast: Lila, a langur who measures every peach; Rafa, a capuchin who juggles oranges when anxious; Auntie Cora, a mandrill matchmaker with a portable radio; Dean Vale, a peacock developer; and Marnie, an elderly macaque customer who buys one plum each Friday.

SCENE 1. EXT. NIGHT MARKET, EVENING

Lila stacks peaches in exact rows. Rafa rolls a cart past her, juggling three oranges and one peach. The peach lands in her display. She catches it before it bruises.

LILA: That one was sorted by firmness.

RAFA: It looked lonely.

They argue about display height. Lila is a langur and can reach the high awning without a ladder. Rafa is a capuchin and climbs the cart to string lights. He hangs them crooked. She straightens them. They keep catching each other's eyes and looking away.

SCENE 2. INT. MARKET OFFICE, LATER

The market manager announces the lease ends in thirty days. Dean Vale plans to replace the stalls with a branded smoothie kiosk. Auntie Cora, a mandrill who runs the radio booth, says the market must raise enough money to buy the building.

Lila proposes a precision tasting event. Rafa proposes a fruit parade. They decide to do both and immediately disagree about whether parade fruit needs labels.

MARNIE, an elderly macaque, enters with a plum. She asks if the Friday discount still applies. Lila says yes, even though the discount ended last month. Rafa notices. He does not mention it.

SCENE 3. EXT. MARKET, DAY

Preparations go badly. Rafa's parade route circles into a bus lane. Lila's tasting table is so carefully arranged that nobody touches it. She has written flavor notes on tiny cards. People read them and leave hungry.

Rafa helps Lila loosen up by blindfolding himself and guessing peaches. He gets every one wrong but makes her laugh. She explains the fruit comes from a family grove that Dean's company tried to buy. He tells her his cart came from his father, who left him no money but a spectacular dent.

They almost kiss. Auntie Cora's radio squawks. The market has lost its power.

SCENE 4. EXT. MARKET, EVENING

Dean's contractors cut the market's electrical line. The night market goes dark. Lila sets out candles. Rafa leads customers in a fruit parade with hand-cranked lanterns. Marnie uses her cane to keep everyone in step.

Lila notices the crowd wants to taste, not read. She removes the cards and lets people choose by smell. Rafa stops juggling and helps her slice samples. Their separate ideas finally fit together. Customers fill the donation jars.

Dean arrives with a photographer, expecting an empty market. Instead, the stalls glow with lanterns and people sing along to Cora's radio. A child hands Dean a peach. He takes a bite and admits it is good.

LILA: You can have one. You can't buy the whole tree.

SCENE 5. INT. COMMUNITY HALL, DAY

The market has raised enough for a down payment, but the bank requires a local ownership group. Lila and Rafa present a plan. Rafa leaves his notes at home. Lila freezes when the projector jams. She starts the presentation from memory, while Rafa tells the story of each stall.

The bank approves a small loan. Dean, who did not expect to care about the market, offers to sell the building below market value if they keep it public. He says the peach changed his mind. Marnie says the peach was overripe. Nobody believes that was the only reason.

The papers are signed. The market stays open.

SCENE 6. EXT. NIGHT MARKET, ONE MONTH LATER

Lila and Rafa share a stall. The sign reads PEACHES AND WHATEVER RAFA FOUND. He has brought plums for Marnie, who pays the old Friday price. Lila tries a peach without measuring it first.

RAFA: How is it?

LILA: A little soft.

RAFA: Like romance?

LILA: Like a peach.

He accepts the correction and offers her the better half. She kisses him, quick and unplanned. Auntie Cora announces it over the radio. The whole market cheers, embarrassing them both.

They turn the radio off, then turn it back on for the music. Lila and Rafa dance between the crates, bump into the table, and catch every peach before it falls.

Marnie keeps the plum, and charges them both for the dance lesson.

Fade out on the market lights, still crooked, still on.
