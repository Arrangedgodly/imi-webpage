# The Creature From Drain Seven

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: classic monster feature escalation, practical effects, and hysterical townspeople*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS*

Logline: A town prepares to battle a terrifying creature emerging from Drain Seven, only to learn its roar comes from a blocked pipe and its enormous shadow belongs to a very small crab.

Cast: Mayor Grindle, a nervous baboon; Pippa, a macaque plumber; Professor Wicket, a hedgehog who studies pond life; Lou, a crane operator; the Creature, a crab with a bent shell; and a chorus of alarmed pigeons.

SCENE 1. EXT. RIVERFRONT, NIGHT

The water ripples. A tremendous groan rolls across the town. Windows shake. Pigeons lift off together and knock over a sign reading QUIET WATERS FESTIVAL.

The mayor appears in pajamas under a ceremonial sash.

MAYOR: A monster has risen from the river. We must form a committee.

Pippa kneels by Drain Seven and hears the same groan. She puts an ear to the grate. It gurgles, then coughs up one rubber duck.

PIPPA: The drain is blocked.

The mayor orders a barricade. Lou brings a crane and hangs a sheet over it to make a giant silhouette. The sheet immediately catches on the hook and resembles a lumpy whale.

SCENE 2. INT. TOWN HALL, MORNING

Professor Wicket presents a charcoal drawing of a creature with six claws and glowing eyes. He admits he drew it from a splash he saw at night. Pippa asks how big the splash was.

WICKET: About this big.

He holds his hands two inches apart. The mayor enlarges the drawing to mural size. The pigeons peck at its painted eyes and are chased from the room.

SCENE 3. EXT. RIVERFRONT, AFTERNOON

A crowd gathers behind the barricade. The mayor gives a speech. Drain Seven groans. The sheet-covered crane sways. The crowd gasps. Lou pulls a rope; the sheet rises, revealing the crane hook wearing a spare boot.

The water burbles. A crab crawls out, pulling a length of plastic ribbon. It clicks its claws. The crowd screams and backs into the barricade, which rolls downhill on its wheels. Everyone follows it toward the river.

Pippa catches the last wheel with her boot. The mayor leaps behind Professor Wicket, who is already hiding in a folding chair.

SCENE 4. INT. DRAIN SEVEN, LATER

Pippa opens the grate. A festival banner, two tennis balls, and a toy sailboat block the pipe. The crab clicks from a dry ledge. Professor Wicket identifies it as a local fiddler crab.

WICKET: It is territorial, but it is not planning an invasion.

The mayor asks whether it could be persuaded to relocate. The crab raises one claw and points to its burrow beneath the river steps. Pippa clears the pipe. Water rushes through. The crab rides the current for a few feet and climbs out at its own burrow.

SCENE 5. EXT. TOWN SQUARE, SUNSET

The mayor announces that the monster threat has been neutralized by a special task force. Pippa holds up the banner and tennis balls. The crowd applauds, though several pigeons boo. Professor Wicket corrects his drawing and adds a scale bar. The creature now appears the size of a thumb.

Lou parks the crane. The boot falls from the hook and lands on the mayor's head. The crowd screams once more. The mayor says it is an encore.

The mayor orders the pigeons to issue an official warning. They fly to every rooftop and return with scraps of bread. The mayor calls it reconnaissance. Pippa explains they are hungry. He says hunger is a form of intelligence.

The town prints posters showing a many-eyed beast. Professor Wicket measures the drawing and notes that it is larger than the town hall. The mayor says the poster needs room for sponsors. A bakery buys space beneath the monster's claws and advertises six-inch rolls. Pippa crosses out the slogan and writes CLEAR THE DRAIN.

At the town meeting, residents submit theories. One claims the monster hates brass bands. Another says it is attracted to umbrellas. They test both ideas. A band plays by the river; the crab hides. Umbrellas open; the crab keeps walking. The mayor calls the tests inconclusive and orders a second round, this time with the band wearing umbrellas.

At night the mayor returns to the river to rehearse his speech for the news. The crab has climbed onto his shoe. He delivers the speech without looking down. When the camera crew arrives, the crab leaves. The mayor asks them to film from the other side so the shoe appears larger.

SCENE 6. INT. MAYOR'S OFFICE, NIGHT

MAYOR: The town is safe.

PIPPA: The drain is clear.

WICKET: The crab is home.

LOU: The boot is mine.

MAYOR: The boot was evidence.

LOU: It was my boot.

PIPPA: Evidence of a boot.

WICKET: Not a monster.

MAYOR: The crowd screamed.

PIPPA: The barricade rolled.

LOU: It had wheels.

MAYOR: We need a sequel.

PIPPA: We need pipe screens.

WICKET: And a scale bar.

LOU: And my boot.

MAYOR: The sign attracts visitors.

PIPPA: The sign blocks the grate.

WICKET: It is badly placed.

MAYOR: Move it slightly.

PIPPA: Away from the drain.

LOU: Away from my crane.

MAYOR: Who will move it?

PIPPA: You will.

MAYOR: I am the mayor.

WICKET: You have hands.

MAYOR: I have a sash.

LOU: Use both.

The mayor lifts the sign. The crab taps the post. He puts it down farther from the grate.

MAYOR: There.

PIPPA: Better.

WICKET: Less monstrous.

LOU: My boot?

MAYOR: Take it.

LOU: Finally.

PIPPA: Move the sign.
MAYOR: Away from water.
LOU: Away from my boot.

FINAL IMAGE: The crab sits in its burrow beneath a new sign reading DRAIN SEVEN MONSTER EXPERIENCE. Pippa turns the sign around. The back reads PLEASE DON'T BLOCK THE PIPE. The crab taps it twice with a claw.
