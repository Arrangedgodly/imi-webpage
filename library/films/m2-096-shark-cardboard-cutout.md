# The Shark Is a Cardboard Cutout

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: aquatic creature thrillers, unseen monsters, and suspense built from reacting characters rather than evidence*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-004*

Logline: An aquarium crew thinks a shark has escaped after a fin appears in the touch pool, but the silhouette belongs to a cardboard exhibit sign.

Cast: Dori, a lemur aquarist; Venn, a macaque security guard; Bapp, a gorilla filmmaker; and Pip, a capuchin volunteer.

SCENE 1. INT. AQUARIUM TOUCH POOL, MORNING

A dark fin moves across the touch pool. Venn sounds an alarm. Dori asks which tank is open. Bapp turns the lights down for dramatic effect. Pip shines a flashlight and sees the fin is attached to a cardboard shark sign reflected in the water.

VENN: It could still be a real shark.

DORI: The pool holds starfish.

Bapp asks Dori to repeat the line more urgently. She asks him to turn the lights back on so she can count the animals. Pip finds all six starfish. Venn asks Dori to record the animal count before declaring an emergency. Pip finds all six starfish. One has moved beneath a rock, leaving a small wet trail. Venn treats the trail as evidence. Dori explains that the pool's circulating water carries it around the stones. Bapp asks for the soundtrack to rise. The pump turns on with an ordinary hum, and the reflection of the cardboard fin ripples across the wall. Pip picks up the sign and tilts it; the shadow slides with it. A visitor arrives early and points at the same shadow from the doorway. Venn asks him to stay back from the pool. Dori turns the sign toward the visitor and shows how the reflection follows it. The visitor laughs and asks if the aquarium has a mirror exhibit. Bapp asks him to repeat the question for the camera, but the visitor has already gone to see the real sharks.

SCENE 2. INT. SECURITY OFFICE, LATER

Venn rewinds the camera footage. The fin appears at the same time a cleaning cart passes. The sign is on the cart, and its cardboard edge wobbles in the reflection. Bapp asks for a close-up of the monitor. The camera feed has a smudge over the image.

Dori checks the actual shark tank. The lid is closed, the water level is normal, and all three sharks are visible. Venn says they should still evacuate the building. Dori asks if he has seen any water outside the tank. He has not. Dori checks the spill sensors, then walks the shark-tank perimeter with Pip. Every hatch is latched. Bapp asks Venn to describe his fear for the camera. Venn says he was startled by the reflection and saw no animal. Dori asks him to file that exact report.

Pip finds the sign in the storage hall. It is missing one cardboard tooth. He shows it to the staff. Bapp asks if the tooth can be the clue to a sequel. Venn says the sign belongs in the new exhibit and should be repaired before opening.

SCENE 3. INT. EXHIBIT WORKROOM, AFTERNOON

The crew repairs the sign with a new support. Bapp proposes a slow pan across the darkened pool, then a sudden splash. Dori says they should test the sign in normal lighting and check the reflection angle. Pip places it at the approved stand. Venn walks past the pool and sees a fin-shaped shadow again.

He calls an emergency. Dori checks the light. It is the sign, now facing the window. The afternoon sun creates a crisp reflection. She rotates the display and installs a matte backing. The shadow disappears. Bapp asks whether the repair can be filmed in reverse so it looks like the shark vanished. Dori says the exhibit should teach visitors to distinguish reflections from animals. Pip holds the sign steady while Venn checks the wall from three viewing angles. The fin appears from none of them. They add the lighting check to the opening routine. Venn asks whether the shark has been defeated. Dori says the glare has been reduced.

SCENE 4. INT. AQUARIUM, PUBLIC OPENING

Visitors arrive. Bapp films the touch-pool presentation. Pip explains that visitors may gently touch the starfish with wet hands. A child asks where the shark went. Dori takes the group to the shark tank and counts three sharks. Venn stands in front of the cardboard sign, blocking the light so it no longer casts a fin on the water.

Bapp asks for one last suspenseful shot. Pip holds up the repaired sign, showing the flat backing and foam support. Dori demonstrates the reflection by moving it past the pool. The shadow is gone. Venn signs the incident report: "Cardboard sign misaligned; no animal escaped." Bapp asks if he can title the footage "Fin at Dawn." Dori suggests "Display Check."

FINAL IMAGE: The sign sits in the exhibit with its support clearly visible. A child points to it, then points to the real sharks swimming calmly behind glass.
