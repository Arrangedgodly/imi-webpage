# Snow Day at the Post Office

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: holiday movie conventions, seasonal misunderstandings, and last-minute community reconciliation*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A snowstorm traps neighbors inside a post office on parade day, where a mislabeled package and a relentlessly musical stamp machine turn the town's seasonal goodwill into a delivery problem.

Cast: Juno, a macaque postal clerk; Perry, a capuchin driver; Aunt Lark, a baboon baker; Finch, a lemur child; and Mr. Moss, a tortoise who hates holiday music.

SCENE 1. INT. POST OFFICE, MORNING

Snow piles against the doors. Juno sorts packages. One box has a red mitten drawn where the address should be. Perry shakes snow off his coat and rings the bell on the stamp machine by accident. It prints a strip of jingle-bell stamps.

JUNO: Parade starts at noon.

PERRY: The roads are closed.

JUNO: Then the parade starts here.

Moss groans. The stamp machine plays the first four notes of a holiday tune.

MOSS: If that thing plays the chorus, I am transferring my mail.

FINCH: You can't transfer mail by humming.

MOSS: Watch me.

He tries to leave, but the door is snowed shut. Aunt Lark enters carrying a pie and asks if anyone has seen her package. She sent mittens to the shelter, but the box has no label.

SCENE 2. INT. SORTING ROOM, LATER

Juno checks the delivery log. The package is addressed to "Whoever needs it." Perry says that is not a route. Finch recognizes the red mitten drawing from Aunt Lark's knitting basket. Lark confirms she packed mittens, a hand warmer, and a note for the shelter.

LARK: I thought I wrote the address.

JUNO: Snow washed it off.

PERRY: We can wait for the roads to clear.

JUNO: The shelter is low on warm clothes.

Moss volunteers to hold the package while they clear the door. The stamp machine's paper strip catches on his shell and wraps around him. He tries to turn; the strip winds tighter.

MOSS: I am now the parade.

Finch cuts the paper free. The machine prints another strip and attaches it to the parcel. Juno pulls the plug. The tune stops mid-note.

SCENE 3. INT. POST OFFICE LOBBY, AFTERNOON

Perry wheels out the mail cart. Its small wheels sink into the snow piled at the threshold. Lark suggests tying it to a sled. Finch offers a cafeteria tray. Moss says he will not be used as a sled. Juno lays the parcel inside a waterproof mail sack and puts it on the tray.

PERRY: We can pull it to the shelter.

MOSS: Across the street?

PERRY: The street is under snow.

MOSS: Then we should wait until it becomes a street again.

Juno says the shelter needs the mittens before evening. She and Perry take the rope, while Finch guides the tray. Lark carries the pie. Moss follows with a shovel, insisting he is supervising.

SCENE 4. EXT. POST OFFICE TO SHELTER, LATER

They pull the tray through the snow. It slides faster than expected and heads toward a snowbank. Juno steers with the rope. Perry plants his boots. Finch rides the tray for three feet before jumping off into a drift. Moss shovels a path, grumbling that the parade would have been easier if it had started inside.

The tray stops beside the shelter door. Lark knocks. A resident opens the door and sees the parcel, the pie, and Moss wrapped in leftover bell stamps. The residents take the warmers and mittens. Finch helps peel the stamps off Moss's shell. Moss allows one small bell sticker to remain, claiming it improves visibility.

SCENE 5. INT. POST OFFICE, EVENING

Before they leave, Finch asks Moss to inspect the route label. He reads every line twice, then admits it is perfectly clear. Juno thanks him and hands him the next parcel to sort. It is addressed to him, care of nowhere in particular. He sets it neatly in the outgoing tray.

The neighbors return to sort the delayed mail. Juno writes the shelter's full address on a new label and shows Perry how to keep it dry. The stamp machine stays unplugged. Moss says the silence is the town's finest holiday tradition. The neighbors begin humming together. He tries not to join, then adds the bass line.

MOSS: I'm checking the key.

LARK: Of course.

Perry offers him a mitten. Moss puts it over one hand and says it is useful, not sentimental. The stamp machine gives one small chime as Juno plugs it in. Moss reaches to turn it off, but the machine prints a bell-shaped stamp onto the corner of his mitten.

Lark offers him a slice of pie. Moss takes it without commenting on the music.

FINAL IMAGE: Moss examines the tiny stamp, sighs, and wears the mitten anyway. The entire group starts the parade song softly while he pretends to check the postal scale.
