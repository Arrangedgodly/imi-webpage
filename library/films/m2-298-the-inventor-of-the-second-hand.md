# The Inventor of the Second Hand

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: inventor biopics, genius-alone montages, dramatic patent disputes, and discoveries credited to one person instead of a working team*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A macaque clockmaker fights to prove she invented a hand that measures seconds, while the workshop's team of primates remembers the countless small jobs that made the clock work.

Cast
- LELA, a macaque clockmaker with quick fingers and an impatient tail.
- OMI, an orangutan lens grinder who can polish tiny gears.
- PIP, a capuchin apprentice who keeps parts sorted by sound.
- NARA, a gibbon shop manager who keeps the records.
- ARCHIVIST VEX, a ring-tailed lemur who documents the town's inventions.

## Scene 1: The Discovery

INT. CLOCK WORKSHOP - NIGHT

Lela tests a clock with two hands. She attaches a thin third hand and watches it move once around the dial.

LELA: It counts the smaller interval.

PIP: It counts one second.

LELA: I made it.

OMI: I ground the gear.

PIP: I sorted the teeth.

NARA: I recorded the measurements.

LELA: I designed the hand.

A newspaper reporter arrives and asks for the inventor's name. Lela replies with her own. The article appears the next morning with a portrait of her alone, holding the clock.

## Scene 2: The Patent

At the town registry, Lela files for a patent. Archivist Vex asks for a record of the design. Lela brings the final drawing but not the work notes. Nara's ledger lists the gear size, the polishing schedule, the test runs, and the first failed hand.

LELA: The design is mine.

VEX: The patent covers the mechanism, not the portrait.

LELA: I created the mechanism.

VEX: Did you test it alone?

Lela recalls Omi grinding the gear, Pip catching a burr, and Nara timing the first cycle. She says the idea was hers but the clock was built by the team.

## Scene 3: The Genius Montage

A biographical film crew enters the workshop. They stage Lela's life as a series of dramatic moments: a single lamp, a page of sketches, a gear spinning in slow motion. The crew asks everyone else to leave frame.

DIRECTOR: We need a breakthrough moment.

LELA: The breakthrough took several weeks.

DIRECTOR: Can we make it one night?

PIP: One night is when we found the gear had a burr.

The director records Lela turning the same screw in several shots. Omi's reflection appears in every polished surface. The camera crops him out, but his hands remain in frame.

## Scene 4: The Dispute

A rival clockmaker claims the second hand is not new; other clocks have used similar parts. The registry schedules a hearing. Lela arrives with the final model, while Nara brings the ledger and Omi brings the first gear. Pip brings the defective gear, which makes a distinct scraping sound.

VEX: The history is not a race to name one person.

LELA: The patent should credit the team.

The hearing officer examines the documents. Lela demonstrates the clock. It loses time because the gear was filed too sharply. Omi polishes it again. Pip listens, then identifies the scraping sound as gone. Nara times the cycle.

The hearing officer asks whether the second hand can be repaired by someone other than Lela. She asks Omi to replace the gear and Pip to sort the spare parts without the ledger. Both succeed. Nara's record lets the team repeat the process, while Lela explains the motion that makes the hand advance once per second.

VEX: The design is new. The work is shared.

LELA: That is the description I want attached to it.

The rival clockmaker admits that his own workshop used a similar mechanism, but with a different gear ratio. The patent officer records both designs rather than making the dispute into a duel.

PIP: Can the museum show the failed part too?

LELA: Especially the failed part. It tells us what we changed.

## Scene 5: The Record

The patent is approved for the mechanism, with all four contributors listed. The newspaper prints a correction. The documentary crew asks to recreate the breakthrough. Lela refuses the single-lamp scene and instead invites them to film the workshop at work.

LELA: The clock has one hand that moves each second. The work had more hands than that.

PIP: Mine sorted the gears.

OMI: Mine held the lens.

NARA: Mine kept the notes.

LELA: Mine drew the first line.

## Scene 6: The Exhibition

The clock is placed in the town museum beside the sketches, the flawed gear, and the ledger. Visitors can hear a recording of the first test: a scrape, a pause, then steady ticks. The crew gives a public demonstration on how to repair it.

## Final Image

The second hand circles the dial. Below it, four names are engraved in equal type, and the ticking continues after the camera leaves the room.
