# The Documentary of a Small Statue

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: earnest art-world documentaries, talking-head interviews, reverent close-ups, and crews who create the conflict they claim to observe*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A film crew documents the unveiling of an almost invisible statue, but its obsessive coverage turns the artists' quiet disagreement into the town's loudest art event.

Cast
- DIRECTOR VELA, a gibbon who believes every silence contains meaning.
- CAMERA OPERATOR NIM, a macaque who keeps drifting into reflective surfaces.
- ARTIST ODO, a capuchin sculptor whose hands make tiny precise forms.
- CURATOR SAI, a ring-tailed lemur who can tell when a label is crooked.
- MAYOR RUL, an orangutan who wants the sculpture to be photographed with him.

## Scene 1: The Unveiling

EXT. TOWN GARDEN - MORNING

A velvet cloth covers a pedestal. Director Vela whispers to the camera. The camera pushes toward the cloth in slow motion.

VELA: We are about to witness a moment the town may not understand until it has passed.

ODO: It is a statue of a seed.

SAI: It is the size of an actual seed.

RUL: The town voted for public art. I promised something people could see.

ODO: They can see it if they look.

Vela nods solemnly. The camera frames a close-up of a fleck of bronze, then loses it.

NIM: I have the seed.

VELA: You have the pedestal.

The mayor pulls the cloth away. Nothing appears to happen. A visitor leans in, sees the small bronze seed, and smiles.

## Scene 2: The Artist's Intention

INT. GARDEN PAVILION - LATER

The film crew conducts separate interviews. Odo demonstrates how the seed was cast from a real seed pod. Vela asks what the work means.

ODO: It means a small thing can be the beginning of a large thing.

VELA: Is the seed beginning a tree, or beginning a conversation about beginnings?

ODO: It is beginning to oxidize.

Sai checks the display label. It lists the title as "Seed" and the material as bronze.

SAI: The label is clear.

VELA: The label may be too clear. Could ambiguity help the audience?

SAI: The audience knows what it is.

Vela asks the mayor for his interpretation. Rul wants the statue moved onto a taller pedestal so it will appear behind him in photographs.

RUL: The sculpture is small. The event needs scale.

ODO: It is small on purpose.

NIM: I can film it with a wide lens.

The lens makes the pedestal look enormous and the seed disappear.

## Scene 3: The Conflict

EXT. GARDEN - AFTERNOON

Vela stages a reconstruction of the unveiling. The mayor stands in his morning position; Odo repeats the cloth pull. The camera crew's lights warm the bronze, making it appear darker than before.

SAI: The color has changed.

NIM: The lights are changing the color.

VELA: That is a visual metaphor for public reaction.

ODO: It is a hot lamp.

A breeze turns the label away from the path. Vela films the visitors stopping to read it. The mayor asks the crew to place a second sign pointing toward the statue. Sai writes a simple arrow. Vela photographs the arrow rather than the artwork.

RUL: The film is about the statue, isn't it?

VELA: The film is about the town's response to the statue.

ODO: The town is trying to find it because the camera keeps standing in front of it.

Nim steps aside. The sculpture becomes visible again.

## Scene 4: The Screening

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - EVENING

The rough cut plays on a screen. It includes seven minutes of cloth, a close-up of the pedestal, and four interviews that do not show the seed. The audience waits.

VELA: The object is absent from the film, which makes its presence stronger.

RUL: Could you show it once?

ODO: The camera did record it.

NIM: The focus was on the label.

SAI: The audience has not seen the statue.

The mayor asks for a pickup shot. This time the crew turns off the lights, steps away, and lets the viewers approach. The seed is visible in the natural light. People lean closer, then step back to see how its shadow falls across the pedestal.

## Scene 5: A Better Frame

VELA: We can include this moment.

ODO: Include the people looking, not just the object.

NIM: I will hold the frame steady.

The crew records one uninterrupted minute. The visitors examine the seed, ask where Odo found its shape, and point out the tiny ridges. The mayor watches without entering the shot. Sai's label remains legible.

RUL: I can stand over there, behind the tree.

SAI: You will be in the reflection.

RUL: Then I will stand behind the camera.

The town hall votes to keep the sculpture in the garden on a low pedestal. The documentary's revised cut places the seed in its first minute, then shows the interviews. Vela removes the long slow-motion unveiling.

## Scene 6: The Premiere

At the community screening, the film ends on Odo's hands polishing the seed. The audience applauds. The mayor does not ask to stand beside it. He asks Odo to teach a workshop on casting small objects.

ODO: Bring something that fits in your palm.

VELA: We should film that.

NIM: After the workshop, when the students are ready.

## Final Image

In the garden, the bronze seed rests at eye level for a child. Its small shadow stretches across the stone, and the camera stays wide enough to show the person looking at it.
