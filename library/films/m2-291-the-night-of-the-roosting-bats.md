# The Night of the Roosting Bats

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: wildlife documentary films, hushed narration, dramatic zooms, and crews who interrupt the behavior they came to observe*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-010*

Logline: A macaque documentarian arrives to film a rare bat roost, but the crew's elaborate camera setup keeps disturbing the colony until they learn to watch from a distance.

Cast
- NARA, a macaque filmmaker who narrates before she knows what will happen.
- KEL, a capuchin sound recordist who can distinguish wingbeats from cable noise.
- SOVA, a ring-tailed lemur bat caretaker who climbs with her tail wrapped around a branch.
- TUM, a gibbon camera operator carrying too many lenses.
- THE COLONY, a roost of fruit bats.

## Scene 1: The Approach

EXT. OLD ORCHARD - DUSK

Nara whispers to the camera while the crew moves through the trees.

NARA: As evening falls, the bats will emerge from their roost in a single silent wave.

KEL: They make a lot of noise.

NARA: We will describe it as a quiet rush.

Sova stops them before they reach the hollow tree. A sign asks visitors to remain behind the rope. Tum has carried a long lens, a short lens, a tripod, and a folding stool.

SOVA: The colony is sensitive to light and movement. Set the camera there and wait.

TUM: The stool is on the wrong side of the rope.

SOVA: Then wait standing.

## Scene 2: The First Shot

Tum raises the camera. The tripod clicks. Several bats stir. Nara steps closer to film their reaction. The bats move deeper into the tree.

NARA: The colony senses our presence.

SOVA: We are not supposed to be this close.

KEL: I hear one wingbeat, then a lot of branches.

Tum swaps lenses. The metal cap drops and bounces away. A bat flutters out, circles once, and returns to the hollow.

NARA: A scout has emerged to assess the danger.

SOVA: It went out to forage and came back because your lens cap startled it.

Nara lowers the camera. She asks Sova where the crew can observe without affecting the roost. Sova points to a hide across the orchard.

## Scene 3: The Hide

INT. OBSERVATION HIDE - NIGHT

The crew settles inside. The camera is fixed through a narrow window. Tum's stool does not fit, so he folds it and stacks the lenses beneath him. Nara begins another narration.

NARA: From this concealed position, we will witness a natural ritual undisturbed.

The window fogs as they breathe. Kel opens a vent. A loud click echoes outside. The bats remain still.

KEL: That was the latch, not a wing.

NARA: The documentary has encountered an acoustic mystery.

Sova passes a hand-drawn chart through the slot. It shows the usual exit direction and warns that the bats may leave in small groups, not one wave.

TUM: That is less dramatic.

SOVA: It is what they do.

## Scene 4: The Colony Emerges

A bat appears at the hollow opening, pauses, then flies toward the orchard. Another follows after a long gap. Nara's voice drops to a whisper.

NARA: A single wing crosses the dark.

KEL: There are two. One is behind the branch.

Tum zooms too far; the image loses focus. He adjusts the lens. The bats disappear from the frame, then reappear at the edge. Nara stops narrating. The camera holds steady as the group flies to the fruit trees.

Sova points toward a low branch where netting has been left from a recent harvest. One bat brushes the loose edge and turns away.

SOVA: That net needs to be removed before more bats arrive.

NARA: Should we film it?

SOVA: We should take it down.

## Scene 5: The Work

The crew leaves the hide and moves to the net. Nara sets the camera on its case. Tum folds the tripod. Kel holds a lamp aimed at the ground. Sova guides them along the path, keeping the beam away from the roost. They loosen the net from the branch and fold it into a bag.

A bat circles back. Everyone freezes. Sova waits until it lands on the far tree before securing the last knot.

TUM: I did not get the shot.

NARA: We got the net down.

KEL: And the bats have kept their route.

Nara asks Sova to explain the colony's routine without turning it into a spectacle. Sova describes how the bats roost, forage, and return to the same trees, and how orchard workers protect the fruit without blocking the route.

## Scene 6: The Film

INT. COMMUNITY HALL - DAY

Nara screens a short film. It contains long quiet shots, a few bats crossing the sky, and the crew's hands removing the net. There is no sudden zoom or ominous score. The audience watches closely.

NARA: The colony did not perform for us. We had to change where we stood.

SOVA: That is the story.

## Final Image

At dusk, the bats leave the hollow one by one. The crew watches from beyond the rope. Nara keeps her camera on its tripod, and the night remains quiet except for wings.
