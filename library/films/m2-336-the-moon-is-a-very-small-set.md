# The Moon Is a Very Small Set

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: science-fiction epics, suspiciously artificial alien landscapes, cryptic transmissions, and explorers who turn every rock into a prophecy*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-012*

CAST
COMMANDER ZI, a gibbon pilot
DR. MIRA, a lemur geologist
PIP, a capuchin signal officer
DIRECTOR ODO, an orangutan
THE MOON BUG, a tiny crablike robot

SCENE 1. INT. SPACECRAFT - ORBIT

The crew approaches a grey moon. A mysterious signal repeats from the surface: click, click, pause. Commander Zi adjusts the landing camera. Director Odo announces that the crew has been chosen for a historic mission. Pip asks if the signal is in the communications band.

PIP: It repeats every twelve seconds.

ODO: The universe is trying to tell us something.

MIRA: It may be a beacon.

The ship descends. Its shadow crosses the moon, which looks suspiciously like a painted ball hanging from wires. Odo tells the crew to ignore the production rigging.

SCENE 2. EXT. MOON SURFACE - DAY

The crew steps out in bulky suits. Each movement is slow and majestic until Pip's tether snags on a rock. The signal continues. Mira collects a sample and discovers the rock is ordinary basalt. Odo asks whether it might be an ancient marker. Mira says it is a rock.

They follow the clicks to a shallow crater. A tiny moon bug is tapping its metal claws on a pebble. It has a blinking blue light and a cracked antenna. Pip kneels to examine it. The robot backs away, dragging a strip of reflective tape.

SCENE 3. INT. SPACECRAFT - LATER

The crew debates whether to send a message back to Earth. Odo wants a dramatic transmission; Pip proposes a short report with the robot's coordinates. Odo starts recording: 'From the edge of known existence...' The microphone cuts out. Mira tests it and finds the connector was not fully seated. Odo tries again, but his helmet reflects his own face over the monitor, making him stop mid-sentence.

MIRA: We can report the facts first.

ODO: I am looking for a memorable opening.

PIP: The wrench is missing. That is memorable to the robot.

The signal repeats. Pip answers with the same rhythm. The moon bug responds by flashing its blue light, then points toward the spacecraft. Commander Zi checks the landing camera and spots the loose panel. The crew heads back outside, finally carrying only one tool kit instead of four emergency cases. Odo asks Pip to record the new signal. Pip plays the clicks back at half speed; it sounds like a tiny drum solo. Mira asks him to restore normal speed before anyone interprets it as a greeting from another galaxy.

 INT. SPACECRAFT - LATER

The crew plays the signal through the translation unit. It converts the clicks into a phrase: PLEASE RETURN MY TOOL. The group falls silent. Odo asks whether the phrase could be a greeting. Mira checks the robot's claw and finds a missing bolt. Pip looks at the reflective tape; it came from the spacecraft's landing strut.

The moon bug points at the craft. Its antenna is bent from contact with the landing gear. Commander Zi asks the crew to inspect the strut. A loose panel has knocked off a small wrench during descent. The robot found it and has been trying to return the tool to its storage slot.

ODO: It contacted us across space to return a wrench?

MIRA: It is its wrench.

SCENE 4. EXT. CRATER - SUNSET

Pip repairs the antenna with a flexible clip from the suit kit. Mira retrieves the wrench. The robot tests its claw and tightens the bolt. The crew tries to leave, but the little robot blocks the path and taps the pebble twice. It points at the spacecraft's loose landing panel.

Commander Zi checks the strut. The panel is not secure. The moon bug uses the wrench to tighten it, then returns the tool to its own case. The crew applauds; the robot blinks once. Odo asks for a heroic farewell. The bug clicks three times, then scuttles back into its crater.

SCENE 5. INT. SPACECRAFT - ORBIT

The crew lifts off safely. Odo records a message for the mission archive: the moon's first contact involved a repair request and a missing wrench. Mira adds the geological description. Pip sends the moon bug's coordinates to the next landing team, along with a warning to secure loose tools.

The signal fades as the ship climbs. The crew waits for one last transmission. Instead, the speaker emits a soft click. Mira checks the channel; it is the loose buckle on Pip's suit tapping against the console.

FINAL IMAGE: The moon bug watches the spacecraft disappear behind the painted moon, then turns toward the quiet crater with its antenna repaired.
