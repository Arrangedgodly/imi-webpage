# The Cloud Over Cedar Town

*Format: Film parody*
*Parody target: weather-disaster films, heroic command-center speeches, and town-wide evacuation spectacle*
*By: IMI Pictures Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-FILMS-002*

Logline: A storm is forecast over Cedar Town, but the mayor's disaster-film instincts keep raising the alarm while the weather reader tries to get everyone to clear drains and charge radios.

Cast: Yara, a macaque weather reader; Mayor Plink, a squirrel; Jo, a gorilla hardware-store owner; Fen, a capuchin radio host; and a flock of starlings.

SCENE 1. INT. TOWN HALL, MORNING

Yara shows the forecast: heavy rain and gusty wind, with the storm likely to pass east of town. Mayor Plink asks if residents should evacuate.

YARA: They should secure awnings and clear gutters.

MAYOR: Before evacuation?

YARA: Instead of evacuation.

Fen broadcasts the warning as "the storm of the century." Yara takes the microphone and corrects him. The mayor lowers the lights to make the forecast look more urgent. The projector shows a cloud drifting away from the town map.

SCENE 2. EXT. MAIN STREET, AFTERNOON

Residents move flowerpots and tie awnings. Jo hands out tarp clips. The mayor drives by in a golf cart with a siren. The noise startles the starlings; they fly east, away from the storm.

MAYOR: The birds are fleeing!

YARA: They are flying toward the clear sky.

Fen's radio report says the storm is moving west. Yara checks the radar and finds the antenna pointed at a brick wall. Fen turns it. The signal clears; the storm is still east.

SCENE 3. INT. HARDWARE STORE, LATER

The mayor tries to buy every tarp in the store. Jo asks if he has a list of buildings that need them. The mayor has a list of camera positions. Yara takes it and turns it over, using the blank back to write a supply count. Residents check off homes as they secure their awnings.

FEN: The town is mobilizing.

YARA: The town is tying down patio furniture.

A gust blows the mayor's sash over his face. He keeps giving instructions from beneath it. Jo lifts the sash; the mayor is facing the wall map.

Jo runs out of tarp clips. Residents ask for more. He opens a box of clothespins and demonstrates one on his own sleeve. It holds. The mayor asks if they are rated for disaster. Jo says they are rated for laundry.

A volunteer clears a drain and finds the mayor's missing speech, a rubber duck, and two tennis balls. The mayor retrieves the speech and calls it a dramatic rescue. Yara asks him to thank the volunteers for clearing the drain. He does, then tries to add a heroic flourish. The group hands him another shovel.

JO: The east gutter is still blocked.

MAYOR: I will lead the operation.

YARA: You can hold the bag.

SCENE 4. INT. RADIO STATION, EVENING

Rain begins. Fen interviews the mayor. He has prepared a speech about courage and sacrifice. Yara gives the wind reading: moderate gusts. The mayor delivers the speech anyway, quietly, to a room of volunteers stacking sandbags.

MAYOR: Cedar Town will stand firm.

FEN: Against what?

MAYOR: The forecast.

Yara reports the drains are clear and the road crews are ready if a branch falls. A porch light flickers across the street. The mayor asks whether to dispatch a rescue team. Fen calls the house. The resident says the light is off because they are saving electricity.

SCENE 5. EXT. CEDAR TOWN, NIGHT

The mayor asks Fen to announce a shelter-in-place order. Yara checks the latest reading and says people can stay home, but the town shelter is open for anyone who needs it. The announcement is calm. Nobody runs. The mayor looks disappointed until Jo hands him a sandbag to carry.

The residents return extra clothespins to Jo, who stores them beside the tarp clips. The forecast was right.

The storm passes through with rain and a few gusts. One awning loosens and flaps into the street. Jo and a neighbor secure it after the wind eases. The mayor starts to pose beside the sandbags; Yara tells him to help carry them back to the store. He does. The starlings return to the town square.

Fen reports the actual conditions. The mayor asks whether he can call it an epic survival story. Yara says it was a wet Tuesday. Fen broadcasts that. The residents wave from their porches, then go inside to dry off.

The forecast was right.

FINAL IMAGE: Plink hangs his poncho over the speech. Water drips onto the word unprecedented. He folds the page and uses it to prop open the window, which sticks until Jo pushes it from outside.
