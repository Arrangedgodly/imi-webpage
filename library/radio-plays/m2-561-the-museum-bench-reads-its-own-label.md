# The Museum Bench Reads Its Own Label

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: culture broadcasts that treat public furniture as an overlooked artist*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Host Vex, an orangutan; curator Mara, a macaque; attendant Pip, a capuchin; installer Odo, a gibbon; visitor Nari, a lemur; visitor Seli, a langur; workshop carpenter Eli, a baboon (on telephone).

SFX: Museum lobby. Footsteps slow beside a wooden bench.

VEX: Today we meet the forgotten artist behind this bench.

MARA: The bench is made by the museum's carpentry shop. The label tells visitors when it was installed.

ODO: I fitted the brass label at the end. It reads, "Lobby bench, built by the museum workshop, 1984."

PIP: It has a carved groove on the left edge. That is from years of bags being set there.

NARI: I thought the groove was part of the design.

VEX: The bench has endured decades without recognition.

MARA: It has served visitors. We can discuss the craft, but let's not invent an artist.

VEX: Who carved the groove?

ODO: No one knows. The maintenance log says it was already there when the bench was refinished.

PIP: We could add a note that the groove is wear, not decoration.

MARA: Good. Odo can add a small card without changing the original brass label.

SFX: Pencil writes on card.

VEX: A mystery hidden in the wood.

NARI: It is a groove where people sit.

VEX: Would the bench like a microphone?

MARA: It is furniture. We can record a carpenter explaining how it was built.

ODO: I can call the workshop. The maker who refinished it in 2004 is still on staff.

SFX: Phone dial, one ring; speakerphone connects.

ODO: Hello, this is Odo at the museum. We have a question about the lobby bench. Was the groove present when you refinished it?

MARA: Ask whether the finish changes over time. That helps care for it.

ELI, on telephone: The groove was already there. The varnish was worn around it. We sanded the surface and sealed it, but did not carve anything.

VEX: Were you making a statement?

ELI: Yes. I left a sign saying the maple bench had wet varnish.

SFX: Telephone disconnects.

VEX: The artist was time.

MARA: No. Visitors and use left a mark. The carpenter can explain the material and the repair.

NARI: I like the bench more now. It has been useful for forty years.

PIP: The clear route around it is important. The next group needs the aisle.

VEX: We should keep the interview short so people may sit.

MARA: Exactly. Odo, put the card on the holder beside the label. Don't block the seat.

A visitor, Seli, approaches and starts to sit on the bench. Vex lifts the microphone toward the seat.

SELI: Are you interviewing me or the bench?

MARA: You can sit. The interview is over there by the label, so the aisle stays open.

VEX: Does the groove feel like history?

SELI: It feels like wood. I have been walking all morning.

ODO: The seat is stable. The brass card is clear of the edge.

PIP: I'll leave the mic stand behind the blue floor tape. It won't cross the path.

SFX: Mic stand moves; bench creaks under Seli's weight.

VEX: The bench has accepted a new guest.

MARA: The guest has sat down. Don't speak for the furniture.

SELI: Is the groove meant to support a bag?

MARA: It is just wear. Please use the seat, and keep luggage on the floor beside you.

ODO: The workshop has confirmed the bench was made of maple. I will add that to the card if the archive record supports it.

VEX: A hardwood revelation.

PIP: The visitor can rest while the group continues.

MARA: That's why the bench is in the lobby.
SFX: Card holder clicks in; bench creaks softly.

ODO: Card reads: "The groove is wear from use. The bench was built by the museum workshop and refinished in 2004."

VEX: A modest biography.

MARA: And an accurate one. Visitors may sit, as long as they leave the route clear.

TAG

NARI: The bench has a seat for every listener.

MARA: Please don't crowd it.
