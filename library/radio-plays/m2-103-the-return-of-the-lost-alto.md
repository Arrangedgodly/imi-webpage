# The Return of the Lost Alto

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio opera rehearsals, grand vocal warmups, and a diva whose high notes keep triggering stage machinery*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Sola, a gibbon soprano; Bass, a gorilla stage manager; Neri, a macaque conductor; and Pip, a capuchin understudy.*

[SFX: Orchestra tunes. A ladder creaks.]

NERI: We are live from the final rehearsal of The Moonlit Acorn, an opera about a squirrel who inherits the forest. Sola, your entrance is after the orchestra's four-bar introduction.

SOLA: My entrance is after the orchestra has earned the privilege of hearing me.

BASS: The orchestra is ready. The ladder is not. It rises to the moon balcony, then sticks.

SOLA: I will descend as the moon rises.

BASS: The moon is a paper disc. It rises when I pull this rope.

PIP: Last time the disc rose before the stage went dark.

NERI: We call that foreshadowing. Today, dark first, moon second, Sola third.

[SFX: Orchestra plays four bars.]

SOLA: [SINGING] O moon, reveal the acorn's secret-

[SFX: Rope strains; paper moon shoots up, then drops with a thud.]

BASS: The moon has revealed it is tied to a sandbag.

SOLA: I was still singing.

NERI: The orchestra did not know whether to follow the note or the moon.

PIP: I heard a spectacular fall in B-flat.

SOLA: My high note moves the moon. I asked for that in my contract.

BASS: The rope is connected to a pulley. Your note shakes the balcony beam, which releases the brake.

SOLA: Then the theater has excellent acoustics.

NERI: We'll continue. Pip, take Sola's place while Bass checks the brake.

PIP: Do I inherit the forest?

NERI: Only if you inherit the note.

PIP: I can sing lower.

SOLA: This part requires a skyward register.

PIP: I have a skyward register. It is just two steps down from yours.

[SFX: Pip sings a warm low note. The orchestra softens; a lantern glows.]

BASS: That note did not move the moon. It did turn on the understudy lamp.

NERI: Excellent. A practical effect.

SOLA: The audience will think the forest has discovered electricity.

PIP: In the libretto, the acorn is a symbol of hope.

SOLA: It is a symbol of a tiny snack. We must respect the text.

NERI: We will try the duet. Sola sings the moon; Pip sings the acorn. Bass, pull only when I raise my baton.

[SFX: Orchestra begins. Sola sings a high note. The moon stays put. Pip sings. The ladder rolls across the stage.] 

BASS: The ladder has entered the duet.

NERI: Why is the ladder moving?

BASS: It has wheels. I tied it to the moon rope so I could retrieve it.

SOLA: The ladder is now the only thing with an entrance cue.

PIP: I can sing it back.

[SFX: Pip gives a soft note. The ladder rolls back and stops beside Sola.]

BASS: That's not singing. That's the stagehand releasing the wheel brake.

PIP: We can tell the audience it is singing.

NERI: The final performance has a live audience. They expect a moon, a forest, and one acorn. We have all three, plus a roaming ladder.

SOLA: Then let the ladder be the rival heir.

BASS: A ladder cannot inherit a forest.

PIP: It can climb one.

NERI: That is the ending. The ladder ascends, the moon hangs still, and Sola chooses the acorn over the throne.

SOLA: I do not choose a snack over a throne.

PIP: You choose it as an emblem.

SOLA: I can accept an emblem if it is roasted.

[SFX: Rehearsal resumes. Orchestra swells, Sola sings a high note; the moon rises gently. The ladder rolls downstage, then stops.] 

BASS: Perfect. The brake held.

NERI: And the ladder found its mark.

SOLA: The moon is in tune, the acorn is safe, and the forest has a new heir.

PIP: Is it the ladder?

SOLA: It's the understudy. The ladder may serve as minister.

[SFX: Orchestra resolves. Applause from two stagehands and one unimpressed squirrel.]

NERI: The opera ends with the acorn planted beneath the moon. Sola bows, Pip takes the final note, and Bass ties down every rope.

[SFX: The paper moon clicks into place. A soft bell marks curtain.]
