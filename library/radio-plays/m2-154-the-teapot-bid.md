# The Teapot Bid

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio auctions, breathless lot descriptions, countdown pressure, and bidders who cannot see the item*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
VEL, a capuchin auction host
MOSS, a macaque caller
POLL, a parrot sound technician

[AUCTION THEME: BRASS FANFARE. A GAVEL TAP.]

VEL: Welcome to Bid by Ear, the only auction where you can't see what you're buying, but our description is so vivid you may think you can. Lot one: a modest ceramic teapot, blue glaze, one chip in the lid, and a handle that remembers every hand.

MOSS: Is it in working condition?

VEL: It has held water. It has poured water. It has survived a shelf. Starting bid: three bananas.

MOSS: Three.

VEL: I hear three. Do I hear four?

POLL: [TWO CLEAR WHISTLES.]

VEL: Four from an enthusiastic bidder on the line!

POLL: [WHISTLES AGAIN.]

VEL: Five! Poll, are those bids?

POLL: I'm testing the new alert whistle.

VEL: The whistle has tested itself into ownership. Six bananas to the room.

MOSS: I'll bid seven. I actually need a teapot.

VEL: Need is a powerful force in radio auctioning. Seven! Do I hear eight?

POLL: [WHISTLES.]

VEL: Eight from the technical booth. Poll is bidding remotely through sound.

POLL: I can't afford it.

VEL: The lot description includes a chip, not a debt. Nine!

[PHONE LINE CRACKLES.]

MOSS: I'll go to ten, but can you tell me the size?

VEL: It is exactly the size of a teapot.

MOSS: Does it whistle?

VEL: It is a ceramic pot, not a kettle.

MOSS: Does it come with tea?

VEL: Tea is a separate lot, currently on a different program.

POLL: [THREE WHISTLES.]

VEL: Eleven! Poll has bid eleven with a triple flourish. I admire that confidence.

POLL: Those are the station's fire alarm test tones.

[VERY BRIEF ALARM. EVERYONE PAUSES.]

VEL: We can pause bidding while our alarm is assessed. For listeners, the teapot remains available and perfectly calm.

MOSS: I'd like to withdraw my bid until I know what the teapot sounds like.

VEL: We can demonstrate. Poll, please pour a little water.

[WATER POURS. A HOLLOW DRIP.]

POLL: It has a hairline crack under the spout.

VEL: A rare feature! The water creates a delicate counter-rhythm.

MOSS: That is a leak.

VEL: It is a leak with a compelling acoustic profile.

MOSS: Eight bananas. I'll use it as a planter.

VEL: The bidder lowers his offer. This has never happened on Bid by Ear. Nine to Poll?

POLL: [ONE WHISTLE.]

VEL: Poll is out. Eight to Moss. Going once, going twice-

[A PLANT POT CLINKS.]

MOSS: Sold. I'm already putting a cutting in it.

VEL: A practical purchase, but this is still a broadcast auction. We must now provide the winner's celebratory description.

MOSS: It has a leaf, damp soil, and a crack that drains onto my saucer.

VEL: A green flourish, a rich earthy aroma, and a controlled trickle.

POLL: [WHISTLES TWICE.]

VEL: Poll is bidding on the saucer.

POLL: No, that is the kettle in the break room. It is boiling.

VEL: Then the kettle has entered our next lot. Listeners, please remain at home; we will describe it without boiling you.

VEL: The teapot is now a planter, and the crack is a drainage feature. Our bidder has won a bargain and a small fern.

POLL: Can I bid on the fern?

VEL: It is already rooted. But next, Lot Two: a whistle that may or may not be a bid.

[THE ALARM WHISTLES. GAVEL TAP.]

MOSS: I'm taking my new pot home before this station sells the table it's on.

POLL: Keep it level as you carry it.

MOSS: It is a plant pot now. If it leaks, the fern gets a drink.

VEL: A remarkably versatile lot, and a new category for our catalog.

VEL: The table is not part of Lot Two.

[AUCTION THEME SWELLS AS FOOTSTEPS EXIT.]

VEL: Yet.
