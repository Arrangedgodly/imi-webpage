# Soil on the Line

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Gardening call-in radio advice shows*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Tilly, a ring-tailed lemur host; Ro, a capuchin rooftop gardener; Moss, a macaque with an invasive mint bed; Elli, a langur fig keeper; the studio parrot, a cockatoo who repeats advice at the wrong time.

[THEME: MORNING BIRDS, A TROWEL SCRAPES A POT, OLD RADIO STATIC.]

HOST TILLY: You're listening to Soil on the Line, where callers describe a plant and I ask what the roots are doing. I'm Tilly, a ring-tailed lemur with dirt in my cuffs. Call one, tell me what is growing.

CALLER RO: My rooftop squash keeps climbing into the weather station. I tied it down, and now the vine is pulling the rain gauge sideways.

TILLY: Does it have a trellis?

RO: It has a satellite dish.

TILLY: That's for weather, not squash. Put a sturdy frame beside the planter and give the vine room to spread. The roots need a deeper box, too. Rooftop beds dry out before the soil below them knows it rained.

RO: I can build a frame. My neighbor says squash needs a firm hand.

TILLY: Squash needs water and sunlight. Your neighbor needs a hobby.

[A BELL. PHONE LINE CRACKLES.] 

TILLY: Next caller, you're on.

CALLER MOSS: My mint has taken over the herb bed. It is in the chives, the thyme, and a very small part of my boot.

TILLY: Mint travels underground. A pot with a bottom will slow it down. Set the pot in the bed with its rim above the soil. Keep the boot. It has become a planter.

MOSS: I was hoping to save the boot.

TILLY: Then move the mint before it learns to lace itself.

CALLER MOSS: I can do that. Also, my neighbor keeps pulling the flowers off it.

TILLY: Mint flowers feed small pollinators. Leave a few. But tell your neighbor to ask before pruning. Gardening is easier when people speak before they grab.

[SFX: A PARROT SQUAWKS "SPEAK BEFORE YOU GRAB!"] 

TILLY: The studio parrot has chosen a slogan. Caller three?

CALLER ELLI: I have a fig tree. The leaves look fine, but the fruit drops while it is still hard. It gets morning sun and afternoon shade. I have been giving it extra water every day.

TILLY: How much water?

ELLI: Until it looks pleased.

TILLY: Trees don't show gratitude by holding fruit. Check the soil a finger's depth down. If it is wet, let it dry before watering again. A fig in a small pot may also need more room for its roots. How long has it been in the same pot?

ELLI: Since before my grandson was born.

TILLY: Then let's see the root ball. Don't yank the trunk. Tip the pot and let the tree slide into your hands.

ELLI: I have long hands. I can do that.

TILLY: Lucky you. If the roots circle tightly, move it to a pot only a little wider. A huge pot stays wet too long.

CALLER ELLI: And the dropped fruit?

TILLY: Pick one up. If it is dry inside, the tree may be stressed. If it is full of tiny holes, call back and describe them. Don't blame the sun until you check the soil.

[SFX: RADIO DIAL TURNS. SOMEONE OPENS A WINDOW.] 

TILLY: We have a note from the station garden. The beans are leaning over the walking path, and the walking path committee wants them removed.

CALLER RO: Could we move the path?

TILLY: Could we tie the beans up and ask the committee to wait a week? The beans were here first.

MOSS: What if the beans need better support?

TILLY: Then we'll build it. I've got bamboo canes under the table. Moss, you've got twine. Ro, you know how to frame a planter. Meet us after the show.

RO: I'll bring the squash frame.

TILLY: Bring the part without the weather station.

[BIRDS CHIRP. TROWEL DINKS AGAINST A POT.] 

TILLY: That's the hour. Check your soil before you blame the sky, give a climbing plant something made for climbing, and leave room for the neighbor to get by. The beans stay. The path gets a little narrower. We'll see you next Saturday.

PRODUCER: Tilly, the sponsor wants you to say their Miracle Soil fixes everything.

TILLY: Does it fix the path?

PRODUCER: No.

TILLY: Does it fix the beans?

PRODUCER: It is soil.

TILLY: Then tell listeners the truth. It holds roots. The Miracle part costs extra.

PRODUCER: The sponsor has asked us not to say "costs extra."

TILLY: Tell them to bring their own wheelbarrow, then.

[THE PARROT SQUAWKS "ASK BEFORE YOU GRAB!" THE THEME FADES.]
