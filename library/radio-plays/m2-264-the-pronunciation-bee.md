# The Pronunciation Bee

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio pronunciation contests, dictionary authority, and hosts who turn regional variations into tie-breaking rounds*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-009*

CAST
HOST BALA, a macaque
JUDGE OMI, a capuchin
CONTESTANT RUK, a gibbon
CONTESTANT EDA, a ring-tailed lemur

[SFX: BELL. AUDIENCE CLAPS.]

BALA: Welcome to The Pronunciation Bee, where callers compete to say words clearly. Our first word is "branch."

RUK: Branch.

EDA: Branch.

OMI: Both contestants have said it correctly.

BALA: We need a winner by the end of the hour.

OMI: Then give them another word.

BALA: "Either."

RUK: Either.

EDA: Either.

OMI: Again, both are acceptable.

BALA: Judge, can you select the more precise one?

OMI: They are the same word, pronounced two ways.

BALA: We will consult the dictionary.

[SFX: HEAVY BOOK OPENS. PAGES RUSTLE.]

OMI: The dictionary says both pronunciations are in use.

BALA: It has avoided the contest.

RUK: I can spell it.

BALA: This is a pronunciation contest.

EDA: I can say it while chewing a seed.

OMI: Please do not add a handicap to a word.

BALA: Our next word: "route."

RUK: Route.

EDA: Route.

OMI: Both again.

BALA: Can we have a listener challenge? Caller, tell us your pronunciation.

[SFX: PHONE CONNECTS.]

CALLER: I say "route." Is that correct?

OMI: Yes.

CALLER: I also say "route." Is that correct?

OMI: Also yes.

BALA: This program is producing no eliminations.

RUK: Perhaps clarity matters more than uniformity.

BALA: That sounds like a slogan, not a score.

EDA: Give us a word with a silent letter.

BALA: "Knot."

RUK: Knot.

EDA: Knot.

OMI: The K is silent in both.

BALA: I will ring the bell for the silent K.

[SFX: BELL.]

RUK: The bell is not silent.

BALA: It is the contest bell. It has different rules.

BALA: We have an extra pronunciation challenge. Contestants, say the word "read" in a sentence.

RUK: I read the first chapter before breakfast.

EDA: I will read the last chapter after the show.

OMI: Both are clear. One is past tense, the other is future tense.

BALA: Can the audience distinguish them by sound?

OMI: Not without the sentences.

BALA: Then the sentences are part of the contest.

RUK: Could I say the sentence again, with more context?

BALA: You may use one sentence, not an entire book.

EDA: I can bring my dictionary.

OMI: That is the book we have already consulted.

BALA: Perhaps we need a larger prize for the next season.

RUK: Perhaps we need a word the contestants pronounce differently.

OMI: That can happen, but it will not make one of them wrong.

OMI: The final round will test each contestant on a sentence. Read this clearly: "The ship's route crossed the branch of the river."

RUK: The ship's route crossed the branch of the river.

EDA: The ship's route crossed the branch of the river.

BALA: I heard a difference.

OMI: Where?

BALA: One of them was faster.

OMI: Speed was not part of the rule.

BALA: It is now. Contestants, read the sentence in half the time.

RUK: The ship's route crossed the branch of the river.

EDA: The ship's route crossed the branch of the river.

OMI: They were both equally fast.

BALA: The audience is clapping for both. We have a tie.

RUK: Can we share the trophy?

EDA: I can hold one handle, and Ruk can hold the other.

[SFX: TROPHY LIFTS.]

OMI: It is a participation bell.

BALA: Then ring it together.

[SFX: BELL RINGS.]

RUK: It has a clear sound.

EDA: It does not have a pronunciation.

BALA: Our winners have resolved the contest by lifting the prize. The dictionary remains open, and listeners may pronounce either answer.

OMI: As long as you can be understood.

BALA: This has been The Pronunciation Bee. Next week, the spelling contest will accept every letter.

[SFX: THEME OUT.]
