# The Choir's Missing Note Is in the Kettle

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live classical radio, reverent music commentary, and hosts who identify every backstage accident as an avant-garde performance*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A chamber choir loses its opening pitch moments before broadcast, while the announcer mistakes a whistling kettle for a daring new arrangement.

Cast: Announcer Sera, a baboon; conductor Lumo, a macaque; singer Tavi, a gibbon; accompanist Neli, a capuchin; stagehand Omi, an orangutan.

[SFX: QUIET STUDIO HUM. A KETTLE WHISTLES IN A RISING NOTE.]

SERA: That is the opening note of the choir's new work, "Clouds at Dawn."

LUMO: It is the kettle.

SERA: The program lists a breathless overture.

LUMO: The program lists an overture after I conduct it.

OMI: The kettle is ready. The singers are not.

ACT ONE

The station's live concert is about to begin. Neli tests the piano; the middle C key makes no sound. Tavi hums the pitch, but a ventilation fan shifts it. Lumo taps his tuning fork and hears the note tremble against the kettle's whistle.

LUMO: We need the piano checked and the water turned off.

SERA: The broadcast clock says we have one minute. Could the kettle join the piece?

TAVI: It has not rehearsed.

SERA: Neither has the clock.

Neli opens the piano lid. The key is not stuck; a felt strip slipped beneath the hammer. Omi asks whether she should pull it out. Lumo says no one should reach into the piano while the lid is open. Neli closes it, then calls the tuner on the backstage phone.

NELI: We can use the tuning fork for the warm-up, but not for the accompaniment.

SERA: We could announce a vocal opening.

LUMO: That is what we rehearsed.

ACT TWO

Sera begins the program with an essay about the piece's mist imagery. The kettle whistles again. She pauses, then speaks of the steam as if it were answering the singers. Tavi is trying to match the pitch, but the kettle rises a semitone and his note follows it.

TAVI: I am not chasing it anymore.

SERA: The soloist has chosen silence.

TAVI: The soloist has chosen the score.

Omi removes the kettle from the hot plate and places it on a cool tray. The whistle fades. Lumo takes the tuning fork, strikes it once, and gives Tavi the starting pitch. The choir hums softly. Sera leans toward the microphone.

SERA: The ensemble has found its note.

LUMO: The ensemble had it. The kettle was louder.

A red light signals the piano tuner. Neli opens the lid only after Omi disconnects the power. The tuner removes the felt strip and tests the middle C. It sounds normally. Neli plays the opening chord. The choir joins, but the station's audio meter dips; a loose microphone cable is brushing against the music stand.

OMI: Stop there. We have a second sound problem.

SERA: The piece is developing backstage.

LUMO: The piece is waiting on a cable.

Tavi holds the stand still while Omi clips the cable to its side. It no longer scrapes. The choir retakes the opening chord at a lower volume. The meter stays steady.

ACT TWO-B

The concert can begin. Sera asks Lumo to describe the work. He says it has three short movements, each built around a different breath pattern. Sera asks whether the first is the kettle movement. Lumo says the first movement is the dawn movement, written before the station bought its kettle.

TAVI: The kettle has no program credit.

NELI: It does have a backstage chair.

Omi unplugs the kettle and carries it from the room. The door squeaks when she opens it. Sera begins to call it a new instrumental color, but Omi returns to oil the hinge before anyone can announce it.

[SFX: HINGE SQUEAK, THEN DOOR CLOSES SOFTLY.]

SERA: We have also resolved the hinge solo.

LUMO: Thank you.

The live clock reaches zero. Lumo raises his hand. The choir sings the first note together; it is clean and stable. The piano enters. Neli plays the chord that was silent earlier. Tavi's solo starts on the printed pitch rather than following any appliance in the building.

ACT THREE

The choir performs the first movement without interruption. At the end, the audience remains quiet for a beat. Sera does not rush to fill it. Lumo lowers his hand and invites the ensemble into the second movement. The piano, choir, and room microphone are balanced. Omi listens from the control booth with one earphone off, ready to catch any cable sound.

The final chord resolves. Sera gives the title and names the conductor, singers and accompanist. She explains that the opening delay came from a slipped felt strip and a hot kettle, not an intentional musical effect. Lumo thanks the tuner, then asks Omi to keep the kettle outside the studio during future rehearsals.

OMI: It can have its own room.

TAVI: It can whistle from a safe distance.

SERA: We will not list it as a guest performer.

SERA: A final note from the hallway.

LUMO: That is the latch.
