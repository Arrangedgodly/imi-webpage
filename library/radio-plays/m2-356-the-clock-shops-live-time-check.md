# The Clock Shop's Live Time Check

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio news segments that insist every local clock match a single studio clock*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-012*

Cast: Reporter Osa, a gibbon; clockmaker Neri, a macaque; host Vint, a capuchin; caller Balu, a lemur.

[SFX: CLOCK SHOP. MANY TICKS AT DIFFERENT SPEEDS. RADIO MIC OPENS.]

VINT: We are live from Neri's clock shop, where every clock is supposed to agree with our studio time.

NERI: They don't. One is for the ferry, one is for the hill observatory, and one is stopped so I can repair it.

OSA: Our producer says the six clocks behind you show six different times.

NERI: They show the times their owners need.

VINT: The listeners want to know which one is correct.

NERI: Correct for what? The ferry clock is four minutes fast so passengers arrive early. The observatory clock follows the telescope schedule. The stopped one is a repair.

[SFX: A SMALL BELL TICKS AND CHIMES.]

OSA: The studio clock just struck twelve.

NERI: That is the shop bell. It rings when the pendulum passes the mark.

VINT: Can you set them all to the studio clock for the broadcast?

NERI: I can set them to the studio clock. Then two customers will miss their appointments.

[SFX: DOOR OPENS. A BELL JINGLES.]

BALU: I'm here for the ferry clock. The boat leaves in twelve minutes.

OSA: We can ask him to synchronize it live.

NERI: He cannot leave with it while we reset it. Give me the case.

[SFX: CLOCK CASE OPENS. A KEY TURNS.]

VINT: The clock says eleven fifty-seven. The studio says twelve.

NERI: Its hands were set to the ferry schedule. The ferry clock is intentionally four minutes fast.

BALU: The boat leaves at twelve-oh-one. If the shop says twelve, I have one minute.

NERI: The shop clock says eleven fifty-seven. The studio clock says twelve. Your ferry clock says twelve-oh-one. You have four minutes by your own dial.

OSA: That's confusing to report.

VINT: We could call it a four-minute head start.

NERI: That's what the ferry office calls it. It is written on the face.

[SFX: PAPER SCRAPES AS NERI OPENS THE CLOCK'S BACK.]

BALU: There is a note inside: "four minutes fast, ferry schedule."

OSA: We have a printed label.

VINT: Could we still set it to noon, then tell him to add four?

NERI: No. I will wind it, return it unchanged, and explain the label.

[SFX: KEY TURNS. TICKING CONTINUES.]

NERI: The mechanism is clean. It keeps the offset accurately. The problem is our segment treated different purposes as a contest.

VINT: The studio clock is our reference for broadcasts.

OSA: The ferry clock is a reference for boarding.

BALU: And the observatory clock is for the telescope. My cousin works there.

[SFX: A LARGE CLOCK STRIKES TWELVE; THE SMALLER FERRY CLOCK CHIMES FOUR MINUTES LATER ON A TEST TONE.]

VINT: That was four minutes later.

NERI: That was its offset sounding through the test chime. Not a malfunction.



VINT: The sponsor wants all six clock faces to match the studio clock for the time check.

NERI: They serve different schedules. If I change the ferry clock, its labeled offset is gone.

OSA: Then we should report the six times honestly instead of staging one matching picture.

OSA: We should tell the listeners to follow the clock that names their appointment.

VINT: Fine. A live time check: the studio clock is noon, the ferry clock is set four minutes ahead, and the repair clock is stopped.

BALU: I have time to catch the boat.

NERI: Take the clock by its case. Don't turn the minute hand.

[SFX: CLOCK CASE CLOSES. BALU LIFTS IT. DOOR BELL JINGLES.]

OSA: We have learned that time can serve different schedules.

VINT: Don't turn that into a philosophy. Just read the label.

OSA: The label says ferry clock, four minutes fast.



[SFX: RADIO MIC CLOSES. DIFFERENT CLOCKS KEEP TICKING.]
