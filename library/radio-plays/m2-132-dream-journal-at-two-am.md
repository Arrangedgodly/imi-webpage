# Dream Journal at 2 A.M.

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night dream-analysis radio shows, symbolic interpretations, and an insomniac caller whose dream is mostly a checklist*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Edda, an orangutan night host; Ciro, a macaque dream analyst; Peli, a capuchin caller; and Jo, a gibbon producer.*

[SFX: Soft piano, a bedside clock ticks.]

EDDA: Welcome to Between the Branches, where listeners share dreams and our guest helps them explore possible meanings. Peli, what did you dream?

PELI: I was carrying a basket through a forest, looking for a blue cup.

CIRO: The basket may represent responsibility. The forest may represent the unknown.

PELI: The cup was in my kitchen.

CIRO: The kitchen may represent the familiar.

PELI: I had to bring the basket home before the rain.

CIRO: The rain may represent a deadline.

EDDA: That seems clear.

CIRO: Dreams rarely are.

JO: The producer's note says the caller had a real basket and cup.

PELI: I was supposed to bring the laundry basket inside. It started raining.

CIRO: The dream may be working through an unfinished task.

EDDA: Did you finish it?

PELI: I brought the basket inside, but I forgot the blue cup outdoors.

CIRO: There is still unfinished business.

PELI: I can get the cup in the morning.

CIRO: The dream indicates an unresolved symbol.

EDDA: We have another caller. Sella dreams of climbing a tree that turns into a staircase.

SELLA: I climb three branches, then the tree has a handrail.

CIRO: The staircase could mean ambition.

SELLA: I was trying to reach the fruit bowl on the landing.

CIRO: The fruit bowl may symbolize reward.

JO: The bowl is on the landing at her house.

SELLA: I put it there so my little ones can't reach it.

EDDA: Does the dream end when you reach the bowl?

SELLA: No. I reach the landing and find the bowl empty.

CIRO: A powerful image of desire without fulfillment.

SELLA: I ate the fruit before bed.

CIRO: Then it may represent a memory.

EDDA: It may also represent dinner.

[SFX: Clock ticks, then strikes two.]

CIRO: Dreams combine ordinary events into new forms. The basket, cup, stairs, and bowl can reflect what's on your mind, but one image rarely has a single meaning.

PELI: So I should get the cup in the morning.

CIRO: If that would help.

PELI: And the basket is inside.

EDDA: Your task is nearly complete.

CIRO: Don't take the dream as a prediction. Use it as a prompt to notice what matters to you.

JO: The studio dream journal says "buy more cups."

EDDA: That's not a dream. That's a grocery note.

JO: I wrote it at 2 a.m.

CIRO: It may be both.

PELI: I will set an alarm to bring in the cup.

EDDA: The bell can remind you without turning the cup into a symbol.

CIRO: Though the bell may symbolize waking up.

EDDA: It will be an alarm.

[SFX: Alarm chirps once.]

JO: We are awake.

EDDA: This has been Between the Branches. The basket is indoors, the cup is outside, and no one needs to solve a dream before sunrise.

EDDA: Our last caller dreamed a tree had a door at the top.

CALLER: I opened it and found my bedroom.

CIRO: The door could reflect a wish to return to a safe place.

CALLER: I had gotten out of bed to close the window.

JO: The window is still open in the studio.

EDDA: Please close it before our symbolic interpretation becomes a weather report.

[SFX: Window slides shut; rain patters outside.]

CALLER: That feels resolved.

CIRO: The dream can still matter to you, but it doesn't need a grand meaning.

EDDA: A closed window, an ordinary task, and a good night's sleep.

[SFX: Piano resolves; the clock ticks quietly.]
