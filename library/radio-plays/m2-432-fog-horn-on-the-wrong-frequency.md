# Fog Horn on the Wrong Frequency

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: maritime radio bulletins, expert voices over crackling static, and broadcasters who mistake interference for dramatic weather*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A harbor announcer reports a fog warning while a bird, a faulty receiver, and a ferry horn each claim the same radio channel.

Cast: Announcer Pavi, a gibbon; harbor master Nema, an orangutan; deckhand Teli, a macaque; technician Venn, a capuchin; caller Reko, a baboon.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC. A FERRY HORN SOUNDS LOW AND DISTANT.]

PAVI: Harbor bulletin. Visibility is falling near the north pier. Please use the marked channel for arrivals.

[SFX: STATIC, THEN A HIGH SQUAWK.]

PAVI: That was an alert from the north pier.

NEMA: It was a gull.

VENN: The receiver is picking up two channels at once.

PAVI: The gull has excellent timing.

ACT ONE

Nema reports that fog has settled over the bay. The ferry at South Landing is waiting for a clear approach. Teli checks the weather recorder and confirms the visibility is low but improving. Pavi asks if the horn should sound again. Nema says one horn is enough; extra blasts could confuse boats using the channel.

REKO: I am on a small skiff near the east marker. I heard three horns.

NEMA: There was one ferry horn and two calls from the gull.

REKO: The gull sounded like a ferry.

VENN: The receiver has a damaged filter. It is echoing the high call as a second low tone.

Pavi asks if the north pier is closed. Nema says it is open for foot traffic but not for small boats while the fog remains. The ferry will wait at South Landing. Pavi repeats the warning; the static cuts the word "south" and leaves only "landing."

ACT TWO

Venn tries to tune the receiver. The harbor master reads the channel number from the station card. Teli hears a bird call between the numbers and answers it by mistake. The gull calls again, followed by a short burst of ferry horn. Pavi asks Nema to identify each sound live.

PAVI: First, the warning tone.

NEMA: No. That is the gull.

PAVI: Second, the gull.

NEMA: That is the ferry horn.

PAVI: We have reversed the order.

Teli moves the receiver away from a metal cabinet. The echo drops but does not disappear. Venn asks him to unplug the spare antenna. Teli pulls the cable; the radio goes completely silent. Nema checks the harbor line on a separate wired handset.

NEMA: The ferry is still at South Landing. No vessel is moving through the fog.

REKO: I can see the east marker again.

PAVI: Do you have visual contact with the pier?

REKO: I have visual contact with a gull standing on the marker.

NEMA: Stay on the safe side of the marker and keep your boat still.

ACT TWO-B

Venn tests the receiver with the spare antenna held away from the cabinet. The ferry call is clear; the gull is faint. He discovers the antenna lead was crossed with a cable from the station's bird microphone. Teli separates the leads and clips them to different sides of the table. Pavi tests the broadcast. One channel carries the weather report; the field microphone stays muted.

PAVI: North pier, visibility low. South Landing, ferry holding. East marker, skiff remains still.

NEMA: Correct.

Teli lowers the bird microphone so it no longer picks up the harbor channel. The gull calls once, clean and high. The ferry gives one low horn from the south. They sound different. Reko says he can now tell which is which, even through the fog.

REKO: The gull is at the marker. The ferry is at the landing.

NEMA: That is the useful distinction.

PAVI: The channel is clear. The fog is not.

Venn says the receiver has been repaired, but the antenna must stay clipped apart. Teli secures both leads. Nema checks the tide report and sees no change in the safe departure time.

ACT THREE

Pavi gives the harbor bulletin again. Small craft should remain moored until visibility improves; the ferry stays at South Landing; foot traffic can use the north pier. No extra horn is needed. Nema confirms the route on the wired handset. Reko reports that the east marker is clear and the gull has flown to the breakwater.

PAVI: We have one ferry, one gull, one channel, and no hidden fleet.

NEMA: We have a fog bank and a repaired receiver.

Teli keeps the bird mic separate while Venn logs the crossed cable. The weather station will issue another report in an hour. Pavi signs off without adding dramatic music. The radio holds a moment of actual quiet before the harbor bell.

TAG

[SFX: A GULL CALLS. A FERRY HORN ANSWERS FROM FARTHER AWAY.]

PAVI: Not the same sound.

FINAL IMAGE: Two labeled cables lie apart on the console while the receiver carries one clear harbor channel.
