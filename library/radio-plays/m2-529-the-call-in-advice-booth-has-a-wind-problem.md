# The Call-In Advice Booth Has a Wind Problem

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: advice radio turns a simple awning snag into a life metaphor*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-018*

Cast: Host Vex, an orangutan; mechanic Mara, a macaque; caller Pip, a capuchin; producer Odo, a gibbon; neighbor Nari, a lemur.

SFX: Radio studio. Wind buffets canvas outside a window.

VEX: Welcome to Fix It With Feelings. Pip, what problem are you facing?

PIP, on phone: The corner of my market awning keeps flapping. I tied it to the fence, but the knot loosens when the wind turns.

VEX: Perhaps the awning is resisting your need for stability.

MARA: It is resisting a knot tied to a fence rail that moves. Is the awning pole still seated?

PIP: The pole is seated. The rope is wrapped around the smooth top rail.

NARI: I can see the fence from the studio. That rail is wet and the rope is sliding.

VEX: The fence is refusing commitment.

MARA: Stop adding tension until Pip moves the tie point to the lower post. The awning needs a fixed anchor.

PIP: I can reach the post from the stall side. The ground is dry.

ODO: The live microphone is picking up the canvas. It sounds like a sail.

VEX: A sail is a symbol of the journey ahead.

NARI: It's a tarp over a fruit stall. The wind is from the north, and the loose edge is lifting toward the walkway.

MARA: Pip, close the side flap first. That will reduce the wind under the canvas.

SFX: Canvas snaps, then softens as a flap clips shut.

PIP: The awning is steadier. I can move the tie rope now.

MARA: Wrap it around the lower post, pass the end through the loop, and pull the loop snug. Don't pull against the cloth edge.

VEX: What does the knot symbolize?

PIP: It symbolizes holding a rope around a post.

NARI: The line is still loose at the base. One more pull, then test it with a small push.

SFX: Rope tightens. Pip presses the post; canvas gives a small flutter and settles.

PIP: It holds. The pole is steady, and the walkway is clear.

VEX: You have solved the awning. What has the awning taught you about relationships?

PIP: That if the anchor moves, the knot won't help.

MARA: That's an accurate description of the hardware.

ODO: The gust is easing. We should secure the microphone cable before the next caller.

NARI: It's already clipped to the desk leg. No one is standing near it.

VEX: The advice booth has become a maintenance desk.

MARA: Because someone called about maintenance.

PIP: I'll check the rope after the wind passes and bring the awning down if it gets stronger.

VEX: Very wise. Can you say it again as a parable?

PIP: No. The fruit is getting wet.

SFX: A crate slides under the awning. Canvas rustles, but stays anchored.

NARI: The walkway is clear. The stall is covered.

VEX: I feel that we have all grown.

MARA: The awning has not moved.


ODO: The caller's line is still open. Pip, before you hang up, can you say whether the old rope is still tied to the fence?

PIP: It is off the top rail and in my hand. I'll coil it so it doesn't trail onto the path.

MARA: Put it in the repair bin. If the rope is frayed, it should not be reused on the awning.

NARI: The side flap remains clipped. The lower post is holding, and the wind has not lifted the canvas again.

VEX: The anchor is firm. What does that tell us about the heart?

PIP: Nothing. It tells us that the post is firm.

ODO: We can end the call. The fruit crates are still under cover, and the path is clear.

MARA: Pip, check the tie once after the next gust. If it loosens, bring the awning down and ask for help.

PIP: Understood. I have the stall covered and the rope coiled.

SFX: Phone line disconnects; wind passes without snapping the canvas.
