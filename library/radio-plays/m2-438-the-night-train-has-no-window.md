# The Night Train Has No Window

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: overnight transit bulletins, station announcements, and hosts who describe a route without checking the passenger's direction*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: An overnight train announcer broadcasts the view from a sealed studio window while a passenger calls to correct the direction of travel.

Cast: Announcer Veli, a macaque; conductor Naro, a baboon; passenger Tavi, a gibbon; engineer Rumi, an orangutan; caller Keni, a capuchin.

[SFX: TRAIN WHEELS, STEADY AND LOW.]

VELI: The night train is moving west through the pine valley. Moonlight falls on the left side of the carriage.

TAVI: I am on the train. We are moving east.

VELI: Our map says west.

RUMI: The map is facing north. The arrow is not the train.

ACT ONE

Veli hosts the overnight route bulletin from a soundproof booth in the station. The train has left platform three and is heading toward the river terminus. Naro reports the departure by radio. Rumi checks the track direction. The studio's route map was turned during an earlier maintenance shift; the north arrow points toward the wall.

KENI: I can see the river on my side.

VELI: That suggests you are on the right side of the train.

KENI: It suggests we are near the river.

Naro says the train is approaching the old mill stop. Veli describes pine trees and moonlight. Tavi says the windows are covered with frost, so passengers cannot see the valley. Veli asks him to describe what he hears instead. The wheels click at a steady rate; the brakes begin to hiss as the train slows.

ACT TWO

Veli uses the studio map to announce the next stop. The map shows the station on the north side of the river, but the train approaches from the south. Rumi explains that the route loops around the quarry before reaching the terminus. Veli wants to call the loop a scenic detour. Naro says it is the scheduled track.

NARO: The train follows the rail.

VELI: The bulletin follows the map.

TAVI: The map is upside down.

RUMI: It is not upside down. It is turned sideways.

Veli asks which platform the train will use. Naro says platform one. The map points to platform four. Rumi calls the station, confirms platform one is open, and asks them to change the announcement. Veli reads the corrected stop. A recorded station chime plays after the announcement, but it is the daytime chime and too loud for the overnight service.

ACT TWO-B

Keni calls from the carriage. The train is slowing, but the station name is muffled by the wheel noise. Veli repeats it more slowly. Naro confirms platform one. Tavi says the left-side doors will open, then checks the sign over the aisle. It says right-side doors. Naro checks the carriage layout; the platform is on the right.

TAVI: I was looking toward the engine.

NARO: From your seat, the platform is on your right.

VELI: I will not describe a side until the conductor confirms it.

Rumi rotates the map so the direction arrow and station label line up. Veli traces the scheduled route with a finger and reads the next two stops. She does not describe scenery; she names what the train can verify. The wheels slow. The brake hiss stops. A door chime rings once.

KENI: We are at the river terminus.

NARO: Platform one. Right-side doors.

Tavi steps onto the platform and confirms the station name from the sign. Keni leaves the train with a folded map. No one tries to guess which side of the moon is visible.

ACT THREE

Veli gives the final overnight bulletin: the train reached the river terminus on schedule, and the next departure leaves from platform two after inspection. Rumi marks the route map with the correct direction and returns it to its clip. Naro reports all passengers have stepped off. Tavi says the train windows are still frosted, but the station signs were clear.

VELI: We can describe the route we know.

RUMI: The map is back in its holder.

NARO: The train is stopped.

The studio plays the night wheels once more, then fades them out. Veli thanks the caller for correcting the direction. Keni asks if the next bulletin will describe the moon. Veli says only if the train can see it. The booth remains dark after the microphone closes.
