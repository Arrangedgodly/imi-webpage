# The Sea-Glass Receiver

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur radio mystery shows that turn signal interference into a coded message before checking the equipment*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-012*

Cast: Radio hobbyist Nima, a lemur; neighbor Tavi, a gibbon; technician Odo, a baboon; host Pella, a capuchin.

[SFX: SURF ON A PEBBLE BEACH. A SHORTWAVE RADIO HISSES, THEN THREE CRACKLES SOUND IN A REGULAR PATTERN.]

PELLA: Welcome to Signal Detectives. Nima has received a strange transmission from the coast.

NIMA: It repeats every twelve seconds: crackle, pause, two crackles.

TAVI: I hear a voice in it.

ODO: What does it say?

TAVI: Maybe "bring the lantern." Or "ring the bell." The radio makes everything sound like an order.

PELLA: Could it be a distress code?

NIMA: We called the coast guard. They have no alert.

ODO: It may be a beacon from a ship.

[SFX: RADIO CRACKLE: ONE, PAUSE, TWO. A METAL CLINK SOUNDS UNDER THE STATIC.]

PELLA: There is a metal sound now.

TAVI: That could be a buoy.

NIMA: The receiver is beside a glass bowl of sea glass. The bowl is sitting on the radio cable.

ODO: Move it.

[SFX: GLASS PIECES RATTLE AS THE BOWL LIFTS. THE STATIC CHANGES; THE CRACKLE PATTERN STOPS.]

TAVI: The signal is gone.

PELLA: The message has ceased.

NIMA: The cable was pinched against the tuning dial. The bowl's weight was turning it each time the table shook.

ODO: That makes a regular pattern?

NIMA: The waves shake the table. The dial shifts a little. It finds the same noisy frequency, then slips away.

PELLA: That is less mysterious than a coded transmission.

TAVI: It is still a signal. It was saying, "Move the bowl."

NIMA: No. It was saying nothing.

[SFX: NIMA TURNS THE DIAL SLOWLY. CLEAR MUSIC FROM A DISTANT STATION FADES IN, THEN OUT.]

ODO: The antenna is loose too. The metal clink is the ground clip tapping the desk.

PELLA: So no ship, no beacon, no secret message.

TAVI: We should record the sound for the show.

NIMA: We can record the interference, but label it as equipment noise.

[SFX: A SMALL RECORDER CLICKS ON. ITS MOTOR WHIRS.]

PELLA: That is not a good episode title.

ODO: How about "The Bowl That Solved the Signal"?

NIMA: The bowl caused the signal.

TAVI: Then we can reenact the mystery honestly.

PELLA: We should archive both versions: the interference and the cleared station.

NIMA: Only if each file states what produced it.

ODO: The first has bowl pressure, cable movement and table vibration. The second has a distant music station.

TAVI: What about the voice I heard?

NIMA: You heard the music through static. Your mind supplied words.

TAVI: It sounded like a warning.

ODO: We can put that in the host notes as a listener impression.

PELLA: Not in the signal transcript.

[SFX: A NEW LABEL IS WRITTEN. A PEN CLICKS SHUT.]

PELLA: File one: bowl pressing cable, three crackles. File two: west band, clear music.

NIMA: Put the bowl on the shelf before the next recording.

[SFX: THE SEA-GLASS BOWL IS MOVED. THE TABLE IS TESTED WITH TWO GENTLE TAPS; THE RADIO STAYS CLEAR.]

ODO: No crackle.

TAVI: The mystery had a very small villain.

NIMA: It was not a villain. It was a heavy bowl on a cable.

PELLA: That is an accurate title, but not very dramatic.

ODO: We can add suspense in the introduction without changing the sound.

NIMA: You can. The recording stays honest.

[SFX: THE BOWL IS SET BACK ON THE CABLE. THE TABLE SHAKES; THREE CRACKLES RETURN.]

PELLA: Listen. It repeats exactly.

NIMA: Lift the bowl and it stops.

[SFX: BOWL LIFTS. STATIC STOPS.]

PELLA: Put it down and it returns on the recording.

[SFX: BOWL SETS DOWN. THREE CRACKLES.]

ODO: That is a sound effect we can explain.

TAVI: And a table we should move away from the radio.

[SFX: TABLE LEGS SCRAPE ACROSS THE FLOOR. THE RECEIVER IS TUNED; THE STATIC CLEARS.]

[SFX: RECORDER CLICKS OFF.]

NIMA: The cable is free, the clip is tight, and the dial stays put.

PELLA: Signal Detectives has solved the case.

TAVI: Was there a case?

PELLA: There was a bowl.

ODO: The sea glass can go back in it.

NIMA: On the shelf, not on the cable.

[SFX: GLASS PIECES POUR INTO THE BOWL. IT IS PLACED ON A WOODEN SHELF. THE RADIO PLAYS A CLEAR, DISTANT SONG.]
