# A Footstep Arrived Before the Actor

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio drama sound design, dramatic pre-laps, and directors who confuse editing order with time travel*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A radio play's footsteps arrive before the actor speaks, and the director tries to turn a playback delay into a time-travel plot.

Cast: Director Reni, a macaque; actor Kalo, a gibbon; editor Piri, a capuchin; engineer Savi, an orangutan; writer Toma, a baboon; recorded gatekeeper voice Velo, a lemur.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON GRAVEL.]

KALO: I have not entered yet.

RENI: The footsteps have.

PIRI: The effects track is two seconds ahead of the dialogue.

TOMA: The hero can hear his future.

SAVI: The hero is waiting for a cue.

ACT ONE

The scene takes place on a forest path. Kalo plays a courier approaching a gate. Piri recorded his footsteps earlier, then placed them before the dialogue to create anticipation. The edit is now so early that the footsteps sound like a different character following him.

KALO: I am alone in this scene.

TOMA: Not anymore.

RENI: We are not adding a ghost because a track moved.

SAVI: I can shift the cue back into sync.

Piri asks for another take. Kalo walks toward the microphone, but his shoes squeak. Reni asks him to use the leather boots from wardrobe. They are too large; one slips off mid-step. Toma suggests the courier has lost a shoe in the woods. Reni says the scene does not need a shoe subplot.

ACT TWO

Savi lines up the waveform. The footsteps start exactly two seconds before Kalo's recorded line. Piri says she pulled them forward to make the entrance feel quick. The digital marker shows that she moved the effect by accident while trimming a breath. Toma wants to keep the early cue and write a line about hearing tomorrow.

TOMA: "Someone has already walked this path."

KALO: Then I should wait until they leave.

RENI: The courier is late, not haunted.

Savi moves the cue back. They play the scene. The footsteps begin, Kalo speaks, then a gate latch clicks. The timing now matches the actor. Reni likes it, but Toma says the scene lost its mystery. Piri says they can make the gate sound uncertain without making time uncertain.

She records the latch twice: one clean click, one rusty scrape. Kalo opens the gate on the second sound. The effect is now tied to his action. Savi places the scrape after the line, not before. Toma asks if the courier could still be nervous. Kalo says he can perform nervousness without a future footprint.

ACT TWO-B

Kalo records a new entrance. He speaks before his footsteps begin, then walks toward the gate. He pauses at the path marker, hears a twig snap, and asks who is there. Piri plays a bird call from the same location. Kalo reacts to it as a bird, not a person. Reni says the actor should not identify sounds the character cannot know. Toma proposes that the courier believes the bird is a message.

KALO: I am carrying a letter. Every sound is not about the letter.

TOMA: The hero knows the plot.

RENI: The character does not.

They keep the bird call and remove Toma's explanatory line. The courier checks the address, then starts walking. His footsteps approach the gate at a measured pace. He stops, knocks, and waits.

KALO: I have a letter for the keeper.

GATEKEEPER VELO (RECORDED): Leave it on the hook. I am opening the door from the inner path.

[SFX: LATCH LIFTS. THE WOODEN GATE OPENS.]

KALO: I heard you. I am leaving the letter here.

The latch closes after the courier hangs the letter on the hook. The bird calls once from the path. No footstep or reply precedes its recorded source.

Piri checks the timeline. She labels the tracks "courier footsteps," "bird," and "gate latch." Savi locks the effect layer so the marker cannot slide while she trims the breath. Toma writes a note to himself: suspense may come from waiting, not from a cue that has escaped its scene.

ACT THREE

The finished scene plays in order. The courier announces his arrival; gravel steps approach. The courier checks the letter, hears a bird, reaches the gate, and knocks. A voice answers. The door opens. Reni says the scene has tension without an unexplained second traveler. Kalo asks whether the listener can tell where the gate is. Piri confirms the latch is centered in the sound field.

Savi saves the mix and exports a backup. Toma asks if the play still contains time travel. Reni says no. He asks if the footsteps can be used in a later episode. Piri says yes, but only after the actor walks. Kalo takes off the oversized boots and returns them to wardrobe.
