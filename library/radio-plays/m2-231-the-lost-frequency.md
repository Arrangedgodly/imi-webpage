# The Lost Frequency

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: emergency signal mystery convention, where a radio crew mistakes its own tuning noise for a message from the mountain*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Broadcaster VELA, lemur; engineer TAVI, macaque; skeptic PIRI, capuchin; caller ODO, baboon; neighbor LONO, a langur (off).

[SFX: RADIO STATIC, THREE SHORT TONES.] 

VELA: We have received a signal from the mountain. Three short tones, then static.

PIRI: That is the station trying to find the weather channel.

TAVI: I was tuning the dial.

VELA: You have confessed to the signal?

TAVI: I have identified the signal.

Odo calls from the valley.

ODO: I heard it on my receiver. It sounded like someone calling for help.

PIRI: Did the call include words?

ODO: No. Just three tones.

VELA: Perhaps the mountain speaks in tones.

TAVI: The mountain is mostly stone.

VELA: Stone can have a voice.

PIRI: Not on our frequency.

Vela asks Tavi to replay the recording. He turns the dial; the same three tones return, followed by static.

[SFX: THREE SHORT TONES, STATIC.] 

VELA: There. A plea.

TAVI: The first tone is the station ID. The second is a test beep. The third is the dial catching.

PIRI: The mountain is asking us to repair the radio.

VELA: We must answer.

She leans into the microphone.

VELA: Unknown voice, we hear you. Please identify your location.

TAVI: That is not how a test tone responds.

VELA: It might be shy.

Piri walks over and steadies the antenna. The static clears. A distant voice says "one, two."

ODO: That is my neighbor checking a fence line.

VELA: Why is your neighbor transmitting from the mountain?

ODO: He is not. He is testing a loudspeaker in the valley.

PIRI: Our station has mistaken the valley for a mountain and a count for a cry.

VELA: We need a stronger story.

Tavi finds a loose cable. Each time it touches the metal shelf, the radio produces the three tones. He tightens it; the signal disappears.

VELA: Try the receiver on battery power. If the mountain follows us, we have a field report.

TAVI: The mountain does not follow a receiver.

PIRI: But the neighbor might.

They carry the radio to the courtyard. The station ID sounds clean. Then the three tones return. Tavi checks the antenna. Piri notices the wind chime above the door striking three times.

[SFX: THREE METAL CHIMES.]

PIRI: The mountain is directly over the doorway.

TAVI: It's our wind chime. The antenna is picking it up.

VELA: Remove the chime.

PIRI: It belongs to the building.

VELA: Then we must interview the building.

Tavi wraps the chime in cloth. The tones vanish. The radio is quiet, and Vela has no mystery left to announce.

VELA: You have silenced the mountain.

TAVI: I have secured the cable.

VELA: What if the mountain was trying to tell us it was loose?

PIRI: Then it has a very practical mountain.

Odo says the neighbor is walking toward the station with the loudspeaker. The broadcast crew listens. The voice grows louder: "One, two, check."

[SFX: VOICE APPROACHES, COUNTING.] 

VELA: The caller is nearing our position.

ODO: He is at the gate.

PIRI: Then let him speak.

Tavi opens the window. The neighbor asks if the station can hear the speaker. Vela answers that they can hear it across the entire region.

LONO (OFF): Good. I'm checking a fence.

VELA: Is anyone in danger?

NEIGHBOR (OFF): Only the fence post.

PIRI: The mountain is cleared.

VELA: Our listeners deserve a correction. The mysterious signal was a station ID, a test beep, a loose cable, and a neighbor.

TAVI: That is not a dramatic correction.

VELA: It is accurate.

The loudspeaker emits one long feedback squeal.

[SFX: FEEDBACK WHINE, CUT.] 

PIRI: Now that sounded like a cry.

TAVI: That is feedback.

VELA: We will investigate after the sponsor message.

PIRI: You mean after you unplug the speaker?

VELA: After the sponsor message.

[SFX: RADIO CLICKS OFF.]

TAVI: That last signal came from the host.

PIRI: Three short tones?

TAVI: No. One very long one.
