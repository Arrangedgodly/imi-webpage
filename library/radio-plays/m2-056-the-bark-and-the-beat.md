# The Bark and the Beat

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: community percussion broadcasts and music instruction shows*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Dalu, a macaque; drummer Kes, a capuchin; teacher Sori, an orangutan; and caller Wren, a young langur who practices on a hollow log.

[SFX: TWO KNOCKS ON WOOD, A THIRD KNOCK LATE.] 

DALU: This is The Bark and the Beat, our community rhythm hour. Kes will teach a simple pattern on a wooden practice drum. Sori will explain how to listen before joining. Wren has called from the garden.

WREN, PHONE: My log makes a different sound at each end. Is that a mistake?

SORI: No. Wood can sound different depending on its size, shape, and how it is supported. We use this drum because it is made for playing. We do not hit living trees.

KES: Let me show the pattern. Two light taps, pause, one low tap.

[SFX: TAP, TAP, PAUSE, LOW TAP.] 

DALU: I hear a heartbeat.

SORI: It is a rhythm. We do not need to call it a heartbeat to enjoy it.

KES: Join when you know the pattern. Don't speed it up.

[SFX: THE PATTERN REPEATS. DALU ENTERS EARLY.] 

SORI: Dalu, you came in before the pause.

DALU: I was excited.

KES: The pause is part of it.

DALU: It sounded empty.

SORI: It gives the next tap a place to land.

[SFX: PATTERN REPEATS. A SOFT PAUSE, THEN THE FINAL TAP.] 

DALU: That does feel different.

WREN: My log has a higher note.

SORI: Try the pattern once on each end. Use a padded mallet, and stop if the wood cracks or the instrument moves.

[SFX: WREN PLAYS THE PATTERN, THEN A HIGHER TONE.] 

KES: That note is clear.

WREN: The first one sounded low.

KES: Then your instrument has two voices. Don't force them to match.

DALU: Our next section is called Drum-Off. Kes and I will compete for the loudest rhythm.

SORI: Why?

DALU: The announcer card says so.

SORI: The card is not a teacher.

KES: Let's play the same pattern softly and loudly. We can compare how the sound changes.

[SFX: SOFT PATTERN, THEN LOUDER PATTERN.] 

WREN: The louder one covers the phone line.

DALU: The caller is right.

SORI: We should lower the volume, not ask the caller to shout.

DALU: No Drum-Off. We can teach dynamics instead.

KES: A soft beat can still be strong. A loud one can still be careful.

[SFX: FOUR DRUMMERS JOIN, EACH AT A DIFFERENT VOLUME.] 

SORI: Listen for the space between the players. If you lose the beat, stop and find it again. No one has to play every moment.

DALU: Wren, can you hear the group?

WREN: Yes. I can hear the high note at the end.

KES: That is your log.

WREN: I want to keep it in the pattern.

SORI: Then make room for it.

[SFX: PATTERN REPEATS, ENDING WITH WREN'S HIGH NOTE.] 

DALU: The beat ends where it began, but the last sound is different.

KES: Because Wren joined us.

DALU: We have learned not to make the loudest rhythm the winner. We played together, left a pause, and listened for a new note.

SORI: And used practice instruments, not the trees.

[SFX: THEME RHYTHM STARTS, TOO FAST. DALU CLAPS OVER IT.]

DALU: The station wants the theme to sound exciting.

SORI: The theme is covering the caller's note.

KES: We can keep the same pattern and let Wren's high tone end it.

DALU: The sponsor asked for a drumroll.

SORI: A drumroll would make a different show.

WREN: I can wait through the pause.

[SFX: TWO TAPS, PAUSE. WREN'S HIGH NOTE.]

DALU: That is more memorable than the drumroll.

KES: Because it belongs to the song we made.

DALU: Send us a rhythm you can safely play at home. Keep the volume kind to your neighbors.

[SFX: THEME RHYTHM, TWO TAPS, PAUSE, ONE TAP.]
