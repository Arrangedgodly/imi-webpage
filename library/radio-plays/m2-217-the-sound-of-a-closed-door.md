# The Sound of a Closed Door

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio documentary convention, where a sound collector records doors in the grove and learns one latch belongs to a bat roost*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Host TAVI, gibbon; collector MIRI, macaque; guide PIRI, capuchin; carpenter ODO, baboon.

[SFX: A WOODEN DOOR CLOSES. CLICK.] 

TAVI: Tonight we follow Miri, who records the sounds of doors. She has collected twenty-seven clicks and one squeak.

MIRI: The squeak is not a door. It is the hinge asking for oil.

Piri leads the crew to the old storage hut. The door closes with a dull thud. Miri records it. Odo says it has a loose latch. Miri asks him not to repair it until she has captured the sound.

ODO: I will wait until the recording ends.

[SFX: RECORD BUTTON CLICKS.] 

The crew moves to the garden gate. It closes with a light wooden clack. A small bat flutters from a hollow post. Piri stops Miri from closing the gate again.

PIRI: This is a roost entrance.

MIRI: The sound is excellent.

PIRI: The bat needs quiet.

Tavi asks if the gate can stay open. Odo checks the path and finds it would block the walkway. He proposes a soft catch that keeps the gate from slamming. Miri records the current closing sound once, then Odo installs the catch.

[SFX: SCREWDRIVER TURNS, SMALL CLICK.] 

The gate closes quietly. The bat remains in the post. Miri says the new sound is a soft stop. Tavi asks if it belongs in the collection. She records it, then labels it "gate closing near roost, revised." 

At the hut, Odo tightens the loose latch. The door now clicks cleanly. Miri asks him to repeat it. Odo opens and closes it once. The latch makes one firm click.

ODO: I will not repeat it for a second take.

TAVI: We got the sound.

Miri compares the garden gate with the old hut door. She asks Odo to close each once while she stands the same distance away. The hut gives a low thump, and the garden gate gives a light clack. Piri says the gate sounds smaller because it is outside. Miri records the room tone around each close: leaves near the garden, quiet wood inside the hut.

Tavi asks if the bat call should be treated like a sound effect. Piri says no; it is an animal in its roost, not part of the crew's recording plan. Miri agrees and labels the call as an unplanned sound captured from a distance. Odo asks whether the softer gate catch changed the bat's routine. Piri listens. The bat calls once, then settles.

Miri plays both door recordings through the headphones. The crew hears that the hut latch clicks after the thud, while the garden gate stops with a soft contact. The bat call sits between them in the recording. Tavi says the sequence tells the whole story without narration.



Piri brings the crew to the library. Its door is heavy and closes slowly. Miri records the hinge, then asks if it needs oil. Odo listens. The hinge is quiet. The door closes without a latch because it has no latch.

MIRI: A door can end without clicking.

Miri checks the level and confirms the microphone has not clipped the quieter call. Tavi writes the distance on the recording card.

Tavi asks what the episode's final sound should be. The bat calls from the hollow post. Miri lowers the microphone and records from a respectful distance. Piri asks whether a bat call counts as a door sound.

MIRI: It is the sound that stopped us from closing one.

[SFX: DISTANT BAT CALL, THEN ROOM TONE.] 

Odo offers to add the clip to the collection. Miri agrees, under the title "A Door Left Alone." The crew walks away without closing the garden gate again.
