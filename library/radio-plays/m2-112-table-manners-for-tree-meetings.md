# Table Manners for Tree Meetings

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio etiquette advice, formal call-in dilemmas, and primate grooming customs mistaken for parliamentary procedure*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Auntie Rell, a macaque manners host; Kipp, a capuchin caller; Mera, an orangutan etiquette expert; Jo, a gibbon producer; and Suri, a ring-tailed lemur supper host.*

[SFX: Polite chime, teacup clink.]

RELL: Welcome to The Considerate Canopy. Tonight we answer a question from Kipp, who says his neighborhood tree meeting keeps ending in grooming disputes.

KIPP: I offered to groom the chairperson's shoulders. They said I was interrupting the vote.

MERA: Grooming can be a greeting, a favor, or a request for lint. It is not automatically a motion.

RELL: Did you ask first?

KIPP: I raised my hand.

MERA: Both hands?

KIPP: One hand. The other was holding a branch.

JO: The committee minutes say "Kipp groomed the agenda."

RELL: Was the agenda dirty?

KIPP: It had sap on it.

MERA: That is practical, but the paper may have torn.

[SFX: Paper rips softly.]

JO: The agenda has indeed torn.

RELL: Our first lesson: do not groom documents. Offer a cloth.

KIPP: What if the chairperson has a leaf stuck in their fur?

MERA: You can say, "Would you like help?"

KIPP: What if the leaf is very obvious?

RELL: It can wait until after the vote.

KIPP: The leaf was on the chairperson's nose.

MERA: It can still wait.

[SFX: Phone rings.]

CALLER SURI: I host a branch supper. My guests keep bringing fruit and then staring at it during conversation.

RELL: Put the fruit in a bowl.

SURI: I did. Then everyone gathered around the bowl and stopped talking.

MERA: Serve it in slices.

SURI: They each took a slice and continued staring at the bowl.

JO: This is an intense neighborhood.

RELL: Try assigning a fruit keeper.

SURI: I assigned myself. Now everyone thinks I have private access.

MERA: Announce that the bowl is open to all.

SURI: They are all reaching for it at once.

RELL: Announce turns.

SURI: They are politely forming a queue that goes around the tree.

JO: How many guests?

SURI: Four.

RELL: That's a long queue.

SURI: They are taking turns grooming each other while they wait.

MERA: At least they're occupied.

[SFX: Chime.]

RELL: One more caller. Ludo asks how to decline a hug from a relative.

LUDO: My aunt wraps both arms around me, and I have no space to say no.

MERA: You can raise one hand before she approaches.

LUDO: She thinks that means I want a high five.

RELL: Then raise both hands.

LUDO: She thinks it means I am surrendering the fruit bowl.

JO: We may need a phrase.

MERA: Try, "Hello, Auntie. Today I have room for a wave."

LUDO: That sounds clear.

RELL: And if she keeps approaching?

LUDO: I can step onto a branch.

MERA: Good boundaries should be kind and visible.

LUDO: I will practice with my cousin.

RELL: Do not practice by climbing away from the studio. We have one exit.

[SFX: Door opens. A guest enters.]

SURI: I brought the fruit bowl.

KIPP: Is it open to all?

SURI: Yes. One slice each, then talk.

MERA: That is an excellent meeting procedure.

KIPP: May I groom the chairperson now?

RELL: Ask after the minutes are dry.

JO: The minutes are on the floor.

RELL: Then offer the cloth.

[SFX: Cloth wipes paper; a leaf falls from a nose.]

MERA: And the leaf?

RELL: It can wait until dessert.

SURI: I have a second question. One guest never speaks because he waits for everyone else to finish.

MERA: Give the conversation a turn-taking object. Whoever holds it speaks, then passes it.

KIPP: What object?

RELL: The fruit bowl.

SURI: They will eat it before the first turn.

JO: Use the serving spoon.

SURI: It is stuck to the agenda.

MERA: Then use the cloth. It has already been offered politely.

KIPP: The cloth is now on the floor.

RELL: We have discovered a new rule: wash the cloth before passing it. That is enough etiquette for one evening.

[SFX: Polite chime, then friendly chatter and fruit slices.]
