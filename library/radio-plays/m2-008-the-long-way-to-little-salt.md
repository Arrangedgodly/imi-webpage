# The Long Way to Little Salt

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Travel podcasts with immersive field recording and personal narration*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Ren, a slow loris travel host; Luz, a langur guide; Ana, a capuchin market keeper; Basil, a goat with a taste for microphone foam.

[SFX: A TRAIN BELL, WHEEL CLACK, WIND BUFFETING A MICROPHONE.]

HOST REN: I'm Ren, a slow loris with a travel recorder and a habit of packing the wrong cable. This week, I'm riding the branch tram to Little Salt, a hillside village where the old market steps end at a spring. The official guide calls it a three-minute walk from the tram stop. The official guide was written by a goat who never climbed the hill.

GUIDE LUZ: The upper path is closed today. A storm loosened two stones. We can take the switchback through the chestnut grove.

REN: How long?

LUZ: Forty minutes if your knees know the song.

[SFX: TRAM DOORS OPEN. FOOTFALLS ON GRAVEL. BIRDS IN THE TREES.] 

REN: We left the tram at the lower bridge. Luz walks with a satchel and a little bell. She is a langur, so the steep slope is easy for her. I have short legs and an expensive microphone. I am taking my time.

LUZ: Stop here. Hear that?

REN: Water, under the path.

LUZ: The spring runs beneath this chestnut tree. The old stone steps followed its sound before anyone built a sign.

REN: The sign says "Old Steps, 0.2 kilometers."

LUZ: The sign is on the old steps. The old steps are under the nettles.

REN: So the local route is a sound map.

LUZ: It's a neighbor map. We know which roofs catch the sun and which doors open for tea.

[SFX: A LITTLE BELL RINGS. A GOAT BLEATS FROM A DISTANCE.] 

REN: We have reached the grove. My recorder has collected wind, my sleeve, and what I think is the sound of my own stomach.

LUZ: That's the goat.

REN: The goat is eating my windscreen.

LUZ: He thinks it's a flower.

REN: It is foam. He has an excellent palate for microphones.

[SFX: GENTLE TUG. FOAM POPS FREE.] 

LUZ: This is Basil. He keeps the grove clear of brambles. He has a bell because he wanders ahead and forgets to come back.

REN: Basil, please return my windscreen.

[GOAT BLEAT. FOOTSTEPS.] 

REN: He has left with it. That is a bold editorial choice.

LUZ: He will bring it to the spring. He goes there every afternoon.

REN: Then our journey has become a chase led by a goat.

[SFX: QUICKER FOOTSTEPS, LEAVES BRUSHING CLOTH.] 

REN: We found the old steps. They run between two walls, and the moss holds the day's heat. Luz says this was once the only path from the lower station to market. Now the rail line has made the hill easier for visitors, but the residents still use the hidden stairs when the tram is full.

LUZ: A path remembers feet. Look at that dip in the stone. Everyone steps to the left to avoid the puddle.

REN: And then the left side gets smooth.

LUZ: Exactly.

[SFX: SPRING WATER. A GOAT CHEWS FOAM.] 

REN: Basil has returned my windscreen. It is damp, but mostly intact. At the spring, market keeper Ana fills clay jars for the tea stall. She tells us the upper path will reopen when the stones are checked and set. Until then, residents and visitors will share the switchback, one bell ring at a time.

ANA: If you hear two rings, stop. Somebody is coming around the blind turn.

REN: I hear one ring now.

LUZ: That is Basil asking for a second windscreen.

REN: He can have the dead foam. The microphone keeps its clothes.

REN: Little Salt is not three minutes from the tram. It is a spring, a grove, a long stair, and one goat with a taste for field recording. I'll take the tram down at dusk. Luz is walking back with Basil, who has been given a piece of apple instead.

LUZ: The old route sign needs a new distance.

REN: What will you put on it?

LUZ: "Forty minutes, unless you stop to listen."

REN: That is a better guide than "0.2 kilometers."

ANA: Add "mind the goat."

LUZ: Basil is not on the route map.

REN: He will be by tomorrow.

BASIL: Baa.

REN: He has reviewed the route sign.

[BELL RINGS TWICE. TRAM HUMS IN DISTANCE. WATER FADES INTO MUSIC.]
