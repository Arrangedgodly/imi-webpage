# The Sculptor's Live Chisel

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: arts radio shows that score a performance by speed and applause while the artist needs quiet to make precise cuts*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Host Mela, a gibbon; sculptor Varo, a capuchin; producer Jani, a macaque; visitor Teko, a lemur.

[SFX: WORKSHOP. CHISEL TAPS STONE ONCE, THEN STOPS.]

MELA: We are live with sculptor Varo. He will finish the stone bird before the hour.

VARO: I will finish the wing if the stone holds. I am not racing the clock.

TEKO: Can the audience hear the stone change after each cut?

VARO: Yes. The chisel leaves a brighter line than the rough edge.

JANI: I can keep the close microphone on the work and leave the scoring meter out of it.

MELA: That is the sound the segment should have followed from the start.

MELA: The sponsor has a stopwatch on the table and wants a fastest-sculpture record.

VARO: I am working on a stone bird. It is not a race, and the stone is not replaceable.

JANI: I can turn the stopwatch away from the live table.

TEKO: That gives me room to hear the tool without waiting for a score.

MELA: The show card says the finished sculpture will be judged by applause.

VARO: The piece will be judged by the artist who made it. Applause is just a response.

[SFX: STOPWATCH BUTTON CLICKS OFF. STONE IS SET ON A PAD.]

JANI: The card now says "work in progress, no speed score."

VARO: Good. I will choose where to cut.

JANI: The show timer gives points for each cut. The audience can applaud for bonus points.

TEKO: I came to hear how he makes the feathers.

[SFX: CHISEL SCRAPES. A SMALL STONE CHIP FALLS.]

MELA: One cut, one point.

VARO: Please do not count. The wing edge is thin.

JANI: The applause meter just added three points.

VARO: It also shook the table.

[SFX: TABLE RATTLES. CHISEL SKIPS, SCRAPES STONE.]

TEKO: The tool slipped.

MELA: The bird's beak is still intact.

VARO: I have a new scratch across the wing. Stop the applause meter.

JANI: It is attached to the judging board. I can mute it.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICKS. ROOM QUIETS.]

MELA: Should we restart the segment?

VARO: No. The mark is on the stone. I can make it part of the feather texture, but I need to decide how.

TEKO: Do you want us to be silent?

VARO: Yes, while I feel the edge and choose a direction.

[SFX: FINGERS BRUSH STONE. CHISEL TAPS FOUR TIMES, SLOW AND EVEN.]

JANI: The timer still counts down.

MELA: Turn it face down. We can end when Varo says he is done.

[SFX: TIMER CASE TURNS OVER. TICKING BECOMES MUFFLED.]

VARO: The scratch runs with the wing. I can widen it into a feather line.

[SFX: CHISEL SCRAPES, STONE CHIP FALLS. BRUSH SWEEPS DUST.]

TEKO: The line now follows the wing curve.

MELA: That is the cut you planned?

VARO: It is the cut the stone allowed. The rest of the feather will stay broad.

JANI: The producer card says "finish by the hour."

VARO: The bird can remain unfinished. I will stop before the beak cracks.

MELA: Then the show ends with a partial wing.

TEKO: I can see the bird. The missing detail does not hide it.

[SFX: CHISEL SETS DOWN. A BRUSH SWEEPS THE BASE.]

VARO: Stop here. The wing is stable and the beak is intact.

MELA: The audience can applaud now.

[SFX: SOFT APPLAUSE, NO TABLE RATTLE.]

JANI: The meter is muted. I will write the finish time on the card, not the score.

TEKO: It took twenty minutes, but nobody was counting the cuts.

MELA: We heard the tool, the stone and the artist thinking.

VARO: That is a better record than the fastest finish.

[SFX: STONE BIRD IS SET ON A PADDED SHELF. MIC CLOSES.]
