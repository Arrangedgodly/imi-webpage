# The Alarm in the Dream

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: bedtime-story podcasts, soothing narration, and stories that keep adding interruptions while promising a calm ending*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A bedtime narrator tries to guide listeners through a moonlit orchard, but the studio's real fire alarm keeps interrupting the dream.

Cast: Narrator Rena, a macaque; producer Vali, a gibbon; safety lead Tumo, an orangutan; musician Pex, a capuchin; listener Omi, a baboon.

[SFX: SOFT HARP. A FIRE ALARM BEEPS.]

RENA: Close your eyes and imagine a quiet orchard under the moon.

TUMO: Please leave the studio by the marked exit.

VALI: That is the actual alarm.

Pex holds a tambourine. He stops before it can join in.

ACT ONE

The crew evacuates the recording room and gathers at the outdoor safety point. Tumo checks the board; a sensor near the kitchenette was triggered by steam from a kettle. The building manager confirms there is no fire and resets the system. Rena asks if the episode can continue. Vali wants to keep the harp bed running so the sleep story does not feel interrupted.

TUMO: We should not record over an alarm.

RENA: I will explain the pause after we confirm the room is safe.

Pex asks if he can remove the tambourine from the script. Vali says the planned scene includes a tiny bell beside the orchard gate. Tumo says no real alarms or safety instructions should be treated as dream sounds. They return only after the manager gives the all-clear.

ACT TWO

Rena restarts the story. A small bell rings in the imagined orchard. Pex plays the cue softly. The recording cuts off at the word "gate" because the alarm resets and beeps once more. Everyone stops. Tumo checks the panel; the same sensor has not reset fully. Steam is still coming from the kettle in the kitchenette.

VALI: The kettle is off.

TUMO: The steam is still near the sensor.

RENA: We cannot tell listeners that is part of the story.

Pex takes the kettle to a ventilated counter, away from the sensor. The steam clears. Tumo tests the alarm panel. The light stays green. Vali asks if they can add a gentle chime to disguise the beep. Tumo says the beep should stay audible in the production log but can be removed from the final story after the all-clear is confirmed.

OMI calls from home to ask why the episode stopped. Rena tells her the studio evacuated and waited for confirmation. Omi says she is glad they did; she thought the sound might be part of the dream. Rena says the published episode will clearly mark the interruption and restart.

ACT TWO-B

The crew records a new take from the gate scene. Rena speaks slowly, but does not use the alarm as a plot device. Pex plays the tiny bell once, on cue. Vali lowers the harp volume. Tumo stays by the panel with a clear view of its status light. The story follows a small bat flying through the orchard to find a roost.

RENA: The bat rests in a hollow branch until the morning light returns.

Pex adds a soft leaf sound. The microphone also catches his sleeve brushing the stand. They stop, move the stand, and record again. The second take has the leaf sound and no sleeve scrape. Rena asks if the listener should imagine the bat sleeping. Omi says the bat is awake and listening for insects; it is not a tiny person tucked into bed.

RENA: Then the bat waits quietly, alert for its next meal.

VALI: That is less cozy.

OMI: It is more bat.

Rena keeps the line. The story ends with the bat leaving the branch to hunt beneath the moon. Tumo sees no alarm signal. Pex sets down the tambourine and keeps his hands away from the safety panel.

ACT THREE

Rena records a short introduction explaining that the earlier take stopped for a real alarm and that the room was checked before recording resumed. The episode then plays the finished story uninterrupted. A listener can skip the introduction if they prefer. Vali names the sound cues and music after the story, rather than inside the sleep scene.

Tumo confirms the kettle is off and the sensor clear. Pex packs the tambourine in its cloth bag. Rena asks if the building is safe to leave. Tumo says the fire alarm remains active and the exit is clear. They switch off the studio lamps and leave through the marked door.

VALI: That was the building door.

RENA: Not a dream cue.
