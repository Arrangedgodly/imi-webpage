# Murder at Mossbell Manor

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Cozy crime audiobooks with an amateur sleuth and village suspects*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Cress, an orangutan inspector; Lord Nutley, a gibbon with a brass heirloom bell; Lady Moss, a langur orchid keeper; Hopper, a hedgehog baker; Pip, a macaque page.

[SFX: RAIN ON A CONSERVATORY ROOF. A CLOCK TICKS, THEN COUGHS.]

NARRATOR: At Mossbell Manor, the prized brass bell vanished during tea. No one was murdered. The bell's owner, Lord Nutley, simply fainted into a fern when he saw its empty hook. Inspector Cress, who had come to deliver a basket of pears, stayed for the investigation.

LORD NUTLEY: The bell is worth a fortune.

INSPECTOR CRESS: Does it ring?

LORD NUTLEY: It rings at the start of the village dance.

INSPECTOR CRESS: Then it is worth a dance.

NARRATOR: Cress was an orangutan with long arms and a slow way of moving through rooms. She noticed the dust on the high shelf had a clean oval where the bell had rested. Beneath it, the rug bore a narrow line that ended at the conservatory door.

LADY MOSS: I was in the drawing room. I heard a small clink, then a sneeze.

MR. HOPPER: I sneezed. The pollen from the tea rose is wicked.

LORD NUTLEY: You were near the bell.

HOPPER: I was near the cake.

NARRATOR: The suspects gathered in the winter garden. There was Lady Moss, who maintained the estate's orchids; Mr. Hopper, a hedgehog baker; and young Pip, a macaque page who could climb a trellis without disturbing a leaf. Pip admitted he had dusted the bell that morning.

PIP: I put it back on its hook.

CRESS: Then why is there a trail to the conservatory?

PIP: The gardener carries compost that way. The rug catches it.

NARRATOR: Cress inspected the hook. It held a short red thread. She smelled it, then held it out to Hopper.

CRESS: Cinnamon.

HOPPER: My apron is red. The bell was near the cake. That's not proof.

CRESS: No. But it tells us where it went. Mr. Hopper, did you take the bell to steady the cake stand?

HOPPER: I borrowed it. The stand wobbles. I was going to return it before the dance.

LORD NUTLEY: You used my heirloom to hold a cake?

HOPPER: I used a brass weight. I didn't know it was a bell. It was wrapped in a tea towel.

NARRATOR: Hopper led them to the kitchen. A cinnamon cake sat under a glass dome. The bell had been placed inside the base of its stand, where the screw had come loose. Cress lifted it free. The stand tilted. Pip caught it with one hand and a tail wrapped around the table leg.

LADY MOSS: The cake is safe.

HOPPER: The bell is safe too. It never rang.

NARRATOR: Lord Nutley began to object, then heard the bell's clear note as Cress tested it. The sound rolled through the conservatory. A flock of finches took wing outside.

LORD NUTLEY: That is the dance bell.

LADY MOSS: Then dance.

NARRATOR: Lord Nutley looked at the brass, then at the empty hook. He had spent the afternoon searching for an expensive object and forgotten why the village rang it. Lady Moss tapped the beat on the windowsill. Hopper set the cake knife aside and joined in, careful not to step on the rug's loose corner. Pip hung upside down from the conservatory rail, keeping time with his tail.

LORD NUTLEY: The bell used to call the gardeners in from the rain.

LADY MOSS: Then let it call them now.

NARRATOR: The gardeners were already outside, listening through the glass. They opened the door and came in, boots muddy, hands clean. The bell had brought the whole house together without asking anyone to check its price.

NARRATOR: She took his hand. Hopper offered cake. Pip rang the bell again, this time on purpose. Lord Nutley danced with Lady Moss, while Cress sat by the warm glass and ate a pear. No one was arrested. Mr. Hopper agreed to mend the cake stand with a proper wooden wedge.

LORD NUTLEY: Inspector, you have saved my heirloom.

CRESS: You saved it. I found it under a cake.

NARRATOR: And that is how the village dance began, a little late, with a repaired stand, a cinnamon smell in the hall, and a bell that had never been worth more than the moment it called everyone together.

HOPPER: Inspector, you forgot your pear.

CRESS: I was saving it for the walk home.

PIP: The walk home is shorter now that the dance has started.

LORD NUTLEY: Stay for another song.

CRESS: I will, provided no one reports another missing heirloom.

[SFX: BELL, FINCHES, A CAKE KNIFE ON A PLATE. MUSIC FADES.]
