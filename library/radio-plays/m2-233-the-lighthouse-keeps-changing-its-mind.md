# The Lighthouse Keeps Changing Its Mind

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: maritime suspense radio convention, where a lighthouse keeper's blinking signal is mistaken for a coded warning*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Keeper TAVI, gibbon; reporter SELI, lemur; mechanic ODO, baboon; sailor MIRI, macaque.

[SFX: WIND, WAVES, LIGHTHOUSE LAMP ROTATING.] 

SELI: At the edge of the black water, a light flashed twice, stopped, then flashed three times.

TAVI: The lamp is on a timer.

SELI: The timing changed.

ODO: The timer is missing one tooth.

MIRI: I saw it from my boat. I thought the lighthouse was telling me to turn back.

SELI: Did you turn back?

MIRI: I did. Then it flashed twice and I thought it was telling me to come closer.

TAVI: It is not a conversation.

Seli records the pattern. The lamp turns, flashes twice, pauses, then goes dark. Odo opens the timer cabinet.

[SFX: CABINET OPENS. GEARS CLICK.]

SELI: Could the pause mean the rocks are especially dangerous?

MIRI: The rocks are always dangerous. That is why the light points at them.

TAVI: The sweep marks the safe channel between them.

SELI: So when the lamp pauses-

TAVI: It does not pause by choice.

Odo turns the gear by hand. The lamp spins too quickly, then stops. Miri calls out from the boat, unable to tell whether the light is warning her or signaling a route.

MIRI: I can see the rocks again, but I cannot tell which side to use.

TAVI: Then we keep it dark until the gear is fixed.

SELI: A dark lighthouse is not a strong opening for my report.

ODO: It is a safe opening for her boat.

Seli asks Miri to stay in the inlet while Tavi checks the backup lantern. The lantern is too weak to cross the bay, but it shows the dock. She guides Miri to tie up rather than risk the uncertain sweep.

ODO: One gear tooth is worn down. It skips every revolution.

SELI: A message in mechanical distress.

TAVI: A machine in mechanical distress.

MIRI: Those sound similar at sea.

Tavi explains that the lamp must keep a steady sweep so boats can mark the rocks. Seli asks him to repeat the pattern for the broadcast. Odo refuses to adjust a safety light for a radio segment.

SELI: We need to know what it means.

TAVI: It means the lamp is broken.

SELI: Could it mean something more urgent?

ODO: It means the tooth has gone missing.

Miri points out that the lamp's beam is pausing on the western rocks. Her boat nearly drifted there when the signal changed. Tavi immediately asks Odo to shut off the lamp while he repairs the gear.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICKS. WAVES LOUDER.] 

SELI: The lighthouse has gone dark.

TAVI: For ten minutes. Miri, stay in the sheltered inlet until we test it.

MIRI: I can hear the waves, but not see the rocks.

Odo fits a spare brass tooth. It catches once, then slips. He removes it and files the edge.

ODO: This gear was made for a smaller lamp.

TAVI: The lamp used to be smaller.

SELI: The lighthouse has grown?

TAVI: The tower has. The lamp has not been upgraded.

Seli lowers the microphone. She asks if they can record the repair instead of the mystery. Odo says only if she stops calling the clicking a coded warning.

[SFX: FILE ON BRASS, GEAR TURNS.] 

The lamp comes on. It sweeps evenly across the bay and the rocks.

MIRI: One steady flash. I can see the safe channel.

SELI: No secret message?

MIRI: The channel is open. That is enough.

Tavi tells Miri to return slowly. The boat engine starts; the lamp keeps its rhythm.

[SFX: BOAT MOTOR, LIGHTHOUSE SWEEP.] 

SELI: The lighthouse has spoken.

TAVI: It has worked.

ODO: For once, let that be the headline.

[SFX: WAVES, THREE EVEN FLASHES.]
