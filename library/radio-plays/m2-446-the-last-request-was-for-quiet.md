# The Last Request Was for Quiet

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night song request shows, sentimental dedications, and presenters who treat every caller's pause as a cue for music*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A night host tries to honor a listener's request for quiet, but the station's automation keeps filling the dedication with louder and louder music.

Cast: Host Mela, a macaque; caller Oren, an orangutan; board operator Kiri, a gibbon; music librarian Tavi, a capuchin; producer Savo, a baboon.

[SFX: SOFT RADIO STATIC. A PIANO CHORD BEGINS.]

MELA: This is Night Requests. Oren is dedicating a song to his neighbor, who has just brought home a sleeping infant.

OREN: I would like one quiet minute for them.

SAVO: The request system has selected a trumpet march.

KIRI: That is not quiet.

TAVI: The system sorts by "night" and "request." It found a song called "Night Parade."

ACT ONE

Mela asks Oren what he wants the dedication to say. He says his neighbor lent him a blanket during the last cold snap, and he wants to thank her without waking anyone. Savo suggests a sentimental orchestral piece. Oren says a spoken thank-you would be enough. The automation starts the march again when Kiri opens the request screen.

MELA: We can stop the music.

SAVO: The show is called Night Requests.

MELA: A request does not have to be a song.

Kiri mutes the music channel. Tavi searches the library for a quiet instrumental, but each choice includes a cymbal crash or a long bell. Oren says the neighbor's baby has finally fallen asleep. The station's preview speaker plays a brass fanfare. Kiri unplugs it before the first chord ends.

ACT TWO

Savo proposes a whispered dedication over a lullaby. Mela asks Oren if he wants his name on air. He says yes, but not his street or the baby's name. Tavi selects a short harp track, checks the waveform, and sees a loud opening note. He trims the cue to begin after it. Kiri tests the new start through headphones; the harp is gentle but still has a sharp pluck.

OREN: That sounds like one of the baby's toys.

MELA: Then we should not use it.

SAVO: We have to fill the time slot.

KIRI: We can leave space.

The automation counts down to the next segment. A red indicator flashes when it detects silence. Tavi disables the auto-fill and sets a manual stop. Mela records a spoken dedication: "Oren thanks the neighbor who lent him a blanket. We hope your household gets a peaceful night." Savo asks for another take with more emotion. Oren says the first is honest.

Mela plays it back. Her voice sounds clear. The station clock ticks in the background. Kiri asks whether the clock should be removed. Oren says it sounds like the quiet room; it is fine. Savo slowly lowers the automated music fader. The studio remains silent for one full minute.

ACT TWO-B

The dedication is ready to air. Tavi puts it in a segment with no song cue. Kiri locks the automation so it cannot choose "Night Parade" again. Savo says listeners may expect music. Mela tells them the caller asked for quiet and the show can honor that request.

[SFX: ONE MINUTE OF ROOM TONE, WITH A CLOCK TICKING ONCE.]

After the minute, Mela speaks the dedication. Oren hears it over the phone and says thank you. No music follows. The automation detects the silence and tries to start a trumpet sting; Kiri cuts it before it reaches the broadcast.

KIRI: The system still thinks quiet is a failure.

TAVI: The system is learning.

SAVO: The system is a playlist.

MELA: And we are the people who can make a choice.

Kiri saves the one-minute segment under "spoken dedications." Savo removes the song request from the queue. Oren asks if the station can send him a copy. Mela says they can, with no music attached.

ACT THREE

The show returns after the dedication. Mela thanks Oren and explains that the request was for a quiet moment, not a track. Tavi identifies the next song by name before playing it. Kiri watches the auto-fill indicator; it stays dark. Savo asks whether they should keep one quiet request option in the system. Mela says yes, as long as it does not secretly select a march.

Oren hears the final sign-off and hangs up. Kiri leaves the automation on manual mode for the next caller. Tavi closes the music library. Savo does not add a sentimental sting to the credits.
