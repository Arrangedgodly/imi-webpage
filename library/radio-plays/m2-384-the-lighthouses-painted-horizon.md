# The Lighthouse's Painted Horizon

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: maritime radio forecasts that mistake a painted horizon marker for a real weather clearing*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Forecaster Lomi, a macaque; keeper Sera, a lemur; deckhand Aro, a gibbon; host Evin, a capuchin.

[SFX: LIGHTHOUSE BALCONY. WIND. A METAL SHUTTER TAPS.]

EVIN: We are watching the storm clouds break over the bay.

LOMI: The horizon is brightening.

SERA: That's the painted calibration stripe on the observation window.

ARO: The real sky is still covered.

EVIN: The weather map shows clearing in the west.

LOMI: I was about to report sun.

SERA: The stripe is fixed to the glass. It helps us align the compass.

[SFX: FINGER TAPS WINDOW. THE STRIPE DOES NOT MOVE.]

ARO: The bright line stays in the same place while the clouds move behind it.

LOMI: I can lower the shutter and look through the open side.

[SFX: SHUTTER CRANKS. WIND RISES.]

EVIN: The audience expected a sunrise report.

SERA: We can report visibility and cloud cover. The sky does not owe the program a sunrise.

EVIN: The weather map is updated every fifteen minutes. It is not live from the balcony.

LOMI: The observation log is live. I can record what I see now and compare it with the next bulletin.

SERA: Keep the painted stripe visible in the window, but add the label. It is an alignment mark, not a forecast.

ARO: The buoy bell has changed pitch as the wind turns.

LOMI: The wind is moving east. That is a separate observation from the clouds.

[SFX: BELL BUOY TOLLS TWICE, THE SECOND NOTE LOWER.]

EVIN: The next report can separate cloud, wind and visibility instead of calling all three "clearing."

LOMI: I'll read them one at a time.

LOMI: From the open side, I see the cloud base over the south pier. It is still low.

ARO: The tide gauge is audible below. Water is rough against the rocks.

[SFX: WATER THUMPS AGAINST STONE. A BELL BUOY TOLLS.]

EVIN: Can we make the stripe flash as a weather cue?

SERA: It is painted. It does not indicate weather.

LOMI: Let's mark the observation time and the direction. West window, six forty, low cloud.

[SFX: PENCIL WRITES IN A LOG.]

ARO: I can report the buoy bell is muffled, but the wind is carrying sound east.

LOMI: That tells us sound direction, not the cloud opening.

EVIN: The studio map says a clear patch should arrive in five minutes.

SERA: I will compare the observation with the map at seven. Until then, the live note stays separate.

LOMI: The stripe helps me align the compass, but it cannot tell me whether the clouds are thinning.

SERA: The map is a forecast, not a view through this window.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC. A WEATHER BULLETIN PLAYS: "CLEARING EXPECTED AFTER SEVEN."]

LOMI: The bulletin says after seven, not now.

EVIN: We'll correct the live report. We mistook the painted stripe for sunlight.

SERA: It was an understandable mistake, but the stripe stays fixed. Look for the clouds moving behind it.

[SFX: WINDOW SHUTTER OPENS FULLY. WIND AND WATER CLEAR.]

ARO: There is a small blue gap by the eastern point. It is real, and it is moving north.

LOMI: One gap in the clouds, not a clear horizon.

EVIN: The report is: low cloud over the south pier, a small gap east of the point, clearing forecast after seven.

LOMI: That's accurate.

SERA: Add the stripe to the window log so the next watch knows what it is.

[SFX: PAPER LABEL PEELS AND STICKS BESIDE THE PAINTED LINE: "ALIGNMENT MARK."]

ARO: The label stays inside, not on the view.

EVIN: Our horizon report ends with a real cloud gap and one labeled stripe.

[SFX: RADIO MIC CLOSES. BUOY BELL SOUNDS THROUGH WIND.]
