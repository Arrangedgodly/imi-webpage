# The Platinum Peel

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Luxury lifestyle interview podcasts and aspirational branding*
*By: IMI Broadcast Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

[SOFT JAZZ. ICE CLINKS IN A GLASS. SOMEONE PEELS A BANANA WITH CEREMONY.] 

HOST CELESTE: You're listening to The Good Life, recorded from the penthouse lounge at Infinite Monkey Industries, where today we meet creative director and founder of Peel Atelier, Sir Bongo. His company makes bespoke banana holders for people who feel the fruit deserves a room of its own.

BONGO: Thank you for having me. I arrived by private branch swing.

CELESTE: I heard the swing is made from reclaimed conference table.

BONGO: It is. The board said it was an expense. I said it was a journey.

CELESTE: The holder itself is striking. Tell us about the material.

BONGO: It is a hand-carved walnut cradle, lined with ethically sourced felt, shaped to hold one banana at rest. The banana is included in the first package, but we don't call it included. We call it the inaugural guest.

CELESTE: What does the guest experience?

BONGO: Stillness. The fruit can rest without being placed in a drawer with batteries and mystery cables. We added a velvet curtain, so the banana can choose privacy.

CELESTE: Can it open the curtain?

BONGO: The curtain opens when a person with the household app grants permission.

CELESTE: I see a small button on the side.

BONGO: That button is the emergency peel release. If the banana has become too ripe, you press and hold for seven seconds. The cradle sends a calendar invite to the owner.

CELESTE: And if the owner is away?

BONGO: The fruit will wait. It has a strong support network.

[SFX: PHONE NOTIFICATION, THEN ANOTHER, THEN SIX.] 

CELESTE: My phone says the sample banana requests "a quieter room." Is that part of the interview?

BONGO: That's the premium tier. The banana has detected the jazz and opted out of the lounge.

CELESTE: We can turn the jazz down.

BONGO: The jazz subscription is tied to the lighting package. To lower the volume, we need to lower the lights, which might affect the fruit's mood.

CELESTE: The fruit is a banana.

BONGO: It's a banana with preferences.

CELESTE: Peel Atelier also sells a companion app. What can it do?

BONGO: It tracks peel tension, freshness, and the household's relationship with breakfast. It never judges. It just sends a note at 8:06: "You have not enjoyed fruit today."

CELESTE: Is that why the sample banana is vibrating?

BONGO: The app is asking whether you want to share the experience on social media.

CELESTE: I didn't agree to that.

BONGO: The checkbox was already selected.

CELESTE: The box says "share with your circle." I thought it meant a fruit bowl.

BONGO: We can change the wording. How about "share with your chosen audience"?

CELESTE: How about no audience?

BONGO: You can choose that in the privacy dashboard.

CELESTE: Where is the dashboard?

BONGO: Inside settings, under "Care." Then "Meaning." Then "Who may witness the banana?"

CELESTE: I have a simpler question. How much is this holder?

BONGO: The Atelier cradle is nine hundred dollars. The felt pad is separate, and the concierge plan is annual.

CELESTE: Does the concierge bring a new banana?

BONGO: It sends a reminder to ask your assistant.

CELESTE: And the assistant asks the grocery clerk.

BONGO: We believe luxury is the thoughtful delegation of inconvenient tasks.

[SFX: A BANANA DROPS ONTO A NORMAL PLATE.] 

CELESTE: Our producer has removed the banana from its cradle and put it on a plate. It seems comfortable.

BONGO: The plate is beautiful. We may add one to next season's line.

CELESTE: It costs seventeen cents.

BONGO: We can work with that. Sir Bongo, thank you for joining us. The Good Life ends here, while our fruit is still on speaking terms with us.

BONGO: May your mornings be curated.

CELESTE: May your banana fit in your lunch bag. Good night.

[JAZZ STOPS. A MUNDANE PLATE TAPS THE TABLE. END.]
