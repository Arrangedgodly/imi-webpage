# The Weather Balloon Calls the Wrong Station

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: remote field-report radio conventions, broadcast delay, and meteorologists who treat a drifting balloon as a correspondent*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-020*

Cast: Host Lio, an orangutan; meteorologist Sena, a macaque; field operator Boro, a baboon; balloon voice Kiri, a gibbon; producer Pip, a capuchin; North Fork host, a lemur.

SFX: Studio radio. Static rises and falls. A faint electronic chirp repeats.

LIO: The weather balloon is twenty kilometers above the valley and transmitting from a location nobody can see. We have its signal now.

SENA: We have a signal, but the balloon does not speak. It sends pressure, temperature, and wind readings.

KIRI, recorded: Good morning, listeners. At altitude, the wind is pushing me east.

PIP: That is a voice.

BORO: That is the test recording I made for the balloon's speaker. It is not part of the weather package.

LIO: The test recording is reporting live?

BORO: No. It is stuck in the receiver's memory. The balloon has no speaker. It is a sealed instrument package.

SENA: The actual telemetry says the balloon is descending slowly and moving east. We need the location before it reaches the farm road.

SFX: Chirp, then the recorded greeting again.

KIRI, recorded: Good morning, listeners. At altitude, the wind is pushing me east.

LIO: It sounds as though the balloon is repeating its position.

SENA: It is repeating a greeting. Pip, can you mute the studio playback without muting the telemetry channel?

PIP: Two faders. The green one is data. The red one is the test voice. I will lower red.

SFX: Fader slides. The voice stops; chirps continue.

BORO: We have altitude and coordinates. The balloon is crossing the county line.

LIO: So it can call another station?

SENA: It cannot call anyone. The tracking network relays its signal. Stations share the same data feed.

PIP: We just got an incoming call from the neighboring station.

LIO: Are they hearing the balloon too?

PIP: They are hearing us. Our studio return audio is routing into their weather line.

SENA: That explains why they called asking who was pushing east.

BORO: I can switch the return audio to the control channel. The data stays on the shared feed.

LIO: Do that. We should not interview a machine through a wiring mistake.

SFX: Switch clicks. A second voice fades in over the phone.

NORTH FORK HOST: Valley station, this is North Fork. We have your host saying the balloon is pushing east.

LIO: It is. The wind is pushing the balloon.

NORTH FORK HOST: Good. Our listeners thought your host had taken up ballooning.

SENA: North Fork, our raw telemetry says the balloon is at three thousand meters and descending. Please keep the farm road clear until recovery crew arrives.

NORTH FORK HOST: Copy. We will send the location to the county team.

KIRI, recorded: Good morning, listeners. At altitude, the wind is pushing me east.

PIP: Red fader is down. That came through the phone line again.

BORO: The receiver has a monitor loop. The test message is on the control return, not the balloon's data channel. I can clear it after I save the telemetry.

SENA: Save the data first. We have the descent track. The balloon will land in a fenced pasture north of Miller's barn.

LIO: Is the fence locked?

BORO: The gate is open. The farmer is already waiting with the recovery net.

PIP: The station clock says the broadcast delay is still active. We are hearing the same test twice because our local monitor is delayed.

SENA: Turn off the monitor loop, keep the recording buffer, and leave telemetry on.

SFX: Button press. Repeated voice ends. Wind data continues as quiet clicks.

BORO: Telemetry saved. The north station has the coordinates. The recovery crew reports the balloon is descending under canopy and the envelope is intact.

LIO: We can close the report without asking the balloon for a quote.

SENA: Correct. The instrument measured wind; it did not forecast the farmer's lunch.

PIP: The test voice can be deleted now.

BORO: I'll keep the file, labeled TEST VOICE - NOT LIVE. It may help the next operator avoid interviewing the receiver.

NORTH FORK HOST: North Fork again. We have the balloon on the ground, safe in the pasture. The farmer says it landed beside a hay bale.

LIO: Thank you. Our field correspondent has landed.

SENA: Our equipment has landed. The correspondent is in the studio.

LIO: We will keep that distinction in the weather log.

SFX: Studio chime. The weather map updates; the voice channel stays silent.

FINAL SIGNOFF: Valley Morning ends with one live forecast, one recovered balloon, and no further greetings from the control panel.
