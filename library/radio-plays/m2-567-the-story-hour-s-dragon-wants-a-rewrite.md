# The Story Hour's Dragon Wants a Rewrite

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: children's radio theater that lets a villain rewrite the story to improve its image*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Narrator Vex, an orangutan; librarian Mara, a macaque; dragon Pip, a capuchin; sound maker Odo, a gibbon; young listener Nari, a lemur.

SFX: Library story room. Page turns; a cardboard flap creaks.

VEX: In a faraway cave, the dragon guarded the bridge and frightened every traveler.

PIP: I would like to change my character. I was not frightening. I was protecting the bridge from a goat.

MARA: This is the printed story. You can add a line in the next performance, but we should not change the current scene mid-reading.

NARI: I thought the dragon was mean.

PIP: I was worried the goat would chew the rope.

ODO: I can make the bridge sound with this cardboard flap. No need for a real rope.

VEX: The villain has requested a rewrite.

MARA: Save the explanation for our group variation after the printed ending. First, Vex must finish the page he started.

VEX: In your proposed version, the traveler asks why he guards the bridge.

PIP: And I say, "The goat ate the last rope, and I need a stronger knot."

NARI: That makes sense. But the traveler still has to cross.

MARA: Exactly. The traveler offers to help tie the new rope, and the dragon lets him pass.

SFX: Odo tests his sound props quietly: a cardboard creak, then a brush drawn over paper. No crossing footsteps.

VEX: You have given my villain a job description. I was promised a terrifying roar.

MARA: We can finish the printed version first. Then we will ask the children what line they want to add next time.

PIP: I will save the new line. First, please finish the printed story before you describe my teeth again.

VEX: I must not let improvisation alter the authorized text.

MARA: It is an improvisation in story hour, not a recorded edition. We will label it as a group variation.

ODO: I can make a note on the program card: "Today's group helped the dragon explain his choice."

NARI: I want to suggest that the goat is friendly, but it needs a fence.

PIP: The dragon can build one with the traveler. He doesn't have to keep everyone off the bridge forever.

VEX: A surprising turn toward cooperation.

MARA: The story still has a problem and a solution. We are not removing the dragon's choice; we are making the reason clear.

SFX: New page marked with pencil; flap creaks once.

SFX: Final printed page turns.

VEX: The traveler chose the path around the valley and reached home before dark. The dragon stayed alone beside his bridge. The end.

PIP: Alone? I have just spent this entire program asking for a knot partner.

MARA: That was the printed ending. Now we can perform our clearly labeled group variation.

MARA: Nari, you are the traveler. Ask the dragon why he blocked the bridge.

NARI: Why are you guarding the bridge?

PIP: The goat ate the rope. If another traveler crosses, it may snap. Will you help me tie a fence?

VEX: Objection. The dragon's motive makes him sound reasonable.

PIP: I can sound fierce while tying a knot.

SFX: Brush drags across paper. The cardboard flap creaks too early.

ODO: That was the crossing cue. The traveler has not tied the fence yet.

VEX: Leave it in. The bridge is collapsing under the weight of this rewrite.

MARA: Nobody is crossing. Odo, hold the flap. Pip, tie the paper rope on the sound table, then give Nari the cue.

NARI: I have the loose end. The goat is on the grass side, not in the path.

PIP: Knot is tied. The dragon opens the bridge.

SFX: Flap creaks once, then footsteps cross; a small goat bleat.

VEX: The villain has granted passage and retained a speaking role.

MARA: The group variation is on its own card. The printed story stays unchanged, and our variation gives the traveler a crossing.

ODO: I am putting the sound card beside the variation, not on top of the original script.

PIP: I can be a dragon who made a bad decision and then fixed it. I don't need the narrator to erase the bridge.

VEX: No sequel?

PIP: Tomorrow. The goat has already asked for a gate.

TAG

NARI: I like this ending.

PIP: The goat likes the fence.
