# The Consumer's Chew Line

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio consumer-watch programs, anonymous product testing, and a studio panel forced to review a hammock no monkey can sit in*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Raska, a baboon host; Yori, a capuchin product tester; Em, a macaque caller; and Mr. Sloop, a gibbon hammock salesman.*

[SFX: Consumer-program theme, studio chair squeaks.]

RASKA: This is The Consumer's Chew Line. Tonight we test the CloudNest hammock, advertised as "weightless comfort for every primate." Yori, how was the first night?

YORI: I did not sleep. Every time I climbed in, it rolled me into the middle like a fruit peel.

SLOOP: That's the embrace feature.

RASKA: It has no instructions.

SLOOP: It is intuitive.

YORI: I intuitively fell out three times.

SLOOP: Then you experienced all three exit options.

EM: I bought one for my grandmother. She sleeps in it, but only after tying the corners to four trees.

SLOOP: The trees are not included.

RASKA: The ad says "hang anywhere."

SLOOP: Anywhere with four strong anchors.

YORI: That is not anywhere. That's a square.

[SFX: Hammock fabric stretches and twangs.]

RASKA: We have brought the hammock into the studio. Yori will demonstrate.

YORI: I will lie down carefully. First I grip the side. Then I place one foot. Then I let go.

[SFX: Fabric snaps taut. Yori slides into the center.]

YORI: I have become a hammock burrito.

SLOOP: It keeps you snug.

RASKA: The label says machine washable. How do you wash it?

SLOOP: Remove the machine from the home and place it in the hammock.

RASKA: That cannot be right.

SLOOP: The fabric is machine washable. We did not specify machine size.

EM: The tag also says "Do not hang near bananas."

SLOOP: Bananas encourage climbing.

YORI: I'm a monkey. Everything encourages climbing.

RASKA: Our independent tester brought a banana into the studio. We will observe the product.

[SFX: Banana peel crinkles. Hammock begins swaying.]

YORI: I have not moved.

RASKA: The hammock is moving toward the banana.

SLOOP: That's the auto-location feature.

RASKA: Your brochure calls it "gravity-balanced."

SLOOP: Gravity is a leading location service.

EM: I have a question about the weight limit.

RASKA: It lists two hundred pounds.

EM: Is that one monkey or a group of monkeys?

SLOOP: A group, if they distribute evenly.

YORI: The hammock rolls all occupants to the center. We cannot distribute anything.

SLOOP: It creates a social gathering.

[SFX: Four studio assistants test hammock. They slide together with a soft thump.]

RASKA: The panel is now one pile.

YORI: We are comfortable, in the sense that none of us can move.

EM: Can the sides be lowered?

SLOOP: Yes. They are supposed to be sides.

RASKA: They have no fasteners.

SLOOP: They are aspirational sides.

YORI: I see the problem. This hammock is designed for a monkey who never climbs in, never moves, and does not need to get out.

SLOOP: That's a restful lifestyle.

RASKA: Our verdict: the CloudNest is sturdy fabric, easy to wash if your machine is enormous, and inconvenient to use if you are awake.

SLOOP: May I respond?

RASKA: Briefly.

SLOOP: We will add a ladder and a banana-proof cover.

YORI: A ladder makes it harder to fall out, but easier to get stuck.

EM: I tied mine to four trees. My grandmother loves it.

RASKA: Then the product has one verified use: a stationary monkey with patient relatives and a grove.

SLOOP: We will update the ad to say "comfort for monkeys with four trees."

[SFX: Hammock creaks.]

YORI: The banana has rolled away. The hammock is still following it.

RASKA: That is our final test result. The CloudNest is not a sleep product. It is an enthusiastic fruit detector.

SLOOP: We can market that.

EM: Please don't. My grandmother already has one.

[SFX: Theme plays as the studio team carefully unrolls itself.]

RASKA: For The Consumer's Chew Line, this is Raska. Check the tag before you trust the hammock.

[SFX: One last fabric twang; transmission ends.]
