# The Field Guide's Missing Call

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: nature broadcasts that insist every unfamiliar sound must belong to a rare species*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-016*

Cast: Host Odo, a gibbon; naturalist Nari, a lemur; recorder Pip, a capuchin; producer Vex, an orangutan; caretaker Mara, a macaque.

SFX: Dawn grove. A three-note call sounds from the trees.

ODO: We are live in the eastern grove, where a mysterious call has puzzled our listeners.

NARI: It is a macaque greeting.

VEX: The field guide says a three-note call belongs to the silver crested glider.

NARI: There is no silver crested glider in this region.

PIP: I have the call recorded. We can compare it.

SFX: The three notes play again. A second, slightly lower call answers.

ODO: Two rare animals?

NARI: Two macaques answering each other.

VEX: Could one be rare?

NARI: It is the same sound at a different distance.

MARA: I hear grooming chatter on the branch above the path.

SFX: Soft lip-smacks and leaf rustle.

ODO: The call is spreading.

NARI: The animals are grooming. The first one is telling the troop where it is.

PIP: I can point the microphone toward the sound.

VEX: Keep it on the rare call.

PIP: Both calls are on the same branch.

NARI: Look at the field guide page.

VEX: It lists the glider call as three notes, descending.

NARI: The macaque call also has three notes.

ODO: Can we play them side by side?

PIP: The recorder has two takes.

SFX: First call, then second. They sound similar.

VEX: One has a leaf scrape between notes.

NARI: That is the recorder picking up the canopy.

MARA: The caller is behind the fig tree.

ODO: The field guide says the glider is nocturnal.

NARI: It is morning.

VEX: Perhaps it stayed up.

NARI: Or perhaps the guide is wrong.

SFX: A small branch shakes. A macaque chitters, then leaps to a neighboring limb.

PIP: There. I can see it.

ODO: The rare glider?

PIP: No. A macaque with a leaf stuck to its back.

MARA: It is trying to groom the leaf off.

VEX: We need a closer take for the broadcast.

NARI: We do not need to call back to the troop or play their sound at them.

ODO: But we need a clean recording.

PIP: We have the first call, the answer, and the leaf moving.

NARI: Mark the time and direction. Don't make them repeat it for us.

NARI: The call came from the macaque. The second one answered from the next tree.

VEX: We have a correction.

ODO: We also have a broadcast title. "The Mystery of the Silver Crested Glider."

NARI: Change it to "A Macaque Says Good Morning."

VEX: That is less mysterious.

MARA: It is more accurate.

PIP: I have marked the first call at this branch and the answer at the next tree.

NARI: Keep both marks on one canopy line. The second animal answered from farther away, but it was still the same troop.

ODO: That gives the sound a place without inventing a new species.

VEX: I'll change the program card before we record the guide.

SFX: The first macaque gives the three-note call again. A second one answers, followed by a quiet grooming chatter.

PIP: I can record the whole exchange.

NARI: Keep the microphone where it is. If you chase them, we'll lose the sound and interrupt the troop.

ODO: The field guide can add a note.

VEX: What will it say?

NARI: "Check the region. Listen to the distance. Ask a naturalist."

MARA: And check your back for leaves.

SFX: Pip brushes a dry leaf off his own shoulder.

PIP: I have one too.

VEX: The rare leaf caller.

NARI: It was on your jacket.

ODO: Today's mystery is solved.

PIP: The call was never missing. It was on the wrong page.

TAG

SFX: Field guide closes. The troop's call continues in the background.

VEX: Does the book include the leaf?

MARA: It can have the bookmark.
