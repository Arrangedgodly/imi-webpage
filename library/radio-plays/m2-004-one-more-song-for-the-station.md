# One More Song for the Station

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Public radio pledge drives*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Dara, a squirrel monkey host; Bean, a golden lion tamarin producer; Caller One, a ring-tailed lemur farmer; Mira, a spectacled langur student; Kiri, a squirrel monkey potter; Caller Two, a capuchin mechanic.

[SFX: ACOUSTIC GUITAR PLAYS THREE CHORDS. A RECEIPT PRINTER CHATTERS.]

HOST DARA: It's pledge week at KMON, your community radio station. I'm Dara, a sleepy squirrel monkey with a list of names and a mug that says World's Best Fundraiser. We don't know who gave us the mug. We do know the handle is loose.

PRODUCER BEAN: Our goal is enough to replace the transmitter fan before summer. The old fan has been making that sound for six months.

[SFX: TRANSMITTER FAN GROANS LIKE A COW.] 

DARA: The cow sound is normal. This is normal radio. If you like hearing local musicians, storm warnings, and Bean's thoughtful silence when the phone rings, please call in.

CALLER ONE: I can give ten dollars, but I want the station to play my nephew's song.

DARA: What's the song called?

CALLER ONE: "My Tractor Has a Secret."

BEAN: Does the tractor have a secret?

CALLER ONE: It has two. One is that it belongs to my sister.

DARA: We'll put your gift on the board and review the song for air. We can't promise a play, but your ten dollars will help keep the antenna cool.

[SFX: PHONE LINE BLINKS.]

DARA: A listener named Kiri has offered to bring the station a fan from her pottery studio. Bean says we need a compatible fan, not a fan in the audience.

BEAN: We appreciate both kinds.

DARA: While Bean checks the part number, I'll tell you why this station matters. Last winter, when the bridge iced over, our caller Tomas read road conditions from the bus depot. He was a capuchin with a tail wrapped around the microphone stand and a thermos balanced on his knee. He stayed on air until the school buses took the longer route.

BEAN: The station is also where the high school orchestra first heard itself on the radio. The violin section asked us to edit out the squeak. We kept one squeak, because it was the conductor's chair.

[SFX: A VIOLIN SQUEAKS ON A RECORDING. BOTH LAUGH.] 

DARA: We have a pledge from the orchestra, five dollars and a handwritten note that says "the chair was innocent." Thank you, Mira and the students.

CALLER TWO: I can't give money. I can give time. I'm a mechanic. If you buy the right fan, I can install it Saturday.

BEAN: That is exactly the help we need. The transmitter room is up a narrow ladder. You should know we share the building with a colony of bats.

CALLER TWO: I can work around bats. I can't work around another cow sound.

DARA: We have six hundred dollars in pledges. The new fan costs nine hundred, and the station has a jar of coins from the reception desk. We are counting it live.

[SFX: COINS POUR ONTO TABLE. A MONKEY COUNTS, THEN STARTS OVER.] 

BEAN: It's two hundred and eighty-four dollars, and a button.

DARA: The button is a fine button. It is not legal tender.

BEAN: A listener from the ferry just called with the rest. He says the station helped him find the open channel when fog covered the harbor. He wants us to keep making local reports. His pledge fills the gap.

DARA: We have reached the fan. Kiri's pottery studio has a compatible unit, and she is lending it until the replacement arrives. She says the studio can manage without it for one week because her kiln has a quieter exhaust.

BEAN: The mechanic will install it Saturday. The transmitter stops groaning right now.

BEAN: The temporary fan is installed.

DARA: How does it sound?

BEAN: Like a fan.

DARA: That's a beautiful noise.

CALLER ONE: I can hear you again.

DARA: We never left.

CALLER ONE: I mean the fan stopped covering your voice.

BEAN: The transmitter is cool. The studio is warm.

MIRA: The orchestra is ready to play the chair song.

DARA: Is it about the chair?

MIRA: No. The chair is the conductor.

BEAN: We cannot broadcast that.

DARA: We just did.

MIRA: The chair would like royalties.

[SFX: OLD FAN SWITCHES OFF. NEW FAN SETTLES INTO A LOW HUM. A BAT FLAPS.]

DARA: I never thought I would miss the cow sound.

BEAN: You can visit the studio.

DARA: Thank you to everyone who called, pledged, lent a fan, offered a hand, or defended the conductor's chair. KMON will stay on the air. We will play one song before the next report. It is not about tractors.

[MUSIC: LOCAL SCHOOL ORCHESTRA PLAYS A BRIGHT, WOBBLY MARCH. END.]
