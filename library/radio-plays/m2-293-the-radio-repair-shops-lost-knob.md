# The Radio Repair Shop's Lost Knob

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio repair broadcasts, repair jargon, and technicians who blame every fault on a missing control*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST PAVA, a macaque
TECH ODO, a baboon
APPRENTICE LUMA, a gibbon
CALLER NOLL, a lemur

[SFX: STATIC, KNOB RATTLES.]

PAVA: We are at the Old Dial Radio Shop, where caller Noll says his receiver has lost its volume knob.

NOLL (PHONE): The sound is too loud. The knob is missing.

ODO: If the knob is missing, you cannot turn it down.

NOLL: I can turn it down. I use the edge of a spoon.

LUMA: That is not a proper control.

ODO: The radio has a spare knob in the drawer.

[SFX: DRAWER OPENS, SMALL PARTS RATTLE.]

LUMA: The drawer has six knobs.

ODO: One is volume.

LUMA: They are all the same size.

PAVA: The repair shop has a mystery.

ODO: They are labeled.

LUMA: The labels fell off.

NOLL: I sent a photograph.

PAVA: The photograph shows a radio with no knobs.

NOLL: I took the knobs off so they would not rattle.

ODO: Then the missing control is not lost.

LUMA: It is in a jar.

[SFX: GLASS JAR SETS DOWN.]

Luma pours the knobs onto a cloth. Odo tests each on the radio. The first changes the station; the second turns the radio on; the third changes tone; the fourth does nothing. The last knob makes the radio louder.

[SFX: STATIC SWELLS.]

PAVA: We have found volume.

ODO: We have found a volume control.

LUMA: The knob turns, but it has no pointer.

NOLL: I need to know which way lowers it.

Odo turns the knob slowly. The loudness drops, then rises again.

ODO: It is a tuning dial.

LUMA: Then the volume control is still missing.

NOLL: The radio has one knob?

ODO: It has several. The labels are missing.

Pava asks Noll to describe the radio's age. He says it came from his aunt and has a small brass plate under the speaker. Luma looks at the plate and reads the model number.

LUMA: The manual shows the volume knob on the left.

ODO: The left-side knob is the one that changes tone.

PAVA: So the manual disagrees with the radio.

LUMA: Or the radio was repaired before.

Noll remembers that his aunt replaced the front panel. The knobs were put back in a different order. Odo checks the wiring and maps each control with a pencil mark.

[SFX: PENCIL SCRATCH, DIAL TURNS.]

ODO: This one is power. This one is tuning. This one is volume.
 
Noll repeats the controls in order while Odo turns each knob. The power switch clicks, the tuning dial finds a station, and the volume lowers the broadcast. A fourth knob changes tone, but a fifth is a blank wooden cap.

[SFX: CLICK, STATION MUSIC, VOLUME LOWERS.]

LUMA: The cap is not connected.

NOLL: That one is decorative.

ODO: We should label it "not a control."

PAVA: A label that says not a control will become the most important label.

Noll asks the shop to send a photograph of the marked radio before returning it. Odo takes the picture, but the paint marks cover the model number.

LUMA: Use pencil on the removable tags.

ODO: The radio has already taught us not to trust its layout.

PAVA: The shop has also learned to photograph before labeling.


LUMA: Label them before we remove them again.

NOLL: Can you send me the labels?

PAVA: We will read them on the radio.

Odo makes a small spoken guide. Noll turns the volume down without using a spoon. The sound drops to a comfortable level.

NOLL: That is better.

ODO: The missing knob was present all along.

LUMA: It was unidentified.

PAVA: The repair shop has solved the case and created a labeling job.

NOLL: Please send the labels with the radio.

ODO: I will mark the controls with paint.

LUMA: Removable paint.

ODO: Permanent pencil.

[SFX: PENCIL TAPS RADIO.]

PAVA: From the Old Dial Radio Shop, the volume is lower, the labels are clear, and the spoon can return to the kitchen.

[SFX: STATIC FADES TO MUSIC.]
