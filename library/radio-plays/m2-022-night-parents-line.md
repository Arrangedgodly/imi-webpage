# Night Parents Line

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Late-night parenting hotlines and miracle-tip segments*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Host Neri, a mandrill family counselor; Caller Sal, a capuchin parent; Jo, Sal's young macaque; Aunt Mave, a gorilla neighbor.

[THEME: A TOY XYLOPHONE, A YAWN, PHONE LINE OPENS.] 

HOST NERI: It is 2:14 a.m. and you are awake. This is Night Parents Line. I'm Neri, and tonight our sponsor says one spoon of moonflower syrup will make every child sleep. Our producer checked. It is a spoon of syrup with a moonflower on the label.

CALLER SAL: My son Jo has been climbing the curtains every night. He says he is practicing for the sunrise race.

NERI: How old is Jo?

SAL: Four months.

NERI: That's a very early race season.

SAL: He crawls up the fabric and squeaks when I lift him down. I tried a reward chart, but he ate it.

NERI: Is there a safe place where he can climb before bedtime?

SAL: The low climbing frame in the yard. We stop using it after dark because it is hard to see.

NERI: Could you add a lantern and make the climbing frame part of the evening routine? Then curtains stay for keeping out the moon, not climbing practice.

SAL: I can ask my neighbor to hang the lantern. My hands are full at bedtime.

NERI: Ask for a specific hour. "Can you hold the lantern at seven?" is easier to answer than "I need help sometime."

SAL: Aunt Mave is next door. She likes holding things.

[SFX: LITTLE HANDS ON CLOTH. A CLIMBING CURTAIN ROD CREAKS.] 

JO: I am practicing.

SAL: Jo, tell Neri good evening.

JO: Good evening. Can I climb the curtains after breakfast?

NERI: The curtains are not a climbing place. Ask if you can use the frame.

JO: Can I climb the frame after breakfast?

SAL: Yes, if the ground is dry.

JO: And the curtains?

SAL: Curtains stay still.

JO: They are very tall.

NERI: Tall does not always mean "for climbing." My office building is tall, and I don't climb that.

SAL: You could.

NERI: I could. My producer would have opinions.

[PHONE BEEPS. A SECOND CALLER JOINS.] 

NERI: We have another parent. Caller, what is keeping you up?

CALLER MO: My twins ask for a different bedtime story every night. They negotiate until the candle burns down.

NERI: Do they choose the story before bedtime?

MO: No. They start negotiating after the book is open.

NERI: Then make a story basket and let each twin choose one book before tooth brushing. If they cannot agree, alternate nights. The basket does not need to settle every dispute.

MO: One twin is a tamarin. One is a lemur. They both want the story with the giant mango.

NERI: Is there a shorter story with a giant mango?

MO: There is a recipe card.

NERI: Read the recipe card dramatically. The story ends when the mango is cut.

MO: That may work.

NERI: If it does not, you have fruit for breakfast.

[SFX: LANTERN SWITCHES ON. A SOFT CRICKET CHIRPS.] 

SAL: Aunt Mave is here. She brought the lantern and a cushion for the yard.

MAVE: I also brought the moonflower syrup.

NERI: Please read the ingredients before giving it to Jo. The label is not a sleep plan.

MAVE: It's mostly sugar.

SAL: Then it is a morning treat.

JO: Can I have it after the race?

SAL: If we all sleep first.

NERI: That's our show. Tonight's plan is a lit climbing frame, one story chosen early, and no magical syrup claims. You know your child better than an advertisement does. If you feel overwhelmed, wake someone you trust. That is what neighbors are for.

[SFX: JO CLIMBS A LOW FRAME, THEN SETTLES ON THE CUSHION.] 

JO: I can see the sunrise from here.

SAL: It's the porch light.

JO: It is a very small sunrise.

NERI: Good night from Night Parents Line. Please put the moonflower syrup somewhere the children cannot find it.

[THE XYLOPHONE PLAYS TWO NOTES. END.]
