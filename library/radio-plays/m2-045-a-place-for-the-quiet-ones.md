# A Place for the Quiet Ones

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live animal rescue adoption broadcasts*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Miri, a capuchin; rescue keeper Sol, a macaque; caller Vera, a baboon; and two orphaned young langurs heard moving in their nesting room.

[SFX: SOFT ROOM TONE. TWO SMALL ANIMALS RUSTLE IN HAY.] 

MIRI: You're listening to Home for the Hour, a broadcast from the Willow Creek Wildlife Center. Tonight we are not matching animals to callers by charm. We are explaining how the center cares for two young langurs recovering from a fall. Sol, what do they need?

SOL: Quiet, warmth, and steady feeding. They were found near a damaged nest. A licensed wildlife team brought them here. They are not ready for release or handling by visitors. The producer has already written "happy reunion" on the running order.

MIRI: A caller wants to know whether they can adopt one. Our producer has placed two heart graphics on the studio board.
SOL: We do not choose homes on a broadcast.
MIRI: The meter says "perfect match."
SOL: The meter is a kitchen timer.
[SFX: TIMER BEEPS.]
MIRI: It has matched them for twelve minutes.
SOL: They need specialist care, not a romance segment.
MIRI: We will turn the meter off.

VERA, PHONE: I have a large room and a climbing frame. I could give one a home.

SOL: Langurs are social animals. These two need to stay together, and they need specialist care. A home setup would not meet their needs.

VERA: Could I sponsor food instead?

SOL: Yes. The center accepts donations, but we do not send animals home with sponsors.

MIRI: That is a clear answer. Can we hear them?

SOL: Only if we keep the microphone outside the room.

[SFX: SOFT SCRATCH, A SMALL CHIRP.] 

MIRI: One moved closer to the basket.

SOL: It may be looking for its sibling. They settle when they can hear each other.

MIRI: The center has a public viewing window. Why can't people go inside?

SOL: Human voices and movement can stress them. The window lets visitors observe without disturbing their recovery.

VERA: I have seen videos where people feed baby monkeys from bottles. It looks easy.

SOL: A video leaves out the training, measurements, and cleaning. A wrong feeding position can make an animal choke. A wrong formula can make it sick. Wildlife care is not a home hobby.

MIRI: How does the center decide when they are ready to go?

SOL: We watch their strength, feeding, social behavior, and response to climbing. They need to find food and move safely. A veterinarian checks them. If the original troop's range is safe, we plan a gradual return with monitoring.

[SFX: CLIPBOARD, SOFT FOOTSTEPS.] 

MIRI: You just checked the warmer.

SOL: It is set to the right temperature. One young one pushed the bedding into the vent, so I cleared it.

MIRI: Can the callers help?

SOL: They can support the center, keep distance from wild animals, and call a rescue group if they find one. They should not pick it up unless a trained responder asks them to.

VERA: If I find a baby animal on the path, I should leave it in place?

SOL: First observe from a distance. Sometimes a parent is nearby. If it appears injured or in danger, call the local wildlife service and follow their directions. Keep pets away.

MIRI: We have another caller who wants to volunteer.

CALLER: Can I clean the room?

SOL: We train volunteers and assign jobs based on experience. This room is restricted. You could help prepare food in the kitchen or wash supplies in the volunteer area.

CALLER: I can wash bowls.

MIRI: Bowls need care too.

[SFX: WATER RUNS IN A DISTANT SINK, THEN STOPS.] 

SOL: The young ones have settled. They are curled together. We are keeping the light low and the door shut.

MIRI: That is our update. Sponsorship helps pay for food and veterinary care. The animals are not prizes, pets, or studio guests. Their best chance is skilled care and a safe return to the wild.

VERA: I will sponsor the food.

SOL: Thank you. We'll send you a report when their care plan allows it.

[SFX: THE SMALL LANGURS RUSTLE, THEN QUIET.] 

MIRI: They have gone to sleep. We will let the room stay quiet.
