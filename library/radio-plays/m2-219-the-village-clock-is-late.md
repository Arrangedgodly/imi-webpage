# The Village Clock Is Late

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio mystery convention, where a village clock rings thirteen times because a monkey is feeding it fallen fruit*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Detective MIRI, macaque; keeper PIRI, gibbon; caller ODO, baboon; clockmaker TAVI, capuchin.

[SFX: CLOCK CHIMES THIRTEEN TIMES.] 

MIRI: At midnight, the village clock struck thirteen.

ODO: I counted.

PIRI: The clock is old. It sometimes repeats the last note.

TAVI: It does not repeat. One hammer is loose.

Miri asks who had access to the tower. Piri says only the keeper and the clockmaker. Odo says he heard scratching inside the case. Tavi opens the lower panel. A small monkey darts out with a fig.

ODO: The intruder has fruit.

The small monkey climbs a beam. Piri calls it a local visitor. Miri asks why it was inside the clock. Tavi finds a pile of figs beneath the gears. The fruit has attracted the monkey and softened the wooden support under the loose hammer.

MIRI: The clock is not haunted.

ODO: It has been fed.

Tavi removes the figs and checks the hammer. The support shifts when he touches it. Piri asks him to stop until the mechanism is secured. Tavi braces the support with a wooden wedge, then tightens the pin. The hammer swings once, cleanly.

[SFX: SINGLE CLOCK CHIME.] 

MIRI: One o'clock, early.

TAVI: We are testing the mechanism.

Piri says the clock has been running late for weeks. Odo says he thought the village had gained extra evenings. Miri asks if anyone set the hands back. Piri says he did, because the clock's chime had drifted. Tavi explains the loose hammer only made the hour sound longer; it did not change the time.

Before setting it, Tavi asks Piri to stand beside the clock and read the hands aloud. Piri says they show 11:53, while the station signal indicates noon. The old clock is seven minutes slow, not one hour late. The extra chime came from the loose hammer touching twice as it swung.

Miri asks the keeper why he reset it by ear. Piri says the tower's bell was difficult to hear from the orchard, so he guessed from the sound. Tavi checks the hands against the signal and moves them forward seven minutes. Odo tests the hammer with a cloth held nearby. It swings freely and strikes once.

[SFX: ONE PRECISE CHIME.] 

The monkey rustles in the lower case. Piri finds the figs and moves them away from the gears. Miri asks if the monkey caused the time drift. Tavi says no; it only made the loose hammer stick. Odo shows the keeper how to check the hinge and the time signal without touching the mechanism.

Piri agrees to use the radio signal at each scheduled check. Miri records the clock face and its sound together. The chime is now a confirmation, not the timekeeper.



They set the clock to the station signal. The hands move forward. The small monkey returns, searching for its figs. Piri gives it one from his pocket, then moves the rest of the fruit away from the tower.

MIRI: That is not evidence.

PIRI: It is hospitality.

The monkey takes the fig and leaves through the hatch. Tavi closes the panel and tests the clock again. The mechanism chimes twelve times, evenly, for noon.

[SFX: TWELVE CLEAR CHIMES, FADE.] 

Odo asks if the mystery is solved. Miri says the clock was late because the keeper kept resetting it by ear. Piri says he will use the station time from now on. Tavi marks the next inspection date on a card and hangs it inside the tower door.

ODO: The case is closed.

A small knock comes from inside the clock.

MIRI: That is the hatch.

PIRI: And it is staying closed.
