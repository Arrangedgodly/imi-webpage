# The Antenna Over the Orchard

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur astronomy broadcasts and live stargazing events*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Kavi, a macaque; Nemi, an orangutan astronomer; Taro, a squirrel monkey student; and Mrs. Venn, a village orchardist listening by phone.

[SFX: NIGHT AIR, CRICKETS, A RADIO DIAL TURNS.] 

KAVI: Welcome to Looking Up, the Bellwether astronomy hour. We are outside the school observatory with a telescope pointed at Saturn. Nemi, what should listeners expect?

NEMI: A bright point with rings. The telescope makes it larger, but the rings may look faint tonight. The view changes with weather, equipment, and where you stand.

KAVI: So no photograph-quality picture?

NEMI: Not through this eyepiece. You get a small view, and it takes time for your eyes to adjust.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON GRAVEL. TELESCOPE MOUNT CREAKS.] 

TARO: The antenna is blocking the telescope.

KAVI: The antenna is three meters away.

TARO: It blocks my view of the sky.

NEMI: The telescope sees above it. Look through the eyepiece.

TARO: I see a circle.

NEMI: That is the cap.

[SFX: CAP REMOVED.] 

TARO: Now I see a brighter circle.

KAVI: The Moon?

NEMI: Saturn. The Moon is below the roof line.

KAVI: We have a caller from the orchard.

VENN, PHONE: I can hear your antenna humming. Is the sky making that sound?

NEMI: Probably not. Is there a pump or light nearby?

VENN: The irrigation pump is running and the barn light is on.

NEMI: Turn off the barn light if it is safe. Leave the pump alone tonight.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICKS. HUM FADES.] 

VENN: The hum stopped.

KAVI: Did you see a star?

VENN: I saw my reflection in the window.

TARO: That's a local object.

KAVI: Nemi, what can listeners see without equipment?

NEMI: Watch how the bright points shift over several hours. Keep phone screens dim so your eyes adjust. Never point a telescope or binoculars at the Sun.

KAVI: Is Saturn moving?

NEMI: It moves in its orbit. From our view, it shifts against the stars over many nights. Earth's rotation makes the whole sky seem to move during one night.

TARO: I thought the planet would cross the sky before we finished.

NEMI: That is not how planets look from here.

[SFX: RADIO TONE. A CHILD WHISPERS INTO PHONE.] 

CHILD: I can see it now. It's a tiny button.

KAVI: A button in the sky?

NEMI: A planet with rings, if you look carefully.

TARO: Can I draw the rings?

NEMI: Yes, but draw what you see. Don't add moons you haven't seen.

KAVI: We have another caller. The barn light is back on. Is the hum back?

VENN: No. I can hear crickets, the pump, and my own kettle.

KAVI: Does the kettle help with astronomy?

VENN: It keeps my hands warm while I wait.

NEMI: Warmth is useful. So is patience. The planet will not hurry for our program.

TARO: Does it know we're watching?

NEMI: No. That is part of the comfort.

KAVI: We did not discover a new planet or hear a message from space. We checked a telescope, found a local electrical hum, and shared a view of Saturn. The town observatory is open until ten. Please follow the path markers, keep lights low, and wait for a turn at the eyepiece.

[SFX: TELESCOPE MOUNT TURNS SLOWLY. CRICKETS CONTINUE.] 

KAVI: Bring a warm coat, a question, and no expectation that the universe will pose for a photograph.

TARO: Remove the cap first.

KAVI: That's the first step. The observatory brochure promises an unforgettable close-up. Nemi, what did I see?

NEMI: A bright dot with a suggestion of rings.

KAVI: We will change the brochure to "a patient look through a telescope."

TARO: And the cap stays in the equipment box.

[SFX: CAP DROPS INTO BOX. TELESCOPE CREAKS, CRICKETS CONTINUE.]

KAVI: Looking Up, where the sky does not sign release forms.
