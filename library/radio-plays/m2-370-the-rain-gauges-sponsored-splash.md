# The Rain Gauge's Sponsored Splash

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: weather demonstrations that exaggerate rainfall by pouring sponsor water into the measuring gauge*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Host Pavo, a macaque; meteorologist Liri, a capuchin; sponsor rep Doss, a lemur; caller Hemi, a gibbon.

[SFX: ROOFTOP RAIN. A METAL RAIN GAUGE TAPS WITH DROPS. MIC OPENS.]

PAVO: Our live rain gauge reads twelve millimeters. Today's broadcast is sponsored by ClearSpring Water.

LIRI: The gauge reads twelve because it has rained twelve millimeters.

DOSS: We have a larger ClearSpring jug for the demonstration. Pouring it into the gauge will make the rainfall visible.

LIRI: That would not be rainfall. It would be bottled water.

PAVO: The camera cannot show millimeters.

LIRI: The gauge has a marked tube. We can read the level.

[SFX: A JUG LIFTS. WATER SLOSHES.]

DOSS: The sponsor wants a dramatic splash.

LIRI: Keep the jug away from the gauge.

PAVO: We could pour into a second clear tube and call it a comparison.

LIRI: Only if it is labeled as water, not rain.

[SFX: JUG SETS DOWN. RAIN CONTINUES.]

PAVO: The sponsor's jug is one liter. I could pour it into a tray so the camera sees a large splash.

LIRI: Then the broadcast would have a splash and no weather information.

DOSS: What if I pour it into a separate container with the ClearSpring label facing the lens?

LIRI: That is fine, as long as the rainfall tube stays untouched and the label says drinking water.

[SFX: JUG SETS BESIDE THE GAUGE. A MEASURED POUR FILLS A CLEAR GLASS, NOT THE RAIN TUBE.]

PAVO: The glass shows the sponsored water. The gauge still shows rain.

DOSS: That looks less like a storm, more like a table.

LIRI: It is a table. The weather is on the roof.

[SFX: A HEAVY DROP HITS THE GAUGE AND THE LEVEL RISES A SMALL AMOUNT.]

HEMI: I'm calling from the garden. The rain gauge at my house has eight millimeters.

PAVO: Our roof gauge has twelve. Which one is right?

LIRI: Both may be right. Rain varies by location. Hemi, is your gauge level and clear?

HEMI: Yes. I emptied it this morning. It has eight now.

DOSS: Could the sponsor pour raise ours to twenty and show that rain can vary?

LIRI: No. That would make a false comparison.

PAVO: We still need a visual for the broadcast.

LIRI: We can show the marked tube beside the sponsor jug, with the labels visible. The rain is twelve. The jug is a liter of drinking water.

[SFX: PAPER LABELS PEEL. ONE IS PRESSED TO THE TUBE: "RAINFALL." ANOTHER TO THE JUG: "DRINKING WATER."]

DOSS: The brand label is hidden by that paper.

LIRI: It can go below the words "drinking water."

[SFX: TAPE STRIPS RIP. THE LOGO IS PLACED UNDER THE CLEAR LABEL.]

PAVO: The camera can see both now.

DOSS: It looks less dramatic than the splash.

HEMI: I can hear the rain on my roof. It is steady, not a downpour.

LIRI: That's useful. Our gauge shows twelve millimeters here, your garden shows eight, and both places had steady rain.

PAVO: Can I ask how much is in the sponsor jug?

LIRI: One liter. It is a volume of water, not a depth of rain.

DOSS: We'll sponsor the forecast, not the measurement.

PAVO: The gauge reads twelve. Has the number changed?

[SFX: RAIN DRIPS INTO GAUGE. LIRI LIFTS THE TUBE, READS MARK.]

LIRI: It has reached thirteen. The rain is still falling.

HEMI: Mine has reached nine.

PAVO: We can say the storm is stronger at the roof than the garden.

LIRI: Not without more readings. We can say both gauges have risen by one since the call.

DOSS: The sponsor would like one big number.

LIRI: Then use one big label: rain, thirteen millimeters at this roof.

[SFX: MIC CLOSES. RAIN CONTINUES. THE JUG REMAINS SEALED.]
