# The Advice Line for Small Problems

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night advice radio show that learns to scale its responses*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST MIRA, a macaque; ADVISER SOL, a slow loris; CALLER FEN, a capuchin; and CALLER TAVI, a gibbon.

[SFX: SOFT PIANO THEME, PHONE LINE CLICKS.] 

MIRA: This is Night Questions, where callers bring us the things that keep them awake. Sol is here to offer perspective.

SOL: I offer questions first. Advice can wait.

MIRA: Fen is on line one.

FEN: I cannot decide whether to sleep in my usual branch or try the lower branch tonight.

MIRA: What is troubling you about the usual branch?

FEN: A leaf fell on me yesterday.

SOL: Was the branch unsafe?

FEN: No.

SOL: Is the leaf still there?

FEN: No.

SOL: What would make the lower branch better?

FEN: I could reach the water dish faster.

MIRA: That sounds like a practical reason.

FEN: But I have never slept there.

SOL: You could sit on it while awake and see how it feels.

FEN: I can do that.

MIRA: One small question, one small experiment. Tavi is on line two.

TAVI: My nest is untidy. I keep moving twigs around, but every time I fix one side, the other side looks uneven.

MIRA: How long have you been moving them?

TAVI: Since dusk.

SOL: Are you trying to make every twig straight?

TAVI: Yes.

SOL: Twigs grow in different shapes. Could you choose the ones that support the edge and leave the rest where they fit?

TAVI: That would mean the nest isn't symmetrical.

SOL: It already isn't. Is it comfortable?

TAVI: Yes.

MIRA: Then the job may be finished.

TAVI: I can hear the wind through one gap.

SOL: Is the gap too wide, or is air moving through it?

TAVI: I don't know.

SOL: Put a leaf near it and see whether it catches. Do not fill the gap just because it exists.

[SFX: LEAF SET NEAR A NEST. WIND RUSTLES IT LIGHTLY.] 

TAVI: It moves, but it does not pull away.

SOL: Then the nest is breathing.

MIRA: That is a useful description.

TAVI: I will stop moving the twigs.

MIRA: We have another caller, a young lemur who says her sleeping leaf keeps curling at the edge.

CALLER: I flatten it, but it curls up again.

SOL: Is it attached to the branch?

CALLER: Yes. I don't want to tear it.

SOL: Then leave it attached. A curling leaf is not always a problem. Does it shade you?

CALLER: It shades my eyes.

MIRA: Does the branch move in the wind?

CALLER: A little.

SOL: You could choose a different place to sleep. You do not have to make the leaf behave.

CALLER: I like this branch.

SOL: Then shift your resting spot a little. See if the leaf still bothers you.

[SOFT LEAVES MOVE. A BODY SETTLES ON A BRANCH.] 

CALLER: It is better here. I can still hear the stream.

MIRA: Another adjustment made without changing the whole tree.

SOL: The tree is not a problem to solve. Find the place that works for you.

FEN: My lower branch is steady. I am staying here tonight.

MIRA: Have you told anyone?

FEN: I told you.

SOL: Now you can rest.

MIRA: We have room for one more piece of advice. If your problem is small, you do not need a large plan. Try one safe change, then notice what happens.

SOL: And if it works, let it work.

[SFX: THE PHONE LINE CLOSES. THE PIANO THEME RETURNS SOFTLY.]

MIRA: We have one more call. A listener says the moon is too bright to sleep.

SOL: Close your eyes.

MIRA: That is your entire advice?

SOL: Sometimes the problem is the moon. Sometimes the problem is looking at it.

FEN: I am sitting on the lower branch. It feels steady.

MIRA: Have you decided?

FEN: I will sleep here tonight and choose again tomorrow.

[SFX: PIANO THEME QUIETS. A LEAF SETTLES INTO A NEST.]
