# Orbit Check Cedar

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Space mission communications and control-room audio logs*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Commander Rima, a bonobo pilot; Dax, a langur antenna specialist; Ground Lead Fen, a macaque; Field probe Cedar, a robotic seed sampler.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC, A STEADY VENTILATION FAN, TWO SOFT CONTROL BEEPS.] 

GROUND FEN: Cedar, this is Ground. Confirm probe separation.

RIMA: Ground, Cedar is clear of the carrier. We have a slow spin and a good signal.

FEN: Copy. The science team wants the seed sample before the next shadow period. Antenna alignment first. They would like a clean picture of the cedar grove crater.

DAX: The crater is behind the probe. We can turn the camera or turn the antenna. We cannot do both with this power budget.

FEN: Can we send the picture later?

RIMA: We can send the data later. Cedar has found a pattern in the dust. The sample arm is reading low density beneath the rim.

DAX: Signal strength is dropping. The antenna is rotating with the probe. It was supposed to lock after separation.

FEN: Is the latch stuck?

DAX: It is holding. The alignment sensor is giving us the camera's orientation instead of the antenna's. Cedar thinks it is pointing at the crater, but the lens is pointed at its own foot.

[SFX: STATIC. A TINY MOTOR WHIRS.] 

RIMA: We have the probe camera. It is showing a close-up of a gray wheel.

FEN: That is the foot.

DAX: It has an excellent texture.

RIMA: The camera can wait. Cedar needs a stable angle to sample the dust. I can put the carrier between Cedar and the sun for twelve minutes. We will lose the wide shot but give the probe enough time to take a core.

FEN: That is outside the imaging plan.

RIMA: It is inside the shadow schedule. The plan gave us a clean crater. The probe gave us a foot.

FEN: Understood. Adjusting carrier attitude. Dax, can you guide the antenna from the carrier telemetry?

DAX: I can use the temperature reading. The side facing sun is warmer. Cedar, rotate until the warm side is left of the dark line.

[SFX: FOUR MOTOR TICKS. A PAUSE.] 

CEDAR: Orientation uncertain. Sampling arm ready.

RIMA: It still thinks the camera is the antenna.

DAX: I'll send a pulse. Cedar, do not turn toward the bright point. Turn toward the signal tone.

[SFX: A PITCH DESCENDS. MOTORS TURN THREE TICKS.] 

CEDAR: Signal acquired. Core sample beginning.

FEN: Ground confirms the carrier is in shadow. Cedar's energy draw is stable. We have the sample arm in the dust.

RIMA: The core is sealed. We have a strip of pale soil, a small stone, and what looks like a cedar needle.

DAX: It may be the needle from the probe's own insulation.

RIMA: We will label it before anyone calls it a discovery.

FEN: The team has approved the delayed image. They say the probe's foot can wait until the next pass.

DAX: The fix is simple. We will separate the camera and antenna orientation sensors in the next update. This probe has taught us the machine needs to know what part it is looking at.

RIMA: And the mission needs to know what question it is asking. We came for a sample, not a postcard.

CEDAR: Sample secured. Communications stable.

FEN: Orbit Check Cedar, operation complete. Return to the carrier. You have room for one image.

RIMA: Take the soil core. Leave the foot.

[SFX: DATA BURST, THEN A QUIET SUCCESS TONE.] 

FEN: Image received. It is a very good picture of the foot.

RIMA: We asked for the soil core.

DAX: The core is in the sample locker.

FEN: The locker is in the image background.

RIMA: It is a small locker.

DAX: The foot is a very large foot.

FEN: The science team can crop the frame.

DAX: The probe has a strong sense of itself.

RIMA: Archive it under "engineering reference," not "landscape."

FEN: Copy. Cedar is aboard, sample intact. Next orbit begins in forty minutes. The mission has its core, the sensors have a fix, and the foot has made history by being the first thing we did not intend to photograph.

[STATIC FADES TO SILENCE. END.]
