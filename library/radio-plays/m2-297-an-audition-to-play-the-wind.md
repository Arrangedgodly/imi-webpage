# An Audition to Play the Wind

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio drama casting conventions, actors competing to perform weather, and a Foley artist whose simple props outshine the audition*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
DIRECTOR SERA, a macaque
ACTOR ODO, a baboon
ACTOR LUMA, a lemur
FOLEY ARTIST PIP, a gibbon
ANNOUNCER TAVI, a capuchin

[SFX: STUDIO SLATE, PAPER RUSTLE.]

TAVI: The station is casting a wind sound for its mountain drama. Three performers have arrived.

SERA: We need wind that changes from a light breeze to a storm.

ODO: I have a resonant voice.

LUMA: I can whistle through my teeth.

PIP: I brought a paper sheet and a small fan.

SERA: The audition is for a voice actor.

PIP: Then why does the script say sound effect?

TAVI: The ad called it a voice role.

Odo steps to the microphone and breathes a low rumble. It sounds like a large animal asleep.

[SFX: ODO RUMBLES.]

LUMA: That is not wind. That is my uncle after soup.

ODO: It has atmosphere.

SERA: We need the breeze first.

Luma whistles. The pitch is high and steady. Sera asks her to make it rise and fall. Luma varies the note, but the microphone catches her breathing between phrases.

[SFX: WHISTLE, INHALE.]

TAVI: The wind has lungs.

LUMA: All wind has moving air.

Pip waves the paper sheet near the microphone. It makes a soft rustle, then a loud flap when the sheet catches the stand.

[SFX: PAPER RUSTLE, FLAP.]

SERA: The storm just knocked down a tree.

PIP: The paper hit the stand.

ODO: That was my cue.

Odo rumbles over the paper flap. The mix becomes a low growl and a crackle.

TAVI: The mountain is growling.

SERA: Stop. We need a breeze building into a storm, not a mountain having a disagreement.

Pip moves the paper farther from the microphone and turns on the fan at its lowest setting. The breeze sounds light. He adds a second sheet, held loose, and the sound grows without a flap.

[SFX: SOFT AIR SWELLS.]

LUMA: I can whistle above it.

SERA: Try one note, then let the paper carry the gust.

Luma gives a brief whistle. Pip increases the fan. Odo adds a quiet rumble only as the storm peaks. This time the sounds build in order.

[SFX: BREEZE, WHISTLE, GUST, LOW RUMBLE.]

TAVI: The wind has arrived.

ODO: I played the storm.

PIP: The fan did.

LUMA: The paper did.

SERA: The sound belongs to the mix.

Odo asks for his name in the credits. Luma asks if the paper gets one too. Tavi says the credits can list the Foley performance, the voice, and the recording team.

PIP: The paper can be listed by material.

SERA: It is copy paper from the station.

TAVI: Can we use the same setup for the next scene?

PIP: If no one writes on it.

Luma signs the paper, making it too stiff to rustle. Pip turns it over and uses the blank side.

[SFX: PAPER CRINKLES.]

SERA: That is now a new sound.

ODO: I can do that.

SERA: No, you cannot.

Pip records the breeze and storm as two separate tracks.
 
Sera asks the actors to audition for the credits. Odo insists his rumble should be listed as "mountain wind," while Luma wants "high-altitude whistle."

TAVI: The credits have more names than the sound.

PIP: The paper is listed as Foley.

ODO: I made the storm dramatic.

SERA: You made a low rumble.

LUMA: I made a breeze.

PIP: I made the wind.

SERA: The fan made the breeze, the paper made the gust, Luma added a whistle, and Odo supplied one rumble at the peak.

ODO: That is a long credit.

TAVI: It is still shorter than the audition.

They record the roles into a slate, then listen back. The slate itself is too loud and covers the first sound. Pip moves it away from the microphone and reads the names after the effect instead.
 The director listens, then asks for one final take with no actor breathing into the microphone.

[SFX: FAN LOW, PAPER SOFT, GUST SWELLS AND FADES.]

TAVI: The role of wind has been cast.

ODO: Who got it?

SERA: The wind.

PIP: And the paper.

LUMA: My whistle is available for the sequel.

[SFX: STUDIO SLATE, MIC CLICKS OFF.]
