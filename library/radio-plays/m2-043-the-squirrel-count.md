# The Squirrel Count

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: citizen science call-in tally broadcasts*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Uma, a macaque; field counter Deni, a squirrel monkey; caller Puck, a capuchin volunteer; and a patient crow who keeps landing on the studio antenna.

[SFX: STUDIO BELL. PENCIL SCRATCHES.] 

UMA: Welcome to The Squirrel Count, a live tally of nests along Bellwether's river path. This is not a race. We are counting active nests, not squirrels, and we will not publish the exact tree locations. Deni is at the field station. How many nests have you counted?

DENI, REMOTE: We have thirty-two confirmed. The volunteer board says forty-one.

UMA: Where did the extra nine come from?

DENI: Puck's group counted the same trees from the footbridge.

PUCK, PHONE: We counted the north bank.

DENI: You counted the reflection of the north bank.

PUCK: The water was very convincing.

[SFX: CROW CAWS, CLOSE TO MICROPHONE.] 

UMA: We have a crow at the studio window. Is it part of the count?

DENI: No. Squirrels build nests. Crows borrow the occasional stick.

PUCK: I marked one nest near the big willow.

DENI: Which big willow?

PUCK: The one by the blue bench.

UMA: We asked callers not to give exact locations on air.

PUCK: I didn't say the street.

UMA: You gave three landmarks.

PUCK: Then I'll say less. It was a tree.

UMA: Thank you. Deni, how do volunteers tell a nest from a leaf pile?

DENI: We observe from a distance, wait for repeated signs of use, and record the date. A single squirrel carrying leaves is not proof of a nest. It could be building, repairing, or taking material elsewhere.

PUCK: What if it carries the same leaf twice?

DENI: Then write down what you saw, not what you think it means.

UMA: That is our first counting rule. Caller three asks whether a nest that is empty at noon counts.

DENI: It may. Squirrels move around. We check more than once and compare observations over several days.

UMA: And if two volunteers see the same nest?

DENI: They should share the tree marker and date. One nest, two observations.

PUCK: I have marked the same one twice.

UMA: We can correct the tally.

PUCK: Does that erase my work?

DENI: It improves the count.

UMA: Your name will stay on the volunteer list. Your extra nine will not.

[SFX: PENCIL ERASER, PAPER TEARS LIGHTLY.] 

PUCK: I have a new number. Thirty-two?

DENI: Thirty-two confirmed and four possible. Keep possible separate until we check.

UMA: The total is now thirty-two, with four notes for follow-up. The crow has left the antenna.

[SFX: WINGS FLAP. METAL ANTENNA TWANGS.] 

UMA: The antenna may need its own observation sheet.

DENI: It is not a nest.

UMA: We will wait for repeated signs.

PUCK: The crow is carrying a stick.

DENI: That is not proof.

PUCK: It is now on your roof.

UMA: We are ending the count before the roof becomes a dataset.

[SFX: THEME MUSIC.] 

UMA: Thanks to every volunteer. We will post the corrected tally without locations. Counts are useful when they are careful. A larger number is not automatically a better one.

PUCK: Can I still bring my binoculars next week?

DENI: Yes. Bring the date sheet too.

UMA: And don't count the reflection.

[SFX: CROW CALL FROM DISTANCE.] 


[SFX: A FRESH TELEPHONE LINE OPENS.] 

UMA: Deni, have you merged the two reports?
DENI: Yes. The south-bank observer saw the same nest from a different angle.
PUCK: I have crossed out the duplicate. I kept the date and my initials.
DENI: That is exactly what we need. We can compare observations without exposing the tree.
UMA: The count is thirty-two confirmed, four possible. Next week the team will check the possible nests from the path, with binoculars and no climbing.
PUCK: I can bring the clipboard.
UMA: Bring it closed until you reach the station.
PUCK: And the crow?
DENI: It can keep the antenna. It was never in our count.
