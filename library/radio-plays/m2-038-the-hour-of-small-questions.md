# The Hour of Small Questions

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: children's radio question-and-answer programs*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Uli, a friendly capuchin; Ada, a macaque science teacher; callers Rafi and Mina, young listeners; and Pip, a mynah bird who repeats whichever answer he hears last.

[SFX: BRIGHT XYLOPHONE THEME. A LITTLE DOOR OPENS.] 

ULI: Welcome to The Hour of Small Questions. Today we answer things that bother you while eating breakfast, walking to school, or staring at a puddle. Ada is here from the neighborhood science room. Pip is here because he found the studio door open.

PIP: Door open. Door open.

ADA: We can close it after the show.

ULI: First caller is Rafi. What is your question?

RAFI, PHONE: Why does a wet shirt feel cold even when the sun is out?

ADA: Water takes heat from your skin as it dries. Moving air can carry the water vapor away, so the shirt cools faster. That's why you may feel cold when a breeze reaches wet fur or cloth.

ULI: Would a thicker shirt dry faster?

ADA: Usually a thin layer dries faster because air can reach more of it. But fabric matters too. A towel holds a lot of water. A smooth cloth may let water spread out. You can test two small pieces outside, as long as you use safe fabric and do not leave them where animals can get tangled.

PIP: Wet shirt cold.

ULI: Pip has summarized the science with three words.

[SFX: PHONE LINE CHIME.] 

ULI: Mina asks why some seeds stick to fur.

MINA, PHONE: I got burrs on my sleeve in the field. Do plants do that on purpose?

ADA: Plants do not make plans the way people do. Some seeds have hooks or rough surfaces. They catch on fur or clothing and travel to a new place. When the seed falls off, it may grow there if the soil and weather are right.

ULI: Is it okay to pull every burr off a wild animal?

ADA: Don't handle an animal you don't know. Burrs can be uncomfortable, but an adult or wildlife helper should decide how to remove one safely. You can remove burrs from your own clothes with a comb, slowly, away from skin.

PIP: Burrs travel.

ULI: We are now a travel show for seeds.

[SFX: A PAPER BAG CRINKLES.] 

ULI: We have a question from the studio. Why does a leaf change color?

ADA: In many trees, green chlorophyll helps leaves use sunlight. In autumn, some trees stop making as much chlorophyll. Other colors that were already in the leaf become easier to see. Some leaves also make red pigments. Weather and the kind of tree affect the colors.

ULI: So the leaf is not turning into a different leaf?

ADA: It is still the same leaf. Its colors change as the tree prepares for colder weather and less daylight.

PIP: Leaf is same.

ULI: Pip has learned something.

PIP: Pip is same.

ADA: Also true.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS.] 

ULI: Rafi has a second question. Why do puddles disappear?

RAFI: I saw one after school. It was gone the next day, but no one drank it.

ADA: Some water soaks into the ground. Some runs into drains. Some evaporates and mixes with the air as water vapor. Sun and wind can make evaporation happen faster. A puddle can disappear in more than one way.

ULI: If we put a bowl outside, will it disappear too?

ADA: It can. Mark the water level and check it later. Put the bowl somewhere stable, away from traffic and where it will not collect insects. Ask an adult before leaving anything outside.

ULI: Can we put a lid on it?

ADA: Then the water cannot evaporate as easily. You have changed the experiment.

PIP: Lid on. Water stays.

ADA: Some may still evaporate inside and condense on the lid, so look closely.

ULI: A tiny water journey under a lid.

[SFX: XYLOPHONE NOTE, THEN SMALL APPLAUSE.] 

ULI: Our producer has prepared a prize wheel for the callers.
ADA: We are answering questions, not ranking children.
ULI: The wheel says "Most Curious." Who spins it?
PIP: Pip is same.
ULI: The wheel can go back in the closet. Pip, what did you learn?
PIP: Door open. Wet shirt cold. Leaf is same.
ADA: Good. We have closed the door and answered three questions.
[SFX: A SMALL PRIZE WHEEL SPINS BY ITSELF, THEN STOPS ON "DOOR."]
ULI: The prize goes to the host who stopped making children spin a wheel.
PIP: Door closed.
ULI: We are closing the studio door ourselves.
ULI: Our questions today were about shirts, seeds, leaves, and puddles. If you try an observation, write down what you see and change only one thing at a time. If you are unsure, ask a grown-up who knows the place you are exploring.

PIP: Door closed.

ADA: Did you close it?

PIP: Someone else did.

[SFX: DOOR LATCH CLICKS. THEME MUSIC RETURNS.]
