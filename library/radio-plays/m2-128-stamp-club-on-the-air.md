# The Stamp Club on the Air

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio hobby-club broadcasts, postal-stamp collecting jargon, and a rare stamp that keeps being used to mail the show notes*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Oren, a baboon radio host; Teli, a gibbon philatelist; Mox, a macaque club member; and Jiri, a capuchin station intern.*

[SFX: Radio theme, envelope slides across a table.]

OREN: Welcome to Stamp Club on the Air. Tonight we discuss the rare Blue Fig stamp, printed for one season and then withdrawn because the fig was purple.

TELI: It is valuable because so few were made.

MOX: I have one. I used it on a letter to the station.

OREN: You mailed a rare stamp?

MOX: It needed to travel.

TELI: Stamps are meant to travel on envelopes, but collectors usually preserve them.

JIRI: The letter is here. The stamp came through the post with a cancellation mark.

MOX: Is that bad?

TELI: It proves the stamp was used. It may be even more interesting.

OREN: Please describe the stamp for listeners.

TELI: It shows a fig, a branch, and the year 11. The ink faded at the top.

MOX: It says "Fig 11."

TELI: It says "Fig. II." Roman numerals.

OREN: The label on the envelope says "For Fig Eleven."

MOX: That was the address.

JIRI: The station's fruit delivery box is marked Fig 11.

TELI: Then the letter may have been delivered to a tree.

MOX: It came to the station.

OREN: Our postal route is very efficient.

[SFX: Envelope opens; paper rustles.]

JIRI: The letter says, "Dear Stamp Club, I have found another Blue Fig. Please tell me if it is rare."

TELI: We need to see it.

MOX: It's attached to the letter.

TELI: That's a printed picture, not a stamp.

OREN: The drawing includes a branch and a fruit.

TELI: It is a fig illustration.

MOX: The letter's sender may have seen the stamp on my envelope and copied it.

JIRI: The sender is the fig tree club at the old orchard.

OREN: Is there a club for fig trees?

MOX: The trees have members who write on their behalf.

TELI: Then we should send them a reply.

JIRI: We need a stamp.

MOX: Use the Blue Fig.

TELI: We cannot use the same rare stamp twice.

OREN: The letter's original stamp is already canceled.

MOX: I have a sheet of ordinary stamps.

TELI: Use one of those. The collection stays safe, the reply gets delivered.

[SFX: Stamp peels and sticks.]

JIRI: The stamp is upside down.

MOX: The letter can rotate.

TELI: The post office can read it either way.

OREN: We are sending the club a note: the Blue Fig is rare, the illustration is not a stamp, and the postmark makes the original more interesting.

MOX: Can we display it at the next meeting?

TELI: Yes. In a protective sleeve, not on the outgoing mail.

JIRI: I will put the envelope in the archive.

OREN: Please don't file it under Fig Eleven.

JIRI: I will use the cabinet labeled "Fig, Roman."

TELI: Better to file it by year and country.

MOX: The year is 11, the country is our orchard.

OREN: The Stamp Club will investigate.

[SFX: A stamp press clicks gently.]

TELI: The Blue Fig remains rare, the drawing is a drawing, and the mail is ready.

MOX: I have one more stamp. It's a banana.

OREN: Is the banana purple?

MOX: No. But the stamp says "blue."

TELI: Bring it next week.

OREN: This has been Stamp Club on the Air. Please collect carefully and address your envelopes to the station, not the fruit.

TELI: The stamp club has received a reply from the orchard. The Blue Fig envelope was delivered to the correct club member.

MOX: Who was it addressed to?

JIRI: Fig Eleven, caretaker of the east row.

OREN: So the tree did not receive the letter, but someone responsible for the tree did.

TELI: The postmark shows it traveled through three branches of the postal route.

MOX: That is a remarkable journey for one stamp.

[SFX: Theme fades under paper rustle.]
