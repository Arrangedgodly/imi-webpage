# The Repair Hour Rattle

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: call-in repair shows, expert confidence, diagnostic sound effects, and the moment a simple fix becomes a studio demonstration*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
FIXER NELL, a gibbon host
CATO, a capuchin caller
DREW, a macaque studio assistant

[TOOLBOX OPENS. A WRENCH CLINKS.]

NELL: Welcome to The Repair Hour, where we listen closely before we reach for the largest tool. Cato, what needs fixing?

CATO: My ceiling fan rattles every time I turn it on. It makes a sound like this.

[CALLER MAKES A LONG, BUZZING RATTLE.]

NELL: That could be a loose screw, an unbalanced blade, or a small drum solo. Is anyone standing under the fan?

CATO: My family is in the kitchen.

NELL: Good. Leave it off until you know what is loose.

DREW: I brought our studio fan to demonstrate.

NELL: Is it unplugged?

DREW: Yes.

NELL: Then we can inspect the blades and their fasteners. We will not put our hands near moving parts.

[FOOTSTEPS. METAL CLINKS.]

DREW: One blade is slightly crooked.

NELL: The blade is bent. We should replace it with a matching part, not bend it back while attached.

CATO: I see the problem. My nephew clipped a paper pinwheel to the fan.

NELL: Please remove it only while the fan is off and unplugged.

[CALLER SHUFFLES. PAPER SPINS.]

CATO: It's not on the blade. It's tied to the pull chain.

NELL: That would make a ticking sound, not a blade rattle.

DREW: Our fan is still quiet. We can try a safe test by rotating it by hand while unplugged.

[HAND TURN. A SOFT RATTLE.]

NELL: There's a screw on the housing that has loosened. I'll snug it with the correct screwdriver.

[SCREWDRIVER TURNS.]

DREW: The rattle stopped.

NELL: On Cato's fan, the pinwheel was touching the chain, and a screw was loose. Two sounds had been performing one mysterious duet.

CATO: I tightened the screw with a butter knife before I called.

NELL: Please don't. The wrong tool can slip and damage the screw. Use the right screwdriver, and turn off the power first.

DREW: We have a second caller.

NELL: What needs repair?

CALLER TWO: My drawer sticks. I keep pulling harder.

NELL: First, empty the drawer. Is anything caught behind it?

CALLER TWO: A wooden spoon.

NELL: Lift the drawer slightly and pull it out gently. Don't force it.

[WOOD SCRAPES, THEN DRAWER SLIDES.]

CALLER TWO: It's free. The spoon is wearing a groove in the cabinet.

NELL: The spoon is not a drawer stop. Put it in the utensil jar.

DREW: Both callers solved their problems without buying the enormous wrench.

NELL: The enormous wrench is for our next demonstration.

DREW: What are we repairing?

NELL: The enormous wrench. It has a loose handle.

[WRENCH HANDLE RATTLES.]

DREW: We can use the smaller wrench.

NELL: Correct. The big tool's problem needs a small tool.

[THEME MUSIC.]

NELL: DREW: We have one more call before the close.

CALLER THREE: My toaster clicks twice, but no toast comes up.

NELL: Is it plugged in, and is the lever staying down?

CALLER THREE: It is plugged in. The lever pops right back up.

NELL: Unplug it, let it cool, and check for a crumb jam. Don't put a metal utensil inside.

[TOASTER UNPLUGS. TRAY SLIDES. CRUMBS RATTLE OUT.]

CALLER THREE: The crumb tray was full. I've emptied it and plugged the toaster back in.

[TOASTER LEVER CLICKS, THEN POPS.]

DREW: It still won't stay down.

NELL: Then stop using it and have it checked. A simple repair show should know when to stop repairing over the radio.

CALLER THREE: I'll take it to the shop.

NELL: Excellent. No one needs to open a toaster to solve this call.

That is The Repair Hour: power off, listen first, and check whether a pinwheel is attached to the thing you are trying to fix.
