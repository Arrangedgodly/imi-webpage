# The Biscuit Ledger

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: True crime documentary podcast conventions*
*By: IMI Broadcast Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

[THEME: a single low cello note, followed by a cash register drawer that refuses to close.]

NARRATOR: At 8:17 on a Tuesday, the snack fund was found empty. At Infinite Monkey Industries, every employee is a monkey, the kitchen has three ceiling bars for swinging, and the snack tin is bolted to the counter after last year's enthusiastic tail-swipe. The tin remained on the counter. The lid was on. The label, written in marker, still said FOR EVERYONE. But inside there was one raisin and a receipt for twelve pounds of premium cashews.

MARA, HOST: I'm Mara Finch. I make small offices sound like places where people vanish. This is The Biscuit Ledger.

[DOOR BUZZER. A fluorescent light hums.] 

MARA: Treasurer Len, when did you last see the fund?

LEN: Monday, just before the planning meeting. It had forty-three dollars and a coupon for two-for-one crackers.

MARA: And on Tuesday?

LEN: Empty.

MARA: Did you hear anything?

LEN: A wrapper. Then someone said, "That's a future expense." I thought they meant the copier.

MARA: The phrase would return.

[DRAMATIC THUD, clearly made by a desk drawer.]

NARRATOR: Everyone had access to the kitchen. Everyone had a reason to want a snack. And everyone had been told that the new wellness policy required mindful chewing.

MARA: We spoke to Jo from design.

JO: I did not take the money. I did take a biscuit. It was in the communal biscuit tin. The tin had a different lid. I now understand that lids are evidence.

MARA: You were seen near the counter at 8:15.

JO: I was looking for my mug. It says World's Okayest Illustrator. It had been moved behind the cashews.

MARA: Which cashews?

JO: The expensive ones. They have their own drawer now.

[ARCHIVAL AUDIO: tin clink, footsteps, someone whispering "protein."]

NARRATOR: A second lead came from the shared calendar. At 8:03, a meeting called "Cost Control, Small Treats" appeared, then disappeared. Its organizer was listed as "Snack Fund."

MARA: Our producer found the invite in a deleted-items folder.

PRODUCER ELI: It had no guests, no agenda, and a location: kitchen, under the poster about trust.

MARA: The poster says, "Trust the process."

ELI: Exactly. The process had a hole in it.

[LOW PERCUSSION.]

MARA: We asked operations chief Bev to explain the cashew receipt.

BEV: Those were purchased for a client visit.

MARA: The receipt says Tuesday. The client visit was last month.

BEV: The client was expected to return.

MARA: Did they?

BEV: No. The cashews did.

MARA: Where are they now?

BEV: In a drawer labeled "miscellaneous morale."

MARA: The drawer was locked.

BEV: It sticks.

[KEYS JANGLE. A drawer scrapes open.] 

NARRATOR: Inside the drawer, investigators found the receipt, a napkin folded into a tiny envelope, and a handwritten note: "Borrowed for urgent joy. Will repay after payroll." The note was signed with a smiley face and the letter B.

MARA: Bev, is that your note?

BEV: It could be anyone's B. We employ several people with curves.

LEN: Bev's handwriting makes every B look like it's trying to leave the page.

BEV: I borrowed the money. I planned to replace it. The tin was on a high shelf, so I used my tail to pull it down. I had no idea it was also holding the coupon and a small grape. The office had been tense, and I'd promised a morale treat. Then the client canceled, the cashews sat there, and I forgot the fund was not a bank.

MARA: Why hide the note?

BEV: I put it in the drawer. The drawer locked itself. I was going to confess after the quarterly review.

MARA: That is six weeks away.

BEV: I was hoping morale improved first.

[THEME returns, now played on a kazoo.]

NARRATOR: Bev repaid the forty-three dollars before lunch. The office voted to replace the tin with a transparent jar and a paper ledger. Jo donated biscuits. Len bought crackers with the coupon. The premium cashews were opened at the next meeting, where everyone agreed they were too salty to justify the suspense.

MARA: The case is closed. The drawer is not. Bev has been assigned to clean it, and the label now reads FOR EVERYONE, WITH RECEIPTS.

[THE CASH DRAWER SNAPS SHUT. END MUSIC.]
