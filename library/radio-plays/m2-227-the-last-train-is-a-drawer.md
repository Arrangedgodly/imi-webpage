# The Last Train Is a Drawer

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: suspenseful railway announcement drama, where station staff treat a stuck timetable drawer like a departing train*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Station announcer VELA, gibbon; clerk MIRI, macaque; porter ODO, baboon; traveler PIRI, capuchin.

[SFX: STATION CLOCK. DISTANT TRAIN WHISTLE.] 

VELA: Attention, travelers. The last train leaves at dusk. The timetable drawer is currently unavailable.

PIRI: Is my train unavailable?

MIRI: No. The drawer is.

ODO: It contains the timetable.

PIRI: Then how do we know which train is mine?

Vela leans close to the microphone.

VELA: The timetable drawer has not opened since morning. We must remain calm.

MIRI: It is stuck because Odo stored a box of whistles on top.

ODO: They are not all whistles. One is a bottle opener.

PIRI: Could the timetable be posted somewhere?

MIRI: It is posted beside the drawer.

PIRI: The sign says "see timetable."

VELA: We are working on a second sign.

Odo lifts the whistles. The drawer slides open an inch, then stops with a groan.

[SFX: WOODEN DRAWER GROAN.]

VELA: The drawer has responded.

MIRI: It has jammed on a paperclip.

ODO: I have a railway key.

PIRI: Does it open the drawer?

ODO: It opens the signal cabinet. I brought it for confidence.

Miri asks Piri where he is going. He says across the hill to deliver a scarf to his aunt before dusk. Vela announces his departure as if calling a royal procession.

VELA: Passenger Piri, carrying one scarf and a determined expression, will board at platform two.

PIRI: There is only one platform.

VELA: The announcement needs scale.

Miri removes the drawer's handle and finds the paperclip wedged in the track. Odo offers his bottle opener.

MIRI: That will bend the wood.

ODO: The opener has a delicate touch.

It slips. The drawer shuts harder.

[SFX: DRAWER SLAMS.]

VELA: We have lost the timetable.

PIRI: It was still in there?

MIRI: Yes.

PIRI: Then I am missing the train without knowing which train.

Vela starts announcing a delay. Miri stops her and pulls the timetable from the gap beneath the drawer. It is folded, but readable.

MIRI: Here. The last train leaves at dusk. Platform one.

PIRI: How do I know which track reaches my aunt's village?

MIRI: We only have one track.

PIRI: The sign says "track two."

ODO: That sign is the reverse side of "track one."

VELA: The station has been saving money on signs.

PIRI: And what if the train leaves before dusk?

MIRI: Then it would not be the last train. It would be an earlier train.

VELA: I admire the confidence of that sentence.

Odo listens at the platform edge. A distant clatter grows louder, then stops.

ODO: That was the luggage cart.

PIRI: It sounded like a train.

ODO: The cart has aspirations.

Miri checks the posted schedule and tells Piri the train is ten minutes away. Piri looks at the stuck drawer as if it might have delayed it.

PIRI: We only have one platform.

VELA: Platform one has the best view of the whole station.

Odo uses the railway key's flat end to lift the drawer rail. Miri frees the paperclip. The drawer slides open smoothly.

[SFX: DRAWER OPENS, SMALL TRIUMPHAL CHIME.]

VELA: The timetable drawer is now operational. The last train remains scheduled for dusk.

PIRI: I can make it.

MIRI: Take the scarf. The train is not going to wait for the announcement.

Vela begins a dramatic farewell. Piri runs for the platform; his footfalls recede.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS, THEN TRAIN WHISTLE.]

ODO: He caught it.

MIRI: We don't know. The whistle could be a bottle opener.

Odo tests his opener. It makes a tiny squeak.

VELA: The train has departed. The drawer remains open for arrivals.

[SFX: DRAWER ROLLS SHUT.]

MIRI: Attention, travelers. The drawer is now the only thing leaving on time.
