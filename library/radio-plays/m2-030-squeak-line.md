# Squeak Line

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Call-in repair hotlines and remote diagnosis shows*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Host Tern, a macaque repairer; Caller Pali, a gibbon toy maker; Caller Wu, an orangutan bicycle courier; assistant Mox, a capuchin apprentice.

[THEME: TOOLBOX OPENS, A SPRING TWANGS, PHONE RINGS.] 

HOST TERN: You're listening to Squeak Line, the only repair hotline where callers describe a sound and our host asks them to make it again. I'm Tern. We do not repair live animals. If your pet is squeaking, call a vet.

CALLER PALI: My wooden toy train goes "clack-clack-clack" and then stops.

TERN: Is the train on a track?

PALI: It has one.

TERN: That's not the same as being on it.

PALI: It is on the table.

TERN: Put it on the track and roll it slowly. What do you hear?

[SFX: WOODEN WHEELS ROLL, CLICK, STOP.] 

PALI: One wheel catches at the seam.

TERN: Check the axle. Is there thread wrapped around it?

PALI: Yes, blue thread.

TERN: Pull it out with tweezers, not your teeth. Then roll the train again.

[SFX: THREAD PULLS FREE. TRAIN ROLLS SMOOTHLY.] 

PALI: It works. The thread came from my scarf.

TERN: Keep the train away from the scarf drawer. A toy does not need a wardrobe.

MOX: Unless it is a doll.

TERN: Then the doll needs one coat, not a closet.

[PHONE BEEPS.] 

TERN: Caller two. What has broken?

WU: My bicycle makes a squeak when I pedal uphill. It goes away when I pedal faster.

TERN: Is the squeak in the front or back?

WU: The back. I can hear it behind me.

TERN: A useful clue. Can you turn the bike over and spin the rear wheel by hand?

WU: I am an orangutan. I can lift the bike, but the handlebars are wide. I need a stand.

MOX: We have a repair stand in the studio.

TERN: You are at your home.

WU: I am in the station parking lot. I came to ask the hotline in person.

TERN: We are on the radio. The hotline is a telephone.

WU: The sign outside said call-in repair show. I called in.

TERN: Fair enough. Bring it inside. Mox, hold the frame.

[SFX: BIKE LIFTS, CHAIN TURNS, A DRY SCRAPE.] 

MOX: The chain is rubbing the guard.

TERN: The guard is bent inward. We can nudge it back with a wooden handle. No metal tool near the chain.

WU: Will that hold on the hill?

TERN: We can test it on the stand, then you test it on level ground. Don't race downhill until the wheel spins freely.

[SFX: HANDLE TAPS METAL. WHEEL SPINS, QUIET.] 

WU: No squeak.

TERN: Now pedal slowly.

[CHAIN CLICKS ONCE.] 

MOX: A new sound.

TERN: The chain skipped. It needs cleaning and tension. That is a second repair, not a reason to ride harder.

WU: I can clean it at home. I thought the squeak meant the wheel was falling off.

TERN: The wheel is fine. The chain needs attention. We have checked the sound and found the part.

MOX: The toy train is rolling. The bicycle is quiet. The repair stand is full.

TERN: That's because the caller parked his bike in it.

WU: I can move it.

TERN: Good. Leave room for the next caller. We have a toy boat with a loose mast and a wooden spoon that thinks it's a kazoo.

MOX: That spoon is musical.

TERN: It is cracked.

WU: Can I stay and listen?

TERN: Yes, after you put the bike on the floor and not across the doorway.

TERN: This has been Squeak Line. Describe the sound, test one thing at a time, and stop if the repair needs a tool you don't have. The train is mended, the courier can finish his route after chain care, and the spoon will have its own segment.

[TOOLBOX CLOSES. A WOODEN SPOON HUMS WHEN BLOWN. END.]
