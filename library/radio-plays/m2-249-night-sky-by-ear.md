# Night Sky by Ear

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur astronomy broadcasts, portentous celestial alerts, and hosts who confuse the planetarium projector with a new star*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-009*

CAST
HOST SENA, a capuchin
ASTRONOMER VEL, a macaque
JAN, a gibbon night custodian
CALLER IRI, a ring-tailed lemur

[SFX: SOFT SPACE MUSIC. PROJECTOR HUMS.]

SENA: Welcome to Night Sky by Ear. We describe the heavens for listeners who cannot see the observatory window. Tonight, astronomer Vel will identify a bright new point of light.

VEL: It is not new. It is Venus.

JAN: The projector shows three Venuses.

VEL: One is Venus. One is a reflection. One is a ceiling lamp.

SENA: We have a caller. Iri, what do you hear?

[SFX: PHONE CLICK.]

IRI: A low hum and someone breathing through a straw.

VEL: That is the projector fan.

JAN: The straw is my tea.

SENA: Could the hum be a signal from the planet?

VEL: It could be a fan.

SENA: We will not dismiss the possibility.

[SFX: PROJECTOR CLICKS. ROOM GOES QUIET.]

JAN: The light is gone.

SENA: The new star has vanished.

VEL: The projector bulb burned out.

JAN: I have a replacement.

[SFX: LADDER OPENS. TOOLBOX CLANKS.]

SENA: While we wait, Vel will describe the constellations.

VEL: The Big Dipper is a group of stars that looks like a ladle.

SENA: What does it sound like?

VEL: It is too far away to make a sound.

IRI: I hear a drip.

[SFX: WATER DRIPS INTO A CUP.]

JAN: The roof is leaking above the window.

VEL: That is closer than the stars.

SENA: We have a local celestial event.

JAN: It is a leak.

VEL: The cup is catching it.

[SFX: DRIP, DRIP, PING.]

SENA: The cup has a clear tone.

VEL: It is a metal cup.

IRI: The tone is rising.

JAN: The water is filling it.

[SFX: CUP PINGS HIGHER.]

SENA: We will chart the pitch.

VEL: We will move the cup.

[SFX: CUP SCRAPES ACROSS TABLE. DRIP MISSES, HITS FLOOR.]

JAN: Now it is charting the floor.

SENA: The leak has shifted position.

VEL: We can fix it with a tarp and a bucket.

SENA: Does the tarp affect our view of Venus?

VEL: The roof does.

IRI: My audio guide has made the dripping sound a star in its quiz.

SENA: What are the answer choices?

IRI: Venus, a kettle, or an underground moon.

VEL: It is a leak falling into a cup.

SENA: That is not one of the choices.

JAN: I can add it. The quiz screen is on the desk.

[SFX: KEYBOARD TAPS.]

JAN: Now the quiz says: identify the object by its pitch, then move it before it overflows.

IRI: I got the answer right, but the cup still filled.

VEL: That is because the quiz does not repair roofs.

SENA: We have discovered an interactive educational feature.

JAN: I will move the cup under the other drip.

[SFX: CUP SLIDES. DRIP LANDS WITH A LOWER PING.]

IRI: It sounds like Saturn.

VEL: It sounds like a different part of the floor.

SENA: We will accept both answers and give the prize to the cup.

JAN: The cup has won enough water for one night.

[SFX: LADDER, TARP UNFURLS, PROJECTOR RETURNS.]

JAN: The bulb is back.

SENA: The new star has returned.

VEL: It is still Venus.

IRI: I can hear the projector again.

SENA: Is that Venus?

IRI: No, it is the fan.

[SFX: FAN HUMS, THEN CUP PINGS.]

SENA: The observatory has presented us with two distinct sounds: a planet we cannot hear and a cup we can.

VEL: That is a useful summary.

JAN: The cup is full.

[SFX: CUP LIFTED. DRIP STOPS.]

SENA: The leak is contained for tonight. Jan has placed the cup beneath it; the roof crew will repair it tomorrow. The stars remain distant.

VEL: And Venus is still where it was.

SENA: Good night from the observatory.

[SFX: SPACE MUSIC OUT, PROJECTOR HUM CONTINUES.]
