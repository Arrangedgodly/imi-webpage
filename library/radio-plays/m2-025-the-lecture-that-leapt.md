# The Lecture That Leapt

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: University lecture recordings and academic jargon*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Professor Odo, an orangutan primatologist; student Suri, a squirrel monkey; student Kef, a gibbon; archivist Mae, a lemur.

[SFX: LECTURE HALL HUM, A CHALK STICK SNAPS.] 

PROFESSOR ODO: Welcome to "The Social Geometry of a Missed Leap." Today we will examine why a monkey jumps, where the monkey lands, and who gets blamed when the branch moves. Please note the four-part model on your handout. I have made it six parts since last week.

SURI: The handout has four boxes.

ODO: The additional parts are conceptual.

KEF: Is that why the final box says "other"?

ODO: "Other" is a rich category.

[SFX: CHALK SCRAPES. STUDENTS SHIFT.] 

ODO: A leap is not merely a distance event. It is a decision made under conditions of visual uncertainty, branch flexion, social witness, and what I call pre-landing hesitation.

SURI: Is pre-landing hesitation the moment before you decide not to jump?

ODO: It can be. It can also be the moment after you jump and realize the branch is occupied.

KEF: What if the branch was occupied before you jumped?

ODO: That is the fourth part. I will explain it after the break.

SURI: The fourth part is on the handout.

ODO: The handout is not the lecture.

[SFX: RECORDING DEVICE BEEPS. ARCHIVIST MAE SPEAKS FROM THE BACK.] 

MAE: The microphone is recording. Please state your name before asking a question, so the archive can distinguish the student from the professor.

ODO: I am Professor Odo.

MAE: We know. The labels are for the future.

ODO: The future can infer from context.

MAE: It has not done so reliably.

ODO: Let's conduct a live demonstration. Suri, would you cross from this low branch to the platform?

SURI: The branch is thin. I would test it first with my back foot and use the lower rail.

ODO: Good. What does that tell us about agency?

SURI: That I have hands and feet.

ODO: That is one interpretation.

SURI: It is a measurable interpretation.

ODO: Fine. Kef, demonstrate the high route.

KEF: I can swing to the platform, but I won't. The support rope is frayed.

ODO: The rope was inspected yesterday.

KEF: By whom?

MAE: The inspection form says "Professor Odo."

ODO: I inspected it before class.

KEF: You looked at it from the floor.

ODO: I assessed the general condition.

SURI: That's not the same as checking the fibers.

[SFX: STUDENTS MURMUR. ROPE CREAKS.] 

ODO: Mae, please pause the recording. We should not let a lecture rope become a research finding.

MAE: I can mark a short interruption. The recorder has a button for that.

ODO: Excellent. The class will inspect the rope together. Kef can reach it, Suri can test the lower tie, and I will hold the ladder.

KEF: The fray is near the platform hook. This route is unsafe.

ODO: Then the demo is canceled. The concept remains.

SURI: The route is part of the concept.

ODO: You are right. I had built a theory around a jump without asking whether it should happen.

MAE: That is a good line for the recording. Can you say your name first?

ODO: Professor Odo, correcting my lecture. A safe route depends on the branch, the landing, and the animal choosing whether to go. We will repair the rope before the next class.

KEF: That fits in four boxes.

ODO: I will keep the fifth as an optional footnote.

MAE: Please define optional.

ODO: It means you may leave it blank.

SURI: Then we have learned something.

ODO: The lecture is over. Please return the chalk to the tray and do not test the rope on your way out.

[SFX: CHALK DROPS INTO TRAY. RECORDING CLICKS OFF.]
