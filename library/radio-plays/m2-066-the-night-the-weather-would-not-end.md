# The Night the Weather Would Not End

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio weather bulletin interrupted by increasingly specific local reports*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: FORECASTER ELI, a lemur; PRODUCER NOMA, a gibbon; FIELD VOICE RUX, a macaque; and callers from Mossbank.

[SFX: WEATHER JINGLE, WIND THROUGH A VENT.] 

ELI: Good evening. The forecast for Mossbank is partly cloudy, with a chance of rain near the creek and a chance of hearing the word "chance" more than once.

NOMA: The station has extended our bulletin by six minutes.

ELI: Is there more weather?

NOMA: The sponsor is late. We need weather to cover the gap.

ELI: We can say the sky is staying where it is.

NOMA: That is not an update.

ELI: It was not expected to move indoors.

[SFX: PHONE LINE OPENS.] 

RUX: This is Rux at the south trail. The cloud is over the ridge, the path is dry, and my sandwich is wrapped.

ELI: Thank you. Any wind?

RUX: My fur is moving.

ELI: Direction?

RUX: Toward my ears.

NOMA: Can you give us a more urgent report?

RUX: The sandwich is still wrapped.

ELI: Listeners, conditions are stable.

NOMA: We cannot have six minutes of stable conditions.

ELI: Then we can report on the barometer.

NOMA: It is a jar with a twig in it.

ELI: The twig is pointing up.

NOMA: It's a twig.

[SFX: ANOTHER LINE OPENS, THEN A DISTANT KNOCK.] 

CALLER: I hear rain on my roof.

ELI: Is the roof outside?

CALLER: Yes.

ELI: Then the rain is localized.

NOMA: Ask whether it is heavy.

CALLER: It sounds like one drop.

ELI: A brief shower has passed through one roof.

NOMA: That's not meteorology.

ELI: It is a direct observation.

NOMA: The station clock says we still have four minutes.

ELI: We can report on the temperature.

NOMA: The thermometer is missing.

ELI: Where was it?

NOMA: Near the open window.

ELI: Then we can report on the breeze.

NOMA: The breeze has stopped.

ELI: It has concluded its local visit.

ELI: The thermometer will have to wait until morning. We are not sending a second field reporter after it.

RUX: I can hear you from outside. Is the window open?

NOMA: Yes.

RUX: Then the breeze is in your studio.

ELI: We have a listener report from the north side of our own window.

NOMA: Close the window.

ELI: If we close it, we may miss the next change.

NOMA: That is not a reason to leave the room open all night.

RUX: The cloud has moved. I can see stars.

ELI: Thank you. The sky has provided an update without paperwork.

NOMA: The sponsor is ready now.

ELI: The forecast has outlasted the delay.

NOMA: The delay has outlasted the forecast.

ELI: Either way, we can close the window.

[THE WIND IN THE VENT STOPS. THE ROOM IS QUIET.] 

NOMA: We have lost the wind sound.

ELI: Perhaps the wind moved.

NOMA: Can we make another one?

ELI: We could open the studio window.

NOMA: Then it will be actual wind.

ELI: The weather has agreed to participate.

[WINDOW OPENS. LEAVES RUSTLE OUTSIDE.]

RUX: The cloud is clearing over the south trail. I can see the ridge.

ELI: Is your sandwich still wrapped?

RUX: No. I have eaten it.

ELI: A sharp change in local conditions.

NOMA: Please do not call lunch a weather event.

ELI: It was the only forecast we could verify.

NOMA: The sponsor is ready.

ELI: Wonderful. Our bulletin can end on time.

NOMA: They want one more minute.

ELI: Then, for the next minute, the weather will continue outside.

[THE STUDIO CLOCK TICKS. THE WINDOW REMAINS OPEN.]

ELI: That is the forecast. Bring a wrap if you plan to eat one.

ELI: The path is dry, the roof is wet, and our studio is finally still.

[SFX: WEATHER JINGLE. WIND THROUGH THE OPEN WINDOW, THEN A STUDIO DOOR CLOSES.]
