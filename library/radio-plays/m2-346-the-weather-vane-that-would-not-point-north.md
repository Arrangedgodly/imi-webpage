# The Weather Vane That Would Not Point North

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: community weather broadcasts that turn a stuck instrument into an optimistic forecast*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-012*

Cast: Forecaster Zela, a langur; engineer Bop, a capuchin; producer Timo, a macaque; gardener Una, a lemur.

[SFX: ROOFTOP WIND. A METAL VANE CREAKS, THEN TAPS A PIPE. STUDIO MICROPHONE OPENS.]

ZELA: Good morning from the rooftop weather desk. Our arrow points east, which means today's wind is arriving from the east.

BOP: It means the vane is stuck.

TIMO: Can we say the wind is being unusually decisive?

UNA: The garden flags below point west.

ZELA: The flags are not certified instruments.

BOP: The vane is bolted to its stand with a strip of last night's sponsor banner. The plastic has wrapped around the bearing.

TIMO: The banner says Bright Day Cereal. That is excellent brand placement.

UNA: It's hiding the arrow.

[SFX: A FLAG FLAPS BELOW. THE VANE TAPS THE PIPE THREE TIMES.]

ZELA: Let's do the forecast first. High clouds, light wind from the east, excellent conditions for kite practice.

BOP: We cannot tell direction until the vane turns freely.

TIMO: We have already announced east. Changing it now sounds uncertain.

UNA: The wind has changed the flags twice while we have been talking.

ZELA: Perhaps it is an east wind with westward moments.

BOP: That is not how wind direction works.

TIMO: Can the technician make it point east for the broadcast? Then it will at least agree with us.

BOP: I can remove the banner and see whether the bearing moves. I am not going to force it toward a sponsor word.

[SFX: LADDER FEET SET ON THE ROOF. BOP CLIMBS THREE RUNGS. A TOOL BAG ZIPS.]

ZELA: We are now showing the brave ascent of our engineer.

BOP: You are on radio. No one can see me.

TIMO: We can add a climbing sound effect.

BOP: I am already climbing. Don't add one.

UNA: The banner is flapping around his wrist.

BOP: I have it. The plastic has cinched around the spindle. I'm cutting the loose strip, not pulling on the arrow.

[SFX: SCISSORS SNIP. BANNER RUSTLES DOWN. THE VANE SPINS FREELY, WHIRLING IN A GUST, THEN SETTLES WITH A CREAK.]

ZELA: It points north.

UNA: The flags and the grass are streaming south now.

BOP: The arrow is pointing into the wind. It shows where the wind comes from. The flags trail away from it.

TIMO: So east was right for the last gust?

BOP: The arrow was stuck. The wind is from the north now.

ZELA: Forecast update: the rooftop instrument was obstructed by a banner, and the current wind is from the north.

TIMO: Could you say sponsored by Bright Day before the obstruction?

UNA: The banner is under Bop's boot.

[SFX: BOP STEPS BACK ONTO THE ROOF. HIS BOOT NUDGES THE BANNER; IT SKIDS ONTO THE LADDER'S TOP RUNG.]

BOP: It's wrapped again. This time it is not on the vane.

ZELA: Is the ladder still secure?

BOP: Yes. I am on the roof. The banner is caught on the top rung and I can lift it clear.

TIMO: We could say the brand is reaching new heights.

UNA: Please let him finish before you turn the weather into an advertisement.

[SFX: FABRIC LIFTS FREE. IT FLAPS OFF ACROSS THE ROOF AND LANDS AGAINST A WATER BARREL.]

BOP: The vane is clear. The banner is out of the mechanism. I am folding it and putting it inside the barrel.

ZELA: A final check: the arrow turns when the wind shifts, and it rests north when there is no cross gust.

[SFX: A SHORT GUST. THE VANE ROTATES, THEN SETTLES.]

UNA: North again.

TIMO: Good. Can we keep the phrase excellent conditions for kite practice?

ZELA: Not today. The wind is gusty, and the kite string is tangled in our flags.

[SFX: A KITE FLUTTERS BELOW. A STRING TWANGS AGAINST THE ROOF RAIL.]

UNA: That's my garden kite. Its string is caught on the rail beside the wind gauge.

BOP: I'll lower it after I come down. The readings are finished.

TIMO: So the broadcast ends with a kite on the roof, a banner in a barrel, and a corrected forecast.

ZELA: It ends with the wind from the north and gusts strong enough to tangle a kite. That is what we know.

[SFX: MICROPHONE CLOSES. THE VANE CREAKS ONCE, FREE AND QUIET.]
