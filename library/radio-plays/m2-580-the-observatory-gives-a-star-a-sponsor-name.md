# The Observatory Gives a Star a Sponsor Name

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public astronomy broadcasts, naming rights, and astronomers asked to read advertisements into the sky*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-020*

Cast: Host Piri, a capuchin; astronomer Vela, an orangutan; telescope operator Kito, a macaque; sponsor rep Daro, a baboon; student Nessa, a gibbon.

SFX: Observatory dome. Telescope motor turns; paper rustles.

PIRI: Welcome to Skywatch Live. Tonight we observe the bright point above the western ridge. Our sponsor would like to call it the Star of Daro Hardware.

VELA: It already has a catalog number. It is not available for naming.

DARO: We only want to sponsor the viewing. The poster says "Meet Daro's Star."

KITO: The telescope is pointed at a bright planet, not a star.

PIRI: The sponsor wants to revise the poster.

DARO: "Meet Daro's Planet" sounds less poetic.

NESSA: Is it a planet or a star? I brought my class to find out.

VELA: It is Venus. It reflects sunlight and moves against the stars over time. It does not produce its own light.

PIRI: Can we say "Venus, presented by Daro Hardware"?

VELA: Yes, as long as we don't imply the sponsor owns it.

DARO: Could the star chart show our logo beside it?

KITO: I am showing the wide sky-chart preview first; it includes the Moon and Venus. The projector overlays labels. I can place a small sponsor tag in the corner without covering the planet.

VELA: Only on the broadcast graphic. The sky stays as it is.

SFX: Projector clicks; a label appears.

PIRI: On the wide chart, the logo is covering the Moon.

KITO: I will move it below the chart, then change to the close telescope view of Venus.

DARO: The horizon line is not visible in the telescope image.

NESSA: Could the label stay on the screen border? I am trying to see Venus.

PIRI: Good suggestion. Kito, put the sponsor line at the bottom, away from the view.

SFX: Keyboard taps; label moves.

KITO: Done. Venus remains centered. The logo is below the image.

VELA: Now give the students a clear view. We should check the focus before reading any sponsor copy.

SFX: Telescope wheel turns slowly; the operator switches from the wide chart to the telescope close view.

NESSA: I see a bright crescent.

VELA: That is Venus through the filter. The crescent shape comes from the angle of sunlight.

DARO: Does the planet have a name in the local language?

VELA: Many cultures have names for it. We can discuss those in the program, with sources, without replacing them with a store name.

PIRI: The sponsor read is due now. Daro, please say the telescope is supported by your shop.

DARO: Daro Hardware supports tonight's public viewing. Our screws hold shelves, not planets.

KITO: That is accurate.

PIRI: Could you add the store hours?

DARO: Nine to five, except tonight. We are open late for pickup after the observatory viewing.

NESSA: Do I need to buy something to use it?

DARO: No. The viewing is free. The shop lends the folding chairs and keeps its pickup counter open late for people collecting supplies after the program.

VELA: That is a useful sponsor message. The telescope belongs to the observatory. Now let's return to the observation.

SFX: Telescope follows a quiet motor track; dome turns.

KITO: A cloud is crossing the target. I will pause the tracking motor while the image is obscured; when it clears, I will reacquire Venus from the updated ephemeris.

PIRI: The live audience is asking whether the planet disappeared.

VELA: It is behind cloud, not gone. We will describe what the instrument sees and wait.

DARO: Could we use the logo to cover the cloud?

KITO: No. A logo cannot improve the weather.

NESSA: I can still see the stars around it.

VELA: Keep looking. The cloud is thinning on the western edge.

SFX: The dome settles; the telescope resumes tracking.

KITO: Cloud has cleared. I have reacquired Venus and corrected the tracking coordinates; focus is sharp again.

PIRI: The broadcast chart updates, and the sponsor line stays at the bottom.

DARO: I would like a copy of the chart for the shop window.

VELA: You may have the public chart. Please leave the catalog number and planet name visible.

DARO: Agreed. I do not want customers asking for a star by my hardware aisle.

NESSA: Can we see Saturn next week?

KITO: If the weather is clear, Saturn is above the horizon, and the observatory confirms the telescope is available, yes. We will announce the viewing once those checks are complete.

PIRI: We have a second sponsored viewing, then.

VELA: We have a second planet. Sponsorship is separate.

FINAL SIGNOFF: Venus remains Venus, the logo stays beneath the image, and the night sky keeps all its names.
