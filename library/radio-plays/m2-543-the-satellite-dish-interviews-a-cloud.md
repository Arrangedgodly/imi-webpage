# The Satellite Dish Interviews a Cloud

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: celebrity interview formats that anthropomorphize routine signal interference*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Host Vex, an orangutan; technician Mara, a macaque; operator Odo, a gibbon; intern Pip, a capuchin; weather reader Nari, a lemur.

SFX: Hilltop relay hut. Wind brushes the metal dish; a monitor beeps softly.

VEX: Tonight we interview the cloud that has interrupted our satellite signal.

MARA: We do not interview clouds. We can explain why the signal meter dips when a storm cell passes.

ODO: The receiver is aligned. The dish is secured and the cable is dry.

PIP: I brought the weather printout. The cloud band is moving east, away from the relay.

NARI, over radio: Rain should reach the valley after sunset. The hilltop is clear right now.

VEX: The cloud is playing hard to get.

MARA: The meter is steady. The equipment is receiving a signal.

SFX: Beep drops; brief static.

VEX: It has entered the studio.

PIP: The audio dropped when you leaned on the cable.

VEX: I did not touch the cable.

ODO: Your coat is caught under the cable guard. Lift it carefully, please.

SFX: Fabric slides; beep returns.

MARA: There is the cause. The connector stayed seated, but the cable was pressed against the table leg.

VEX: The cloud has made a demand.

PIP: The coat did. Move your chair to the marked side, so the cable stays clear.

Odo places a small guard over the wire and tapes its route along the wall. Mara checks the meter without adjusting the dish.

ODO: Signal is stable. No need to climb outside or point the dish at the cloud.

VEX: We have lost the guest.

MARA: We never had one. Nari, can you give the forecast update?

NARI: The cloud band is east of the hill and still moving away. The valley forecast remains light rain around eight.

VEX: I had prepared questions about its childhood.

PIP: Ask the weather reader about the forecast, then.

VEX: Nari, how did the cloud form its ambitions?

NARI: Warm air rose over the valley. The moisture condensed. There are no ambitions on the chart.

MARA: The forecast is about rain timing, not the cloud's inner life. Listeners need to know whether to cover the garden beds.

NARI: Rain begins around eight. It should last less than an hour. Secure loose covers before dusk, and don't leave tools outside.

PIP: I'll put that on the community board after the broadcast.

VEX: Does the cloud have a message for the audience?

NARI: The weather station has a message: bring in the watering cans if they are empty, so they don't fill with rain.

SFX: Mara turns a page; wind lowers.

MARA: That is the useful update. Odo, read the signal one last time.

ODO: Stable. Cable route clear, dish alignment unchanged.

VEX: The guest declined to speak, but the technology has shared a monologue.

MARA: The signal check is finished. Keep the forecast card ready while Pip reads the studio indicators.

SFX: Monitor clicks to standby. Wind remains outside.

PIP: The sponsor light is blinking, but the signal meter is steady. It is the small amber indicator, not the receiver alert.

VEX: Could that be the cloud's second message?

MARA: It is the sponsor board. Keep the two readings separate; one is the receiver and one is our studio equipment.

ODO: I can switch off the board. It is not needed during a weather update.

SFX: Switch clicks; amber hum stops.

VEX: Can the amber light make a weather statement?

MARA: No. It is the sponsor board indicator. We turned it off; the receiver reading stays on its own meter.

PIP: I'll log the receiver reading separately from the forecast time.

NARI: Rain is still expected around eight. That is the forecast; the cloud has no microphone.

VEX: My guest has no voice, no ambitions, and no sponsorship.

MARA: We have enough for the report. End on the weather, not the lamp.
