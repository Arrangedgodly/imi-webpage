# The Last Leaf in the Weather Vane

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: nature documentaries that turn common behavior into grand survival sagas*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-011*

Cast: Narrator Pava, gibbon; field scientist Miri, macaque; sound recordist Odo, baboon; groundskeeper Nara, capuchin.

[SFX: WIND THROUGH LEAVES.]

PAVA: At dawn, one dry leaf clings to the weather vane.

MIRI: It is caught on the wooden arrow.

ODO: I am recording the wind.

NARA: It will fall when I clean the vane.

ACT ONE

Pava narrates the leaf's struggle against the breeze. Miri says the vane points east. Odo records a gust, but his microphone catches Nara sweeping the path.

PAVA: The sound of winter approaches.

NARA: It is a broom.

Pava asks Miri if the leaf has a chance. Miri says it will likely fall when the wind shifts. Pava describes the leaf as "testing the edge of its world." Nara asks if the program can wait until after she cleans.

ACT TWO

Odo lowers the microphone closer to the vane. The leaf rattles. Pava narrates the rattle as a warning. Miri says the weather vane is rotating because the breeze changed direction.

[SFX: WOODEN VANE CREAKS.]

The leaf slips but catches under the arrow. Pava calls it a narrow escape. Nara reaches for it with a soft brush, but Pava asks her to pause for a close-up. Nara says the leaf is not a performer.

Miri explains that the vane has a notch where leaves collect. Nara slides the brush beneath the leaf, but a gust pushes it back under the arrow. Odo records the rattle.

ACT TWO-B

Miri records the vane turning in the breeze. The arrow swings from east to north, while the leaf stays pinned under its wooden tip. Pava narrates each direction as a journey. Nara asks him to keep the microphone away from the path so she can sweep safely. Odo compares the rustle of the broom with the rattle of the leaf. The broom makes a soft brush; the leaf makes a dry click against the vane. Miri explains that the vane's notch catches lightweight debris. Pava asks if the leaf has adapted to its environment. Nara says it is stuck. Pava refuses the simple word and calls it "temporarily sheltered." The wind turns the arrow. The leaf slips halfway out. Odo moves the microphone closer, but Miri pulls him back before he bumps the post. Nara uses the brush, not her hand, to free the leaf without climbing or shaking the vane. It drifts to the path and lands beside the swept pile.

Nara holds the leaf next to the vane so Miri can show where it caught. The notch is barely wide enough for one dry stem. Pava says the leaf found a shelter, then lost it when the wind changed. Nara places it in the compost basket, where it will stay out of the path. Odo records the soft thump. The wind turns the arrow again, but this time the vane moves freely.

Pava asks whether the leaf should have a name in the field log. Nara suggests "leaf." Pava tries "last leaf of the vane." Miri says it is the only leaf there today. Odo records the simple name, then carries the compost basket back to the shed.

ACT THREE

Pava records a final field note: the leaf was caught in the vane's notch and has been brushed into the compost basket. Miri says the weather vane now turns freely. Odo plays the sound once: a dry click, a sweep, then the basket's soft thump.

NARA: It will break down with the other leaves.

PAVA: A quiet return to the earth.

ODO: One basket. No second journey.

TAG

A second leaf lands on the vane.

PAVA: The cycle begins again.

NARA: I just swept that.

[SFX: BROOM SWEEPS.]
