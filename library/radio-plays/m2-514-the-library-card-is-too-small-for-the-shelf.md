# The Library Card Is Too Small for the Shelf

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: children's learning programs that turn simple access problems into counting games*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-018*

Cast: Librarian Mara, a macaque; presenter Vex, an orangutan; reader Pip, a capuchin; helper Odo, a gibbon; producer Seli, a lemur.

SFX: Children's library. Books slide across a shelf.

VEX: Today's lesson is how to borrow a book.

PIP: My card won't fit in the return slot.

MARA: That's a book slot, not a card slot.

ODO: The card tray is on the desk, behind the big dictionary.

VEX: We can start with shapes. What shape is a library card?

PIP: A rectangle. I know because it has four corners and my name.

SELI: The book Pip wants is on the top shelf.

PIP: It's the one with the moon rabbits.

MARA: I'll bring the stool.

VEX: Before you climb, count the steps.

PIP: One step on the stool. Then the shelf.

SFX: Stool legs scrape against the floor.

ODO: The stool rocks on the rug.

MARA: Don't climb yet. Odo, can you hold the rug edge while I move the stool onto the flat boards?

SFX: Rug pulls clear; stool settles.

VEX: This safety delay is not in the lesson plan.

MARA: It is in the room.

PIP: I can reach the book if it is pulled forward.

SFX: Book scrapes; the stack wobbles.

MARA: Stop pulling. The atlas behind it is supporting the row.

ODO: I'll set the atlas on a cart while Mara steadies the shelf.

SFX: Atlas shifts onto a cart. The moon-rabbit book slides forward.

PIP: I have it.

SELI: The spine says "Moon Rabbits at Home."

VEX: The program has demonstrated rectangles, counting, and teamwork.

MARA: It has demonstrated that this shelf needs a lower copy of the book.

PIP: Can I take this one today?

MARA: Yes. Now the check-out tray.

SFX: Dictionary lifts; card tray slides out.

PIP: I can't reach it from here.

VEX: We can count the inches between the desk and the reader.

MARA: Or move the tray to the low shelf beside the desk.

ODO: I'll clear that space. The picture books are light enough to move together.

SFX: Books transfer to a small cart. Tray slides onto the lower shelf.

PIP: I can reach it standing on the floor.

MARA: Put your card in the tray.

SFX: Card slides into a low tray.

ODO: I can read the name and scan the number.

VEX: The card is still a rectangle.

PIP: And it fits where it belongs.

MARA: I'll stamp the date on the slip inside the book. Bring it back next Friday.

SFX: Date stamp presses into the paper. Receipt tears from the pad.

PIP: I can keep the receipt inside the cover.

ODO: The due date is large enough to read without opening the book.

VEX: We could count the days until return.

PIP: I can put Friday on my calendar. I don't need a countdown for each day.

SELI: The check-out tray has a picture of a card now. The book-return picture stays by the taller slot.

MARA: We should add a note that a grown-up can help with the return box.

ODO: I'll place a small step stool beside it and keep the aisle clear.

SFX: Stool legs settle against the wall, away from the walking path.

PIP: I can carry the book safely without climbing.

VEX: The program has one more learning objective.

MARA: It's called arranging a library so readers can use it.

SELI: The book return slot is higher than the check-out tray.

PIP: I'll bring the book back with Mara, and we can ask someone to lower the return box too.

ODO: I'll write that on the maintenance list, not the borrowing lesson.

VEX: Our final question: what comes after borrowing?

PIP: Reading the moon-rabbit book.

SFX: Page opens; a paper moon rustles.

MARA: That's a good answer.
