# The Lost and Found Hour

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: call-in radio show for misplaced community objects*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST FARA, a lemur; CATALOGUER NIM, a squirrel monkey; CALLER DEX, a macaque; and CALLER VEE, a capuchin.

[SFX: RADIO THEME, DRAWER SLIDING OPEN.] 

FARA: This is The Lost and Found Hour. If you have misplaced something, describe it clearly. If you have found something, please do not give it a new name until we know what it is.

NIM: We have one item on the table: a curved stick with a polished end.

FARA: Is it a walking stick?

NIM: It is too short.

FARA: A spoon?

NIM: It is too thick.

FARA: We will let the caller decide. Dex, what did you lose?

DEX: A stick I use to reach fruit on the high shelf.

NIM: What does it look like?

DEX: Long, straight, and a little bent near the end.

FARA: This stick is short and curved.

DEX: That could be it after someone shortened it.

NIM: I did shorten it. It was blocking the drawer.

DEX: You cut my fruit stick to fit the lost-and-found drawer?

NIM: I thought it was abandoned.

FARA: The first lesson of lost property is to ask before modifying it.

NIM: I have added that to the label.

[THE DRAWER OPENS. ITEMS RATTLE.] 

NIM: We also have a single blue bead, a dry leaf, and a small cup.

VEE: I lost a cup.

FARA: Describe it.

VEE: Small, blue, and round.

NIM: This cup is brown and has no handle.

VEE: It might look blue in moonlight.

FARA: Can you identify the bottom?

VEE: It has a scratch shaped like a crescent.

[THE CUP TURNS. A LIGHT TAP.] 

NIM: There is a scratch. It is shaped like a root.

VEE: That is the one. I use it for water when I sit by the east tree.

FARA: The cup has been found. The bead?

NIM: No one has claimed it.

DEX: I can use it as a marker for my tool shelf.

NIM: You cannot claim an item just because it is useful.

FARA: Is that the rule?

NIM: I just made it up.

FARA: Then let's ask listeners. If the bead is yours, call in and describe it. Until then, it stays here.

FARA: We have another caller. They found a dry leaf beside the water dish and think it may belong to someone.

NIM: What color is it?

CALLER: Brown.

NIM: That describes most dry leaves.

CALLER: It has a bite mark.

FARA: That also describes many leaves.

NIM: We need a more useful detail. Was it used as a bookmark, a roof patch, or a snack?

CALLER: It was folded into a little cup.

FARA: A leaf cup! Does anyone recognize it?

DEX: I made one to carry water to the low branch. It leaked, so I left it by the dish.

NIM: The cup is found. It has been flattened now.

DEX: That's fine. It was not a very good cup.

FARA: Do you want it back?

DEX: No. I have learned that leaves are better at holding water when they are still on a tree.

NIM: I can mark it for compost.

FARA: Ask first.

NIM: Dex, may I compost the leaf cup?

DEX: Yes.

[LEAF DROPS INTO A COMPOST BIN.] 

FARA: Our caller has retrieved the idea, if not the object.

NIM: The found-property drawer is getting organized.

FARA: We have not found the owner of the blue bead.

DEX: It may belong to the bead dish.

NIM: The dish is not missing.

FARA: We'll leave them both here until someone asks.

[THE BEAD ROLLS IN A SMALL DISH AND STOPS.] 

DEX: My stick is still too short.

NIM: I can help you find a new one from the branch pile.

DEX: I can choose one. You can label it after.

FARA: The lost item is not always the item we need. Sometimes we need a better process for finding it.

NIM: The drawer is full. I should sort the items by where they were found.

FARA: Sort them by the day first. Then we can call their owners.

NIM: And the bead?

FARA: It stays in the dish.

[SFX: THEME MUSIC. THE BEAD ROLLS ONCE, THEN SETTLES.]
