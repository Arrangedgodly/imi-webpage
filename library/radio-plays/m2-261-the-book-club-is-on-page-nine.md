# The Book Club Is on Page Nine

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live radio book clubs, dramatic readings, and panelists who argue over a sentence before anyone finishes the chapter*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-009*

CAST
HOST LOMA, a capuchin
READER FENN, a macaque
CRITIC RUE, a gibbon
CALLER SAI, a ring-tailed lemur

[SFX: RADIO THEME. PAGES TURN.]

LOMA: Welcome to Branch Marks, the live radio book club. Tonight we are discussing The Lantern Under the Stairs. Fenn will read the opening paragraph.

FENN: The lantern waited beside the stair, although no one had asked it to.

RUE: I hear a book about consent.

LOMA: We are one sentence in.

RUE: A sentence can contain a whole staircase.

FENN: The next line says the keeper came downstairs to find the lantern.

LOMA: Why did the lantern wait?

RUE: We must not assume it wanted company.

FENN: The keeper says, "You can come with me if you like."

LOMA: Now we have an invitation.

RUE: Or pressure from the person holding the stairs.

[SFX: PHONE LINE CLICKS.]

LOMA: Our caller Sai has read the chapter. What did you think?

SAI: I thought the lantern was waiting because it was lit.

FENN: The text says it was unlit.

SAI: Then perhaps it was waiting to be lit.

RUE: That changes the whole first act.

LOMA: Is there an act structure?

RUE: There is a staircase. The author has made a choice.

FENN: Page two reveals the keeper's name: Orla.

LOMA: Is Orla the lantern?

RUE: We cannot rule it out.

SAI: The book says, "Orla carried the lantern up the stairs."

FENN: The keeper and lantern are separate nouns.

RUE: Nouns can disguise a relationship.

LOMA: Before we move on, let's hear from listeners who have only read the cover.

[SFX: PHONE BUTTON BEEPS.]

SAI: I have read page nine.

LOMA: We are on page two.

SAI: I skipped ahead. Page nine says the lantern was returned to the cupboard.

RUE: Returned by whom?

SAI: Orla.

RUE: The keeper has taken it back. A powerful ending.

FENN: We have not read the middle.

LOMA: Can you summarize the middle, Fenn?

FENN: Orla walks upstairs. She meets a mouse. They look in a cupboard. Then the chapter ends.

RUE: The mouse is the true keeper.

SAI: There is no mouse on the page nine summary.

RUE: That is how you know it matters.

LOMA: We should read the text ourselves instead of inventing missing chapters.

FENN: The library copy is missing pages three through eight.

[SFX: PAGES FLUTTER.]

LOMA: The book club has become an investigation.

RUE: I can call the library.

SAI: I have the copy. The missing pages are stuck together at the binding.

FENN: Can you separate them carefully?

SAI: Yes. Page three: Orla asks the mouse to carry a matchbox.

RUE: The mouse is a courier.

SAI: Page four: the mouse carries the matchbox upstairs.

RUE: A courier with an upward trajectory.

FENN: Page five says Orla lights the lantern and thanks the mouse.

LOMA: Then the lantern waited because it needed a flame.

RUE: And the mouse delivered the means.

SAI: Page nine says Orla puts it away after the stairs are lit.

LOMA: We have read the full arc. The lantern waits, the mouse brings a match, and Orla returns it to the cupboard.

RUE: I revise my interpretation. The book is about returning borrowed things.

SAI: The last page says Orla will lend the lantern to the next keeper.

RUE: So it is a sequel after all.

LOMA: A sequel with a responsible checkout process.

FENN: It is also about reading all the pages.

LOMA: Our discussion concludes with one final line from the chapter.

FENN: "The lantern waited quietly for the next evening."

RUE: A sequel hook.

LOMA: We will not speculate until it is published. This has been Branch Marks. Please bring all pages next week.

[SFX: BOOK CLOSES. THEME OUT.]
