# The Echo That Took the Wrong Trail

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: hiking safety radio programs that turn practical route calls into overly polished adventure narration*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-012*

Cast: Ranger Lio, a macaque; host Veya, a gibbon; hiker Paku, a capuchin; dispatcher Rell, a lemur.

[SFX: MOUNTAIN WIND. RADIO STATIC. A HANDHELD RADIO CLICKS.]

VEYA: You are listening to Trail Talk, broadcasting live from the ridge. Our guest hiker is approaching the famous Echo Notch.

PAKU: I'm at the junction sign. It says the notch is closed because a rockfall covered the trail.

LIO: Correct. Use the lower path back to the ranger cabin.

VEYA: The notch is known for returning a call three times.

PAKU: I called once. Something answered, "turn left."

RELL: The radio monitor played that back.

LIO: The repeater at the notch is delayed. It repeats the last transmission over the lower channel.

VEYA: That sounds like an unusually helpful echo.

PAKU: It sent me toward the closed path.

LIO: Stay at the sign. I am walking up from the cabin.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON GRAVEL. WIND THROUGH PINE NEEDLES. A RADIO BEEPS.]

VEYA: Our listeners are hearing a dramatic rescue in progress.

LIO: It's a short walk to a sign. There is no rescue.

RELL: I can switch the repeater to the return channel.

PAKU: I hear you twice now.

RELL: That's my test. Sorry. The repeater has two delay settings and both are enabled.

VEYA: Could you call it a double echo?

LIO: We should call it a faulty setting. Paku, can you read the sign aloud so I know you are at the correct junction?

PAKU: "Echo Notch closed. Lower trail to cabin, one hundred meters."

LIO: Good. Stay on this side of the marker. Don't take the left fork.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC, THEN PAKU'S WORDS REPEAT: "DON'T TAKE THE LEFT FORK." A SECOND TIME, LOUDER.]

PAKU: The radio just told me not to take the left fork after I already heard it.

VEYA: The echo is confirming the instruction.

LIO: It is repeating. It isn't confirmation.

RELL: I found the cause. Someone routed the outgoing channel into the return input. I can mute the return while leaving the ranger channel active.

LIO: Do it from the cabin. Don't change settings while you are walking.

[SFX: BUTTONS CLICK OVER RADIO. A TONE SOUNDS. THE ECHO STOPS.]

PAKU: I hear one voice now.

VEYA: One voice from the ridge.

LIO: Paku, turn toward the painted blue arrow and follow it down.

PAKU: I see the arrow. It points to a trail marker, not a left fork.

LIO: Right. The marker is on the right-hand path.

VEYA: We are watching him choose the safe route.

PAKU: You cannot see me on the radio.

VEYA: We can hear your boots.

[SFX: TWO FOOTSTEPS, THEN A STONE SKIDS DOWNHILL.]

PAKU: One loose stone. I'm stepping around it.

LIO: That's the rockfall edge. Keep to the painted side.

RELL: The repeater is now transmitting one time only. I have a test call from the cabin.

LIO: Go ahead.

RELL: Ranger cabin to ridge junction. Do you hear this once?

PAKU: Once.

LIO: Good. I am at the marker now. We can walk down together.

VEYA: The hiker and ranger meet at the junction. The mountain has given us a lesson in echoes.

PAKU: The mountain did nothing. Your repeater repeated the same sentence twice.

VEYA: That's a more accurate ending.

RELL: And Trail Talk will stop using the repeater as a sound effect.

VEYA: The station has an effects library. I could play a mountain echo under the ranger's directions.

RELL: That is the library echo, not the repeater. It would make a second copy on every receiver.

LIO: Keep the channel open for the hiker. We have no reason to decorate the instruction.

[SFX: A WIND GUST BLOWS A PAPER MAP. PAKU CATCHES IT AGAINST HIS JACKET.]

PAKU: The map tried to take the lower trail without me. I have it now.

VEYA: Our guest has recovered the map, and the ranger has the correct route.

[SFX: TWO SETS OF FOOTSTEPS DESCEND THE LOWER TRAIL. RADIO CLICKS OFF; WIND REMAINS.]
