# The Postcard From the Volcano Arrives Early

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio travel bulletins, dramatic geological updates, and a postal service that delivers bad news out of order*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-020*

Cast: Host Ana, a gibbon; geologist Kito, an orangutan; postmaster Tavi, a macaque; island guide Boro, a baboon; caller Miri, a capuchin.

SFX: Coastal radio desk. A gull cries; paper slides across the table.

ANA: Our field desk has received a postcard from the volcano, dated tomorrow. It says, "The mountain is warm, the village is calm, and the post office has moved uphill." Geologist Kito, is this a warning?

KITO: It is a postcard. The volcano does not write.

TAVI: The card came through the island mail bag this morning. It has no sender, only a stamp from the summit postbox.

BORO: The postbox is on the ridge. It is not a post office. It is a wooden box for hikers.

ANA: Why is the card dated tomorrow?

TAVI: The ink ran when the rain hit. The date might say today.

KITO: The seismic monitor shows ordinary low-level activity. I will not call it an eruption forecast from a postcard.

SFX: Radio static, then a warning tone.

ANA: We have an alert.

KITO: That is the station's weather warning for lightning.

BORO: The lightning is over the harbor. The ridge is clear.

MIRI, on phone: I am at the village post office. The sign says we have moved uphill.

TAVI: The old sign was taken down for repairs. I painted a new one and left it leaning against the summit postbox instead of mounting it.

ANA: Why does it say the post office moved?

TAVI: Because the sign is upside down. It says POST OFFICE MOVED UPHILL when read from the back.

BORO: The post office is still in the village. The trail postbox is uphill.

ANA: We have two locations and one sign.

KITO: Before the radio turns this into a mountain evacuation, let me give the actual monitoring report. There is no elevated tremor. We have routine steam from the eastern vent, and the public trail remains open.

ANA: Can we confirm the postcard's route?

TAVI: The postmark is from the ridge box. The stamp says COLLECTED 16:00. The card arrived before dawn because our mail truck makes the island loop overnight.

MIRI: Then why is it dated tomorrow?

TAVI: The writer drew a little sun with a face. It looked like a one. The date is 10/2, not 10/3.

ANA: The postcard is from today, not tomorrow.

BORO: I am hiking to the postbox now. I can bring the sign back to the village and put it on the right way around.

KITO: Stay on the marked path and call from the ridge shelter. The rain may return.

SFX: Footsteps, then a door closes. Radio crackle.

BORO: I am at the shelter. The sign is here, and I can read it from the front. It says POSTBOX UPHILL. The post office is not moving.

ANA: And the mountain?

KITO: Still showing ordinary activity. We have three monitoring points and no unusual change.

TAVI: The postcard's message continues on the reverse. It says, "Tell the office the box is full of cards from hikers who all ask whether the post office moved."

MIRI: I wrote that. The sign was facing the trail, and everybody asked me.

ANA: Why mail the question to the post office?

MIRI: I wanted to see if it would arrive before the next person asked.

TAVI: It did. The mail truck reached town at five this morning.

KITO: This is not a volcanic signal. It is a sign problem with excellent delivery.

BORO: I have carried the sign down. I am turning it so the trail side reads POSTBOX UPHILL and the village side reads POST OFFICE HERE.

SFX: Wooden sign flips and slots into a post.

TAVI: I can confirm both sides from the radio desk. The arrows point in opposite directions, correctly.

ANA: The public trail notice will say the route is open. The post office remains in the village. Geologist, please repeat the monitoring report, so no one hears only our first dramatic sentence.

KITO: Routine steam, no unusual tremor, no eruption warning. Follow official trail signs and the weather notice.

MIRI: I have another postcard.

ANA: From the volcano?

MIRI: From the lighthouse. It says the mail truck arrived early.

TAVI: That one is probably true.

ANA: We will not issue a geological alert for a punctual truck.

SFX: Station bell, one clear note; paper tucked into a mail slot.

FINAL SIGNOFF: The mountain is calm, the signs now face the right way, and the post office is exactly where its address says.
