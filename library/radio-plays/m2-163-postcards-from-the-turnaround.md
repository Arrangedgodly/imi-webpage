# Postcards from the Turnaround

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: travel radio features, breathless destination descriptions, and a host who insists every stop is a hidden gem*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
YARA, a capuchin travel host
SOL, a gibbon bus guide
MEL, a macaque caller

[TRAVEL THEME WITH UKULELE. BUS ENGINE IDLES.]

YARA: Welcome to Postcards from the Turnaround, our series on places worth visiting even if they are on your usual route. Today: the scenic bus turnaround at Fern Hill.

SOL: The bus makes one complete circle and returns to the same stop.

YARA: Sol, describe the view.

SOL: There is a hedge, a bench, and a sign that says "No standing in the turnaround."

YARA: A hidden gem! The hedge offers an intimate encounter with local greenery.

SOL: It's six feet long.

YARA: Compact, yet immersive. The bench provides a perfect vantage point for watching the bus approach and leave.

[BUS AIR BRAKES.]

SOL: That is the bus approaching.

YARA: And now it departs. A moving panorama!

SOL: The same panorama just moved past us twice.

[PHONE LINE OPENS.]

YARA: We have a caller from the area. Mel, what makes Fern Hill special?

MEL: The bus is the only one that goes to the market. The turnaround keeps it from backing into the hedge.

YARA: You've discovered the destination's secret history.

MEL: It isn't secret. The driver told everyone before we got on.

YARA: Can you recommend a local experience?

MEL: There's a bakery three blocks away. They make raisin rolls.

YARA: A culinary stop. Sol, can we add it to our itinerary?

SOL: We have seven minutes before the next bus. If we walk quickly, we can get a roll and return.

YARA: We'll take our listeners with us through vivid description. Step off the bus. Feel the gentle breeze.

[BUS DOORS OPEN. WIND.]

SOL: That's exhaust from the bus.

YARA: A warm, urban breeze. We pass a fence, turn left at a mailbox, then cross by the shop.

MEL: You missed the bakery. It's on the right.

YARA: We are exploring the less obvious route.

SOL: We're walking past it.

YARA: The bakery has a red awning. We can see it from here.

MEL: That's the laundromat. The bakery has a yellow awning.

[BUS HORN.]

SOL: The next bus is boarding.

YARA: Our travel feature must include a departure, even if the destination is within walking distance. We have two options: take the bus around again, or cross the street and buy the roll.

MEL: The crosswalk is right here. We can go when the signal changes.

YARA: A local custom. We wait for the little walking figure.

[PEDESTRIAN SIGNAL BEEPS. FOOTSTEPS CROSS.]

SOL: The bakery is open. The rolls are warm.

YARA: The flavor is rich, with a delicate raisin finish.

MEL: That's cinnamon. The raisin is in the middle.

YARA: Every bite reveals another district.

[BUS ENGINE STARTS.]

SOL: We should head back. The bus leaves in one minute.

YARA: Our excursion has been complete: one loop, one crosswalk, and a pastry. The turnaround was never the destination; it was how the destination stayed safe.

MEL: I'll be getting off at the market.

YARA: The market stop is two loops away. We can guide you by sound: first comes the bakery bell, then the market gate, then Sol says "this is not the turnaround."

SOL: I say that because people keep asking if the hedge is the market.

MEL: It does look like a hedge.

YARA: And now the bus has arrived. Our field recording captures the doors, the ramp, and one passenger saying, "I've been here before."

SOL: That passenger lives on Fern Hill.

YARA: Familiarity is the finest travel souvenir.

YARA: Listeners can ride with Mel. Ask for the Fern Hill route, then look for the yellow awning.

SOL: And don't stand in the turnaround.

YARA: This has been Postcards from the Turnaround. Our next feature visits a bench that faces a slightly different hedge.

[UKULELE THEME. BUS PULLS AWAY.]
