# Night Sky Hotline Holds Forever

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: call-in science hotlines, long hold music, and expert guests forced to answer questions before callers reach the line*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: An astronomer opens a live night-sky hotline, but the callers are stuck in the station's queue while the host keeps answering their questions on hold music.

Cast: Host Neli, a macaque; astronomer Tavi, an orangutan; board operator Varo, a baboon; caller Peli, a capuchin; caller Rumi, a gibbon.

[SFX: HOLD MUSIC, THREE NOTES, LOOPING.]

NELI: We are taking questions about tonight's meteor shower.

VARO: Caller one is on hold.

TAVI: I can hear them through the hold music.

PELI: I have been waiting to ask if the bright point is a planet.

NELI: It may be. We will take your question live.

ACT ONE

The hotline is open, but the switchboard places every caller into a queue. Varo says the station only has one live line. Neli asks callers to stay on hold while Tavi checks the sky chart. The music loops twice. Peli asks whether the sound is part of the astronomy program. Neli says it is the phone system.

RUMI: I called to ask about a moving light.

TAVI: If it blinks steadily, it may be an aircraft. If it moves slowly and stays steady, it may be a satellite. We need details.

VARO: Caller two is still on hold.

Neli reads the next question before Varo can connect the caller: "What is the bright object near the moon?" Tavi says it could be a planet, but the station needs the date, direction, and time to identify it. Neli asks if he can answer from the music. Tavi says the three-note loop gives no astronomical information.

ACT TWO

Varo tries to transfer Peli to the live line. The hold music continues underneath. Peli says she sees a bright point moving west and then disappearing behind a cloud. Tavi asks whether it blinked. Peli says no. Rumi says she saw a similar point from the hill. Neli announces "a possible satellite," but Tavi asks them not to confirm until they compare the time and direction.

TAVI: The light could be a satellite, aircraft, or something else. The observation alone is not enough.

NELI: We do have two witnesses.

TAVI: We have two reports, not a plotted path.

Varo puts Rumi on the line. Her call has a three-second delay; she answers questions before Neli asks them. Neli asks if the point blinked, and Rumi says "no" while Neli is still saying the word "point." Varo tries to mute the hold music but cuts off the astronomer's mic instead.

NELI: I have lost the expert.

TAVI: I am still here. The phone system has muted me.

ACT TWO-B

Varo routes the callers one at a time, without hold music. Peli describes a steady light that crossed the western sky in under a minute. Rumi gives a similar time but says her view disappeared behind the hill rather than a cloud. Tavi checks the chart and identifies a satellite pass for that time, visible from both locations.

TAVI: That is likely the satellite. The reports match its predicted track.

NELI: And the meteor shower?

TAVI: Still expected later. A satellite is not a meteor.

Rumi asks whether a meteor can be predicted to the minute. Tavi says showers have a broad peak, but individual meteors appear unpredictably. Neli asks Varo to remove the hold loop; the station has now played it nine times. Varo replaces it with a single tone, then puts the callers back in sequence.

Peli asks one final question: can she photograph the meteor shower with her phone? Tavi says she can try a wide exposure from a dark, safe location, but she may not capture anything. Neli asks if listeners should stare at the screen while walking. Tavi says no; look up while standing safely.

ACT THREE

The hotline takes the last two callers live. There is no hold music. Tavi explains the difference between the satellite and the meteor shower, and Neli repeats the expected time window without promising a sighting. Varo confirms the queue is empty. Rumi says the satellite is already gone from her view. Peli says the sky is still clear.

Tavi invites listeners to report observations with time and direction. The station will compare them after the broadcast. Neli does not label any new light before checking. Varo switches off the phone system's looping music and leaves the line open only long enough to thank the callers.

VARO: That was the last loop.

NELI: It has been identified.
