# Clicker School for the Curious

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio pet-training advice segments, reward timing, and a capuchin learner who treats every click as permission to climb*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Brin, a lemur training-show host; Doti, an orangutan trainer; Kex, a capuchin learner; and Salla, a macaque caller.*

[SFX: Training whistle, one click, leaves rustle.]

BRIN: Welcome to Clicker School, where we teach useful habits with a clear sound and a small reward. Doti, demonstrate the click.

DOTI: Click means the behavior was right. Then reward immediately.

[SFX: Click.]

KEX: [CLIMBS ON A STOOL.]

DOTI: We are not teaching climbing.

KEX: You clicked.

BRIN: What behavior did you reward?

DOTI: I clicked to test the sound.

KEX: I heard permission.

DOTI: That's why timing matters.

SALLA: My young macaque learned to open the fruit cupboard. We clicked when she shut it, but now she opens and shuts it repeatedly.

DOTI: The click must mark the behavior you want. Don't click while the cupboard is open.

SALLA: We clicked when it was closed.

BRIN: How many times?

SALLA: Every time she closed it. She has practiced for hours.

DOTI: Then she has learned a very reliable closing behavior.

SALLA: She also expects a raisin every time.

BRIN: That sounds correct.

DOTI: We can gradually reduce rewards after the habit is established.

KEX: [CLIMBS DOWN, THEN LOOKS AT THE CLICKER.]

DOTI: No click for looking at the clicker.

KEX: I was waiting for the sound.

BRIN: Our next exercise: teach a monkey to return a sponge.

DOTI: Place sponge on floor. Say "bring." Click when it is picked up, then reward when it is placed in your hand.

[SFX: Sponge drops.]

KEX: [PICKS UP SPONGE AND CARRIES IT TO THE FRUIT TABLE.]

DOTI: The sponge is not fruit.

KEX: I was bringing it to the table.

DOTI: Bring it to my hand.

KEX: Which hand?

DOTI: The open one.

KEX: Both are open.

BRIN: There are two targets.

DOTI: I will hold one hand closed. Kex, bring the sponge.

[SFX: Click.]

KEX: [PLACES SPONGE IN DOTI'S HAND.]

DOTI: Good. Here is a raisin.

KEX: I can do it again.

DOTI: One repetition is enough for this segment.

KEX: [DROPS SPONGE.]

BRIN: He is asking to repeat.

DOTI: He is testing whether the click happens automatically.

SALLA: My cupboard learner does that too. She shuts the door, waits, then opens it again.

DOTI: Don't reward the waiting. Reward the exact action you want, and keep the cue consistent.

BRIN: Kex, what does the click mean?

KEX: That I did the behavior Doti asked for, not that I may climb the studio.

[SFX: Click.]

KEX: [LOOKS AT STOOL.]

DOTI: That was a test click.

KEX: Then I will stay on the floor.

BRIN: Excellent learning.

DOTI: He deserves a raisin.

KEX: I stayed on the floor.

DOTI: Correct.

[SFX: Raisin offered; Kex takes it and puts the clicker on the stool.]

BRIN: We have trained our learner to leave the clicker alone.

KEX: Only because you put it out of reach.

DOTI: We will count that as environmental design.

SALLA: And I will move the cupboard latch higher.

BRIN: Clicker School ends with a clear cue, a timely reward, and no unauthorized climbing.

DOTI: We will finish with one cue: "down."

KEX: [CLIMBS DOWN FROM THE STOOL.]

DOTI: Good. The cue describes the movement, not the end of the lesson.

KEX: I heard "down," and I went down.

SALLA: My cupboard learner could use that clarity.

DOTI: Give one cue, mark the right behavior, then reward. Don't add a new word halfway through.

[SFX: Click. Kex looks at the stool, then keeps both feet on the floor.]

BRIN: No click was given for staying. The habit can rest.

[SFX: Clicker clicks once; feet remain on floor.]
