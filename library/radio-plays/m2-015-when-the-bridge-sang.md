# When the Bridge Sang

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Archival history specials and public-memory documentaries*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Ona, a mandrill archivist; Daro, a gibbon historian; Mina, a capuchin descendant; Tess, a langur apprentice heard on tape; Nami, her aunt and bridge builder.

[ARCHIVE TAPE: WIND ON WIRES, A DISTANT BELL, TAPE HISS.]

HOST ONA: This is The Shelf of Years. I'm Ona, a mandrill archivist. Tonight we open a box marked "First Canopy Bridge, 1932." The label says it was built by a famous engineer. The recording inside says something else.

ARCHIVE VOICE, TESS: Rope is ready. The crossing is ready. I am not ready to be the first one across.

ONA: The voice belongs to Tess, a young langur apprentice. Her name does not appear on the plaque. The bridge connected the lower orchard to the north grove. Our guest is historian Daro, who spent six years tracing the original crew.

DARO: The official history names one engineer, a baboon named Hux. He drew the first plan. The field notes list twenty-seven other workers, including Tess and her aunt, Nami.

ONA: Why did the plaque leave them out?

DARO: The ceremony program had room for one name. Later histories copied the program. One neat sentence became the whole story.

[ARCHIVE TAPE: ROPE PULLED TIGHT. A WOODEN PLANK GROANS.] 

ONA: We have another recording. This is Nami testing the bridge. She crosses with a basket of stones, pauses at the middle, and asks someone to move the north anchor.

DARO: The anchor was set in loose soil. Hux's sketch missed that. Nami found the problem by walking the span with weight on her back.

ONA: The bridge was changed before opening?

DARO: Yes. The whole crew shifted it toward the rock ridge. Tess tied the side cables, and the orchard troop carried the planks. Hux revised the plan. He was a good engineer. He did not build it alone.

ONA: Who preserved these recordings?

DARO: Nami's daughter. She stored the reel in a flour tin. The archive labeled it "kitchen sounds" and shelved it for seventy years.

ONA: We invited the bridge crew's descendants to hear the tape. One of them, Mina, recognizes the voice.

MINA: That's my grandmother. She used to hum the rope check before every crossing. I thought it was a song she made up.

ONA: Can you hum it for us?

[MINA HUMS FOUR NOTES. A SECOND VOICE JOINS, THEN A THIRD.] 

MINA: It means pull, listen, lean, wait. The rope answers in a different pitch when it is tight.

DARO: The maintenance log records those four checks. The engineer's name is in the log too, but so are the weavers, haulers, and the monkey who kept the kettle boiling.

ONA: The bridge is still standing. We walked it this afternoon. The north anchor sits in stone now, exactly where Nami asked them to move it.

MINA: My grandmother brought food across that bridge for forty years. She used to say the planks remembered every foot.

ONA: The museum has agreed to update the plaque. It will list the crew and keep Hux's name. The archive will also attach Tess's voice to the catalog, so the recording no longer sits under "kitchen sounds."

DARO: That label made the tape easy to ignore. The bridge made it impossible to forget.

ONA: History often survives in the wrong box. We found the builders because a family kept a recording after the official story had no space for them.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON WOOD. THE BRIDGE HUMS WITH A LOW, RESONANT NOTE.] 

MINA: Pull, listen, lean, wait.

ONA: The updated plaque goes up tomorrow. We will read every name aloud at the opening, then cross together. The kettle will be there too.

ONA: This has been The Shelf of Years. The bridge is not a monument to one clever hand. It is a working road, held up by a crew. Good night.

MINA: I brought the kettle.

DARO: Is it the original one?

MINA: No. The original kettle was used until the handle fell off.

ONA: Does the archive have it?

DARO: We do. It is filed under "miscellaneous metal."

MINA: Then it should go beside the bridge tape.

ONA: It has no recording.

MINA: It has a dent where Tess tapped the beat.

DARO: That is a better label.

ONA: We will update the shelf tomorrow.

MINA: Put "kettle, used for tea" on the card.

DARO: Can I write "historically resonant kettle"?

MINA: No.

ONA: Good. A clear label at last.

DARO: The archive has a stamp for that.

[ARCHIVE TAPE CLICKS OFF. FOUR NOTES HUM AGAIN. END.]
