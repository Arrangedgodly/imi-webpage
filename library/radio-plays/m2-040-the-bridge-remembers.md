# The Bridge Remembers

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: local oral history and anniversary radio documentaries*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Arin, a macaque; Dossa, a retired baboon ferry operator; Kel, a young gibbon sound editor; and callers who remember the old footbridge.

[SFX: WATER UNDER A WOODEN BRIDGE. FOOTSTEPS, THEN A MICROPHONE TAP.] 

ARIN: Welcome to Remember This Place, a special broadcast from the new footbridge at Reed Bend. The bridge opened this morning. We are joined by Dossa, who worked the ferry crossing for thirty years. Before we begin, Kel, can you hear me?

KEL: I can hear you. I can also hear a microphone tapping the railing.

ARIN: That may be history.

DOSSA: It is the wind moving your stand.

[SFX: METAL STAND CLINKS.] 

ARIN: Tell us what the crossing was like before the bridge.

DOSSA: The ferry carried people, baskets, lumber, and one goat that refused to face the river. It was slower than a bridge, but it ran in all weather except when the water got too high.

ARIN: Were you frightened?

DOSSA: Sometimes. The river is not impressed by your schedule. You learn to watch its surface and listen for branches before you decide to go.

ARIN: The anniversary brochure calls the ferry a symbol of progress.

DOSSA: It was a boat.

ARIN: I will edit the brochure.

[SFX: PAPER FOLDS.] 

KEL: I found an old recording from the ferry booth. The date is on the reel, but the speaker names are missing.

ARIN: Let's hear it.

[SFX: ARCHIVAL TAPE HISS.] 

YOUNG DOSSA, RECORDING: The east rope is frayed. Do not pull it until the carpenter comes.

UNKNOWN VOICE: I can pull it gently.

YOUNG DOSSA: Gentle is not a kind of rope.

[SFX: TAPE STOPS.] 

DOSSA: That was me. I had less gray fur.

ARIN: And less patience?

DOSSA: More patience. The rope was new then.

KEL: Who was the other voice?

DOSSA: Mera, the carpenter. She built the first rail on the east bank. We argued about the length because the ferry landed at different spots in high and low water.

ARIN: Did you find a solution?

DOSSA: She made the rail longer and put two marks on it. One for low water, one for high. People could wait at the right place without guessing.

[SFX: TWO CALLER LINES BEEP.] 

ARIN: We have callers. First, Nella remembers waiting with her school class.

NELLA, PHONE: We used to count the blue bolts on the ferry ramp. There were seven.

DOSSA: There were eight. One was covered by paint.

NELLA: I knew there was a mystery.

ARIN: What did the bridge change for you?

NELLA: I can walk to the clinic without checking the tide. The ferry was kind, but I had to plan the whole morning around it.

ARIN: Next caller, Rook says he misses the ferry horn.

ROOK, PHONE: The bridge is quiet. The horn meant you were almost home.

DOSSA: The horn also meant the ferry was coming whether your basket was ready or not.

ROOK: It kept me organized.

KEL: We can keep the horn recording in the archive.

ARIN: Can we play it at the bridge opening?

DOSSA: Play it once. Don't make people wait for a boat that is gone.

[SFX: OLD FERRY HORN, DISTANT, THEN WATER.] 

ARIN: The bridge has no horn. It has a handrail, a ramp, and a plaque with Mera's name. The town has kept the ferry bell in the library, beside a photograph of Dossa at the crossing.

DOSSA: I look very serious in that photograph.

KEL: You were looking at a rope.

DOSSA: I was always looking at a rope.

ARIN: Thank you for remembering the work, not only the view. The bridge opens now. Please use the path at a walking pace. We will let the first crossing happen without a microphone in anyone's face.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS CROSS THE BRIDGE. WATER CONTINUES BELOW.] 

ARIN: The bridge remembers nothing. The people do. We have the recording if we need to remember too.

[SFX: TAPE CLICKS OFF.]
