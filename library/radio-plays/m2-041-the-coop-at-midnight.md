# The Coop at Midnight

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: serialized rural radio adventure dramas*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: June, a macaque radio mechanic; Rill, a gibbon courier; Boro, a baboon farmer; and Narrator, who insists every small interruption is a cliffhanger.

[SFX: NIGHT CRICKETS. A RADIO HUMS, THEN POPS.] 

NARRATOR: Midnight at the coop. A mysterious sound has shattered the silence. Is it a warning? A visitor? The end of the egg supply?

JUNE: It's the radio.

RILL: The radio is making a noise.

JUNE: Yes. We have established the radio is noisy.

NARRATOR: But why?

BORO: The battery is dead.

[SFX: RADIO HUM STOPS.] 

NARRATOR: The silence is worse.

JUNE: It is just quieter.

RILL: I brought a fresh battery.

JUNE: Why did you bring a battery to the coop?

RILL: The radio asked for one.

[SFX: DOOR OPENS. COOP HENS CLUCK.] 

NARRATOR: An unexpected arrival.

BORO: Those are chickens.

NARRATOR: They know something.

JUNE: They know the feeder is empty.

RILL: I can refill it.

BORO: Not with the radio battery.

[SFX: GRAIN POURS INTO FEEDER.] 

NARRATOR: The feed is restored, but a larger mystery remains. Why did the radio fail at the exact hour the courier arrived?

JUNE: Because the charger is unplugged.

RILL: I unplugged it to use the wall outlet.

BORO: For what?

RILL: My kettle.

JUNE: You brought a kettle to the coop?

RILL: The night is cold.

NARRATOR: A kettle. A battery. An empty feeder. Could these clues be connected?

BORO: The kettle needs an outlet.

JUNE: The radio needs power.

RILL: The feeder needs grain.

NARRATOR: Three needs. One room.

JUNE: We can use the charged battery for the radio and the lantern. The kettle stays in the house.

RILL: But I already filled it.

BORO: Then pour it into a thermos.

[SFX: WATER POURS. LID SCREWS ON.] 

NARRATOR: The crew has made a choice. But a new sound rises outside.

[SFX: WIND RATTLES THE COOP DOOR.] 

RILL: The latch is loose.

JUNE: That's the sound.

NARRATOR: Or is it?

BORO: It is the latch.

RILL: I can fix it.

JUNE: Hold the door while I fit the pin.

[SFX: METAL PIN TAPS INTO PLACE. WIND RATTLES, DOOR STAYS SHUT.] 

NARRATOR: The latch holds. The hens settle. The radio comes alive.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC, THEN A CLEAR LOCAL STATION.] 

RADIO HOST: This is the one a.m. farm report. Temperatures will drop before dawn. Bring loose tools indoors and check gates before sleeping.

BORO: That's why I came out. I left the hoe by the path.

JUNE: The radio wasn't a warning. It was the reminder.

NARRATOR: The true mystery was responsibility.

RILL: Please don't say that like a cliffhanger.

NARRATOR: Will Boro find the hoe?

BORO: I already found it.

[SFX: A ROOSTER STIRS, THEN SETTLES.]

RILL: The hens are awake.
JUNE: They heard the radio.
BORO: The radio is low.
NARRATOR: But what secret did the hens hear?
JUNE: That the feeder is full.
RILL: And the latch is fixed.
BORO: And the kettle is in the thermos.
NARRATOR: The night has one more surprise.
[SFX: A TIN CUP ROLLS ACROSS THE FLOOR.]
RILL: That was the cup.
JUNE: It fell off the shelf.
NARRATOR: An object in motion.
BORO: A cup on the floor.
JUNE: We can pick it up.
RILL: I will get it.
NARRATOR: The courier approaches the cup.
RILL: It is empty.
JUNE: Set it upright.
BORO: Now the floor is clear.
NARRATOR: The coop is safe, the radio is working, and the animals are fed.
JUNE: That sounds like a good ending.
NARRATOR: But every ending is a beginning.
BORO: Not tonight.
NARRATOR: We shall see.

NARRATOR: In the next episode, will the kettle return to its home?

RILL: It is in my bag.

NARRATOR: To be continued.

[SFX: CRICKETS. THE RADIO PLAYS A SOFT WALTZ.]
