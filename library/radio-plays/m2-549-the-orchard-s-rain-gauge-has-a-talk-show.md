# The Orchard's Rain Gauge Has a Talk Show

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night talk formats that turn an ordinary measurement into a celebrity anecdote*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Host Nari, a lemur; grower Mara, a macaque; host Vex, an orangutan; recorder Odo, a gibbon; gardener Pip, a capuchin.

SFX: Orchard shed. A single rain drip falls into a metal gauge.

NARI: We are broadcasting from the orchard after last night's rain. Today's guest is the rain gauge.

VEX: It has seen every storm and never once complained.

MARA: The gauge records rainfall. It doesn't have memories. It reads twelve millimeters since midnight.

ODO: I wrote the reading on the field sheet at six. The tube was emptied after the reading.

PIP: The soil is damp around the young trees. We can delay watering until tomorrow.

VEX: Gauge, what was the most dramatic storm you witnessed?

MARA: Ask Odo for the log. The gauge doesn't speak.

ODO: The log shows a heavier storm in spring, but the current reading is the one we use for the watering plan.

NARI: Vex, tell listeners why one gauge is placed in the open clearing.

VEX: It enjoys a wide view.

MARA: We placed it away from roof edges and branches, where falling drops would distort the reading.

SFX: Leaf rustle, then a sharp drop hits the shed roof.

PIP: That drip landed on the shed, not in the gauge.

VEX: A surprise guest has interrupted us.

ODO: The gauge is under the clearing, and it hasn't changed. The drop from the roof cannot reach it.

NARI: How will we know when to water the saplings?

MARA: We'll check the soil, not just the gauge. The young trees need moisture at the roots; today's rain reached them.

PIP: I can test one bed with the wooden probe. It slides in easily and comes out damp.

VEX: Could we give the probe a microphone?

MARA: No. Keep the tool clean and put it back in the shed.

SFX: Wooden probe enters soil and is withdrawn.

PIP: Bed is damp to the first joint. No watering today.

ODO: I'll mark the gauge reading and the soil check separately on the field sheet.

NARI: Why separately?

ODO: Rainfall is a measurement of what fell in the gauge. Soil condition tells us what the roots received. One number cannot replace the other.

NARI: A listener asks whether the gauge reading means the orchard should skip water for the whole week.

MARA: No. It describes rainfall since the last reading. We check each bed and the forecast again tomorrow.

VEX: The guest refuses to make a long-term prediction.

ODO: Rainfall totals do not tell us whether every tree received the same amount. The shed roof can divert water from the row nearest the fence.

PIP: I checked the young trees in that row separately. The soil is damp, but the mulch is thin.

MARA: Add mulch there, but do not water today. Keep the trunk base clear.

VEX: An exclusive revelation from the orchard floor.

NARI: It's a practical note. The older trees have deeper roots, so we don't treat all rows alike.

PIP: I'll carry one bucket of mulch to the dry-looking bed. The hose remains coiled.

SFX: Bucket lifts; a few dry leaves stir.

ODO: Field sheet now has three entries: rain gauge, soil probe, and mulch note.

NARI: The hose timer is still set for this afternoon. Should I cancel it?

MARA: Yes. Pip checked the young trees, and their soil is damp. The timer can be reset tomorrow if the forecast changes.

PIP: I will leave the hose coiled beside the shed. No one should leave it across the path.

ODO: I have crossed out today's watering on the schedule and initialed the change. The reading stays on the field sheet for the next shift.

VEX: The garden's leading guest has changed the calendar.

NARI: The gardeners changed one watering task after checking the ground.

VEX: The gauge has declined to answer questions.

MARA: It never answered them. The gardeners checked the ground.
