# Sorting Room on the Air

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio postal-sorting demonstrations, parcel-size confusion, and a station's undeliverable box that keeps being sent to itself*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Miri, a baboon radio host; Den, a capuchin postal sorter; Kava, a macaque station clerk; and Hali, an orangutan caller.*

[SFX: Conveyor belt, parcels slide, a stamp thumps.]

MIRI: Tonight on the Sorting Room, Den shows how parcels are routed by destination and size. What is the first package?

DEN: A small box addressed to the east grove.

KAVA: The east grove is divided into East Grove and East Grove Annex.

MIRI: Which one is on the label?

DEN: It says "east grove."

KAVA: The box is small. We can place it in either route bin.

DEN: Size does not determine destination.

MIRI: Our caller has a parcel that keeps returning.

HALI: I mailed a package to the station. It came back to my nest.

KAVA: The label says "Return to sender."

HALI: I wrote that as a joke.

DEN: The sorter read it as an instruction.

MIRI: What is inside?

HALI: A radio I borrowed from the station.

KAVA: It should be returned to the station.

HALI: That is where I sent it.

DEN: The station is also the sender.

MIRI: We have a loop. Can we open the box?

HALI: Yes. The radio is still inside.

[SFX: Box opens; a small radio clicks on.]

RADIO: This is the Sorting Room. Please return this radio to the station.

KAVA: The radio is asking for itself.

DEN: We will remove the joke label and write the actual destination.

HALI: The station is the actual destination.

MIRI: Then the parcel needs a station delivery tag.

KAVA: We can hand it across the desk.

DEN: That is not how the postal route works.

KAVA: It is how this route works.

[SFX: Parcel slides across the studio desk.]

MIRI: The station has received the radio.

HALI: Could you send me a new envelope? I want to mail a thank-you note.

DEN: We can give you one. Write the station's address and your return address.

HALI: If it returns to me, I will know it was delivered.

KAVA: That's not what a return address means.

MIRI: The next parcel is long and narrow. It is labeled "Do not bend."

DEN: Put it flat on the belt.

KAVA: The belt has a curve.

DEN: Then carry it around the curve.

MIRI: The parcel is a rolled map.

KAVA: It cannot be folded.

DEN: It can be unrolled at the destination.

HALI: I recognize that map. It shows the station.

KAVA: The station is at the center.

DEN: The map is upside down.

MIRI: Turn it to face north.

[SFX: Paper unrolls, then reverses.]

KAVA: Now the station is at the bottom.

DEN: That's correct.

MIRI: The map has reached the right route bin. The radio has reached the station, and Hali has her envelope.

HALI: I will write "To the station" on the parcel, not "return to sender."

KAVA: Clear labels help.

DEN: Correct sorting depends on the destination, not the parcel shape or the joke in the corner.

MIRI: This has been the Sorting Room. The belt is empty, the map points north, and the station radio is back at the station.

MIRI: One more parcel is still on the belt. It has no street name, only a drawing of a tree.

DEN: The drawing matches the east grove. We can route it to the grove's caretaker, not the tree itself.

KAVA: The caretaker signed for the radio and returned the old envelope to Hali.

HALI: I will write a proper address on my next letter.

MIRI: Clear destination, correct bin, and a person who can actually receive the parcel.

[SFX: Conveyor stops; the radio gives a clear tuning tone.]
