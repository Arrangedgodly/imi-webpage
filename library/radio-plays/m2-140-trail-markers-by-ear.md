# Trail Markers by Ear

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio trail-building field reports, directional marker descriptions, and a crew that places every arrow toward the sound engineer's lunch*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Olly, a gibbon field host; Gama, a macaque trail steward; Turu, an orangutan builder; and Efi, a capuchin sound recordist.*

[SFX: Boots on gravel, hammer taps, bird calls.]

OLLY: We are live on the new ridge trail. The crew is installing markers to help walkers find the overlook. Gama, how should the arrows point?

GAMA: Toward the trail, away from the cliff, and toward the overlook.

TURU: The first marker is up. Arrow points east toward the picnic area.

OLLY: The overlook is west.

TURU: The marker faces the direction of the wind.

GAMA: Wind direction isn't the route.

EFI: The lunch basket is east, past the picnic area.

OLLY: Why is the arrow pointed at lunch?

EFI: I was recording the wrapper crinkle when I opened my snack.

TURU: I heard it in my monitor and turned the marker toward the sound.

GAMA: Your snack wrapper is not the overlook.

[SFX: Kettle whistles faintly.]

TURU: Turning the first arrow west. I have unplugged the snack monitor.

[SFX: Screwdriver turns; wrapper crinkle cuts off.]

OLLY: We will test the route by walking it. The first marker now points toward a stand of pines.

GAMA: That is the correct trail.

TURU: The second marker points back toward the start.

GAMA: It should point forward.

EFI: The basket is in the picnic area, east of the trailhead.

OLLY: The marker may be hearing the basket.

GAMA: Markers do not hear.

TURU: The arrow sits on a loose screw. It swings when the wind catches its broad face.

GAMA: Tighten it. Use a fixed bracket.

[SFX: Screwdriver turns; the loose sign stops rattling.]

TURU: The arrow stays in place.

OLLY: The crew has reached a fork in the trail. One branch goes to the overlook, one to the water tank.

GAMA: Put a marker at eye height, with the destination name beneath the arrow.

TURU: The sign says "Overlook."

OLLY: The arrow points to the water tank.

TURU: The lettering fits on the wrong side.

GAMA: Turn the sign around.

[SFX: Wood marker rotates; a screw tightens.]

EFI: I can hear my snack wrapper from here.

OLLY: Please stop using lunch as a navigation signal.

EFI: I am the sound recordist. I document what I hear.

GAMA: Then record the birds, the wind, and the hammer. The lunch will wait.

[SFX: Birds, wind, hammer.]

TURU: The marker is fixed. The arrow points west to the overlook; the water tank is marked on the other path.

OLLY: We will follow the signs without listening for food.

EFI: I am recording a steady footstep.

GAMA: That is your own boot.

OLLY: We have reached the overlook. The ridge opens above the valley.

TURU: The final marker points back down the safe trail.

GAMA: Correct. A return arrow points toward the path, not the edge.

EFI: I hear the kettle from the picnic area.

OLLY: The return path takes us to the trailhead, then east to the picnic area.

EFI: I will turn off the recorder before opening my snack.

[SFX: Crew walks downhill; a marker stays fixed in its bracket.]

TURU: All markers are fixed, all paths are labeled, and no arrows point at food.

GAMA: We have one finishing task. Record the trail directions for tomorrow's hikers.

OLLY: The overlook trail goes west from the entrance. At the fork, follow the blue marker. The picnic area is east of the trailhead.

EFI: My snack wrapper is still in the recording from earlier.

GAMA: That was the sound that sent us east. It is not a trail signal.

OLLY: The broadcast has played the clip on air. Three listeners are now walking toward the picnic area.

EFI: I can meet them there and hand out the trail map.

TURU: Put the snack clip under "field sound," not "navigation."

GAMA: The directions are corrected, the marker test is complete, and the lunch area is marked separately.

[SFX: Hammer secures the sign; footsteps return to the trailhead.]
