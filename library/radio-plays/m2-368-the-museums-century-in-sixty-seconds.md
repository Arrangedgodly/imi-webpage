# The Museum's Century in Sixty Seconds

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: historical radio reenactments that compress long events into a speed-run and use the wrong sound cues*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Producer Neri, a capuchin; historian Lema, a macaque; host Varo, a gibbon; technician Pali, a lemur.

[SFX: RADIO STUDIO. A BELL RINGS, THEN A CROWD RECORDING STARTS.]

VARO: In one minute, we will hear the founding of the river museum, the opening of its first gallery, and its hundredth anniversary.

LEMA: Those happened in three different years.

NERI: The speed-run track joins them. We have exactly sixty seconds.

PALI: It begins with the museum bell, then the first visitor, then the anniversary speech.

[SFX: BELL, CROWD, THEN A MODERN MICROPHONE FEEDBACK SQUEAL.]

VARO: The museum was founded in 1920. The first gallery opened in 1924. In 2020, its hundredth anniversary was delayed.

LEMA: It was delayed in 2020, then held two years later. The track has the dates wrong.

NERI: We can speed up the corrected narration.

LEMA: Then listeners won't understand it. The dates explain why the anniversary happened later.

VARO: The cue sheet says "add marching feet" between founding and opening.

PALI: Those footsteps come from the anniversary parade, not the original gallery opening.

NERI: The short version needs drama.

LEMA: History does not need a marching sound every time a date changes.

[SFX: PALI REWINDS. FOOTSTEPS PLAY BACKWARD, THEN STOP.]

VARO: Our sixty seconds are running.

LEMA: Then use sixty seconds to tell one accurate thing. The first gallery opened after volunteers moved the collection from a schoolroom.

NERI: That's not the full century.

LEMA: It is a reason the museum exists.

PALI: The recording has an interview with a volunteer. The file is labeled "opening day."

[SFX: PLAY BUTTON. OLD RECORDING CRACKLES.]

VOLUNTEER RECORDING: We carried the display cases in two at a time. The bell rang because we bumped the front door.

VARO: That's the same bell we used for the founding.

LEMA: It belongs to the opening day recording. The founding has no bell sound.

NERI: I can cut the anniversary applause from the middle and place the interview after the date.

PALI: The applause is baked into the next track. I can lower that channel and leave the voice clear.

[SFX: SLIDER MOVES. OLD VOICE PLAYS AGAIN, CLEANER.]

VOLUNTEER RECORDING: We carried the display cases in two at a time. The bell rang because we bumped the front door.

VARO: That gives us a scene.

LEMA: Say the founding date, then the gallery opening, then mention the delayed anniversary. We do not need to pretend they were one afternoon.

NERI: We can keep the minute format as a limit, not a reason to scramble events.

PALI: I will label the sound cues with their years.

[SFX: KEYBOARD TAPS. THREE BEEPS.]

NERI: The producer asks if the parade can play under the anniversary date.

LEMA: It can play when we mention the anniversary in 2022. It cannot play under the 1920 founding.

PALI: I have separated the parade file from the door bell and the volunteer interview.

[SFX: THREE TRACK BUTTONS CLICK ONCE EACH, THEN GO SILENT.]

VARO: The first year gets no invented footsteps.

LEMA: And the opening day gets the real bell, because the volunteer explained why it rang.

NERI: The cue sheet now names each track and year.

VARO: Ready for the recording?

LEMA: Read it once. Let the dates breathe.

[SFX: MIC OPENS.]

VARO: The river museum began in 1920. Four years later, volunteers carried cases from a schoolroom into the first gallery. The door bell rang when a case bumped the frame. A century later, the museum's anniversary celebration took place in 2022, after the public gathering was postponed.

[SFX: ORIGINAL DOOR BELL RINGS, THEN THE VOLUNTEER'S OLD RECORDING FADES IN.]

VOLUNTEER RECORDING: We carried the display cases in two at a time.

VARO: One minute, one century, and each sound in its own year.

NERI: That is a better ending than the parade.

PALI: The marching feet stay in the anniversary file.

LEMA: Good. They belong to that day.

[SFX: RECORDING STOPS. A LABEL PRINTER FEEDS OUT THREE DATE TAGS.]
