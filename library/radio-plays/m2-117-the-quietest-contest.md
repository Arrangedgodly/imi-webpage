# The Quietest Contest

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio coverage of a silent reading contest, studio narration etiquette, and competitors whose page-turns become an accidental soundtrack*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Sela, an orangutan host; Kitt, a macaque librarian; Voro, a gibbon contestant; and Tamsin, a capuchin sound technician.*

[SFX: A clock ticks. The studio holds its breath.]

SELA: Welcome to the Quietest Contest, live from the town library. Twelve readers compete to finish one chapter without making a sound. Kitt, what are the rules?

KITT: No speaking, humming, tapping, or turning pages more loudly than a whisper.

VORO: Is breathing allowed?

KITT: Quietly.

TAMSIN: We have microphones beneath each chair and one above the book table.

SELA: Does the overhead microphone help?

TAMSIN: It can hear a bookmark fall in the next room.

[SFX: A bookmark drops; a tiny bell rings.]

KITT: That is our practice bell. During the contest, it will mark any sound.

SELA: The readers are seated. Begin.

[SFX: Clock ticks; pages turn softly.]

SELA: Voro has opened to chapter one. Kitt, is that allowed?

KITT: The chapter is assigned. He may start anywhere in it.

SELA: He appears to be reading with his eyes closed.

VORO: I am listening to the words.

KITT: That's called reading aloud in your head.

SELA: A minute has passed. No bells.

TAMSIN: I heard a squeak from the rear row.

KITT: That was a chair.

TAMSIN: The chair is not competing.

SELA: Could its occupant be disqualified?

KITT: The contestant is allowed one chair adjustment.

[SFX: Chair shifts gently.]

SELA: The adjustment sounds like a distant door.

VORO: I am trying to read the chapter, but the host keeps describing the silence.

SELA: I will lower my voice.

KITT: A whispered broadcast still counts as sound.

SELA: Then I'll narrate with my eyebrows.

TAMSIN: The microphones cannot hear eyebrows.

[SFX: Page turns; the practice bell rings.]

SELA: Who rang it?

TAMSIN: Contestant four turned a page. The page has a glossy finish.

KITT: The contest allows page turns.

TAMSIN: The bell does not know that.

VORO: Can the bell be disqualified?

KITT: The bell is also not competing.

SELA: Contestant four has switched to a different book. The new book has rough pages.

TAMSIN: Those pages make a sound like dry leaves.

KITT: The library does contain books.

SELA: The contest is becoming a forest.

[SFX: Several page turns ripple through the room.]

TAMSIN: The overhead mic has picked up a faint rustle from the ventilation.

KITT: That is the air system.

SELA: The air system is louder than the readers.

VORO: We can turn it off.

KITT: Then everyone will be too warm to read.

SELA: Leave it on. The contest includes ambient library noise.

TAMSIN: Does that mean the bell should ignore pages?

KITT: It should ignore the permitted sounds.

TAMSIN: I can adjust the threshold.

[SFX: A dial turns; the bell gives a dull chime.]

SELA: The bell has become quieter than the silence.

TAMSIN: It's still working.

KITT: We have five minutes left. Voro has reached the last page.

VORO: I will wait until the others finish.

SELA: That is considerate.

KITT: It is also the rule. All contestants finish together.

VORO: Then I will reread the first paragraph.

[SFX: Page turns softly.]

TAMSIN: The audience is holding still. Even the chairs have stopped squeaking.

SELA: The final minute begins. Readers close your books when ready.

[SFX: A series of gentle covers closing, one after another.]

KITT: Voro is last. His book is closed. No contestant triggered the bell.

SELA: We have a tie: twelve quiet readers.

KITT: The prize is a library card and the right to choose next month's quiet contest.

VORO: I choose a page-turning contest.

TAMSIN: We will need different microphones.

SELA: And a bell that knows pages are allowed.

[SFX: Bell rings once; the audience applauds silently with a soft rustle of hands.]

KITT: The books are back on the shelves, the contestants are quiet, and the air system wins the sound award.

[SFX: Ventilation hum remains as the broadcast fades.]
