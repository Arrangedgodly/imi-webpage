# The Music Box Restoration Hour

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio antique restoration shows, meticulous repair narration, and an heirloom music box whose tune was never meant to play in order*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Hal, a gibbon restoration host; Eme, an orangutan instrument conservator; Pax, a macaque caller; and Runi, a capuchin assistant.*

[SFX: Small gears, a winding key, one broken melody.]

HAL: Welcome to Keep It Turning, where we restore delicate objects by sound. Tonight Eme repairs a music box sent by Pax.

PAX: It plays three notes, pauses, then repeats the first note. It was my aunt's.

EME: The cylinder is intact, but one pin is bent. We will inspect it before winding.

RUNI: Can we play the full tune?

EME: Not yet. The spring is under tension.

HAL: What happens if you wind it too far?

EME: It can jam or break. We turn it gently and stop at resistance.

PAX: I wound it once. It made a squeak.

[SFX: A squeak.]

EME: That is not the box. Runi's chair is squeaking.

RUNI: I am sitting very still.

HAL: The box's first note is bright; the second is lower.

EME: The bent pin strikes two teeth. That explains the extra note.

PAX: It used to play a melody with a pause in the middle.

EME: A pause may be part of the tune.

HAL: Is a music box meant to pause?

EME: Some cylinders include rests. We need the original music card to compare.

PAX: The card says "For Auntie, from the garden."

RUNI: It has three notes drawn on it.

EME: Those are the first notes. There is a rest, then a rising phrase.

HAL: The box has been playing the beginning twice because the pin is bent.

PAX: I thought it was broken.

EME: It is damaged, but the pause is intentional. I can adjust the pin and preserve the rest.

[SFX: Tiny tool taps; a gear turns.]

RUNI: Is that the correct pitch?

EME: We will test one turn at a time.

[SFX: Three notes, a pause, then a clear rising phrase.]

PAX: That's the tune. The pause is where she used to say my name.

HAL: That is a memory the box cannot play.

EME: We can preserve the music without forcing it to fill every moment.

RUNI: The spring is still tight.

EME: I will release it safely and store the box upright.

PAX: Could we play it at the family gathering?

EME: Yes, after the repair is tested and the key is used gently.

HAL: We have restored the pin, confirmed the pause, and kept the spring under control.

RUNI: My chair is still squeaking.

EME: That is a separate repair.

[SFX: Chair shifts; squeak repeats.]

HAL: The box will go back to Pax with a note to wind it slowly.

PAX: I will tell the family the pause belongs in the song.

EME: Let the melody breathe. Do not fill the rest with extra turns.

[SFX: Box plays the short tune: three notes, a pause, a rising phrase.]

HAL: This has been Keep It Turning. The spring is safe, the pin is straight, and the chair has become our next project.

PAX: I have brought the box to the family gathering. My aunt recognizes the first three notes.

[SFX: The small tune plays; family members hum softly.]

RUNI: The pause comes before the rising phrase.

EME: That is where the memory belongs. The repair lets the tune play as it was made.

HAL: The box has been returned, and the family can hear it safely.

PAX: I will keep the winding key beside the box, not inside it.

PAX: The box is going back on the shelf beside the garden photograph.

EME: Keep it dry, wind it gently, and let the rest stay a rest.

[SFX: Tool case closes; chair squeaks once.]
