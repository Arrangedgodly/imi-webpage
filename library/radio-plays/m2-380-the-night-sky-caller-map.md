# The Night-Sky Caller Map

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: astronomy call-in programs that plot callers' star descriptions without checking which direction they face*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Host Veli, a gibbon; astronomer Rani, a lemur; operator Desto, a macaque; caller Fala, a capuchin.

[SFX: RADIO STUDIO AT NIGHT. PHONE LINES CLICK. PAPER MAP UNFOLDS.]

VELI: Welcome to Sky Calls. Describe a bright star and we will mark it on our live map.

FALA: I see a bright one just above the tree line. It is flickering red and white.

RANI: Are you looking north, south, east or west?

FALA: Toward the window.

DESTO: The map has four windows marked.

VELI: We can mark it by brightness and color.

RANI: We need direction and time. It could be a planet, a star or an aircraft light.

[SFX: PENCIL CIRCLES A STAR ON THE MAP.]

FALA: I hear an aircraft too.

RANI: Then the bright light may be a plane turning.

VELI: The show title promises a star map. We need a mark.

RANI: We can mark a temporary observation as unconfirmed.

DESTO: The caller's local time is nine forty. Our chart shows Venus low in the west.

FALA: The light is moving right.

RANI: A star won't move across that field in a few seconds. Ask Fala to compare it to the roof peak.

VELI: Fala, is it moving relative to the roof?

FALA: Yes. It passed behind the chimney.

RANI: That's an aircraft. Remove the star mark.

[SFX: ERASER RUBS PAPER.]

VELI: Can we still take another call?

DESTO: Line two says they see three bright points over the water.

RANI: Ask which way they face and what time they looked.

VELI: Caller, are you near the north pier?

CALLER: I am at the south beach, facing east. It is ten fifteen.

VELI: Our caller form has only one location box. It says "near the station."

DESTO: I can add a field for caller position and facing direction. The current map needs a separate origin.

RANI: Add observation time too. The stars move across the chart over an evening, even if the pattern stays together.

FALA: My call was at nine forty. The beach caller looked at ten fifteen.

VELI: That's why their notes cannot share one horizon mark.

[SFX: PAPER RULER SLIDES BETWEEN THE TWO MAPS. A PENCIL WRITES "9:40 WEST" AND "10:15 EAST."]

DESTO: Each mark now says where and when it was seen.

RANI: That's a chart we can use tomorrow, instead of a collage of bright dots.

RANI: The three points may be the summer triangle, but the chart should show their location from the south beach.

DESTO: Our studio map assumes the caller is standing at the tower.

VELI: We have been marking every caller on one local map.

RANI: That shifts the horizon. We need separate caller locations.

[SFX: PAPER MAP TURNS. A SECOND MAP UNFOLDS.]

FALA: My window faces west. The aircraft went behind the chimney.

RANI: I'll put the call in the west-sky log, not on the star chart.

DESTO: The second caller is marked at the south beach, looking east, ten fifteen.

VELI: The three points are now in the right field.

RANI: They match the chart. We can say likely summer triangle, unconfirmed from the phone description.

CALLER: I see it now. The highest point is Vega.

RANI: That confirms the pattern.

VELI: The map has two caller positions and no wandering plane.

DESTO: I'll keep the sheets separate. The tower map is for the station and the beach map is for the caller.

FALA: The aircraft light is gone.

RANI: It was never a star. The caller described exactly what they saw, and the map needed their direction.

VELI: Sky Calls will ask for location, direction and time before marking the chart.

[SFX: PENCIL ADDS THREE LABELS. PHONE LINE CLICKS CLOSED; NIGHT INSECTS SOUND OUTSIDE.]
