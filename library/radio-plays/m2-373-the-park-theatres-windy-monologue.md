# The Park Theatre's Windy Monologue

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: outdoor theatre broadcasts that treat wind moving the script as an improvised performance*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Actor Mena, a lemur; director Yori, a gibbon; sound operator Vek, a macaque; audience member Tali, a capuchin.

[SFX: PARK AT DUSK. LEAVES RUSTLE. A PAPER SCRIPT FLAPS AGAINST A STAND.]

YORI: Mena, begin the monologue when the cue light comes on.

[SFX: LIGHT SWITCH CLICKS. PAPER FLAPS.]

MENA: "I have waited by the gate for the messenger..."

[SFX: GUST LIFTS SCRIPT PAGES. PAPER SCATTERS.]

MENA: My next line is on the path.

TALI: The page landed by my chair.

YORI: Hold the scene. Vek, can you get the script without crossing the audience?

VEK: I have the spare copy on the sound table.

MENA: The program note says the wind in this scene is a ghost.

YORI: The wind effect is a drum roll. The real wind is lifting the prop pages.

TALI: One page is under my seat.

MENA: I can improvise from the story.

YORI: The broadcast is carrying every rustle. We should secure the pages before continuing.

[SFX: CLIPBOARD CLAMPS SHUT. VEK'S SHOES CROSS GRAVEL.]

VEK: I found page four. It has the ending.

YORI: Keep it face down. We are not at the ending.

MENA: The script has two page clips, but the top one is missing.

VEK: There is a clothespin in the prop basket.

YORI: Use that. Don't tape paper to the stand; the paper needs to turn.

[SFX: CLOTHESPIN OPENS AND CLIPS PAPER. GUST RATTLES IT.]

MENA: It holds, but the stand is still moving.

TALI: The stand sits on a loose board. I can hear it rock.

YORI: Don't move it during the scene. The microphone stand is stable; the script stand isn't.

VEK: I can wedge the board after Mena steps away.

MENA: I have a line about the gate, then I cross to the bench. The paper stand is outside my path.

YORI: Let's test the sequence without the wind drum.

[SFX: DRUM ROLL STARTS, THEN CUTS.]

MENA: "I have waited by the gate for the messenger. The lamp is low, and the road is empty."

[SFX: SCRIPT PAGE TURNS UNDER CONTROL. CLOTHESPIN CLICKS.]

MENA: "If no one comes, I will carry the letter myself."

TALI: That line is on page three.

MENA: Page three is secure.

YORI: The scene can resume. Vek, wedge the board while the audience is quiet.

[SFX: WOODEN WEDGE SLIDES UNDER BOARD. ROCKING STOPS.]

VEK: Stand is stable.

TALI: The audience is laughing because the pages blew toward us.

MENA: I can acknowledge the wind after the scene, but I won't turn the missing line into a joke in the middle.

VEK: The microphone caught the pages before the clothespin. I'll mark that segment as a pause, not a ghost effect.

YORI: Good. The play can have one actual gust in its sound bed. No loop.

[SFX: SOUND OPERATOR SLIDES A FADER DOWN. LEAVES REMAIN AUDIBLE, PAPER DOES NOT.]

MENA: I have the whole page now. I can finish the monologue as written.

YORI: Cue light.

[SFX: LIGHT SWITCH CLICKS. LEAVES RUSTLE.]

MENA: "I have waited by the gate for the messenger. The lamp is low, and the road is empty. If no one comes, I will carry the letter myself."

[SFX: MENA'S FOOTSTEPS CROSS GRAVEL TO THE BENCH. A LETTER LIFTS FROM THE SEAT.]

MENA: "It was left here all along."

TALI: The page stayed on the stand this time.

YORI: And the wind is still real, not a ghost.

VEK: The microphone caught the lines, not the paper flapping.

MENA: The audience can hear the leaves without losing the scene.

[SFX: APPLAUSE. CLOTHESPIN REMAINS CLOSED. A SINGLE LEAF SCRAPES ACROSS THE PATH.]

YORI: That's the end. We don't need to repeat it for a gust.

[SFX: LIGHTS DIM. PARK WIND CONTINUES.]
