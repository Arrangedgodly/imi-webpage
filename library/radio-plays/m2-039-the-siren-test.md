# The Siren Test

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: emergency preparedness broadcast spots and civil alert tests*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Announcer Jessa, a macaque; Hal, a careful baboon siren technician; Mina, a capuchin crossing guard; and caller Odo, a gibbon who keeps confusing the practice tone with his lunch timer.

[SFX: STATION IDENTIFICATION TONE. PAPER RUSTLES.] 

JESSA: At noon today, Bellwether will test its public siren. The siren will sound for one minute, stop, then sound for ten seconds. This is a scheduled test. There is no emergency. Hal, can we say that one more time?

HAL: Yes. There is no emergency.

JESSA: Is there an emergency about saying there is no emergency?

HAL: No.

JESSA: Good. Today's program is called Ready, Set, Listen. We will tell you what the test sounds like, what the town will do, and which things it will not do. It will not send you to the hill, launch a boat, or ask you to buy a special siren hat.

[SFX: PHONE LINE OPENS.] 

ODO: My kitchen timer sounds exactly like the test tone.

JESSA: What does your timer time?

ODO: Bread.

HAL: A timer should not imitate the siren. What model is it?

ODO: A little red one.

JESSA: That is not the model.

HAL: Keep your timer inside. During the test, listen for the announcement before you decide what the tone means. The town siren has a clear rising note. Your timer probably has a bell.

ODO: It has a rising note.

JESSA: Does it rise for one minute?

ODO: No. The bread would burn.

[SFX: SOFT LAUGHTER.] 

JESSA: We have a caller from the school crossing.

MINA, REMOTE: I will keep children on the sidewalk until the test ends. Drivers have been asking whether they should stop.

HAL: The test does not change road rules. Drivers should follow the crossing guard and posted signs. They do not need to pull over unless they are already in a safe place.

MINA: We have a delivery cart approaching. I can hear it over the line.

[SFX: CART WHEELS ON PAVEMENT.] 

JESSA: Is that the siren?

MINA: No. It's a cart with a squeaky wheel.

HAL: The town should consider a separate sound for carts.

JESSA: That would require a committee.

[SFX: DOOR OPENS IN STUDIO.] 

HAL: The control panel is ready. We have tested the backup power and confirmed the all-clear message follows the second tone.

JESSA: What if someone misses the all-clear?

HAL: The test notice is on the town website, library board, and radio. If there were a real alert, the same channels would give instructions. Residents should use the routes listed for their neighborhood, not follow someone else's route because it looks busy.

JESSA: What if I don't have a radio?

HAL: Your neighbors can share the notice. The library has a printed copy. A test is useful if people know what to expect before it begins.

[SFX: CLOCK TICKS TO NOON.] 

JESSA: The station has prepared a special test countdown, a test jingle, and a test disclaimer.
HAL: We need the siren and the all-clear. The jingle is not part of the test.
JESSA: I will put the disclaimer after the jingle.
HAL: Then listeners will think the jingle is the alert.
JESSA: We will cancel the jingle.
[SFX: A BUTTON CLICKS. A TINY FANFARE SOUNDS.]
JESSA: That was the cancel button.
HAL: It has its own fanfare?
JESSA: The station has a button for everything.
JESSA: We are now testing the siren. This is scheduled. There is no emergency.

[SFX: DISTANT SIREN, ONE MINUTE. FADE OUT.] 

ODO, PHONE: My timer is ringing too.

JESSA: Is the bread done?

ODO: It is. That is a real emergency for the butter.

[SFX: SIREN STOPS. SHORT PAUSE. TEN-SECOND TONE.] 

HAL: All-clear test. The equipment worked.

JESSA: And the bakery worked. Thank you for listening. If you heard the tone, you do not need to call the station to report that you heard the tone. We heard it too.

[SFX: PHONE LINES FLASH, THEN QUIET.] 

JESSA: We have one caller anyway.

CALLER: Was that the test?

JESSA: Yes.

CALLER: Good. My timer is still going.

HAL: Check the bread.
JESSA: And keep the test notice where neighbors can find it.
ODO: I will put it beside the timer.
JESSA: That way nobody mistakes the toast for a warning.
ODO: Unless it burns.
HAL: Then use the fire plan, not the siren plan.

[SFX: STATION MUSIC. A KITCHEN TIMER STOPS.]
