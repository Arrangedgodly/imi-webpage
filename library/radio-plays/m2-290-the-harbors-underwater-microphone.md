# The Harbor's Underwater Microphone

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: field-report broadcasts, waterproof equipment tests, and reporters who confuse transmitted bubbles with spoken answers*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST NIMA, a gibbon
DIVER KET, a macaque
ENGINEER VEX, an orangutan
HARBOR GUIDE PAVA, a lemur

[SFX: BOAT ROCKS, MICROPHONE CABLE UNWINDS.]

NIMA: We are testing the harbor's new underwater microphone.
 
Pava asks Ket to swim a short distance from the pier and tap the water twice, then wait. The first tap arrives cleanly; the second is masked by the boat's hull creak.

[SFX: TAP, HULL CREAK.]

VEX: We cannot separate the sounds.

KET: The boat is moving against the rope.

PAVA: We can slacken the line from shore.

NIMA: Is that safe?

PAVA: The boat is tied at two points. We can ease one line and keep the other secure.

Omi releases a little slack. The hull stops rubbing the pier. Ket repeats the test. Both taps arrive distinctly, followed by a faint bubbling sound.

NIMA: That is clearer.

VEX: The microphone has improved.

KET: The boat stopped scraping.

PAVA: That is the actual improvement.
 Diver Ket will describe what the harbor sounds like below the surface.

VEX: The microphone is sealed and rated for shallow water.

PAVA: Is the recorder sealed too?

VEX: It is in the dry case.

[SFX: SPLASH.]

KET (MUFFLED): I am in the water.

NIMA: What do you hear?

KET: Bubbles. Rope. A boat hull creaking.

[SFX: BUBBLES, LOW CREAK.]

PAVA: The microphone is picking up the dock line.

VEX: That is part of the harbor soundscape.

KET: I see a crab tapping the pier post.

[SFX: TWO TAPS.]

NIMA: The crab has answered our first question.

VEX: It has not. It has tapped twice.

PAVA: The microphone cable is rubbing against the ladder.

[SFX: SCRAPE, SCRAPE.]

NIMA: The ladder is repeating itself.

KET: Can you lower the cable away from it?

VEX: I can. Keep the microphone still.

The sound changes. A low pulsing noise comes through the headphones.

NIMA: Something is approaching.

PAVA: That is the harbor pump.

VEX: The pump is on the west wall.

KET: I am near the east ladder.

PAVA: Sound travels underwater. The pump may sound closer than it is.

Nima asks whether the broadcast can identify a boat by its engine. Vex says not without a clear reference recording. Ket hears a motor start above the water. The microphone captures only a low vibration.

[SFX: MOTOR VIBRATION.]

NIMA: Is it a boat?

KET: It is a boat.

PAVA: The boat is still tied to the dock.

VEX: We have captured the sound of an engine through the pier.

NIMA: It sounds like a distant drum.

KET: It sounds like a dock.

Vex asks Ket to tap the metal ladder once so they can compare the signal. The tap comes through sharply, nearly overwhelming the headphones.

[SFX: METAL TAP, HEADPHONE POP.]

NIMA: We have our reference.

VEX: Turn the gain down.

PAVA: The crab has left the post.

KET: I see it under the rock.

NIMA: Can it answer another question?

KET: It is moving away.

VEX: That is not a response.

They finish the test and pull up the microphone. The cable comes up dripping, but the recorder's dry case is damp around its lid. Vex wipes it and discovers the gasket was folded under one edge.

PAVA: The sound equipment worked.

VEX: The case did not seal.

NIMA: The interview is over.

KET: The crab had better answers.

Vex replaces the gasket before packing the recorder. The next test will stay on shore until the seal is checked.

[SFX: CASE OPENS, CLOTH WIPES, LATCH CLICKS.]

NIMA: The underwater report ends with two taps, one pump, and a crab that declined to comment.

[SFX: WATER LAPS, BOAT ROPE CREAKS.]
