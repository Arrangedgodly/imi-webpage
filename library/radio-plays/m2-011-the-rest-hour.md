# The Rest Hour

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Wellness interview podcasts and polished self-care advice*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Noma, a squirrel monkey host; Palli, a sloth night-shift seed keeper; Evi, a capuchin caregiver; Bean, a tamarin producer.

[INTRO: SOFT CHIMES, A BIRD CALL, A PHONE ALARM THAT WILL NOT STOP.]

HOST NOMA: Welcome to The Rest Hour, where we talk about rest without asking you to optimize it. I'm Noma, a squirrel monkey. My guest is Palli, a sloth and night-shift seed keeper. Palli, the alarm is yours.

PALLI: It is my third alarm. The first two told me I should have gone to sleep earlier.

NOMA: Did they help?

PALLI: They made me feel behind before my day started.

NOMA: You work when the grove sleeps. What has actually helped?

PALLI: I changed one light. The old lantern in the seed room was bright white, and it kept the moths away from the blossoms. I made a shade from woven palm. The room is dimmer. The flowers opened again, and I stop work when they close.

NOMA: That sounds practical, not magical.

PALLI: A practical thing can feel like a gift.

[SFX: LANTERN CHAIN CREAKS. MOTHS WING SOFTLY.] 

NOMA: You also told our producer you tried a morning routine.

PALLI: I did. It had seven steps. I forgot the second step every day.

NOMA: What did you keep?

PALLI: Drink water. Sit by the window. Ask if I am tired or merely grumpy because I missed a meal.

NOMA: Can you tell the difference?

PALLI: Usually by the time I sit down, I can.

NOMA: I like that. The wellness shelf at the station is full of plans that assume we are all built the same. You're slow by nature, but the night schedule is hard even for you. What do you say to a listener who thinks rest must be earned?

PALLI: I ask whether they would make a seedling earn water. Then I remind them that I don't know their life. If the house is loud or work is stacked up, resting may need help from another person. Ask for a turn. Don't expect a candle to solve a schedule.

NOMA: That is a better answer than buying the candle I saw advertised on the way in.

PALLI: The candle is nice. It smells like cedar. I bought one.

NOMA: So the ad got one thing right.

BEAN: Quick message from our sponsor, RestCloud. It offers a personalized wind-down routine with fourteen choices and a reminder to choose a routine.

PALLI: Does it turn off the alarm?

BEAN: It sends you a notification that the alarm is still on.

NOMA: That's a reminder to rest that interrupts rest.

PALLI: I use a lamp switch. It has one setting called off.

[SFX: A SWITCH CLICKS. THE ALARM STOPS FOR ONE BEAT, THEN STARTS AGAIN.]

NOMA: The sponsor's ad has an alarm in it.

BEAN: I'll ask them to remove it after my breathing exercise.

[SFX: A MOUSE RUNS ACROSS PAPER. A PHONE BUZZES.] 

NOMA: Our producer says we have a caller. Evi, you're on.

CALLER EVI: I'm a capuchin caregiver. I have a new baby at home, and everyone sends me lists. Eat this, stretch that, take a walk, nap while the baby naps. The baby naps for nine minutes. I spend eight of them finding my shoes.

PALLI: Then the shoes are not the first step. Could you sit where you already are for those nine minutes?

EVI: I usually start folding cloth.

NOMA: Can someone else fold it?

EVI: My sister is visiting tomorrow. I can ask her to take the laundry while I rest.

PALLI: Tell her what rest looks like to you. Quiet, food, or another pair of hands. People guess badly, even when they love you.

EVI: I want soup and no advice.

NOMA: We have soup in the station kitchen. It is a little salty, but no one will advise you while you eat it.

EVI: I'll come by.

NOMA: Palli, last question. What is your rest plan tonight?

PALLI: Feed the moths their proper flowers, turn off the bright lantern, and sleep until my body wakes me. If I wake tired, I will sleep longer.

NOMA: Our plan is to turn off the alarm.

[SFX: PHONE ALARM STOPS. THE ROOM GOES QUIET.] 

PALLI: That was the fourth alarm.

NOMA: You can keep the fourth. Just give the first three to the seed bank.

NOMA: This was The Rest Hour. You have heard one sloth's routine, not a rule for every body. If you are tired, ask what is keeping you awake. If you cannot fix it alone, tell someone. We will be here next week, assuming we wake up.

[THEME RETURNS, UNHURRIED. MOTHS HUM.]
