# The Flower That Opens Off Air

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio live nature observation, hushed anticipation, and a station that cannot sell its sponsors a silent broadcast*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Asha, a gibbon nature host; Niko, a macaque botanist; Venn, an orangutan sound engineer; and Fia, a capuchin caller.*

[SFX: Night insects, soft wind, a recorder clicks on.]

ASHA: We are live beside the moonflower, which opens for one short hour after dusk. Niko, what are the signs?

NIKO: The petals loosen, then curl outward. Please keep your voice low.

VENN: The microphone is low. The host is not.

ASHA: I am whispering.

NIKO: The flower responds to temperature and light, not conversation.

FIA: I am calling from the garden gate. I can see it from here.

ASHA: Has it opened?

FIA: No. I hear you asking.

NIKO: It may still need time.

[SFX: A petal creaks softly.]

ASHA: We heard a movement. Was that the flower?

NIKO: It could be a leaf.

VENN: I have isolated the sound. It is a moth touching the microphone.

ASHA: We will not mislabel it.

FIA: The flower has a pale edge now.

NIKO: That is the outer petal loosening. We are close.

ASHA: Our listeners are holding still. The garden is quiet.

VENN: The station monitor is still transmitting our breathing.

NIKO: Slow breaths are fine.

ASHA: We will describe what we see in detail. The bud is shaped like a folded fan, its pale tip angled toward the moon.

NIKO: Please stop describing it.

ASHA: Why?

NIKO: Every time you narrate, your voice rises. The flower is not reacting, but the listeners need a quiet moment to hear the insects.

VENN: The petals are beginning to open.

[SFX: Gentle unfurling, barely audible.]

ASHA: It's happening. The flower is opening.

NIKO: You spoke.

ASHA: I did.

VENN: The microphone picked up the chair.

FIA: I can still see it. The petals are wider.

NIKO: We should leave it to open naturally.

ASHA: How long does it take?

NIKO: Between ten and twenty minutes.

ASHA: We have twelve minutes in our broadcast.

VENN: Then we can stay on air without talking.

ASHA: That is unusual for a live nature program.

NIKO: It is more unusual to fill every second with commentary.

FIA: I will hold the line open.

[SFX: Insects, slow breaths, distant leaves. One minute passes in a quiet bed of sound.]

[SFX: A dead-air alarm chirps in the studio headphones.]

VENN: The automation thinks we have gone silent. It is about to play the station's sponsor spot.

ASHA: The spot has a brass jingle. It will drown out the flower.

NIKO: The bloom is almost open. Can we keep the feed alive without speaking?

VENN: I can send the insect microphone as a room-tone bed and hold the sponsor cue.

[SFX: The alarm stops. Insects fill the broadcast channel; a tiny relay clicks.]

ASHA: The program is still on air, and no announcer has spoken.

FIA: The station's listener line is quiet too. I can hear the petals.

VENN: The sponsor will receive its credit after the observation. The automated jingle is safely muted.

[SFX: A petal opens; a soft pulse of insects continues.]

NIKO: There. The flower has opened while the signal stayed alive.

ASHA: The radio can carry silence as long as someone remembers to mute the promo.

VENN: The flower is fully open.

ASHA: I will describe it after one more silent minute.

NIKO: Good.

[SFX: Quiet night garden; a moth flutters once.]

ASHA: The petals have opened into a white star, and the scent is faintly sweet. The flower will close at dawn.

FIA: I saw it too. The silence made it easier to notice.

VENN: The recording has captured the insect chorus and the gentle petal movement.

NIKO: We can end before the flower closes.

ASHA: This has been Night Bloom, brought to you by

[SFX: A brass jingle erupts, then cuts off after one blaring note.]

VENN: The sponsor has bought the closing silence too.

ASHA: Excellent. Please enjoy our premium silence.

FIA: Can I get it without the adverts?

[SFX: Recorder clicks off; insects remain in the garden.]
