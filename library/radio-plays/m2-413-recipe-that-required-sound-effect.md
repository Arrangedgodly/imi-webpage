# The Recipe That Required a Sound Effect

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio cooking shows that use theatrical sound design and sponsor stunts instead of clear cooking instructions*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-014*

Cast: Host Dendi, an orangutan; chef Koro, a macaque; sound artist Pavi, a gibbon; caller Teni, a capuchin.

[SFX: STUDIO KITCHEN. PAN SIZZLES. A COCONUT DROPS ON WOOD.] 

DENDI: Welcome to Stir It Live. Tonight we make toasted seed cakes.

KORO: The seeds are already in the pan. Keep the heat low and stir until they smell warm.

PAVI: We need a thunder sound when the batter goes in. The sponsor sent a coconut.

KORO: Why is a coconut in a seed cake recipe?

PAVI: It makes a big boom on the table.

[SFX: COCONUT ROLLS; THUNK AGAINST A CABINET.] 

TENI: I am following at home. Do I add coconut?

KORO: No. Use seed meal, water, and a spoonful of syrup. The coconut is a sound prop.

DENDI: The recipe card says the cake should arrive with a dramatic impact.

KORO: That is not a cooking step. Pour the batter slowly into the warm pan.

[SFX: BATTER POURS. IT SIZZLES SOFTLY.] 

PAVI: That sound is too small. The audience expects a crash.

DENDI: We can make the crash after the cooking.

DENDI: The sponsor card says "crackling coconut crunch."

KORO: The coconut is whole, and the seeds are already toasted.

PAVI: The card arrived before the recipe was chosen.

TENI: That explains why my grocery list had coconut written in a different color.

KORO: Cross it out. The seed cakes do not need a coconut, and the sound does not belong in the pan.

[SFX: PENCIL SCRATCHES THROUGH THE WORD.]

DENDI: We have corrected the list before anyone cracks a tooth.

KORO: No. We should describe what is happening, not hide it with noise.

TENI: My pan is quiet. I thought I had done it wrong.

KORO: A quiet pan means the heat may be too low. You should hear a gentle sizzle, not a bang.

[SFX: FLAME KNOB CLICKS; SIZZLE GROWS SLIGHTLY.] 

DENDI: The studio cake is setting at the edge. The center is still wet.

PAVI: The sponsor's coconut is rolling toward the microphone cable.

[SFX: COCONUT ROLLS FASTER; CABLE TUGS; MIC STAND WOBBLES.] 

KORO: Catch it before it pulls the mixer down.

[SFX: HAND CATCHES COCONUT. MIC STAND SETTLES.] 

DENDI: The sound effect has become a kitchen hazard.

PAVI: I can record a coconut thump in the sound booth after we finish.

KORO: The cake needs turning. Use the flat spatula and lift from the edge.

[SFX: SPATULA SLIDES; CAKE TURNS WITH A SOFT TAP.] 

TENI: Mine broke in half.

KORO: That is all right. The first side may have set too long. Press the two pieces together while the center is warm.

DENDI: Our studio cake has a split too.

PAVI: I can add a crack sound.

DENDI: Let it be a real crack. It smells good.

[SFX: PLATE SLIDES UNDER CAKE; SEEDS SPRINKLE.] 

KORO: The second side is browned. Turn off the heat and let it rest for a minute.

TENI: The cake is holding together now.

PAVI: I have the coconut in the booth. Ready for the big impact?

KORO: Not under the recipe. The sound can come after the food is plated, as a joke.

DENDI: Listeners have made the cake. Now we can hear the sponsor's coconut.

[SFX: COCONUT DROPS INTO A PILLOW; A TINY, SAD THUMP.] 

PAVI: The pillow softened it.

TENI: That sounds like a seed cake landing on a cushion.

KORO: It is a coconut in a pillow. Eat the cake while it is warm.

DENDI: Recipe recap: low heat, gentle sizzle, turn once, rest before serving.

PAVI: No crash required.

[SFX: FORK TAPS PLATE; HAPPY HUMS AROUND THE STUDIO.] 

TENI: Mine tastes good, and the radio did not frighten my pan.

DENDI: Stir It Live ends with a quiet bite and one unnecessary coconut.

[SFX: COCONUT ROLLS INTO THE PILLOW AGAIN; EVERYONE LAUGHS.]
