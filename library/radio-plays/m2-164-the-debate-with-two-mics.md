# The Debate with Two Mics

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio political debates, strict response clocks, canned applause, and candidates who answer a different question*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
MODERATOR VALE, a ring-tailed lemur
CANDIDATE PIP, a capuchin
CANDIDATE RO, a macaque

[DEBATE THEME. APPLAUSE BUTTON.]

VALE: Welcome to the Kettle City mayoral debate. Each candidate gets thirty seconds per answer. Our first topic is the pothole outside the library. Candidate Pip?

PIP: Our city deserves a future in which every road reflects our values. I will create a bold vision for smoother journeys.

VALE: That was twenty seconds. How will you repair the pothole?

PIP: We will listen to the road.

RO: You cannot listen to a pothole.

PIP: You can listen to residents who have fallen into it.

VALE: Candidate Ro, your plan?

RO: I'll put a cone around it today, ask the road crew to fill it tomorrow, and post a detour sign until the patch is set.

[APPLAUSE BUTTON FIRES TOO EARLY.]

VALE: Please wait for the moderator before applauding.

RO: That was the applause button, not the audience.

VALE: Our second topic is late buses. Candidate Ro?

RO: We should publish the real schedule and add a bus on the morning route. I will ride the route this week.

PIP: I support a bold, transformative approach to punctuality.

VALE: What does that mean for the current schedule?

PIP: The schedule must be transformed.

VALE: Is that a yes to publishing it?

PIP: It is a yes to a future where schedules are visible.

RO: The schedules are already posted in the station.

PIP: Then we will post them more visibly.

[THE APPLAUSE BUTTON FIRES AGAIN.]

VALE: The device appears to favor Candidate Pip. We will unplug it.

[BUTTON UNPLUGS. A SMALL CLUNK.]

VALE: Final question: how will you keep the debate within its time limit?

RO: Answer the question and stop when the bell rings.

PIP: I believe time is a shared resource. We need a community conversation that lets every voice be heard.

VALE: You have twelve seconds left.

PIP: And in those twelve seconds-

[BELL RINGS.]

PIP: I yield to the future.

RO: That means he ran out of time.

VALE: We'll take questions from the radio audience. Caller, what would you like to ask?

CALLER: The pothole is full of water. Can we put a cone there before somebody steps in?

RO: Yes. I have one in my bag.

PIP: I support the cone as a symbol of shared safety.

VALE: Does either candidate have a cone?

[RO SETS DOWN A CONE. IT TOPPLES.]

RO: The pavement slopes. I'll put it on the curb and mark the dry edge.

CALLER: I can help hold it until the road crew arrives.

PIP: I'll take a photo beside it.

VALE: The caller has one more question: who will check that the road crew arrives?

RO: I'll meet them at eight and make sure the repair is safe before traffic returns.

PIP: I will support the repair from a position that photographs well.

VALE: Candidate Pip, will you also help with the cone?

PIP: I can hold it for the camera.

CALLER: Hold it until the crew gets here.

PIP: I can do both.

VALE: The pothole has a cone, the detour is marked, and the crew has been called. Our debate produced one immediate action.

PIP: I will stand beside the cone and listen to the road.

RO: I'll go ride the bus route.

VALE: That concludes the debate. The candidates have left by their preferred method of transportation.

[BUS BRAKES OUTSIDE. FOOTSTEPS.]

VALE: Candidate Pip is waiting for the bus with the cone. Candidate Ro has walked to the bus stop to check the schedule.

[DEBATE THEME RETURNS.]

VALE: The cone remains at the library. It has not declared a platform.
