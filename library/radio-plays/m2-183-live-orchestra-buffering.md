# The Live Orchestra Is Buffering

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: classical radio broadcasts that treat every tiny performance glitch as a historic premiere*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-007*

CAST
HOST ARIN, a gibbon
CONDUCTOR VOSS, a gorilla
ANNOUNCER MIRA, a macaque
ENGINEER TUP, a capuchin

[SFX: ORCHESTRA TUNING. A SINGLE OBOE NOTE HANGS IN THE AIR.]

ARIN: Welcome to the live broadcast of the Hilltop Chamber Orchestra. The players are ready, the conductor is ready, and the network is waiting for the signal.

TUP: The signal is not ready.

MIRA: The red light says we are live.

TUP: The red light is part of the rehearsal board.

[THE ORCHESTRA STOPS TUNING. A CHAIR CREAKS.]

VOSS: Is the broadcast on?

ARIN: We are now broadcasting the question of whether we are broadcasting.

[THE BROADCAST LIGHT TURNS ON. A SHORT BEEP SOUNDS IN THE CONTROL ROOM.]

TUP: We are live now.

VOSS: Are the microphones on?

MIRA: Only the room microphone.

[THE ORCHESTRA TUNES AGAIN. THE OBOE PLAYS A SCALE. THE ROOM MIC SENDS IT TO THE MONITOR A BEAT LATE.] 

ARIN: We have a second oboe.

VOSS: We have one oboe and a delayed speaker.

### Scene One: The Opening

[MIRA TAPS THE GLASS.]

MIRA: We have a three-second delay. Begin when I raise my hand.

[VOSS RAISES HIS BATON. TUP'S SIGNAL LIGHT TURNS GREEN. THE ORCHESTRA PLAYS A MAJESTIC CHORD.]

ARIN: The program has begun.

TUP: The signal is stable.

[THE CHORD ENDS. A SECOND CHORD PLAYS THROUGH THE MONITOR A BEAT LATE.]

VOSS: We have an echo.

TUP: That is the monitor return. It was not in the auditorium.

ARIN: The hall has answered the orchestra.

VOSS: The hall has one set of musicians.

### Scene Two: The Intermission

[THE MUSIC CONTINUES. A PAGE TURNS LOUDLY IN THE FRONT ROW.]

ARIN: A page has turned. We remain with the music.

MIRA: You have made it sound like an announcement.

ARIN: I am giving the audience context.

[TUP LOWERS THE ROOM MICROPHONE. THE PAGE TURN BECOMES QUIET. THE CELLOS ENTER, BUT THEIR CHANNEL IS MUTED.] 

VOSS: Cellos, we cannot hear you.

TUP: The board says they are muted.

[HE OPENS THE CELLO CHANNEL. IT RETURNS WITH A LOUD HUM. THE PLAYERS STOP.]

[TUP SWITCHES THE CELLO CHANNEL OFF, THEN MUTES THE ROOM MIC. THE HUM STOPS. HE BRINGS THE CELLO FADER UP SLOWLY. A CLEAN LOW NOTE SOUNDS.] 

VOSS: We have the instrument.

ARIN: The audience has the note.

TUP: And the ventilation fan is no longer soloing.

TUP: Now we can hear the electricity.

VOSS: Can you remove the hum without removing the cellos?

TUP: Working on it.

[MIRA CUES THE ANNOUNCER.]

MIRA: We will take a short intermission while the engineer restores the cello signal.

ARIN: In radio, intermission means we keep speaking.

MIRA: It can mean we let the room settle.

[THEY STOP TALKING. A QUIET PAGE TURN, THEN A CELLO NOTE.]

### Scene Three: The Coda

[TUP RESTORES THE CELLO CHANNEL. THE ORCHESTRA RESUMES AT THE FINAL MOVEMENT. THE MONITOR RETURN IS OFF.] 

VOSS: From the top of the movement.

ARIN: We have already broadcast the opening.

VOSS: Then from the top of the coda.

[THE ORCHESTRA PLAYS. THE CELLOS ARE CLEAR; A PHONE VIBRATES IN THE CONTROL BOOTH.] 

TUP: That is mine.

[MIRA PUTS THE PHONE IN A METAL DRAWER. THE MUSIC CONTINUES TO A CLEAN FINAL CHORD.]

ARIN: The Hilltop Chamber Orchestra has completed the performance.

VOSS: Without an encore?

TUP: The delay is clear. We can play the recorded coda if the audience wants one.

[THE ORCHESTRA PLAYS A SHORT ENCORE. THIS TIME THE MONITOR RETURN IS SILENT.] 

### Tag

[THE LAST NOTE FADES. TUP'S GREEN SIGNAL LIGHT TURNS RED.]

MIRA: Now we are off air.

ARIN: Was that the audience applauding?

VOSS: No. It was the ventilation fan.

[SFX: THE FAN CLICKS OFF. A SMALL, REAL AUDIENCE APPLAUSE RISES.]
