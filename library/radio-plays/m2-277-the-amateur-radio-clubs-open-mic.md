# The Amateur Radio Club's Open Mic

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur radio contests, call-sign etiquette, and hobbyists who treat accidental cross-talk as a historic transmission*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST ODO, a capuchin
CLUB PRESIDENT TAVI, a lemur
OPERATOR SAVA, a macaque
CALLER BRAM, a gibbon
TECH PIP, a squirrel monkey
DISTANT OPERATOR, a lemur on the coastal frequency
VOICE A, a macaque using the shared frequency
VOICE B, a gibbon using the shared frequency

[SFX: STATIC, DIAL TURNS.]

ODO: We are at the Canopy Amateur Radio Club's open mic night. Tavi has brought the club's antique receiver.

TAVI: It can pick up stations from the coast.

SAVA: It can pick up the kitchen timer.

[SFX: TIMER DINGS THROUGH STATIC.]

TAVI: That is a signal from the kitchen.

PIP: The receiver has an open wire at the antenna.

TAVI: It has history.

PIP: The wire has a break.

ODO: The club will now begin its call-sign contest.

SAVA: First rule: listen before you transmit.

BRAM: I have been listening for forty minutes.

TAVI: You haven't called in.

BRAM: I was listening.

SAVA: That is the rule.

Odo gives the cue. Sava transmits her call sign. A burst of static covers the last letters.

[SFX: STATIC SWELLS.]

SAVA: I will repeat.

PIP: Wait. Another operator is using the frequency.

[SFX: TWO VOICES CROSS.]

VOICE A: Kilo - 

VOICE B:  - Hotel - 

ODO: We have a duet.

TAVI: That is not a duet. It is interference.

BRAM: They are harmonizing.

PIP: The antenna is touching the metal gutter.

TAVI: The gutter has excellent reception.

Sava moves the receiver. The voices separate, but the timer rings again. Tavi tries to count it as a call sign.

TAVI: That could be T-I-M-E-R.

SAVA: It is the kitchen.

BRAM: The kitchen is on the air.

Pip secures the antenna with a wooden spacer. The static drops. Tavi proudly says the club is now reaching distant listeners. A voice answers from the building's basement.

VOICE (FILTERED): This is the boiler room. Please stop transmitting into the thermostat.

ODO: We have reached the boiler room.

PIP: The antenna wire runs alongside the building controls.

TAVI: It has connected the club with infrastructure.
 
Bram asks for a second attempt at the call-sign contest. He takes the headset, waits through a full minute of static, then hears a weather station testing its microphone.

[SFX: STATIC, A SINGLE TEST TONE.]

BRAM: I heard the tone before the voice.

SAVA: That counts as listening.

TAVI: The contest is for a complete call sign.

BRAM: I can give the tone a call sign.

PIP: No.

The club places a paper map over the antenna route and sees it running beside a metal gutter. They move the wire away from the gutter, secure it with the spacer, then test again. The next transmission arrives clear enough to log without guessing at missing letters.


SAVA: It has connected us with a thermostat.

Bram asks to make the next call. He reads his call sign so slowly that the radio times out.

[SFX: TRANSMISSION BEEP, CUTS OFF.]

BRAM: I was giving the letters room.

SAVA: The machine needs the whole call before the timer.

ODO: We have heard each letter, but not in the same transmission.

TAVI: Can they be assembled later?

PIP: This is radio, not a puzzle.

They take turns checking the frequency, waiting for silence, and speaking briefly. Sava makes a clean call. A distant operator answers with a clear return call. The club cheers, then remembers the microphone is open.

[SFX: CHEERING BLEEDS INTO TRANSMISSION.]

DISTANT OPERATOR: Are you celebrating or transmitting?

SAVA: Both, but separately next time.

Tavi asks Pip to log the contact. Pip writes the time, frequency, and call signs. The timer dings again.

ODO: The kitchen has made the final contact.

TAVI: We should answer.

SAVA: We should turn off the timer.

[SFX: TIMER SWITCH CLICKS OFF.]

PIP: The club has completed one clean exchange, one boiler-room complaint, and one kitchen transmission.

ODO: This is the Amateur Radio Club. We sign off after we listen.

[SFX: STATIC FALLS TO QUIET.]
