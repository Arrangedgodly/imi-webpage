# The Night Market Broadcast Loses Its Bell

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live market radio that mistakes ambiance for a countdown*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-018*

Cast: Host Mara, a macaque; sound engineer Odo, a gibbon; vendor Pip, a capuchin; caller Vex, an orangutan; night watch Seli, a lemur.

SFX: Night market after closing. A distant stall bell rings once, then a cart wheel squeaks.

MARA: You're listening to Market After Dark, the only broadcast that stays open after the stalls close. Odo, give us the famous bell.

ODO: The bell is missing.

MARA: Our opening bell?

ODO: The one we ring at the top of every hour. The hook is empty, and the sound-effects tray has a bell-shaped dust mark.

VEX, on phone: This is a crisis. The market cannot be open without its bell.

PIP: The market is closed. I put my bell in the fruit crate so nobody would mistake my cart for an open stall.

MARA: That's a sensible place to keep it. Which crate?

PIP: The one beside the table. I moved the crate when the rain came in.

SFX: Wooden crate slides. Something metal rolls inside and gives a muffled ring.

ODO: There it is. I can mike the crate and make our hourly signal.

MARA: Don't. It is still packed with pears. We need a bell, not a crate that sounds like a haunted pantry.

VEX: Can we call it a new market tradition? The crate of mystery rings at unpredictable intervals.

PIP: Then people will think my pears are haunted and ask for a discount.

SFX: A pear rolls; Odo catches it against the table.

ODO: The bell is under the folded canvas. If I lift the crate's corner, Pip can take it out.

PIP: Wait until I move the pears. They're ripe, and the floor is uneven.

MARA: We can keep the broadcast going without an hourly bell. Odo, give us the sound of the market settling.

ODO: Cart wheels cooling, awning ropes, one sleepy pigeon.

SFX: Rope creaks; a pigeon coos. A small bell rings clearly.

PIP: I found it in the crate. The handle is bent, but it rings.

VEX: That is our cue. Ring it for the audience.

PIP: Only once, and after the pears are back in place.

MARA: The stall's owner decides when the bell rings. This is a market, not a game show.

SFX: Pears settle gently in the crate. Pip rings the handbell once.

ODO: Clean tone. No crate resonance.

VEX: A successful recovery. Could we ring it again for the replay?

PIP: No. That one meant I was ready to open tomorrow. The market is still shut tonight.

MARA: Then our close is the honest sound of a closed market. Odo, take the bell off the live mic and hang it on Pip's hook.

SFX: Hook taps. Bell rests in place.

MARA: Before morning, we should check the hook. We will not announce an opening at midnight.

VEX: Can I say "the market sleeps"?

PIP: You can say it, but don't put a blanket over my cart.

SFX: Cloth rustles as Odo folds the broadcast blanket into its case.


MARA: Before we finish, let us make sure the bell is hanging safely. Pip, does the bent handle catch on the hook?

PIP: It slides on, but the hook points outward. If the cart bumps the wall, the bell could fall.

ODO: Pip, hold the bell while I turn the hook inward with the small wrench. The market board has a spare hook in the maintenance box.

VEX: A repair during the closing broadcast. Our listeners can hear craftsmanship.

MARA: They can hear the wrench, but not the repair itself. Tell them what you are doing, and keep the bell away from the microphone.

SFX: Wrench turns twice. Hook taps the board.

ODO: The hook points toward the wall now. Pip, test the handle without ringing it.

PIP: I've hung it back, and it stays. I'll take the bell down in the morning and ring it after I open the stall.

VEX: No midnight reprise?

PIP: No midnight opening.

TAG

ODO: The bell is secure.

MARA: The market is closed.

VEX: And the pears are merely pears.
