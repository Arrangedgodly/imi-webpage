# Night Watch Forecast

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Shipping forecasts and coastal radio bulletins*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Lark, a capuchin announcer; Marmot, a ground squirrel forecaster; Miri, a marmoset skipper; Otto, a macaque harbor patrol; Old Finch, a gibbon ferry pilot.

[SFX: WIND THROUGH A HARBOR MAST. A BELL RINGS THREE TIMES.]

ANNOUNCER LARK: This is the Night Watch Forecast, broadcast from Gull Point to vessels in the western channel. The tide is turning. The fog is sitting low. The moon is pretending not to be involved.

WEATHER MARMOT: Pressure is falling over the outer shoals. Wind east by southeast, strong enough to take a hat and make it somebody else's problem. Visibility, two lengths of fishing boat, or one optimistic seal.

LARK: We have a small vessel reporting a green light where no green light should be. Caller, identify yourself.

CALLER: This is Miri, skipper of the *Tern's Errand*. We are near the bell buoy. Our charts say the channel is clear, but we keep hearing rocks beneath us.

MARMOT: Rocks do not usually announce themselves.

MIRI: These do. Tap, tap, scrape.

[SFX: RADIO STATIC. THREE TAPS, THEN A LONG SCRAPE.] 

LARK: We heard that. It came through the transmitter.

MARMOT: A seal is knocking a shell against the buoy chain. The sound carries across the fog. That green light may be the floating kelp markers, not a channel lamp.

MIRI: We use those markers to find the eelgrass beds. The current has pulled them loose.

LARK: All vessels, hold position outside the shoal. Harbor patrol, can you confirm the markers are drifting?

PATROL OTTO: Confirmed. I am looking at three green lights. One is attached to a barrel, one is attached to a very annoyed seal, and one is a shrimp boat's cabin lamp.

MARMOT: The shrimp boat is heading east. The shoal is under Miri. She should turn west and follow the bell buoy until she hears two bells close together.

MIRI: I hear one.

OTTO: Then you're still outside the inner channel. I can guide you with the patrol horn.

[SFX: TWO LOW HORN BLASTS. DISTANTLY, THE BUOY ANSWERS WITH A CLANG.] 

LARK: The forecast remains fog, east wind, falling pressure. To crews in the west channel, the bell buoy is your friend. The loose green lights are not. Please do not follow anything that blinks back.

MIRI: Copy. Turning west. The tapping is moving away.

MARMOT: That will be the seal taking its shell with it.

OTTO: I have the floating markers in tow. We will put them back at dawn, when we can see where the grass begins.

LARK: One further report. The *Tern's Errand* has cleared the shoal and is returning to harbor. No damage. The crew says the fog lifted just enough to show a line of pale water. That is not a safe passage. That is moonlight on the eelgrass.

MIRI: We nearly steered by the prettiest thing in the bay.

MARMOT: The bay is full of pretty mistakes.

LARK: Night Watch closing. The eastern swell is moderate. The western channel is closed until sunrise. Otto will reset the markers with the harbor crew. Miri will bring the shell back to its beach instead of keeping it as a souvenir.

MIRI: It is a very nice shell.

OTTO: It has a very nice owner.

LARK: We have another vessel calling. This is Old Finch on the north buoy. I can see the patrol light, but I cannot hear it over the foghorn from the fish market.

MARMOT: The foghorn is sounding the lunch signal. The market has moved its bell tower closer to the water after the pier repair.

OTTO: I can relay the signal with the boat horn. Old Finch, listen for one short blast, then wait for the buoy. Do not answer the market bell.

OLD FINCH: Understood. One short blast. The buoy answers twice. The market bell is now offering soup.

LARK: The soup is not a navigational aid. We will say that again at tomorrow's harbor meeting.

MIRI: I can help replace the markers in the morning. The *Tern's Errand* has a flat deck and a good view of the grass line.

OTTO: Bring gloves. The marker chain is covered with barnacles.

MIRI: My deckhand is a crab. He'll consider that a welcome committee.

[SFX: A SMALL SEAL BARKS. SHELL TAPS TWICE AGAINST THE DECK, THEN SPLASHES INTO WATER.] 

LARK: The tide is turning. The fog is thinning. And the green lights are back where the eelgrass ends. Good night to every vessel still listening.

MIRI: I can see the markers.

OTTO: I can see the shrimp boat.

MARMOT: Can the shrimp boat see us?

OLD FINCH: It can hear the soup bell.

LARK: That is not our emergency signal.

OLD FINCH: Then why does it sound so urgent?

MIRI: Because someone burned the broth.

[THE BUOY BELL RINGS THREE TIMES. TRANSMITTER FADES.]
