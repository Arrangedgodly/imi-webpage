# Echo on Platform Four

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Serialized radio detective adventures with cliffhanger pacing*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Inspector Vela, a baboon detective; Nix, a squirrel monkey signal operator; Dodo, a gibbon train guard; Aunt Tern, an elderly marmoset vendor.

[SFX: NIGHT TRAIN BRAKES. A STATION CLOCK TICKS TOO LOUDLY.] 

ANNOUNCER: Previously on Echo on Platform Four, Inspector Vela found a silver button in an empty train car. Tonight, the station clock strikes thirteen, and the midnight train arrives without a driver.

[SFX: CLOCK STRIKES TWELVE, THEN ONE EXTRA.] 

NIX: Inspector, platform four is calling your name.

VELA: Platforms don't call.

NIX: This one did. The announcement speaker said "Vela" twice.

VELA: Did the station operator hear it?

NIX: I am the station operator. I heard the echo.

[SFX: SPEAKER CRACKLES, "VELA... VELA..."] 

VELA: Trace it.

NIX: The voice came from the empty ticket booth, bounced off the tunnel, and returned through the platform speaker.

VELA: That explains the echo. It does not explain the train.

[TRAIN DOORS HISS OPEN.] 

NARRATOR: The train stood silent. Every window was dark. On the driver's seat lay a second silver button, wet with rain. Vela climbed aboard. As a baboon she could grip the overhead rails, even when the car rocked. Nix stayed on the platform and read the signal board.

NIX: No route is scheduled. The tunnel switch is set for maintenance.

VELA: Then this car came from the yard.

DODO: Not the yard. I pushed it out of the museum siding. It carries the old station speakers. The museum is restoring the car and returned it to the wrong track.

VELA: Why is there a button on the seat?

DODO: The restoration team lost a set of uniform buttons. The silver one belongs to the driver who used to work this platform.

VELA: And why does the speaker say my name?

DODO: Because you were on the station tour recording. The sound system is playing a test loop. You said your name at the start.

NIX: The test loop is still running. The station clock is one minute fast, so it struck thirteen because the bell mechanism is stuck between gears.

VELA: Three mysteries, one maintenance list.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS INTO TUNNEL. A WINDY ECHO.] 

NARRATOR: Aunt Tern, who sold roasted seeds by the stairs, had been listening. She remembered the old driver, a marmoset called Red, who always wore a silver button at his collar.

TERN: Red used to stop the train if a bat crossed the tunnel. He said the dark belonged to the bats first.

VELA: The museum car was moved during the bat survey. Did anyone check the tunnel?

NIX: The maintenance light shows movement near the switch.

VELA: Not a ghost, then. A bat.

[SFX: SMALL WINGS FLUTTER. A SWITCH CLICKS.] 

NARRATOR: A colony of bats had settled above the tunnel lamp. Their wings crossed the motion sensor and triggered the old test circuit. Dodo switched the track light to a dim red lamp. The bats remained in place. Nix turned off the loop and reset the clock gear.

DODO: The car will go back to the museum siding at first light.

VELA: And the buttons?

TERN: I'll give them to the museum. Red would like them displayed where people can learn why he stopped the train.

NIX: The platform is quiet again. No one is calling your name.

VELA: Good. I was beginning to charge for appearances.

ANNOUNCER: The midnight train has been accounted for. The bats have their tunnel, the clock tells the right hour, and Inspector Vela has found her next case: who ordered a station clock with thirteen numbers?

VELA: Nobody. The clock face is missing one.

ANNOUNCER: Next time, on Echo on Platform Four.

VELA: The clock is right now.

NIX: It is still thirteen minutes past twelve.

VELA: That is a time.

DODO: The museum car is going back to the siding.

TERN: I will put the buttons in a tin.

NIX: Label the tin.

TERN: "Buttons, not ghosts."

VELA: Better than "miscellaneous."

[TRAIN BELL. BATS WING SOFTLY. END.]
