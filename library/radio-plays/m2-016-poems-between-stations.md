# Poems Between Stations

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Public radio poetry hours and earnest literary discussion*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Host Ada, a langur poet; Fenn, a macaque sound engineer; Bibi, a capuchin caller; Old Moss, an orangutan busker.

[INTRO: A BOWL OF WATER IS STRUCK WITH A SPOON. A LONG PAUSE.] 

HOST ADA: Welcome to Poems Between Stations, the hour where we read new verse and leave enough silence for the listener to decide whether the broadcast has stopped. I'm Ada. Tonight's subject is home. Our first poem comes from Bibi, a capuchin who writes on fallen leaves.

BIBI: "The branch bends at dusk. / My grandmother saves the warm end. / I sleep facing rain."

ADA: Thank you. That's a fine image. Fenn, how long was the silence after the poem?

FENN: Eight seconds. The station manager thought the line dropped.

ADA: It was a poem.

FENN: The manager asked me to add a small chime after every stanza, just in case.

ADA: Poetry is not an elevator ride.

[SFX: PHONE LINE. A CHIME SOUNDS ACCIDENTALLY.] 

ADA: Our next caller, Old Moss, has written a poem about the bus stop where he sleeps between songs.

MOSS: "The bus is late. / I count the pigeons twice. / One pigeon counts me."

ADA: That last line changes who is watching whom.

MOSS: It is a very judgmental pigeon.

FENN: We have a message from the manager. He says the poem needs a clearer ending.

MOSS: The pigeon flew away.

ADA: That is the ending.

FENN: He wants a resolution.

MOSS: I got on the bus.

ADA: There. The poem has moved.

[A BELL RINGS. THE ROOM'S DOOR OPENS.] 

ADA: We have a visitor. It's Juno, the station manager. He has brought a red pencil.

JUNO: I just want to know what the listener should feel.

ADA: What did you feel?

JUNO: I felt the bus was late.

MOSS: It was.

JUNO: Then we agree.

ADA: Let's hear the poem again, with the sound of the stop. Fenn, can you play the field recording?

[SFX: DISTANT BUS BRAKES, PIGEONS, A CAN ROLLS ALONG CONCRETE.] 

MOSS: "The bus is late. / I count the pigeons twice. / One pigeon counts me."

JUNO: I heard the can after the last line. It sounded like someone leaving.

MOSS: It was my lunch tin. I always drop it.

ADA: The recording belongs with the poem. But the tin does not need to be a metaphor.

JUNO: Could it be a small metaphor?

MOSS: It's a tin. I keep peanuts in it.

ADA: And now we know what the poem leaves out. The empty space gives the listener a place to stand. It does not mean we should fill it with a lunch tin.

FENN: The station feed is still live. The manager has put a chime in the silence again.

[CHIME.] 

ADA: Juno, please remove the chime.

JUNO: I was testing it.

MOSS: We can all hear that.

ADA: We'll end with one more piece. Mine is about a branch that broke in a storm and the nest that moved to the next tree.

ADA: "A split in the bark. / Rain keeps a seat for the bird. / Morning finds a fork."

MOSS: I think the bird found a safer tree.

ADA: It did. That may be the poem's ending, even if the fork stays.

FENN: I have muted the chime.

JUNO: I have stopped writing endings in red.

ADA: Poems Between Stations will return next week. Bring a poem, not an explanation of what everyone should feel. Leave the silence where it belongs.

JUNO: Should I announce the silence on the schedule?

ADA: No. It will arrive when it arrives.

FENN: The program guide needs a duration.

MOSS: Put down "long enough to hear the bus."

JUNO: That is not a number.

ADA: It is how we will know the poem is over.

MOSS: The bus is here now.

JUNO: Is that the bus from your poem?

MOSS: No, this one is on time.

ADA: A rare and moving turn.

[THE WATER BOWL RINGS ONCE, THEN SETTLES. END MUSIC.]
