# The Telescope's Unwanted Guest

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio astronomy demonstrations that mistake local interference for an alien message and stage responses before checking the source*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Astronomer Belu, a capuchin; host Naro, a macaque; technician Viri, a lemur; student Omi, a gibbon.

[SFX: RADIO TELESCOPE MOTOR TURNS. HEADPHONES HISS.]

NARO: We are listening to the night sky. A repeating signal has appeared on the monitor.

OMI: It is three clicks, a pause, then three more.

BELU: It could be interference from a nearby transmitter. We need to check before calling it a signal from space.

OMI: The sponsor wants to give the visitor a name and play a reply tone after the clicks.

BELU: A name would imply a source we have not found. The clicks stopped when the telescope motor stopped, then matched the maintenance gate.

VIRI: I can isolate the gate frequency and log the time it sends its locking pulse.

NARO: If we have that, we can demonstrate the cause without making a character out of it.

OMI: The reply tone is in the soundboard. I'll leave it muted.

[SFX: BUTTON CLICKS. THE TONE CUTS OFF BEFORE IT PLAYS.]

BELU: That keeps the recording useful. A listener can hear the three clicks and the quiet motor test.

NARO: We will call it interference, then.

NARO: The segment title is "The Visitor Who Knocked."

VIRI: The telescope is pointed at the north field. The pattern started when the motor turned.

[SFX: MOTOR TURNS. THREE CLICKS, PAUSE, THREE CLICKS.]

OMI: There it is again.

BELU: Viri, stop the motor for a moment.

[SFX: MOTOR STOPS. CLICKS STOP.]

NARO: The visitor has gone quiet.

BELU: Or the telescope motor is making the sound.

VIRI: The click relay is on the same power strip as the receiver. It may be sending a pulse into the audio line.

NARO: We can ask the sky a question and wait for a reply.

BELU: That would make the recording harder to diagnose.

OMI: I have a question ready.

BELU: Save it until we know what we're hearing.

[SFX: POWER STRIP SWITCHES. RECEIVER HUM CONTINUES, MOTOR OFF.]

VIRI: I separated the relay power. Start the motor once.

[SFX: MOTOR TURNS. NO CLICKS. HISS ONLY.]

OMI: The clicking stopped.

NARO: The signal was local.

BELU: We have ruled out the relay, but we still need to check the radio band for other equipment.

VIRI: The maintenance shed has a timed gate. It sends a pulse when it locks.

[SFX: FAR-OFF GATE CLUNKS. THREE CLICKS SOUND THROUGH THE RECEIVER.]

OMI: The gate is three hundred meters away.

BELU: The receiver picked up its control signal on a nearby frequency. We can filter that out and compare the sky recording again.

NARO: The audience heard the clicks. We should explain the source.

BELU: Yes. We should not pretend the gate is answering.

[SFX: FILTER KNOB TURNS. HISS NARROWS. MOTOR TURNS AGAIN, NO CLICKS.]

VIRI: Clean pass. The telescope is turning, and the receiver is quiet.

OMI: Can I ask the sky something now?

BELU: You can ask a question, but we may not receive an answer.

NARO: Ask one that fits in a sentence.

OMI: "What is beyond the dark?"

[SFX: SILENCE, HISS, DISTANT NIGHT INSECTS THROUGH AN OPEN WINDOW.]

OMI: No reply.

BELU: There is no expected reply. We are listening for natural radio sources and measuring their patterns.

NARO: The title promised a visitor.

VIRI: It was the gate relay. The visitor was a maintenance schedule.

NARO: We can change the title before the archive file goes up.

OMI: Call it "Three Clicks at the Telescope."

BELU: Add "local gate signal" in the description.

NARO: The telescope is back on the north field. We have a quiet recording.

[SFX: MOTOR TURNS. RECEIVER HISS STAYS EVEN.]

VIRI: No clicks, no relay pulse.

BELU: Save this as the clean baseline.

NARO: And cancel the scripted response.

OMI: I was going to say, "We hear you."

BELU: We do not know who "you" would be.

[SFX: RECORD BUTTON CLICKS OFF. TELESCOPE MOTOR HUMS THROUGH ITS FULL TURN.]
