# The Insect Choir's Sponsor Note

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: wildlife radio performances that ask field researchers to arrange animal calls into a sponsored tune*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-014*

Cast: Host Vela, a lemur; ecologist Turi, a gibbon; recordist Pavo, a macaque; sponsor voice Nemi, a capuchin.

[SFX: NIGHT MEADOW. CRICKETS AND FROGS CALL IN UNEVEN PATTERNS.]

VELA: Tonight the meadow choir will perform the ClearSpring jingle.

TURI: These insects are calling to one another. They are not a choir.

PAVO: I have recorded their natural timing. I did not cue them.

NEMI: The sponsor wants a tag line saying the frogs are "singing for ClearSpring."

TURI: They call when conditions suit them. We cannot claim the sponsor caused a call.

VELA: What if the sponsor tag comes after the recording?

PAVO: Keep the field audio separate from the sponsor mic. The tag can follow without covering the calls.

[SFX: TWO TRACKS PLAY. MEADOW CALLS ON ONE; SPONSOR VOICE ON THE OTHER. PAVO FADES THE SPONSOR TRACK OUT.]

NEMI: The sponsor name drowned out the second frog.

PAVO: The faders are independent. I can move the tag after the field clip.

TURI: The frogs do not need a musical cue or a product mention.

VELA: The introduction will name the location and date. The sponsor comes after the natural recording.

[SFX: CLIPBOARD OPENS. PENCIL WRITES "NIGHT MEADOW, 10:14 P.M."]

NEMI: The ad is shorter than the chorus.

TURI: It is an ad. It can wait until the chorus ends.

NEMI: The sponsor supplied a rhythm chart. We can play the calls on the beat.

TURI: Playing over them will mask the calls and may disturb the animals.

VELA: We can ask the listeners to clap along.

[SFX: TWO CLAPS. A FROG CALLS ONCE; CRICKETS CONTINUE.]

TURI: They did not follow the claps.

NEMI: The brief asks for a musical ending.

PAVO: The recorder has three separate channels: crickets, frogs and stream. We can mix them at their recorded levels.

VELA: Could we add a bell on the last frog call?

TURI: No. That would imply the frog ended on cue.

[SFX: PAVO TURNS A DIAL. STREAM SOUNDS LOWER; CRICKETS CLEAR.]

PAVO: The stream was too loud in the first mix. I can adjust it without changing the call.

NEMI: The sponsor's jingle is eight seconds.

TURI: The call pattern has no eight-second loop. It changes with the temperature and the time.

VELA: I will introduce the recording, then leave it alone.

[SFX: MIC OPENS.]

VELA: This is a field recording of the meadow at night. Crickets and frogs call at their own pace. The sponsor supports the field recorder, not the animals.

[SFX: CRICKETS CALL IN A ROLLING PATTERN. A FROG CALLS TWICE. STREAM RUNS UNDERNEATH.]

NEMI: That is not on the rhythm chart.

TURI: It is the actual recording.

PAVO: The calls overlap, then the crickets continue after the frog stops.

VELA: We can end when the recording ends.

[SFX: MEADOW SOUNDS CONTINUE. A NIGHT BIRD CALLS ONCE.]

NEMI: The sponsor note says to fade after the first frog.

PAVO: That would cut off the cricket bed. The clip is already trimmed at the recorder's stop.

TURI: Let the meadow finish.

[SFX: CRICKETS SLOW. STREAM REMAINS. RECORDER CLICKS OFF.]

VELA: The clip ends there. No arranged note, no applause.

TURI: The field log records temperature and time. Neither one is a beat.

PAVO: I will save the uncut recording beside the broadcast mix.

VELA: The listeners can hear how the calls overlap without our chart.

NEMI: I can change the promotion to "field recording supported by ClearSpring."

TURI: That says what the sponsor did without claiming the frogs performed for them.

[SFX: KEYBOARD TAPS. NEW TAG SAVES.]

PAVO: The original levels are preserved.

VELA: The meadow can keep calling after we leave.

[SFX: MIC CLOSES; NATURAL NIGHT CALLS REMAIN.]
