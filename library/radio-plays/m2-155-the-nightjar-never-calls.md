# The Nightjar Never Calls

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: hushed wildlife field-recording documentaries, reverent narration, and a producer who keeps contaminating the silence*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
EFA, a ring-tailed lemur field naturalist
JUNO, a capuchin sound engineer
NARRATOR, a gibbon presenter

[NIGHT FOREST AMBIENCE. CRICKETS. A DISTANT OWL.]

NARRATOR: We enter the forest at midnight, where every sound reveals a secret life. Efa waits beneath the broad leaves for the rare nightjar, a bird whose call has never been captured by radio.

EFA: I have waited here for three evenings. I can distinguish the nightjar from the kettle frog, the bark beetle, and Juno's stomach.

JUNO: The kettle frog whistles like this.

[JUICE BOX STRAW SQUEAKS.]

EFA: That is the exact sound I mean.

NARRATOR: The forest holds its breath. In these moments, even a leaf may be a clue.

[A LEAF FALLS. IT HITS JUNO'S MICROPHONE. LOUD THUMP.]

JUNO: Sorry. It landed on the mic.

NARRATOR: A dramatic footfall from a hidden forest giant.

EFA: It was one leaf.

NARRATOR: The hidden giant is perhaps the legendary single leaf.

[CRICKETS CONTINUE. A RADIO STATIC BURST.]

JUNO: Our headphones are picking up another station.

VOICE FROM RADIO: You're listening to the midnight gardening hour. Mulch deeply, sleep soundly.

NARRATOR: A message from the nocturnal world. The forest offers advice on how to lie beneath the soil.

EFA: That is the station across the valley. Turn the dial left.

[JUNO TURNS DIAL. STATIC STOPS.]

JUNO: Fixed. Now, quiet.

[A LONG PAUSE. ONE SOFT, HIGH CHIRP.]

EFA: There. That is the nightjar.

NARRATOR: At last, the secret voice of the forest. We have recorded history.

JUNO: That was my jacket zipper. It caught when I leaned forward.

NARRATOR: History is often made by fabric.

EFA: The bird is still out there. Its call has three notes, each lower than the last. The chirp we heard only had one.

NARRATOR: Efa remains patient, and the audience remains patient with her. The canopy gleams under the moon.

[A DRIP LANDS IN A METAL CUP. DING.]

JUNO: That cup is catching water from the branch. I should move it; the drip is clipping the recording.

EFA: Please don't. The nightjar sometimes answers the drip.

[PAUSE. DRIP. PAUSE. A THREE-NOTE BIRD CALL, SOFT AND DESCENDING.]

EFA: There. Cleanly recorded. No zipper, no radio, no giant leaf.

JUNO: It answered the drip.

NARRATOR: A duet between rainfall and bird. We have brought you the rarest broadcast in the forest.

EFA: We should leave the cup here for the bird. It can have the last drop.

[JUNO LIFTS THE CUP; ONE FINAL DRIP FALLS. THE BIRD CALLS THREE NOTES.]

NARRATOR: The nightjar has performed an encore. Our field recording is complete.

JUNO: And the cup is empty.

EFA: We'll leave it anyway. The bird has earned its stage.

JUNO: The cup is dented now. That last drop fell from the branch and hit the rim.

EFA: A perfect percussion note. The bird may be composing around us.

NARRATOR: We should distinguish observation from imagination. The call was real; the composition is ours.

JUNO: I can roll again with a wider microphone. If the bird calls, we catch the whole forest. If it does not, we still have the drip.

EFA: Set the mic beside the fern, not near the nest. We can record from here without crowding it.

[SOFT FOOTSTEPS RETREAT. A DISTANT NIGHTJAR CALL, THEN CRICKETS.]

JUNO: Levels are good. No clipping.

EFA: And no one had to ask for an encore.

NARRATOR: The nightjar has given us a final call at a distance, exactly where it belongs.

NARRATOR: At dawn, we withdraw without disturbing the performer. The nightjar never calls for an audience; it calls because the forest is listening.

[A SECOND CALL. THEN A SOFT METAL DING.]

JUNO: The cup is back under the drip.

EFA: Then the show goes on.

[NIGHT AMBIENCE FADES. A FINAL THREE-NOTE CALL.]
