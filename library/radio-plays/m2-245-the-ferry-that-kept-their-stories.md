# The Ferry That Kept Their Stories

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio oral-history specials, reverent archival narration, and interviewees who remember the same ferry trip in incompatible detail*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-009*

CAST
NARRATOR SORREL, a macaque
MRS. DELL, a capuchin ferry passenger
CAPTAIN RUS, a gibbon skipper
YANI, a ring-tailed lemur deckhand

[SFX: SOFT PIANO THEME. WATER LAPS. FERRY HORN.]

SORREL: For forty years, the little ferry carried residents across the inlet. Tonight, we replay the crossing that everyone remembers differently.

DELL: I remember the horn sounded before we left.

RUS: We sounded it after we left.

YANI: The rope was still tied when the horn went.

[SFX: FERRY HORN. ROPE CREAKS.]

SORREL: We have restored the original recording.

RUS: That is not the original recording.

SORREL: It is a recreation based on three interviews.

DELL: The water was much louder.

[SFX: WATER LAPS, THEN A LARGE SPLASH.]

YANI: That was because Rus dropped the anchor.

RUS: I was checking the depth.

DELL: He was checking whether the anchor still worked.

SORREL: Our archive contains no sound of an anchor.

YANI: You have just recorded it.

[SFX: ANCHOR CLANKS.]

SORREL: The crossing began at dusk. The passengers boarded with baskets, instruments, and a wooden chair.

DELL: The chair was not on board.

YANI: The chair was on board.

RUS: It belonged to the ferry.

DELL: It was my chair.

[SFX: WOODEN CHAIR SCRAPES ACROSS DECK.]

SORREL: We asked each witness where the chair sat.

DELL: Near the bow.

YANI: Near the wheel.

RUS: In the way.

DELL: It was a very good chair.

SORREL: The recording suggests it moved during the crossing.

[SFX: CHAIR SCRAPES, WATER SPLASHES.]

YANI: The tide turned. The chair slid toward the rail.

RUS: I caught it.

DELL: The chair caught the rope.

[SFX: ROPE TIGHTENS. HORN BLASTS.]

RUS: That was the horn.

YANI: The rope was still tied.

SORREL: The archive cannot determine whether the horn came before or after the rope was released.

DELL: It came when I shouted.

YANI: You shouted after the chair moved.

RUS: You both shouted.

[SFX: THREE VOICES OVERLAP, THEN CUT TO QUIET WATER.]

SORREL: Before the crossing reached the other bank, we found a second recording. The skipper describes the chair slipping toward the rail.

[SFX: OLD TAPE DECK CLICKS. ARCHIVAL HISS.]

RUS, ON TAPE: The chair was moving. I put my foot against it.

YANI: You put your foot on my tail.

RUS, ON TAPE: I thought it was the rope.

DELL: That is not how I remember it.

SORREL: The tape does not settle the argument. It adds a fourth version.

[SFX: TAPE CLICKS OFF. WATER LAPS.]

SORREL: In the storage room, Yani showed us the rope mark on the chair leg. The mark is shallow, but the recorder picked up a scrape when she turned it toward the microphone.

[SFX: WOOD SCRAPES SOFTLY.]

YANI: That is the sound it made when the tide shifted.

DELL: I remember a much louder scrape.

RUS: You were standing beside the horn.

DELL: I was protecting my chair.

SORREL: We tested the chair on the studio floor. It does not move unless someone pushes it.

[SFX: CHAIR SCRAPES A FEW INCHES.]

YANI: There. That is how far it slid.

RUS: The deck was wet. It slid farther on the ferry.

DELL: The rope caught its leg before it reached the rail.

SORREL: The interviews agree on one detail: nobody let the chair go overboard. The rest belongs to memory.

SORREL: The ferry reached the other bank. The chair was safe, and the passengers still disagreed. The chair is in the ferry's storage room. Yani and Rus are carrying it to the waiting shelter, where it can hear the next crossing.

DELL: I said the chair was near the bow.

RUS: It was near the wheel.

YANI: It was in the way.

[SFX: CHAIR CARRIED ACROSS FLOOR, SET DOWN IN SHELTER, FERRY HORN, PIANO THEME OUT.]
