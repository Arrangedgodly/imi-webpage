# The Bridge Report

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio documentary following a bridge inspection committee*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: REPORTER LIO, a lemur; ENGINEER SORA, a macaque; COUNCIL MEMBER VENN, a gibbon; and a crossing troop.

[SFX: RIVER WATER, FOOTSTEPS ON A WOODEN BRIDGE.] 

LIO: We are at the old footbridge, where a committee has gathered to decide whether it is safe to cross. The bridge is wooden, low, and currently being crossed by three monkeys carrying a basket.

VENN: The committee must hear the report before the public uses it.

SORA: I have inspected the boards. One is loose at the west end. The rest are stable.

VENN: How many tests did you conduct?

SORA: I walked across it, checked the supports, and tapped the boards.

VENN: Did you use the official inspection mallet?

SORA: I used my hand.

VENN: The official mallet produces a more consistent sound.

LIO: We can hear the difference now.

[SFX: A MALLET TAPS THE BRIDGE. THE LOOSE BOARD RATTLES.] 

SORA: That is the loose board. We should replace it before anyone crosses.

VENN: Can we mark it with a sign?

SORA: A sign won't hold the board in place.

VENN: It will warn people.

SORA: The people already heard it.

LIO: Council member, what would make you comfortable reopening the bridge?

VENN: A unanimous vote.

SORA: There are only three of us, and we already agree the board is loose.

VENN: We agree it is loose. We have not agreed whether to call it loose or uneven.

SORA: A loose board can catch a foot. An uneven board can do the same.

LIO: Is that distinction useful to someone walking across?

VENN: It may be useful to the record.

CROSSER: I can hold the rope while you decide.

SORA: No. The bridge should remain closed until the board is fixed.

VENN: Then we need a temporary notice.

SORA: Say "Closed for repair. Use the marked path."

VENN: That is very short.

LIO: It fits on the sign.

[CHALK SCRATCHES ON WOOD.] 

VENN: I have written it. The letters are large enough to read from the path.

CROSSER: Now we can carry the repair board across the long way.

SORA: The long way stays open. Keep the board level and away from the river.

[THEY WALK. THE BOARD BRUSHES A BUSH, THEN CLEARS IT.] 

LIO: A second small delay.

CROSSER: The bush moved. The board is still level.

VENN: We can record that as an obstacle successfully negotiated.

SORA: Or we can keep walking.

[THE THREE CROSSERS RETURN, CARRYING AN EMPTY BASKET.] 

CROSSER: We used the other side. The loose board is easy to avoid.

VENN: Did you read the sign?

CROSSER: There is no sign.

VENN: We are discussing one.

SORA: The safest step is to close the bridge until we fix the board.

LIO: That will send visitors to the long path around the river.

SORA: The long path is open.

VENN: We should consult the map.

[MAP UNFOLDS. WIND BLOWS IT AGAINST THE MICROPHONE.] 

LIO: The map is touching the bridge.

VENN: It is a large map.

SORA: The long path follows the bank. We can place markers at both ends and ask people to use it for the afternoon.

VENN: The committee has not approved the markers.

CROSSER: We have some stones. We can set them by the path.

VENN: Are the stones official?

SORA: They are heavy and visible.

VENN: Then they are provisionally official.

[STONES SET DOWN. A ROPE TIES ACROSS THE BRIDGE ENTRANCE.] 

LIO: The crossing is closed while the repair is made. The troop is already following the markers.

VENN: We have reached a decision before the meeting ended.

SORA: The meeting can now end.

VENN: We still need a closing statement.

SORA: Say the bridge is closed, the route is marked, and the repair crew is coming.

VENN: That is only one sentence.

LIO: The bridge has given us a short report.

[SFX: WOODEN BOARD LIFTS, A NEW BOARD SETS INTO PLACE, HAMMER TAPS THREE TIMES.] 

SORA: The replacement is level. I have checked the support.

VENN: The committee approves crossing.

CROSSER: We already came back around.

LIO: Then the bridge is open again, and the long path remains marked for anyone who wants a walk.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS CROSS, RIVER WATER CONTINUES.]
