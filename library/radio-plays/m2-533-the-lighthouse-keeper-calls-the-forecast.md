# The Lighthouse Keeper Calls the Forecast

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: maritime radio turns a lamp maintenance check into a ghost story*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-018*

Cast: Keeper Mara, a macaque; announcer Vex, an orangutan; technician Odo, a gibbon; lookout Pip, a capuchin; harbor caller Seli, a lemur.

SFX: Lighthouse room. Wind outside; lamp turns with a soft mechanical tick.

VEX: Reports say a light is flashing from the north rocks. Is it a distress signal?

MARA: It's our rotating lamp reflecting off wet glass. The light is on schedule.

PIP: I can see the reflection move across the window every twelve seconds.

ODO: The service panel says the lamp is turning normally. I should inspect the outside glass after the gust passes.

SELI, over radio: Harbor station has no vessel near the rocks. Visibility is two miles and improving.

VEX: The light appears, disappears, then appears again. That sounds like a message.

MARA: It sounds like a rotating beam. Don't send a rescue call without a vessel report.

SFX: Wind rattles shutters; lamp tick continues.

PIP: A second light is showing below it.

ODO: That's the wet railing reflecting the beam. The angle changes as the lamp rotates.

VEX: Could we call it the ghost of the old keeper?

MARA: Not while listeners may confuse a story with a warning. We can explain the reflection after we verify the lamp.

ODO: The gust is down. I'll latch the door before stepping onto the balcony.

SFX: Door latch; boots on a metal step. Wind briefly rises.

MARA: Stay clipped to the rail. Clean only the outer pane from the sheltered side.

ODO: The glass is wet with salt spray. One wipe, and the lower reflection is gone.

PIP: The beam is still moving over the north rocks, but there is only one light now.

SELI: Harbor station confirms no distress call and no vessel. The weather is still clear enough for the marked channel.

VEX: Our ghost has vanished.

MARA: The glare has vanished. The lamp is working, the window is clean, and the radio remains on watch.

SFX: Door opens; Odo returns indoors. Latch clicks.

ODO: Glass cleaned. Safety line unhooked only after I was back inside.

PIP: I'll log the salt spray and the time. If the lower reflection returns, we check the pane before we guess at a message.

VEX: Can I tell the story after the forecast?

MARA: You can tell it as a story and say that the real signal was a reflection.

SELI: Harbor station standing by. No boats reported near the north rocks.


ODO: I'll enter the cleaning in the maintenance log: outer pane wiped from the sheltered balcony, latch secured, line removed after return indoors.

MARA: Good. The lamp's rotation is visible through the clear glass. We can verify each turn without opening the door again.

PIP: One sweep over the north rocks, then across the harbor. It is steady.

SELI: The station has updated its weather board. Visibility is three miles now. No craft are waiting for a distress response.

VEX: If the reflection was so ordinary, why did it look like a second signal?

ODO: The salt film doubled the beam on the lower pane. It moved with the lamp and disappeared when the glass was cleaned.

MARA: That is our answer. Keep the radio on the harbor frequency and the ghost story in the archive program.

SFX: Logbook closes; lamp continues its measured tick.


VEX: Listeners can hear the lamp continue behind us.

MARA: That steady tick is the rotation mechanism, not a coded message. The harbor line remains open for actual vessel calls.

SELI: No vessel has reported trouble.

TAG

VEX: If you hear a ghost tonight, check the window.

MARA: And check the actual radio too.
