# The Listener Sent Back the Sound

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: listener voicemail podcasts, user-submitted audio roundups, and hosts who play a clip before finding out who recorded it*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A listener sends a mysterious two-note sound to a podcast, but the host plays the return message before checking the sender or the playback device.

Cast: Host Rala, a gibbon; editor Seko, a macaque; listener Domi, an orangutan; producer Piri, a capuchin; caller Vano, a baboon.

[SFX: TWO NOTES, LOW THEN HIGH.]

RALA: A listener sent us this sound with no explanation.

SEKO: It came from our own outgoing message.

PIRI: We asked listeners to send a sound back.

RALA: I thought it was from the archive.

DOMI: I recorded it in the kitchen. It is the toaster timer.

ACT ONE

The podcast invites listeners to submit ordinary sounds they cannot identify. Domi's file arrived without a name because the upload form strips sender labels from the public feed. Rala wants to play it as a mystery. Seko opens the file details and sees it was recorded at the station's test desk, not in Domi's kitchen.

VANO: I sent the same two notes from my porch.

PIRI: Did you record the toaster?

VANO: I heard it from the neighbor's window.

RALA: We have a regional phenomenon.

SEKO: We have two recordings of the same timer.

Piri asks whether Domi can explain the recording. She says her toaster gives two notes when the toast is ready, but the second note sometimes arrives late. She made a recording when she tested the upload button. The station then played the audio back to her to confirm receipt; she had recorded that playback too.

ACT TWO

Rala asks Seko to play the station's outgoing message. It says, "We received your sound. Thank you." Then the two notes play. Rala says the notes may be a hidden station signature. Seko points out that the test desk uses the same toaster as the kitchen. Piri asks if the original appliance can be switched off for a clean recording.

DOMI: I already turned it off.

[SFX: TWO NOTES, LOW THEN HIGH.]

RALA: It sounded again.

DOMI: That was the message coming through your studio.

Seko mutes the return channel. The notes stop. Vano laughs on the phone and says his neighbor's toaster is now famous. Rala asks whether any other listeners submitted the same sound. Piri checks the inbox. Three people sent the same two notes from different kitchens; all had heard the station test message replayed through their speakers.

ACT TWO-B

Seko builds a clear playback chain: original file, station confirmation, listener's recording of the confirmation. The two notes appear once in the original, then again in the feedback recording. Rala asks whether the show can still treat the sound as a mystery. Domi says it is a toaster timer. Vano says it is also a station echo. Piri says both descriptions are accurate.

RALA: I wanted a mystery.

SEKO: You have a source and a transmission path.

PIRI: We should ask permission before playing the kitchen recordings.

Domi agrees to be credited as the first sender. Vano declines credit and asks the show to stop sending test audio at night. Seko changes the upload confirmation so it uses a spoken chime only once and does not replay the submitted clip. Rala records a new explanation. The timer sounds again in the kitchen; this time it is captured as an original, not a return.

DOMI: That is the toaster.

RALA: And this one is the station.

ACT THREE

The episode plays the original toaster sound, then the station's confirmation once. Rala identifies the source and explains how the sound looped back through the listener recording. Domi confirms the toast was ready. Vano confirms the neighboring appliance is now quiet. Seko removes the extra duplicate from the final edit and updates the upload process.

Piri asks Rala to read the sender's name before each clip. Rala does so. The program thanks Domi for the recording and tells listeners they can submit sounds without sharing a home address or private conversation. The end music begins after the final playback, not before.
