# The Dig Site's Unscheduled Trumpet

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live archaeology broadcasts that turn every buried object into a dramatic discovery before its context is checked*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Reporter Sela, a macaque; archaeologist Umi, a lemur; volunteer Peko, a gibbon; producer Aru, a capuchin.

[SFX: OPEN FIELD. BRUSHES SWEEP DIRT. A DISTANT TRUMPET PLAYS TWO NOTES.]

SELA: We are live at the old hill fort, where volunteers are uncovering a brass object.

UMI: We have not identified it. Peko found it near the east wall, not in a sealed layer.

ARU: The trailer says "royal trumpet revealed."

PEKO: I found a short brass tube. It has a hole at one end and a handle.

UMI: It may be part of a modern survey tool. Don't lift it until we check around it.

[SFX: BRUSHES SWEEP. METAL CLINKS AGAIN.]

SELA: The audience just heard a trumpet.

UMI: That sound came from beyond the excavation fence.

ARU: The sound effect is queued. It makes the dig feel like a discovery.

SELA: The audience heard it live.

PEKO: There's a brass-band rehearsal in the school field.

[SFX: DISTANT TRUMPET PLAYS THE SAME TWO NOTES.]

ARU: That's not our effect. I can mute the cue.

UMI: Please do. It covers the site sounds and makes every metal scrape seem important.

[SFX: BUTTON CLICKS. DISTANT TRUMPET REMAINS, BRUSHING CLEAR.]

PEKO: The tube has a screw thread under the dirt.

UMI: That's useful. We can expose the end with a wooden tool, slowly.

[SFX: WOODEN TOOL SCRAPES DIRT. A SMALL SCREW TURNS.]

SELA: The field producer wants a re-enactment of the object being found.

UMI: We have already found it. A re-enactment would put a second version in the trench and confuse the grid log.

ARU: We can use the sound of the brush and the school trumpet from where they were heard.

PEKO: The trumpet is outside the fence. The tube is in square E-14.

UMI: Correct. I'll write both locations in the field record.

[SFX: PENCIL SCRATCHES. A PAGE IS TURNED.]

SELA: The broadcast can carry the real distance between them.

SELA: It sounds like a mouthpiece.

UMI: It could fit one. That still doesn't tell us its age or purpose.

ARU: Can we call it a musical artifact for the segment title?

UMI: No. The field notebook says a brass survey tube was used in the 1960s to mark soil depth.

SELA: It is not polished like a trumpet mouthpiece.

UMI: The dirt still covers the stamped side. We should brush it before guessing.

PEKO: There is a number on the side. I can read "S-14."

UMI: That matches the survey inventory. It belongs to the modern field team.

SELA: So the buried trumpet is a depth marker.

ARU: The trailer is already posted.

SELA: We can correct it live. The object is a survey tube from a later soil check, not an ancient trumpet.

PEKO: The school band has stopped.

[SFX: TRUMPET CUTS OFF. A SHORT VOICE CALLS FROM FARTHER AWAY.]

UMI: Now we can hear the excavation again. Peko, leave the tube where it is until we photograph its position.

PEKO: I will mark the grid square, then step back.

[SFX: PENCIL WRITES ON A BOARD. CAMERA SHUTTER CLICKS.]

SELA: The grid is labeled E-14. The object is still in place.

UMI: We have documented it. I'll lift it only after the site photograph and the inventory entry.

ARU: The audience expected a trumpet reveal.

UMI: They got a better answer. A survey team left its marker, and the old fort has a school band nearby.

[SFX: TUBE LIFTS INTO A PADDED TRAY. WOODEN TRAY SLIDES CLOSED.]

SELA: The survey tube is documented and in its padded tray. No trumpet reveal.

ARU: I can make a replacement fanfare with this metal tape measure. No recorder needed.

[SFX: ARU PULLS THE METAL TAPE MEASURE ACROSS THE TABLE. IT ZIPS AND SNAPS TO ITS STOP.]

PEKO: That is a very short royal procession.

UMI: It is a measuring tape. Put it back before it lands in the trench.

[SFX: TAPE MEASURE RETRACTS WITH A WHIR. ARU SETS IT BESIDE THE TOOL BOX.]

ARU: Can we put "royal tape measure" in the trailer?

SELA: Only if the school band can measure the king.

[SFX: DISTANT SCHOOL TRUMPET PLAYS ONE NOTE. BRUSHES RETURN TO THE SOIL.]
