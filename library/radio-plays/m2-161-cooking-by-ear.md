# Cooking by Ear

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live radio cooking demonstrations, sensory adjectives, sponsor plugs, and a host who refuses to abandon the recipe*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
CHEF LULU, a capuchin host
MOTI, a gibbon studio assistant
CALLER, a tamarin listener

[COOKING SHOW THEME. A PAN SIZZLES.]

LULU: Welcome to Cooking by Ear, where every meal begins with a sound. Today we're making a banana pancake. Moti, pass the bowl.

MOTI: This one?

LULU: That's the microphone basket. The bowl is on your left.

[METAL CLANG.]

LULU: A crisp bowl sound. Listeners, picture a warm golden crust.

MOTI: We haven't mixed anything yet.

LULU: We're building anticipation. First, one cup of flour.

MOTI: The measuring cup is missing.

LULU: Use the mug.

MOTI: Which mug?

LULU: The one with the handle.

MOTI: All four mugs have handles.

LULU: Then select the mug with the most confident handle.

[PHONE LINE CLICKS.]

CALLER: Chef, I'm making the recipe at home. How much flour is a confident mug?

LULU: About one mug's worth.

CALLER: I have a very small mug.

LULU: Then the pancakes will be dainty. They'll look like little silver dollars.

MOTI: We are not making pancakes for currency.

LULU: Next, mash one banana. Moti, is the banana ripe?

MOTI: It's green.

LULU: Green banana gives the batter a bright, grassy note.

MOTI: It gives it a hard, starchy lump.

[POUNDING.]

LULU: We'll soften it with the sponsor's new Whisk-O-Matic.

MOTI: This is a spoon.

LULU: A manual Whisk-O-Matic. It has a handle and a future.

CALLER: Should I add sugar?

LULU: A pinch.

CALLER: How big a pinch?

LULU: The pinch that makes your grandmother nod.

MOTI: Mine would nod at any dessert.

LULU: Then use a teaspoon. We are adding milk now, slowly, until the mixture looks like a friendly cloud.

[POURING. SPLATTER.] 

MOTI: It looks like a floor cloud.

LULU: A rustic cloud. Heat the pan and pour in one ladle.

MOTI: The pan is still cold.

LULU: We can narrate while it warms. Listen to that gentle sizzle.

[LONG SILENCE.]

CALLER: I don't hear one.

LULU: A very subtle sizzle. The Whisk-O-Matic's silence lets the banana shine.

MOTI: The burner is off.

LULU: Thank you, Moti. Turn it on, and we'll start again. The first pancake is a practice pancake.

[FLAME CLICKS. BATTER HITS PAN. SIZZLE.]

LULU: There! A bright, crisp sizzle. Flip when the edges look set.

MOTI: They're set. The pancake is folded in half.

LULU: That's a pocket pancake. We can fill it with fruit.

CALLER: Mine came out round.

MOTI: Does it sound crisp?

CALLER: It sounds like a plate scraping the pan.

LULU: Turn the heat down and wait for small bubbles. The pancake should loosen when it is ready.

[SPATULA SCRAPES, THEN A SOFT FLIP.]

CALLER: It flipped! The edges are brown, and the middle is cooked.

MOTI: We can describe that without inventing a new appliance.

LULU: The gentle scrape of success. Our listener has made a perfect pancake by following the sound, not my confidence.

LULU: Then you've made a classic. Moti, how is our pocket pancake?

MOTI: It has a crispy edge and a soft middle. Actually delicious.

LULU: And the caller's round pancake?

CALLER: Also delicious. My small mug made six tiny ones.

LULU: A dainty stack! Today's lesson: measure by listening, then turn the burner on.

MOTI: Better lesson: check the burner before describing the sizzle.

CALLER: I will make another batch for the neighbors.

LULU: Use a smaller bowl if they are dainty.

LULU: Next week, we make toast. The sound is already prepared.

[TOASTER POPS BEFORE ANY BREAD GOES IN.]

MOTI: We'll unplug it first.

[THEME MUSIC.]

LULU: A safe and savory sign-off from Cooking by Ear.
