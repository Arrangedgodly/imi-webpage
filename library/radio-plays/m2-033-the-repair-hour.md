# The Repair Hour

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio call-in home repair advice shows*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Mae, a calm macaque; Ed, a gibbon mechanic; caller Luma, a capuchin caretaker; caller Obo, a young baboon; and the station's elderly wall clock, which is heard throughout.

[SFX: STUDIO CLOCK TICKS. A WRENCH CLINKS.] 

MAE: This is The Repair Hour. We can help you identify a problem and decide what to check next. We cannot hear every machine through a phone, so if a repair involves wiring, gas, or heavy lifting, stop and call a qualified worker. Ed is here with a toolbox and an expression that says he has already heard the machine.

ED: I have heard several machines. None of them asked permission.

MAE: Our first caller is Luma, who says the community center door slams even when nobody pushes it.

LUMA, PHONE: It started after we replaced the hinge. The door closes so fast it scares the children. I tightened the screws, but that made it worse.

ED: Do not tighten them again. Is there a closer arm at the top?

LUMA: Yes. It has a small screw marked S.

ED: That may adjust speed, but the labels vary. Don't turn it until you know the model. Read the brand and number from the plate.

LUMA: It says HingeCo 18-B.

ED: That's a hydraulic closer. The speed valve can be adjusted a little, but the latch valve controls the last part. If the door is slamming, the closer may be misaligned. Check whether the arm is bent or the body is loose. If the screws are stripped, the door needs a repair person. Keep people clear until it is fixed.

MAE: Can Luma close the center door manually?

ED: Yes, gently, if it opens freely and does not drag. Put a sign up, and don't leave it propped in a way that blocks the exit.

LUMA: I'll post one now.

[SFX: PHONE CLICK. A PAPER SIGN IS TAPED UP.] 

MAE: Next caller, Obo has a wheelbarrow that squeaks and pulls left.

OBO, PHONE: It carries compost. I greased the wheel, and now the squeak is louder.

ED: What did you grease?

OBO: The axle.

ED: Was the wheel straight?

OBO: I don't know.

ED: Set it down and check whether the wheel wobbles side to side. Don't lift it by yourself if it's loaded. Is the tire rubbing against the frame?

OBO: It touches the left side when I push.

ED: That's likely why it pulls. The axle may have shifted or the frame may be bent. Empty it, lay it on its side, and see whether the washers sit evenly. If the frame is cracked, replace the barrow. Compost is heavy enough without a steering problem.

MAE: Obo, can you check it safely?

OBO: My aunt can help. She has a pair of gloves and a level.

ED: A level won't tell you if the wheel is true, but it can show whether the handles sit at the same height.

[SFX: DISTANT METAL SCRAPE.] 

MAE: We have an unexpected sound from the studio clock. It has stopped.

ED: Has it been wound?

MAE: It is a battery clock.

ED: Then check the battery.

MAE: I changed it yesterday.

ED: Did you put it in the right way?

[SFX: STUDIO CLOCK STARTS TICKING AGAIN.] 

MAE: It is running. The battery cover was loose.

ED: A repair hour without a repair is still a repair hour.

MAE: Our last caller asks how to stop a drawer from sticking. The drawer is full of tools.

ED: Empty it first. Look for a screw or a bent runner. Don't force it while loaded. If the cabinet tilts, check the feet. A drawer can bind because the furniture is not level. If it has a rail, clean the dust before adding lubricant. Grease over dirt makes a paste.

CALLER: I can empty it. I was hoping you would say I need a new cabinet.

ED: Do you want a new cabinet?

CALLER: I want someone else to empty it.

MAE: We can recommend a helper, but we cannot recommend a miracle.

[SFX: CLOCK TICKS. TOOLBOX CLOSES.] 

MAE: This has been The Repair Hour. Describe the problem, check the safe basics, and stop when the repair is beyond your tools. Ed will be back next Saturday.

ED: If the clock is still working.

MAE: It is. For now.

[SFX: THE CLOCK TICKS INTO THE MUSIC.]
