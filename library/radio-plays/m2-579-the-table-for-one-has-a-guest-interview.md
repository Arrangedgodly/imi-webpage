# The Table for One Has a Guest Interview

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: restaurant call-in radio, intimate food criticism, and hosts who make a solo diner sound like breaking news*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-020*

Cast: Host Mako, a gibbon; restaurant critic Sela, a macaque; server Odo, an orangutan; solo diner Niko, a capuchin; producer Rani, a baboon.

SFX: Small restaurant. Cutlery clinks; rain taps the window.

MAKO: We are live from the Lantern Table, where our critic Sela has reserved a table for one. Is the room quiet?

SELA: It is a restaurant. There are six tables and a kettle that whistles when it is ready.

RANI: Our sound board labels the kettle as a second guest.

ODO: The kettle does not need a chair.

MAKO: We will discuss dining alone. Sela, do you feel observed?

SELA: Only because you have a microphone pointed at me.

NIKO: I am the diner at the next table. I came alone on purpose. Could you point the microphone at the food instead?

MAKO: Our guest has entered the program.

NIKO: I have not entered anything. I ordered soup.

ODO: The soup is ready. The one-person table has a fresh napkin and a menu.

SELA: I ordered the mushroom stew. It is good, but the menu lists two sides that arrive automatically.

ODO: The kitchen sends bread and pickled beans with every stew.

NIKO: I ordered tea. The menu gave me bread too.

MAKO: A complimentary bread incident.

SELA: It is a small slice. The issue is that the table is built for one, and now there are three plates.

ODO: I can move the side plate to the center. Nobody has to share unless they want to.

NIKO: Please leave it at the edge. I may want another spoon.

RANI: Our sponsor asked whether we can call this a three-course solo experience.

SELA: No. It is soup, bread, and beans. They are not courses; they arrived together.

MAKO: The broadcast has a dramatic pacing problem.

NIKO: The soup is cooling while you decide what it is.

SFX: Spoon taps bowl.

SELA: I can taste it now. The broth has pepper and roasted onion. It is hot but not scalding.

MAKO: How does the table feel?

NIKO: It has one chair. I feel seated.

ODO: The restaurant can add a second chair if another person joins you.

NIKO: I came alone. I would like to finish the meal without being interviewed about the chair.

RANI: The station's live chat asks whether solo diners can request half portions.

ODO: Yes. The kitchen has a half-bowl option. It is not on today's menu board because the board lists only full portions.

SELA: That is useful. A menu should say what people can ask for.

MAKO: Could we announce the half-bowl as a special revelation?

SELA: You can simply tell listeners it is available.

MAKO: The producer is removing the dramatic music.

SFX: A flourish starts, then stops mid-note.

RANI: I have muted it. The kettle was also on the music channel.

ODO: The kettle is whistling now. The tea is ready.

MAKO: A live sound cue at last.

NIKO: My tea has arrived. It came in a cup, not a second chair.

SELA: The restaurant lets people order tea without buying a full meal. It also has a quiet corner by the window.

NIKO: I chose this table because it faces the rain. The view is company enough.

MAKO: May I ask one final question?

NIKO: You may ask; I may decline.

MAKO: Would you recommend the Lantern Table to someone dining alone?

NIKO: Yes. The food is good, the staff do not mind one person, and the microphone eventually moved.

ODO: We can leave the microphone on the empty chair until you finish.

SELA: No. Put it back in its case.

RANI: Case is closed. The microphone is not a diner.

SFX: Microphone cable winds into a case. The room ambience returns.

MAKO: Our critic has finished her review. Our diner has finished the stew. The one-person table is now a one-person table again.

FINAL SIGNOFF: The Lantern Table welcomes solo guests, and no chair is required for the kettle.
