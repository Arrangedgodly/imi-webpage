# The Forty-Second Lap

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: marathon race broadcasts, relentless split times, athlete backstories, and commentators who keep extending the finish line*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
ANNOUNCER JO, a lemur
COMMENTATOR TESS, a capuchin
RUNNER BEE, a macaque

[CHEERING CROWD. STARTING HORN.]

JO: Welcome to the Kettle City fun run. Runners are off! The course is one lap around the park, then across the finish line.

TESS: Bee leads the pack. She has trained for three weeks, mostly by running to catch the bus.

JO: A practical regimen. The runners approach the first marker. Bee is ahead by two steps.

TESS: She looks focused. Her shoes are tied, her bib is straight, and she has not yet noticed the snack table.

[FOOTSTEPS. A BELL.]

JO: Bee passes the halfway sign. The crowd cheers. A volunteer offers her water.

BEE: Thank you. Is the finish after the next bend?

JO: It is after the park gate.

TESS: The announcer's map shows a gate, a pond, and a little star. The star may indicate the finish.

JO: The star indicates the snack table.

TESS: That is a dangerous piece of map information.

[FOOTSTEPS TURN. CROWD NOISE.]

JO: Bee rounds the bend. She has reached the park gate and crossed the finish line. She wins!

[CHEERS. A HORN.]

TESS: The next runner is still on the course. Bee has received a medal and a banana.

BEE: I thought the race had another lap. The sign said "42."

JO: That is the race number, not the lap count.

TESS: Bee appears to be jogging toward the pond anyway.

BEE: I'm cooling down. The volunteer said the water station is near the pond.

JO: The water station is just beyond the finish, on the right.

TESS: She has passed it and is now circling the pond. The crowd is following her.

JO: They think the race has restarted.

[CHEERING MOVES AWAY, THEN BACK.]

BEE: Why is everyone running behind me?

TESS: You've become the unofficial leader of a second lap.

JO: Bee, turn left at the bench and return to the finish area.

BEE: I thought the forty-second lap was the special one.

TESS: The number is 42. There are only two laps in this race.

BEE: Then I've completed one lap and a bonus lap.

JO: A generous interpretation. The race director is approaching with the clipboard.

[CLIPBOARD FLIPS.]

DIRECTOR: Bee has won the official race. The second lap was optional, but it was not part of the standings.

BEE: Can I keep the medal?

DIRECTOR: Yes. And the banana.

TESS: A strong finish and a recovery loop. The crowd has reached the water station.

JO: The race is complete. Volunteers are directing the runners to the picnic lawn.

BEE: I'll walk this time.

[FOOTSTEPS SLOW.]

TESS: She has changed pace. The other runners are slowing with her.

JO: The crowd is no longer chasing the leader. They're walking to the picnic.

[CROWD CHEERS FROM THE PICNIC LAWN.]

TESS: Bee has changed her shoes and is walking the route backward with the race volunteer. She wants to know where the forty-two sign confused the runners.

JO: The sign was posted at the start and showed the race number in larger type than the lap count.

BEE: They should put the word "two" first. I followed the biggest number.

TESS: The director is writing a new sign now. "Two Laps - Race 42."

JO: A small edit with a large effect. The runner who followed Bee has arrived at the picnic without an extra loop.

BEE: I'll share my banana with them.

TESS: An excellent sportsmanship report. The medal stays with Bee; the extra lap becomes a story, not an official time.

TESS: The race director has moved the sign so it reads "Race 42: Two Laps."

JO: That should prevent another bonus lap.

BEE: What does the star mean?

JO: Snack table.

BEE: I'll take the banana to the snack table.

[CHEERS FADE INTO PICNIC SOUNDS.]

TESS: The winner has finished, the course is clear, and the picnic has begun.

JO: Join us next year for race 43, featuring the same park and a newly labeled star.

[THEME MUSIC. A STARTING HORN BLOWS IN THE DISTANCE.]
