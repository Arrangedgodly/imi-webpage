# The Siren Test Was on the Wrong Beach

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: coastal public-safety bulletins, recorded warning tests, and broadcasters who mistake a rehearsal for an active alert*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A beach siren test plays through the wrong radio feed, and the local station must distinguish a scheduled check from a real warning.

Cast: Announcer Piri, a macaque; safety officer Nema, a gibbon; technician Ralo, an orangutan; beach keeper Odo, a capuchin; caller Tavi, a baboon.

[SFX: A DISTANT SIREN, THREE SHORT TONES.]

PIRI: We are receiving a coastal alert.

NEMA: That is the scheduled siren test from South Beach.

RALO: Our board has labeled it North Beach.

ODO: I am at North Beach. The siren is silent here.

ACT ONE

The station is testing its public alert feed before the weekend. Piri sees a red light and announces a warning before Nema can confirm the location. Odo calls from North Beach; visitors there are calm and the flag is green. Ralo checks the source label and discovers a patch cable from the South Beach test line plugged into the North Beach channel.

PIRI: Did the test begin at the scheduled time?

NEMA: Yes. It was announced yesterday.

PIRI: Then it is not an emergency.

NEMA: It still needs to be reported accurately.

Ralo mutes the alert feed. Tavi calls to ask if people should leave the beach. Nema says the North Beach siren did not sound and there is no active warning there. She asks him to stay tuned for the corrected bulletin, not to repeat the mistaken location.

ACT TWO

Ralo reroutes the South Beach test to the correct channel. The siren plays again, this time with a spoken test message and location. Piri reads it aloud: South Beach, scheduled system test, no action required. The sound is clear but the recording begins before the message, so listeners hear the siren without context.

NEMA: Put the words first, then the tone.

RALO: The public alert standard begins with the tone.

NEMA: The station announcement can introduce the test before the tone.

PIRI: We must not alter the emergency signal itself.

Ralo separates the station introduction from the test recording. Odo checks the North Beach board. The flag remains green; the beach is open. Piri tells the audience that the previous bulletin was mislabeled, and corrects it on air. She says the test was from South Beach and the North Beach siren remains inactive.

Tavi asks if a real warning would use the same tone. Nema says the tone is recognized, but listeners must follow the accompanying location and instruction. The station cannot infer an emergency from a test signal alone. Ralo updates the channel label and reads it back to Nema.

ACT TWO-B

The crew runs a second test with the introduction first. Piri announces: "This is a scheduled siren test at South Beach. No action is needed." Then Ralo plays the official tone and recorded instructions. The message identifies the beach and says it is only a test. Odo listens from North Beach; he hears nothing from the wrong speaker.

ODO: North Beach is still quiet.

NEMA: Good. The channels are separate.

Ralo checks the source labels again. North is connected to its standby channel; South is connected to the test recorder. The staff rehearses what to say if an alert arrives outside the scheduled test. Piri will confirm location with Nema before announcing; Nema will read the official instruction without paraphrasing.

Tavi says the corrected test sounded less dramatic. Nema says the goal is recognition, not drama. Piri removes the red "LIVE ALERT" card and replaces it with "SCHEDULED TEST." The siren plays once more at the correct beach.

ACT THREE

Piri gives the correction clearly: the earlier sound came from a scheduled South Beach test and was routed to the wrong channel; North Beach was not under warning. The station confirms the test is complete. Odo reports the North Beach flag is still green and the shoreline remains open. Nema says the source label has been checked and the next test will use the corrected routing.

Ralo saves the test recording and locks its channel path. Tavi asks whether the station can keep a copy of the spoken introduction. Piri says yes, but the siren remains the official recording. The team closes the test line. No new tone plays after the correction.

TAG

[SFX: A RADIO CHANNEL CLICKS OFF.]

PIRI: That was the test line ending.
