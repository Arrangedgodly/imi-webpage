# The Aquarium's Shark Has a Press Agent

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: celebrity interview shows that turn animal care into image management*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Host Vex, an orangutan; aquarist Mara, a macaque; keeper Pip, a capuchin; publicist Odo, a gibbon; caller Nari, a lemur; aquarium visitors (nonspeaking).

SFX: Aquarium gallery rail. Filter hum and water movement. Visitors speak quietly in the distance.

MARA: We are at the blue shark viewing rail. This is the afternoon routine: he swims the same circuit, the feeding is at two, and the lights dim at five. Please do not tap the glass.

PIP: His loop runs from the rock shelter to the far pane and back. The tank is operating normally.

VEX: And today, our star has a publicist.

ODO: I drafted a press card. It says, "The misunderstood loner is ready to win your heart."

MARA: He is a shark in a habitat, not a celebrity. That line could make visitors expect a performance.

ODO: I can revise the image. Maybe a portrait and three catchphrases.

PIP: No photo of him pressing against the glass. It encourages people to tap for a response.

VEX: The publicist is censoring the star.

MARA: The keeper is protecting the animal's routine. That's why we're reporting from the rail first.

SFX: Two light taps on glass.

PIP: Someone has tapped the panel. I'll ask them to stop.

MARA: Please do. The sign is beside your hand.

SFX: Tapping stops; footsteps move away.

VEX: The celebrity has set a boundary.

MARA: The visitor heard the keeper. We can return to the booth now.

SFX: Headphones lift; measured footsteps across gallery; booth door opens and shuts. Filter hum softens.

VEX: Welcome back to Star Habitat. Our guest cannot leave the studio, because it is not in the studio. The shark is in the exhibit tank.

MARA: We moved here from the viewing rail so we could talk without asking visitors to gather around the glass.

ODO: I represent the shark's public image.

PIP: You represent the press card. I keep his habitat.

NARI, on phone: I heard there was a shark interview. Is he getting a new tank? I came last week and thought he was hiding because the lights were dim.

MARA: The tank is unchanged. The dim period is part of his daily cycle. He is not required to pose when guests arrive.

VEX: Could the public talk be titled, "Where Is the Star?"

PIP: Better: "How the Shark Uses His Habitat." We can explain his routine and answer questions about the tank.

ODO: I will take off the catchphrases and write the care schedule. Should I include a photograph?

MARA: A diagram of the viewing area, feeding time, and no-tapping reminder. No celebrity portrait.

NARI: That would have answered my question. Can listeners hear the shark on a recording?

MARA: We don't have an interview recording of this shark. We can use the sound of the filtration system if we label it as the pump, not his voice.

VEX: The pump could have a supporting role.

ODO: It's equipment. I'll print the visitor card and carry it back to the rail.

SFX: Printer feeds one card; paper tears. Booth door opens; footsteps cross gallery through Odo's wireless microphone, which remains live on the booth monitor.

PIP: Odo is at the viewing rail now. Through the monitor, I can hear the holder click into place.

SFX: Plastic holder clicks; footsteps return to booth.

ODO: Card is displayed beside the tank, away from the glass. It reads: "Feeding at two; dim period at five; please don't tap; ask the aquarist."

MARA: That is the public update. The shark continues his ordinary circuit, and visitors can watch without interrupting him.

VEX: The star has declined to comment.

PIP: The animal is swimming. We have said all we need to say.

VEX: Then Star Habitat is signing off.

MARA: Sign off from the booth. Pip, return to the floor and keep the visitor rail staffed.

SFX: Fader lowers; filtration hum continues.

TAG

ODO: New headline: "Aquarist Explains the Shark's Schedule."

MARA: Better. Leave the glass alone.
