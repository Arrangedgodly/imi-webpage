# The Choir's Missing Pitch Pipe

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: concert radio etiquette, tuning rituals, and conductors who mistake preliminary notes for the performance*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST SERA, a macaque
CONDUCTOR ODO, a gibbon
SINGER VELA, a capuchin
ARCHIVIST TAVI, a lemur
ENGINEER PIP, a squirrel monkey

[SFX: HALL ROOM TONE, CHAIRS SHIFT.]

SERA: The Canopy Choir is about to perform its spring concert. Conductor Odo cannot find the pitch pipe.

ODO: It was on the stand.

TAVI: The stand is empty.

VELA: I have already tuned.

ODO: You tuned to what?

VELA: The radiator.

[SFX: RADIATOR HISS.]

PIP: The radiator is not a musical reference.

VELA: It held one steady note.

SERA: The audience can hear the choir searching for a pitch.

ODO: We are not searching. We are preparing.

TAVI: The concert starts in four minutes.

Odo asks each section to hum its starting note. The basses hum low, the altos follow, and the sopranos sing above them. The notes do not match.

[SFX: THREE NOTES CLASH.]

ODO: Stop.

VELA: I was matching the radiator.

PIP: The radiator just shut off.

[SFX: HISS STOPS.]

ODO: We need the pitch pipe.
 
Pip searches the music stand, then checks the piano bench. The pitch pipe is not there. Odo tells the choir to hum softly while they wait; the basses hum one note and the sopranos drift to another.

[SFX: TWO NOTES WANDER.]

TAVI: The archive has a tuning fork.

ODO: That fork is for the display case alarm.

TAVI: It is a fork.

PIP: It is calibrated to the alarm.

SERA: The choir is tuning to a security device.

The alarm sounds when Tavi taps the fork against the case. Everyone stops humming. Odo tells them not to touch the display again.

[SFX: ALARM BEEP, SWITCH CLICKS OFF.]

Vela locates the pitch pipe under the parade programs. The concert can begin, but the choir must first restore the alarm and silence the fork.


TAVI checks the archive box. It contains three programs, two pencils, and a small whistle. Odo blows it; the sound is too high.

TAVI: That is the whistle from last year's parade.

SERA: The choir has inherited a parade pitch.

Vela asks whether they can tune from the piano. The piano is locked for maintenance. Pip says they can use a tone generator, but the battery is low.

[SFX: ELECTRONIC TONE WOBBLES.]

PIP: It will hold one note.

ODO: Then we need only one.

The tone fades halfway through. The choir keeps humming, each section sustaining a different pitch. Odo raises his hands to stop them, but the audience mistakes the gesture for a cue.

[SFX: APPLAUSE STARTS.]

SERA: The concert has begun.

TAVI: It has not.

ODO: We cannot leave them hanging.

Vela asks the choir to hold the note that most closely matches the fading tone. The basses choose one pitch, the altos another. Odo listens, then lowers his hands to stop the sound.

[SFX: HUM ENDS.]

PIP: The pitch pipe was inside the program box.

TAVI: I found it beneath the pencils.

ODO: We have a pitch.

He blows the pipe. The note matches the choir's chosen pitch. The singers align. Odo looks toward the audience and announces that the opening hum was an intentional warm-up.

SERA: Was it?

ODO: It is now.

The choir begins the concert with a clean chord. Vela sings to the pitch pipe's note. The radiator stays quiet. At the end, the audience applauds the music, not the search.

[SFX: CHORD, APPLAUSE.]

TAVI: The pitch pipe is back in its case.

PIP: The parade whistle is in the archive.

ODO: Label both.

SERA: And please do not tune to the radiator next year.

VELA: Unless the pipe goes missing.

[SFX: CASE LATCH, ROOM TONE.]
