# The Floating Market Announces a Low Tide

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live market radio, nautical warnings, and vendors who treat a lower waterline as a scheduling crisis*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-020*

Cast: Presenter Kela, a macaque; market master Eno, a gibbon; boat vendor Rafi, an orangutan; harbor worker Tima, a baboon; caller Sori, a capuchin.

SFX: Harbor in midmorning. Ropes creak; water laps under planks.

KELA: This is the Floating Market report. The water is lower than yesterday, and several vendors are asking whether the market is closing.

ENO: The tide is going out on schedule. The market remains open until the noon bell.

RAFI: My boat is three inches lower against the dock. I cannot reach the top shelf.

TIMA: The dock floats with the tide. Your boat is still tied to it.

KELA: The dock may be sinking.

TIMA: It is moving down with the water. The mooring posts slide through their guides.

SORI, by phone: I am at the west landing. The fruit boat looks lower than the fish boat.

ENO: The fruit boat is loaded with oranges. Weight changes how deep a boat sits.

RAFI: I have twelve crates, not a flood.

KELA: The market is now comparing boat draft.

TIMA: The harbor gauge is marked in blue. Water is two feet below high tide. That is normal for this hour.

SFX: A small bell rings once near the fruit boat.

ENO: That is not the noon bell. We opened at sunrise.

KELA: Our producer's clock says nine-thirty.

TIMA: The dock clock is six hours slow after yesterday's repair. Do not use it for the tide report.

RAFI: This small bell is my scale alarm, not the dock clock.

SFX: A metal bell rings again near the fish boat.

RAFI: My cargo scale chimes when the platform is uneven. The boat is tilted because the crates are all on one side.

ENO: Move half the oranges to the lower deck, then check the boat's balance.

KELA: Is it safe to unload?

RAFI: The dock is secured. I can shift the crates one at a time.

SFX: Crate slides, then settles. Scale chime stops.

TIMA: The hull sits level now. The dock remains at its marked guide height.

KELA: So the low water was a tide, the bell was a scale, and the boat was listing under oranges.

ENO: Three separate things. All normal.

SORI: The west landing is asking whether to move its boats.

ENO: No. The channel is deep enough, and the tide chart says water will turn at ten. Keep to the marked line until then.

TIMA: I will read the actual depth gauge at the west landing and radio the result.

SFX: A rope is secured; oars dip once, then stop.

TIMA: West landing reports four feet at the dock, safe for the listed boats. The gauge is working.

KELA: Can we get a statement from the orange seller?

RAFI: I am a boat vendor. I sell oranges and fish baskets. The oranges are now spread evenly.

KELA: Your boat is steady, and the market is open.

RAFI: My top shelf is still too high.

ENO: Use the step at the dock.

RAFI: The step is on my boat.

TIMA: I can move the dock ladder to the fruit boat. It hooks over the rail and stays secured.

SFX: Ladder hooks into place. Metal clicks twice.

RAFI: Now I can reach the shelf without leaning out.

KELA: We have a practical adjustment, not a market closure.

ENO: Vendors can keep selling through the low tide. At the turn, the floating docks will rise slowly with the water.

KELA: We will report the turn when the harbor gauge confirms it.

SFX: Water laps, a rope eases gently.

TIMA: The gauge is moving upward. Tide has turned at ten, as forecast.

RAFI: My boat has not moved away from the dock.

KELA: That is what a mooring does.

ENO: It keeps the market together while the harbor moves.

FINAL SIGNOFF: The fruit boat is balanced, the water has turned, and the floating market remains open.
