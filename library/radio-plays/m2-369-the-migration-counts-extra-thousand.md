# The Migration Count's Extra Thousand

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: wildlife radio coverage that inflates a migration estimate by counting repeated calls from the same nearby flock*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Reporter Eru, a gibbon; field scientist Nima, a lemur; producer Savo, a macaque; caller Deki, a capuchin.

[SFX: WINDY RIDGE. MANY BIRD CALLS. RADIO MIC OPENS.]

ERU: The dusk migration has begun. Our counter has recorded one thousand calls in ten minutes.

NIMA: It has recorded one thousand sounds, not one thousand birds.

SAVO: The sponsor promised a banner if we reach the thousand-bird mark.

DEKI: I am listening from the lower ridge. The same flock passed my station twice.

ERU: The birds are circling?

NIMA: The wind is pushing them back along the ridge. Their calls bounce off the rock and trigger our microphone again.

[SFX: THREE BIRD CALLS. A DIGITAL COUNTER BEEPS RAPIDLY.]

SAVO: The number just jumped to twelve hundred.

NIMA: Those are echoes. The counter has no way to distinguish a call from its return.

ERU: Can we face the microphone toward the valley?

NIMA: We can lower its gain and use the directional mic, but we should not follow or approach the birds.

[SFX: TRIPOD LEGS ADJUST. A KNOB CLICKS DOWN.]

SAVO: The sponsor message is ready: "A thousand birds pass the ridge."

NIMA: We haven't counted birds. We have a sample of calls.

ERU: Give me the field estimate and how you reached it.

NIMA: We can count separate groups crossing the far saddle. One group, then another, if they stay distinct. Right now the cloud line hides the gap.

[SFX: WIND GUSTS. CALLS DISTANT, THEN CLOSE.]

DEKI: From the lower ridge, I see one group go west, then return east. They are following the valley updraft.

SAVO: The same flock twice?

DEKI: Yes. I can recognize the broken white feather on the lead bird. It has crossed my view four times.

ERU: The counter says two thousand.

NIMA: The counter is measuring repeated calls and repeated passes. We should reset the reading and log the flock movement, not declare a population.

SAVO: The sponsor wants a number before the weather break.

ERU: We can give a count of calls recorded, with the method and its limit.

NIMA: We have twenty minutes of audio and no reliable count of individuals. The directional mic will help separate the flock from echoes, but not identify every bird.

[SFX: COUNTER RESET BUTTON CLICKS. DIGITAL BEEP.]

SAVO: The display is zero.

SAVO: The banner printer has already made "One Thousand Birds" in large letters.

NIMA: Turn it around. The number came from the echo counter.

DEKI: The wind has shifted. I can hear the flock from one direction now, not bouncing off the rock.

ERU: We can record a clean sample, but the individual count still requires a separate method.

SAVO: I'll print "Call sample: six" on the reverse and leave the population claim off.

[SFX: BANNER ROLLS UP. PRINTER FEEDS A NEW, SHORT LABEL.]

NIMA: That is honest and small enough to fit beside the microphone.

ERU: We will start a fresh sample when the birds clear the ridge.

[SFX: WIND FADES. A FINAL SMALL GROUP CALLS FROM FARTHER AWAY.]

NIMA: That group is moving north. The calls are distinct now.

ERU: The counter records six calls. We can say six calls, not six birds.

SAVO: The banner says "One Thousand Voices."

NIMA: That is also false if the same birds called twice.

SAVO: I will replace it with "Migration Call Sample, Ridge Station." It fits on the banner.

DEKI: You can hear the flock moving farther away.

[SFX: BIRD CALLS DIM INTO WIND.]

ERU: At dusk, the ridge recorded calls from a moving flock, with returns from the rock and repeated passes. We cannot give an individual count tonight.

SAVO: The sponsor can wait for a proper count.

NIMA: And the birds can keep moving without us following them.

[SFX: RADIO CLICKS OFF. WIND CONTINUES; COUNTER REMAINS AT SIX.]
