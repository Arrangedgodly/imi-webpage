# The Radio Play That Advertised Itself

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: sponsored radio theatre, intrusive product placements, and a dramatic cast trying to keep a commercial out of the plot*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
NARRATOR, a ring-tailed lemur
CAPTAIN PIP, a macaque actor
MISTRESS LARK, a capuchin actor
MR. BOLT, a gibbon sponsor representative

[DRAMATIC MUSIC. RAIN AND THUNDER.]

NARRATOR: In a storm-torn harbor, Captain Pip searched the dark for the missing map. His lantern flickered. His courage did not.

PIP: I will find the map before dawn, even if I must search every wet crate in this port.

LARK: Captain, take this lantern. The flame is weak, but the glass is strong.

BOLT: And protected by Bolt Brand Lantern Glass, the glass that keeps a flame visible through ordinary weather!

[THUNDER CLAPS.]

PIP: Who spoke?

LARK: The narrator's sponsor representative. He has joined our scene.

BOLT: I was asked to provide a short message.

NARRATOR: The plot must continue. Captain, inspect the crates.

PIP: This crate contains rope, this one contains sailcloth, and this one contains a very large jar.

BOLT: Protect that jar with Bolt Brand Glassware.

LARK: It is a wooden crate.

BOLT: Our glassware does not protect wooden crates. It protects jars.

PIP: We are looking for a map, not selling a jar.

BOLT: Every story can be improved by a well-placed product.

[FOOTSTEPS. A CRATE OPENS.]

PIP: Here is a scrap of paper. It shows a line from the old lighthouse to the east pier.

LARK: That is the map!

BOLT: Keep it dry with Bolt Brand Waterproof Wrap.

NARRATOR: The map is already dry.

BOLT: Then the wrap has done its job invisibly.

PIP: We should follow the line to the lighthouse.

LARK: The tide is high. We'll need to wait until it falls.

BOLT: While we wait, may I mention the Bolt Brand Tide Clock?

PIP: Does it tell the tide?

BOLT: It is a regular clock with a picture of a wave.

LARK: That does not tell us when the tide falls.

BOLT: It reminds you that tides exist.

[THUNDER FADES. WATER RECEDE SOUND.]

NARRATOR: The tide had begun to fall. The captain and his companion hurried toward the lighthouse.

PIP: The path is clear. I can see the beacon.

LARK: The map ends here. There is a small mark beside the lighthouse door.

PIP: It says "under the step."

[STONE LIFTS. PAPER RUSTLES.]

PIP: The harbor map is safe. We've found the route home.

BOLT: And now you can store it in a Bolt Brand Document Case.

LARK: We found it under a stone step. It is not in danger now.

PIP: We can return it to the harbor office. They will decide where it belongs.

NARRATOR: Captain Pip had recovered the map. He had outlasted the storm and resisted every product pitch.

BOLT: The glass was still excellent.

PIP: The lantern helped. The map helped. The pitch did not.

LARK: We could use the tide clock as a coaster.

BOLT: It is not moisture-resistant.

[THEME MUSIC SWELLS.]

NARRATOR: This story was brought to you by the Harbor Office, which asks that recovered maps be returned before opening time.

BOLT: Bolt Brand Glass protects lanterns, jars, and some metaphorical ideas.

PIP: We're done now.

BOLT: One final message-

LARK: Good night.

[DOOR CLOSES ON BOLT. THUNDER RUMBLES SOFTLY.]

BOLT: The Harbor Office is not a Bolt Brand customer.

NARRATOR: Then the harbor office may decline the sponsorship.

BOLT: They can use the glass anyway.

LARK: The lantern is on loan from the theater.

BOLT: I can offer the theater a discounted glass replacement.

PIP: You have turned a recovered map into a sales presentation.

BOLT: It's the format I was given.

NARRATOR: Our story is complete. Captain Pip has returned the map, the tide has gone down, and the actors are leaving the harbor set.

[RAIN STOPS. FOOTSTEPS ON DRY BOARDS.]

LARK: The storm has ended. The lantern is still lit.

PIP: And the sponsor has finally stopped speaking.

NARRATOR: The map was returned. The sponsor representative was left outside with a tide clock.

BOLT: It is almost low tide.

[THEME ENDS.]
