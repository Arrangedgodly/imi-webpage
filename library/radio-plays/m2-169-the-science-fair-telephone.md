# The Science Fair Telephone

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: educational radio, expert interviews, sensational demonstrations, and science hosts who call every result groundbreaking*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
HOST MIRA, a gibbon
TESS, a capuchin student
DR. KIP, a macaque science teacher

[SCIENCE THEME. BUBBLING LIQUID.]

MIRA: Welcome to Wonder Lab Radio, where curiosity is loud and the experiments are safely described. Today, Tess will show us how sound travels through a string telephone.

TESS: I have two paper cups, a length of string, and a helper at the other end.

MIRA: The string is stretched tight. What happens when you speak into the cup?

TESS: The cup vibrates, the string carries the vibration, and the second cup makes the sound again.

DR. KIP: A remarkable journey, from voice to paper to string to ear.

MIRA: Can we hear it?

[STRING TWANGS.]

TESS: Hello, Dr. Kip.

DR. KIP: The sound arrived with excellent clarity.

MIRA: Listeners, you have heard science travel through a thread.

DR. KIP: Now let's test what happens if the string is loose.

TESS: It should not carry the sound as well.

[STRING GOES SLACK.]

TESS: Hello?

DR. KIP: I can still hear you because you are standing beside me.

MIRA: An unexpected confounding variable. We'll move Dr. Kip behind the curtain.

[FOOTSTEPS, CURTAIN DRAWN.]

TESS: Can you hear me now?

DR. KIP: I hear the cup tapping the table.

MIRA: A breakthrough! The table may be transmitting our message.

TESS: The cup is hitting it. I should hold the cup still.

[SECOND ATTEMPT. STRING TWANGS.]

TESS: The string is still loose. You can hear me through the wall, but not the cup.

DR. KIP: Correct. Tighten the string, and we'll compare.

[STRING PULLED TAUT.]

TESS: Hello, Dr. Kip.

DR. KIP: The cup's paper is vibrating. I hear you clearly.

MIRA: We have confirmed the string telephone. Next, can we make it longer?

TESS: We can connect another piece, but each knot and bend may weaken the signal.

DR. KIP: The audience will want to see it.

MIRA: This is radio. They can imagine the knot.

TESS: I'll connect the string to the other cup. There are now three cups in a line.

[THREE CUP TAPS.]

MIRA: The message travels from Tess to Dr. Kip and back to Tess?

TESS: No, we each have one cup. The third cup is for the stage manager.

[STAGE MANAGER SPEAKS THROUGH THIRD CUP, MUFFLED.]

MIRA: What did they say?

TESS: "Stop pulling the string. It's tied to the microphone stand."

[STAND TIPS SLIGHTLY.]

DR. KIP: The stand is now part of the experiment.

TESS: It is not. I'll untie the string and use a second chair.

MIRA: We have a safe adjustment. The message is clear again.

DR. KIP: Science works best when we change one thing at a time.

TESS: Let's make one last comparison. I'll keep the string tight and speak softly.

[STRING TELEPHONE WHISPERS.]

DR. KIP: I heard you. Now I'll speak, and you listen.

[DR. KIP WHISPERS THROUGH THE CUP.]

TESS: You said, "The stand is not part of the experiment."

MIRA: A message successfully delivered. The stand remains upright, and the cups are not attached to it.

DR. KIP: We have a working telephone and a clear result. The audience can try this with paper cups and a safe length of string.

MIRA: And when we don't attach the microphone stand to a paper cup.

TESS: My result is that a tight string carries sound better than a loose string, if both cups are held and the path is clear.

DR. KIP: A solid demonstration.

MIRA: Next week, we test whether a shadow can fit inside a paper bag.

TESS: It can, if the bag blocks the light.

MIRA: There goes the cliffhanger.

[SCIENCE THEME. THE STRING TWANGS ONCE MORE.]
