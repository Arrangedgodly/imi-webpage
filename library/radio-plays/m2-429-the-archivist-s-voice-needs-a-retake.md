# The Archivist's Voice Needs a Retake

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: museum audio guides, archival restoration documentaries, and reverent narration that mistakes every scratch for historical significance*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: An archive team restores a century-old recording of a market bell, but a narrator keeps turning every scratch and cough into a historical clue.

Cast: Archivist Reni, a macaque; narrator Joma, a baboon; technician Pavi, a gibbon; historian Tavi, an orangutan; intern Seli, a capuchin.

[SFX: TAPE HISS. A BELL RINGS, THEN A COUGH.]

JOMA: The cough of the final bell keeper.

RENI: The label says the recording technician had a cold.

TAVI: The bell was recorded at the south market in 1922.

PAVI: I can remove the hiss and leave the cough.

JOMA: The cough is our emotional bridge.

ACT ONE

Reni places the original wax cylinder in its padded case. Pavi transfers a copy to the archive computer. The first playback includes a bell, a cough, a cart wheel, and a voice announcing pears. Joma wants to call the cart wheel a signal from the old harbor. Tavi says the market is inland.

SELI: Could the wheel have come from a cart?

TAVI: That is the most likely explanation.

JOMA: It lacks mystery.

RENI: It has a source.

Pavi isolates the bell. The first strike is strong; the second is quieter because the recording stylus skipped. Joma describes it as a response from a distant tower. Reni asks the archive label to be read aloud: one market bell, one wax cylinder, two strikes due to a recording flaw.

ACT TWO

Seli cleans the digital copy. She removes a low hum but keeps the bell and market voice. Joma listens and asks for more atmosphere. Pavi adds a faint room tone from the original room. The hum returns, along with a loud scrape.

PAVI: That is the chair moving in the archive booth.

JOMA: Could it be a door opening in 1922?

RENI: The microphone was recorded in 1922. Our chair is not.

Tavi reads an inventory note describing the recording setup: one cylinder machine on a table, microphone pointed toward the market bell, operator seated beside it. Joma asks if the operator could have been afraid of the bell. Tavi says no diary or interview supports that. The cough is likely from the technician's cold, as the label states.

Reni asks Pavi to preserve the source file before any more filters are applied. Pavi saves a clean copy and labels the restoration steps. Joma asks to hear the original. The raw recording includes hiss, crackle, and the cart wheel. He hears a faint voice under the bell and calls it a secret message.

SELI: It sounds like "pears are ripe."

TAVI: That is the market caller.

JOMA: A message about pears.

ACT TWO-B

Pavi plays the original and cleaned versions back-to-back. The cleaned copy makes the words clearer but softens the wheel and cough. Reni asks which version will be used in the museum. Tavi says both: the archival original for fidelity and the cleaned excerpt for accessibility, with a note describing the restoration.

JOMA: Could I narrate the cough as the moment the operator realized history was happening?

RENI: No. We do not know that.

JOMA: Could I say the bell startled him?

PAVI: We do know the cough follows the bell, but not why.

Seli writes a caption: "A bell rings twice on the cylinder. The second strike is likely a recording skip. A cough and a caller can also be heard." Joma reads it. He asks whether "likely" can be made more dramatic. Reni says it can be spoken slowly, not changed.

Pavi demonstrates the cleaned excerpt. The bell sounds once with a brief crackle. The market caller says pears are ripe. The cart wheel rolls past. Joma begins to improvise about a secret procession, then stops when Tavi points to the inventory note. Joma chooses a simpler line: "A market day, preserved with its ordinary sounds."

ACT THREE

The archive recording is ready for the museum guide. Reni places the original cylinder back in its case. Pavi verifies that the source file and restoration copy are both saved. Seli reads the caption into the audio guide, naming the bell, cart and fruit caller without inventing a second story. Joma records the introduction in one calm take.

The guide plays the original first, then the cleaned excerpt. Visitors can choose either track. The cough remains on the archival version; the restoration note explains the added clarity and the removed hum. Tavi approves the text. Joma asks if the scratch should be described as a wound in the archive.

RENI: It is a scratch in wax.

JOMA: I will say scratch.

PAVI: The machine is ready to stop.

[SFX: PLAYBACK STOPS. CASE LATCH CLOSES.]
