# The Noon Bell Network

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live local radio event coverage*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host Leda, a macaque announcer; Sol, a nervous capuchin engineer; Mr. Pomm, a slow loris clock repairer; Councilor Venn, a gibbon who loves speeches; and callers from across Bellwether.

[SFX: STUDIO FAN, PAPER SHUFFLE. A CLOCK TICKS TOO LOUDLY.] 

LEDA: Good morning to everyone in Bellwether. You are listening to the Noon Bell Network, broadcasting live from the town library. At precisely twelve, every public clock in town will strike once together. At least, that is what the committee promised in its poster. I am looking at six clocks in this room. They show five different times and one weather forecast.

SOL: The weather clock is not broken. It is receiving the harbor signal.

LEDA: We are twenty minutes from noon. Sol, is the transmitter ready? Our graphics desk has labeled this a "townwide synchronization event."

SOL: The transmitter is ready. The tower clock is not. It lost seven minutes during last night's wind.

LEDA: Can we set it?

SOL: Yes, if someone tells me what time it is.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS. SOL ANSWERS.] 

SOL: This is the studio. What is your clock showing?

CALLER ONE: Mine says ten past eleven. It always says ten past eleven because my uncle stopped winding it before the bridge was built.

LEDA: Thank you. We have confirmed the clock is old.

[SFX: SECOND LINE BEEPS.] 

CALLER TWO: The clock at the bakery is fast. It says noon when the bread is ready.

LEDA: Is that a clock or a business policy?

CALLER TWO: Both.

[SFX: DOOR OPENS. MR. POMM ENTERS WITH A TOOL ROLL.] 

POMM: The tower clock is still connected to the bell rope. If you set it while the bell swings, it will drag the minute hand backward.

SOL: The bell isn't swinging.

POMM: It will when the pigeons land on it.

LEDA: We have a pigeon report. Stay with us.

[SFX: RADIO DIAL. DISTANT BIRD WINGS.] 

LEDA: We are now connected to the tower roof, where Councilor Venn is preparing a brief address. He has brought a second page in case the first page ends early.

VENN, REMOTE: Citizens, today our clocks will show that Bellwether moves forward together.

POMM: They do not need to move forward. They need to agree.

VENN: That is what I said, in a more inspiring way.

LEDA: Councilor, please hold the speech until the clocks work.

VENN: I have already used the word "together" twice, and the speech is only one page.

LEDA: You may use it again at noon.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS.] 

CALLER THREE: I have the courthouse clock. It stopped at six because the janitor put a mop handle through the hands to keep pigeons off.

SOL: That explains the scrape in the line.

LEDA: We are receiving reports from six buildings. Has anyone seen the town clock repairer?

POMM: I am in the studio.

LEDA: Can you repair all six clocks by noon?

POMM: I can repair the tower clock. The others need their own caretakers. A town cannot send one slow loris to every wall.

LEDA: Sol, could the radio give the town a shared time signal?

SOL: Yes. We can count down from the library clock if we know it is correct.

POMM: The library clock is correct. It is slow by one second, which is within the maker's tolerance.

LEDA: We have one reliable clock and twenty minutes. Sol, connect the signal. The committee's dramatic countdown graphic may now stop flashing. Mr. Pomm, make sure the bell rope stays clear.

[SFX: SWITCHES CLICK. A CLEAN TONE SOUNDS.] 

SOL: Signal is live. If you are listening in town, set your clock to the next chime. Do not climb a ladder while holding a clock. Ask someone to help.

VENN, REMOTE: Can I say "together" now?

LEDA: You may say it after the bell.

[SFX: TICKING GROWS. THE ROOM QUIETS.] 

LEDA: The graphic says ten seconds. It has been saying ten seconds for a minute. We will count from the clock, not the graphic. Ten. Nine. Eight.

[SFX: ONE BELL STRIKES, THEN A SCATTER OF DISTANT BELLS. SOME LATE, SOME EARLY.] 

LEDA: There they are. The bells did not ring at precisely the same time. But we heard every one of them.

CALLER ONE: My uncle's clock woke up.

CALLER TWO: The bread is ready.

VENN, REMOTE: Bellwether moves forward together.

POMM: Some clocks are still late.

LEDA: We will keep broadcasting until the last one catches up, or until Councilor Venn reaches page two, whichever happens first.

[SFX: CLOCK TICK, THEN MUSIC FROM A SMALL BRASS BAND OUTSIDE.]
