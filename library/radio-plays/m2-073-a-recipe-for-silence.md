# A Recipe for Silence

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio cooking show using sound instead of visuals*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST SENA, a capuchin; CHEF ODO, a macaque; and PRODUCER VEE, a gibbon.

[SFX: COOKING SHOW JINGLE, POT SET ON A TABLE.] 

SENA: Welcome to Kitchen by Ear, the cooking program for listeners who cannot see our stove and should not need to. Today Odo is making a warm leaf wrap.

ODO: First, choose a clean leaf. It should bend without cracking.

[SFX: LEAF FOLDS GENTLY.] 

SENA: Listeners can hear that it is ready.

VEE: Can we make the fold louder?

ODO: If I press it harder, it will tear.

VEE: We want a satisfying sound.

ODO: We want dinner.

VEE: Before the wrap, can we explain the ingredients in a more dramatic way?

ODO: The fruit is ripe. The leaf is clean. The seed is crushed.

VEE: What if the fruit has a past?

ODO: It grew on a tree.

SENA: That is a good place to begin.

VEE: Could the leaf enter from the left?

ODO: Leaves do not have entrances.

VEE: We can rustle it on cue.

[SFX: LEAF RUSTLES ONCE, THEN TWICE.] 

SENA: That second rustle sounded like a reply.

ODO: It was my hand moving the leaf.

VEE: The leaf is part of the ensemble.

ODO: If we give it a role, listeners may think it speaks.

SENA: We can say exactly what happened. Odo picked up a leaf and folded it.

VEE: That is plain.

SENA: Cooking is plain until you taste it.

ODO: The fruit is already ripe enough to mash. If it is too firm, set it aside for tomorrow instead of forcing it into the wrap.

VEE: We could record a countdown until it ripens.

ODO: It may take a day or two.

SENA: Our program is seven minutes.

VEE: We can repeat the episode.

ODO: The fruit will not ripen faster because we repeat the episode.

[SFX: ONE LEAF FOLD. THE SEED SHELL CRACKS GENTLY.] 

SENA: There is the sound we need: a soft fold, a small crack, and no one pretending the leaf has lines.

SENA: Next, place soft fruit in the center and fold the edges inward.

[SFX: FRUIT SETS DOWN. LEAF RUSTLES.] 

VEE: That sounds small.

ODO: It is one serving.

VEE: Our sponsor sent a giant bowl.

ODO: The bowl is not an ingredient.

SENA: Odo, what are you adding now?

ODO: A pinch of crushed seed.

[SFX: SEED SHELL CRACKS.] 

SENA: That was a clear crack.

VEE: We can use it as a transition.

ODO: Please don't crack the rest for sound. The seeds are food.

VEE: I have a bag of dry leaves.

ODO: Those are for the compost basket.

VEE: They rustle beautifully.

SENA: Let's keep the cooking sounds true to the recipe.

ODO: Now fold the leaf over the fruit. Press along the edge, not the middle, or the filling will slide out.

[LEAF FOLDS. FRUIT SQUEEZES OUT WITH A SOFT SQUISH.] 

SENA: Something escaped.

ODO: The fruit was too ripe. We can put it back and use a wider leaf.

VEE: Can we call that a second course?

ODO: We can call it a repair.

[ODO SETS A NEW LEAF ON THE TABLE AND TRANSFERS THE FRUIT.] 

SENA: The sound is different now. More rustle, less squish.

ODO: This leaf has a thicker edge. That holds the fruit.

VEE: I can add a drum roll while you wrap it.

ODO: A drum roll will not hold it closed.

SENA: The wrap is finished. What should listeners do next?

ODO: Eat it while it is fresh. If you are saving it, use another leaf around the outside and set it in a cool place.

VEE: What about plating?

ODO: Put it on a clean surface.

VEE: No garnish?

ODO: A small leaf is a garnish if you want one. You don't need to build a forest on the plate.

[ODO OPENS THE WRAP. A QUIET, CRISP BITE.] 

SENA: That is the first bite, and the loudest review we need.

VEE: Can we replay it?

ODO: No. It is gone.

SENA: Kitchen by Ear: clear directions, real ingredients, and a microphone that lets supper stay supper.

[SFX: JINGLE. A LEAF RUSTLES AS THE EMPTY BOWL IS WASHED.]
