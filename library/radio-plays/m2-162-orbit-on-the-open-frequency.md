# Orbit on the Open Frequency

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur radio space-watch broadcasts, crackly signals, and confident interpretations of incomplete transmissions*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
NOVA, a ring-tailed lemur host
BIX, a macaque antenna operator
PIPER, a squirrel monkey caller

[SOFT ELECTRONIC HUM. STATIC RISES AND FALLS.]

NOVA: This is Orbit on the Open Frequency. We listen to the night sky for messages from distant spacecraft. Bix, what have you picked up?

BIX: A repeating signal, three pulses and a pause.

NOVA: Three pulses may mean greeting, warning, or a spacecraft asking us to repeat the question.

BIX: It could be interference from the bakery downstairs. Their timer beeps like that.

NOVA: The universe has many kitchens. Let's isolate the frequency.

[KNOB TURNS. STATIC CLEARS TO THREE BEEPS.]

NOVA: The signal is clearer now. Three notes, a pause, then one note.

BIX: The bakery timer is still beeping.

NOVA: Perhaps the baker is communicating with Mars.

[PHONE RINGS.]

NOVA: We have a caller. Piper, you're on the air.

PIPER: I can hear your signal from the roof. I'm looking at the antenna, and the wire is touching the metal vent.

BIX: That would explain the pulse.

NOVA: Or the vent is transmitting from a distant intelligence.

PIPER: It's our own roof vent. I can see steam coming out of it.

NOVA: We must consider the possibility of a warm-blooded spacecraft.

BIX: I'll move the wire away from the vent.

[ANTENNA CLINKS. STATIC POPS.]

NOVA: The signal has changed. We now hear a low hum, followed by what sounds like a door opening.

PIPER: That's the elevator at the bakery. I'm on the shared roof, not outer space.

NOVA: A local sound, then. But local sounds can still be mysterious.

BIX: I have a cleaner signal at another frequency. It's a weather satellite passing overhead.

NOVA: Can we hear its message?

BIX: It's sending cloud data. We'll decode it.

[STATIC. A TINY MELODY.]

NOVA: I hear a voice: "Please leave the loading bay clear."

PIPER: That's my radio. The bakery uses the same channel to call the delivery cart.

NOVA: The message is repeating. It may be a warning to Earth.

BIX: It's a warning to keep the flour delivery space open.

NOVA: We have learned that the sky contains many signals, and some require moving a cart.

[WHEELS ROLL. A DOOR SHUTS. THE STATIC FADES.]

PIPER: The delivery is through. The vent is clear. The antenna wire is free.

BIX: Now I have something. A faint sequence, not the bakery, not the elevator, not our transmitter.

NOVA: Three pulses, then a pause?

BIX: Yes. The space station is passing overhead.

NOVA: What does it say?

BIX: It's a tracking beacon. No words, just timing.

NOVA: Then we will answer in the language of timing.

[THREE TAPS ON A METAL CUP. PAUSE. ONE TAP.]

PIPER: That's the same rhythm as the bakery timer.

NOVA: We can't rule anything out.

BIX: I have the satellite plotted and the roof equipment working. That's a good night's watch.

NOVA: To our listeners: if you hear a strange transmission, check the cable before naming the planet.

PIPER: And check whether the bakery is open.

[STATIC SWELLS. A SINGLE BEEP, THEN TWO.]

BIX: There's another burst. It's coming from the weather satellite, not the roof.

NOVA: Could it be a reply to our cup signal?

BIX: No. The data says cloud cover will increase after midnight.

NOVA: Then our listeners can prepare for a cloudy sky without assuming it has a message.

PIPER: The bakery closes at ten.

NOVA: That signal is urgent. We have twenty minutes to buy a cinnamon roll.

BIX: You can walk downstairs.

NOVA: A short-range expedition! We'll document it after the broadcast.

NOVA: Tomorrow's broadcast will monitor the moon's reflection in a puddle. Until then, keep your antenna pointed upward and your delivery bay clear.

[THEME HUM. THREE BEEPS. A PAUSE. ONE BEEP.]
