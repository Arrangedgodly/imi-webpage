# The Pet Show by Ear

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio talent contests, audience voting, and pet competitions judged through overconfident sound-only commentary*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A radio pet contest asks listeners to identify animals by sound, but the contestants keep choosing the studio technician instead.

Cast: Host Lemi, a gibbon; judge Pavo, a macaque; owner Reka, an orangutan; caller Suni, a capuchin; sound operator Daro, a baboon.

[SFX: A SOFT RATTLE, THEN A SHORT CHIRP.]

LEMI: Welcome to Best Pet by Ear. Is that a bird, a hamster, or a very small door?

PAVO: I say bird.

REKA: That is my tortoise walking across its water dish.

DARO: It is the mic stand shaking.

LEMI: The competition has begun.

ACT ONE

The rules say each owner submits one short recording, and listeners vote for the most recognizable pet sound. Reka's tortoise makes a soft scrape. Suni has sent a parrot call. A third entry is a rabbit thumping once in a quiet room. Pavo asks for dramatic descriptions; Lemi reminds him the show is audio-only.

PAVO: The tortoise approaches its bowl with purpose.

REKA: It approaches the lettuce.

PAVO: The rabbit thump conveys urgency.

Suni calls in to clarify that her parrot repeats the microwave timer, not a greeting. Daro plays the clip. The parrot says "three minutes" in a clear voice, then imitates the timer's beep. Lemi asks if that is a pet sound or an appliance. Suni says the parrot learned it from the kitchen.

ACT TWO

The listener poll opens. Daro receives three calls, all voting for the microphone rattle. Lemi asks if the voters heard the entries. They say yes; the rattle sounded like a tiny animal running over gravel. Daro stops touching the stand. The sound disappears.

LEMI: Our leading contestant has been disqualified.

DARO: I was not a contestant.

PAVO: The audience has decided otherwise.

Suni asks whether the parrot can enter the final round with its timer imitation. Pavo says it has to make a natural sound. Reka says tortoises rarely perform on cue. Lemi asks if they can replay the raw recordings without commentary. Pavo agrees, but announces each pet with an elaborate title: "Sir Shell of the Lettuce Bowl," "The Rabbit Who Knocked," and "The Parrot Who Knows the Oven."

Daro plays the clips. The tortoise scrapes once; rabbit thumps; parrot calls, then says "three minutes." The audience line erupts with votes for the parrot. Pavo says the timer voice is not a call. Suni asks if the parrot can be judged for mimicry. Lemi checks the rules; mimicry is permitted.

ACT TWO-B

Pavo asks each owner to give one sentence about the pet before the final vote. Reka says her tortoise likes warm lettuce and ignores applause. Suni says the parrot uses the timer because the family says it every day. The rabbit's owner is not on the call, so Lemi reads the written note: "He thumps when he hears the treat jar."

PAVO: We have personality, motivation, and a treat jar.

LEMI: We have three animals, not three acts.

The audience votes again. The parrot wins by one call. Pavo asks Suni to repeat the sound. The parrot says "three minutes," then adds "where is lettuce?" Reka says her tortoise has been looking at the lettuce for the entire recording. Daro asks if the rattle can be removed from the final mix. It is gone now that the mic stand is secured.

ACT THREE

Lemi announces the parrot as winner of the sound-only contest for mimicry. The tortoise receives the calmest arrival award, which Pavo invents on the spot. Reka says the tortoise is not competing for awards. Lemi says it will not need to attend the ceremony. Suni gives the parrot a small treat after the microphone is off.

Pavo closes the show by asking listeners to vote for next week's pet. Daro checks the stand and discovers the rattle came from a loose metal clip, not an animal. He tightens it. Lemi says the show has identified its final sound. Pavo says it was not a contestant, but it did receive the most votes.

TAG

[SFX: ONE METAL CLIP SNAPS INTO PLACE.]

DARO: Not a pet.

FINAL IMAGE: The loose clip sits beside the scorecard while the parrot's timer voice fades into the quiet studio.
