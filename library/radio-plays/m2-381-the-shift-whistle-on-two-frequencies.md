# The Shift Whistle on Two Frequencies

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: industrial heritage radio shows that confuse a recorded shift whistle with a live rail signal*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Host Nalo, a gibbon; mill keeper Feni, a macaque; rail dispatcher Oru, a lemur; visitor Kasa, a capuchin.

[SFX: OLD MILL YARD. A WHISTLE SOUNDS LONG, THEN TWO SHORT BLASTS. RADIO MIC OPENS.]

NALO: We are broadcasting the historic shift whistle from the mill tower.

FENI: That was our archive recording. The yard speaker played it at noon.

ORU: The rail line uses a similar pattern for a crossing alert.

KASA: I heard it from the road and stopped my cart.

NALO: The old whistle is part of the mill tour.

ORU: The live crossing signal is on the east track. It just sounded too.

[SFX: TWO SHORT BLASTS SOUND FARTHER AWAY, FOLLOWED BY A TRAIN BELL.]

FENI: That one came from the railway.

KASA: They sound alike.

ORU: The train is approaching the east crossing. Stay behind the painted line.

NALO: Can we play the archive whistle again for comparison?

ORU: Not while a train is approaching. The extra sound could confuse people.

FENI: I'll shut the yard speaker off.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICKS. THE YARD SPEAKER STOPS.]

NALO: The program producer wanted both whistles layered for a dramatic sound.

KASA: I would rather know which one means move away from the track.

ORU: The rail crossing has a bell and flashing lamps. The mill whistle has neither.

[SFX: RAIL BELL RINGS. TRAIN WHEELS APPROACH.]

FENI: I can hear the bell now. The mill speaker is silent.

NALO: The train has passed. The crossing is clear.

ORU: Wait until the lamps stop flashing before anyone crosses.

[SFX: TRAIN FADES. BELL STOPS. TWO RELAYS CLICK; LAMPS GO DARK.]

KASA: The road is clear.

FENI: The mill archive file says "noon shift change." It should never play on a live crossing feed.

NALO: The same speaker line serves the tour and heritage broadcast.

ORU: Separate the mill channel from the rail channel. The crossing signal needs its own warning circuit.

NALO: The tour script says the shift whistle will be followed by a live crossing warning.

ORU: Remove that line. The rail alarm is not part of the mill visit.

NALO: The studio wants a reprise. We could layer the archive whistle under the rail bell and call it a two-tone heritage special.

ORU: No. The bell tells people a train is coming. It does not belong under a program effect.

KASA: I thought the mill whistle meant the same thing when it sounded outside.

FENI: The indoor recording can keep its full sound. The rail signal keeps its bell and lamps.

NALO: Then the program needs two separate clips, with the place named before each one.

ORU: That will make the edit clearer and the crossing quieter.

FENI: I will move the archive player indoors now. The outside yard speaker stays off during train hours.

[SFX: CABLE UNPLUGS FROM THE MUTED YARD SPEAKER AND CONNECTS TO THE VISITOR ROOM JACK.]

FENI: The recording is indoors. The yard speaker is disconnected.

NALO: Add the date and source to the audio guide.

[SFX: LABEL PRINTER CLICKS OUT: "MILL ARCHIVE, NOON SHIFT, RECORDED 1958."]

KASA: Now the archive whistle is in the visitor room and the rail circuit is outside.

ORU: I will test the crossing bell once. No archive file on this line.

[SFX: A CONTROL TEST BELL RINGS ONCE. NO WHISTLE.]

FENI: One rail bell, one circuit.

NALO: We can play the mill recording inside after the test.

[SFX: INDOOR SPEAKER PLAYS ONE ARCHIVE WHISTLE. OUTSIDE, THE TRACK IS QUIET.]

KASA: The sounds stay in their own places.

ORU: And the warning circuit stays separate from the tour.

FENI: I will label the clips "mill recording" and "crossing signal."

KASA: I can tell the archive from the real bell now.

[SFX: RADIO MIC CLICKS OFF. MILL WHEEL CREAKS; DISTANT TRACK REMAINS SILENT.]
