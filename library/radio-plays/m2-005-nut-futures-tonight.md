# Nut Futures Tonight

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Financial pundit panels and breathless market analysis*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Glenn, a macaque anchor; Tova, a tree squirrel analyst; Milt, a baboon trader; Wren, a gibbon caller from the northern grove; the producer, a marmoset watching the feed.

[SFX: MARKET BELL, THEN A WALNUT CRACKS.]

HOST GLENN: Good evening. This is Nut Futures Tonight, the show that watches the price of acorns so you can watch us argue about the price of acorns. I'm Glenn, a macaque in a tie. With me are analyst Tova, a tree squirrel who reads bark futures, and trader Milt, a baboon who once bought a barrel of chestnuts because it looked lonely.

GLENN: The winter reserve index fell four points. Tova, is the forest in trouble?

TOVA: The reserve index counts only acorns. It doesn't count beech nuts, pine seeds, or the mushrooms every dormouse has been drying for months. A four-point drop means one warehouse got damp. It doesn't mean everyone is hungry.

MILT: It means acorns are getting cheaper. I'm buying.

TOVA: Your portfolio is a sack under your desk.

MILT: It is a diversified sack. There are three kinds of nuts.

GLENN: We have a caller from the northern grove. What are you seeing?

CALLER WREN: I'm a wren, and the trees are full. We had rain late in spring, so the acorns came in unevenly. The south slope has plenty. The ridge has almost none. The market screen calls it a shortage, but my neighbors trade across the hill.

GLENN: Milt, does that change your position?

MILT: I am maintaining a cautious optimism with a small leveraged basket.

TOVA: You just said you were buying.

MILT: I bought a basket. It has no nuts in it yet.

[SFX: A CHART PRINTER RUNS, THEN STOPS.] 

GLENN: Our screen is showing a sudden spike. The acorn index just climbed twelve points. Is this a rally?

TOVA: Wait. The data feed has switched from nuts per cart to nuts per squirrel. Someone changed the unit.

MILT: That sounds bullish.

TOVA: It sounds like a spreadsheet error.

GLENN: Can we confirm it?

PRODUCER, OFF MIC: The signal comes from the east warehouse. Their scales are set to count each shell as a separate nut.

TOVA: The number is inflated by empty shells. Don't trade on it.

MILT: I have already placed an order.

GLENN: Cancel it.

MILT: The button is asking me to confirm that I am certain I want to cancel my certainty.

TOVA: Use the phone.

MILT: I don't have a phone. I have a burrow bell.

[SFX: BELL RINGS. A PAUSE. SOMEONE ANSWERS FROM FAR AWAY.] 

MILT: Cancel my order! No, not the order for lunch. The acorn order!

GLENN: While Milt sorts that out, Tova, what should listeners watch?

TOVA: Local stores. If your village has enough food and a dry place to keep it, the number on this screen is only a number. If your stores are low, ask nearby groves what they can spare before a distant trader buys everything on rumor.

CALLER WREN: Our grove can send two carts of pine seeds to the ridge. We have more than we can bury before thaw.

GLENN: That sounds less dramatic than a rally.

TOVA: It will also feed animals.

GLENN: We have a question from a listener who wants to know whether the pine seed carts will lower the acorn price.

TOVA: They might lower demand for acorns in that village. They will not refill the east warehouse. Different food, different stores, same hungry squirrels.

MILT: Does the pear count as a hedge?

TOVA: It counts as lunch.

[SFX: BURROW BELL RINGS AGAIN.] 

MILT: The order is canceled. I lost three minutes and gained no nuts.

TOVA: That is the first sound investment you've made.

GLENN: At the close, the corrected acorn index is flat. The east warehouse has agreed to recalibrate its scales. Milt has bought a real lunch basket, which contains one pear and enough room for honest accounting.

MILT: I don't like the pear's outlook.

GLENN: This has been Nut Futures Tonight. Remember, a chart cannot tell you what is growing behind your house. Go look.

MILT: And bring a lunch basket.

[THE MARKET BELL RINGS. A PEAR ROLLS OFF A TABLE. END.]
