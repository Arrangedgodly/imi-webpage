# The Origami Crane Keeps Unfolding

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: craft broadcasts that promise a perfect result before the paper is even flat*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-016*

Cast: Host Mara, a macaque; paper artist Seli, a lemur; assistant Pip, a capuchin; producer Vex, an orangutan; caller Odo, a gibbon.

SFX: Studio table. Paper rustles. A desk bell signals the start.

MARA: Welcome to Fold Along Friday. Today Seli will make a practice paper crane.

VEX: The companion camera is on, so viewers can follow the folds as well as the audio.

SELI: This is my practice bird. It has a head, two wings, and no interest in your stopwatch.

VEX: The card says seven steps.

SELI: It takes more than seven steps.

VEX: The card says seven.

SELI: The card doesn't have fingers.

PIP: I have two hands and a tail.

SELI: Keep the tail off the paper.

MARA: We are live. What do we need?

SELI: One square sheet, flat on the table.

SFX: Paper flaps, then lands.

PIP: My sheet is trying to leave.

SELI: Put a smooth stone on one corner.

VEX: The stone is not in the supply list.

MARA: It is in the studio. Use it.

SELI: First fold the square diagonally, point to point. Crease lightly.

SFX: Fold. A crisp crease.

PIP: My corner is a little past the other one.

SELI: Flatten it before the crease sets.

VEX: We should keep the pace. Step two.

SELI: We have to open it again.

VEX: Why?

SELI: So we can use the line in the next fold.

VEX: The audience will think we are undoing progress.

MARA: Let them hear the paper.

SFX: Paper opens. It crackles.

ODO, on phone: Is the crane already done?

MARA: Caller Odo, we're on step one.

ODO: I heard "open it again."

SELI: It is part of the fold.

VEX: The instruction card says "make a bird."

SELI: That is not an instruction.

PIP: My paper has a mountain and a valley.

SELI: Good. Fold both side corners to the center line.

SFX: Two careful creases. Another paper flaps.

PIP: The stone is holding the corner now. My hand can make the crease.

MARA: Progress.

VEX: We need a close-up.

SFX: Camera arm squeaks over the table. A light clicks on.

SELI: Don't touch the sheet.

VEX: I am only bringing the camera closer.

SFX: Camera arm taps the table. Paper springs open.

PIP: The crane has unfolded.

SELI: The fold was not locked. We'll start that step again.

VEX: Could we say it was a deliberate reset?

MARA: No. We can say what happened.

ODO: I am still listening. My page is flat now.

SELI: Then press each crease with the back of a spoon. Fold both sides to the center again. Hold the bottom point.

SFX: Spoon glides over paper. Two folds settle.

PIP: The wings are narrow.

SELI: They're supposed to be. Fold the top edge down, then open it. We use that crease to reverse the point.

VEX: Are we opening the paper again?

SELI: Yes.

VEX: Listeners are going to think origami is mostly undoing things.

SELI: It is mostly deciding what should stay folded.

MARA: That sounds like the show.

SFX: Paper turns. Pip carefully reverses the center point; the sheet resists.

PIP: It is tearing at the corner.

SELI: Stop. Lift the fold. Move the camera arm away, and use the uncreased edge.

SFX: Camera arm swings aside. Paper eases flat.

PIP: It holds.

SELI: Now bring one narrow point up for the neck and the other for the tail. Bend the neck tip back to make the head. Fold one wing down on each side and press both creases.

PIP: One wing is longer.

SELI: Make the other one match by eye. Paper is forgiving.

VEX: The seven-step card says "perfect symmetry."

SELI: The crane only needs to stand.

SFX: Seli sets her practice crane upright. It tips, then balances against the studio stone.

MARA: We have a crane.

ODO: Mine stands too.

VEX: The camera lost the close-up.

PIP: I can hold mine beside the microphone.

SFX: Crane taps the mic stand and stays upright.

MARA: The audience heard every crease.

SELI: That is the useful part.

VEX: What is our closing instruction?

SELI: If it opens again, fold it again.

TAG

SFX: Paper unfolds softly.

VEX: Was that another crane?

PIP: No. That's my cue card.
