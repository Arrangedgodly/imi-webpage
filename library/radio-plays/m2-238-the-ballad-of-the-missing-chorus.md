# The Ballad of the Missing Chorus

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live community singalong radio convention, where the host keeps insisting a chorus is present though nobody can remember it*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Host KITO, macaque; pianist SAVA, lemur; singer PAVA, gibbon; caller ODO, baboon.

[SFX: PIANO INTRO, CUTS OFF.] 

KITO: Welcome to the community singalong. Tonight we sing "A Basket by the Door."

PAVA: I know the first verse.

SAVA: I have the sheet music.

ODO: I remember the chorus.

KITO: Wonderful. Please sing it.

ODO: I remember that I remember it.

KITO: That's not the chorus.

ODO: It is the beginning of remembering.

Sava plays the melody. Pava sings the verse about setting a basket by the door. At the point where the chorus should begin, everyone stops.

[SFX: PIANO NOTE HANGS.] 

KITO: The chorus is traditionally sung by everyone.

PAVA: It is usually sung after the verse.

SAVA: The sheet has a blank space.

ODO: That's where the chorus goes.

KITO: We can fill it with a hum.

They hum. Sava plays a different chord; Pava stops.

PAVA: That is not the song.

SAVA: It's a chord.

ODO: It could be the chorus if we all agree.

KITO: The audience is listening. Let us ask a caller.

Miri calls from the village. She says the chorus begins with "Bring it in." Odo says it begins with "Set it down." Pava thinks both lines appear in a different song.

KITO: We have two possible openings.

SAVA: And no middle.

Miri hums a tune into the phone. The line crackles; the hum sounds like a squeaky wheel.

ODO: That is the chorus.

PAVA: It is the phone.

Sava tries to write down the tune. The paper slides under the piano. Kito asks the audience to keep humming while she retrieves it. The callers hum in different keys.

[SFX: FOUR HUMS, PHONE STATIC.] 

KITO: This is a remarkable arrangement.

SAVA: It is not an arrangement. It is four wrong notes.

ODO: Perhaps the chorus was meant to be sung by four people who disagree.

Pava proposes they sing only the verse. Kito refuses, saying the program promised a chorus. Odo suggests repeating the first line louder. They try it; the room becomes more confident, but still does not remember the words.

Sava asks each person to contribute one phrase. Pava offers "bring the basket," Odo suggests "mind the step," and Kito says "the door is open." The three phrases do not rhyme, but they do describe what to do with a basket.

SAVA: I can put them in the same measure.

PAVA: The song is about a basket, not a safety notice.

ODO: It is by the door. A safety notice is relevant.

KITO: We should not sing instructions.

ODO: We just sang about waiting for someone to bring it inside.

KITO: That's different. It has a melody.

Sava plays a low note for "mind the step" and a high one for "door is open." The phrases begin to fit. Odo enters early, Miri comes in late, and Kito is left alone. Sava changes the chord so they can find the beat together. KITO: "A basket by the door-"

ALL: A basket by the door-

KITO: "Waiting for-"

ALL: Waiting for-

They stop again.

PAVA: Waiting for someone to bring it inside.

ODO: That is the chorus.

SAVA: It is the next line of the verse.

KITO: It is both now.

Sava plays the melody, and everyone sings the new line. It fits. Miri on the phone joins in, half a beat late.

[SFX: PIANO AND VOICES, FINISH.] 

KITO: We have completed the song.

ODO: Did we remember the chorus?

PAVA: No. We wrote one.

KITO: A community tradition begins with someone forgetting the old words.

SAVA: The sheet music is under the piano.

PAVA: Leave it there. We may need it next week.

[SFX: PIANO PLAYS THE NEW CHORUS ONCE.]
