# The Unlabeled Tape at Station Nine

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio archive mysteries, preservation rules, and producers who treat a damaged recording as a secret broadcast*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
ARCHIVIST SERA, a gibbon
HOST TAVI, a macaque
TECH ODO, a baboon
INTERN PIP, a capuchin

[SFX: REEL DECK WHIRS, TAPE HISS.]

TAVI: We are in the station archive, where archivist Sera has found a reel marked only "Nine."

SERA: It has no date, title, or recording speed.

ODO: It is spooled backward.

PIP: I found it in the box labeled "miscellaneous."

TAVI: Every mystery begins in a box labeled miscellaneous.

SERA: Do not play it until we check the leader tape.

ODO: I already checked. The reel is brittle.

TAVI: It could contain a lost broadcast.

SERA: It could contain a test tone.

PIP: Or a person saying "testing."

[SFX: TAPE INSERTS, BUTTON CLICKS.]

Sera plays two seconds at low volume. A voice says "nine," followed by a chair scrape.

TAVI: A secret code.

ODO: A station identification.

SERA: Or someone counting down.

PIP: The chair scrape sounds like our studio chair.

TAVI: This recording is old.

PIP: Chairs were invented a long time ago.

Sera checks the tape box. A faded route card is stuck under the reel. It lists "Studio 9, chair test, no broadcast."
 
Sera checks the reel's edge with a magnifier and asks Odo to inspect the spool for damage. He finds a splice where the paper leader meets the magnetic tape.

ODO: The splice is loose.

PIP: Is that why it says nine?

SERA: The label may have been written by whoever tested the chair.

TAVI: Could it be a countdown for a live show?

SERA: A countdown would need a date or program note.

Pip compares the handwriting to the station's old engineering logs. The number nine matches a studio test sheet. The log lists several chairs by number, each tested for squeaks before a broadcast.

[SFX: PAGES TURN.]

TAVI: Studio Nine had three chairs.

ODO: Which one is on the tape?

SERA: We can tell by the scrape pattern, but we should not play more than necessary.

They listen to one short section and hear a chair roll, stop, then squeak. The old sheet labels the third chair "too loud."

PIP: The chair was rejected.

TAVI: It still made it into the archive.


TAVI: The secret code is a chair test.

ODO: We have solved the mystery.

SERA: Not yet. The reel is still backward.

She rewinds the tape to the beginning and turns the reel correctly. The recording plays: "Nine, eight, seven," then a sequence of chair scrapes and a voice asking for a new stool.

[SFX: TAPE PLAYBACK, COUNTDOWN, SCRAPE.]

TAVI: It is a countdown.

ODO: It is a chair audition.

PIP: The stool was not selected.

SERA: The recording has no broadcast. We should not edit it into a mystery.

TAVI: Can we preserve it as a studio test?

SERA: Yes. We need to record the condition, the date found, and the playback settings.

ODO: I will write down the speed.

PIP: The reel is still brittle.

SERA: We will digitize a short segment, then store the original flat.

Tavi asks whether the public can hear the clip. Sera says the station can play the restored test with a label explaining what it is. Odo starts recording, but his chair squeaks over the playback.

[SFX: LIVE CHAIR SCRAPE, TAPE CHAIR SCRAPE.]

TAVI: The old chair and the new chair are talking.

ODO: The new chair is asking for a replacement.

PIP: It has identified itself.

SERA: No. We will record the tape without the live room.

They move the deck to a quiet table and capture the short countdown cleanly. The original reel goes into a protective box labeled "Studio 9 chair test; no broadcast; fragile."

[SFX: BOX LID CLOSES.]

TAVI: The mystery has become a record.

SERA: The record was always there.

ODO: The chair test has passed.

PIP: The chair did not get the job.

[SFX: REEL STOPS, QUIET ROOM.]
