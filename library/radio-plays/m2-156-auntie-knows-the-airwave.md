# Auntie Knows the Airwave

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio advice call-ins, breathy empathy, and a host who prescribes a slogan before hearing the whole problem*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
AUNTIE VEE, a macaque advice host
KITO, a capuchin caller
RUE, a ring-tailed lemur producer

[WARM PIANO THEME. PHONE LINE OPENS.]

AUNTIE VEE: Welcome to Auntie Knows the Airwave, the program that gives you a sentence you can carry around even when the advice is too heavy. Kito, what is on your heart?

KITO: My neighbor borrows my ladder every week. He returns it clean, but he parks it in front of my door.

AUNTIE VEE: That sounds like a boundary asking to be spoken aloud.

KITO: The ladder is taller than the door. I cannot get out.

AUNTIE VEE: Then your neighbor has placed a boundary in front of your boundary.

RUE: Is there a second exit?

KITO: Yes, the window. But I'm on the second floor.

AUNTIE VEE: Do not climb through it. The station's liability consultant has asked me to add that sentence to all advice.

[THE PIANO PLAYS A SOFT RESOLVING CHORD.]

AUNTIE VEE: I recommend a direct conversation. Say, "Please return my ladder to the shed, not my doorway."

KITO: I did. He thought I meant the shed door, so now the ladder blocks the shed.

RUE: Does he know it's your ladder?

KITO: He borrowed it from me.

AUNTIE VEE: We're hearing a pattern. Perhaps he is unable to recognize property when it is tall.

KITO: He recognizes it. He writes my name on the handle before he brings it over.

AUNTIE VEE: Then the handle is carrying your message for you. How does that feel?

KITO: Heavy. The ladder is aluminum.

[THE THEME SWELLS.]

AUNTIE VEE: Listeners, when an object is heavy, let your feelings be light. Rue, bring me the slogan card.

RUE: It says, "Speak your truth, then make room for an answer."

AUNTIE VEE: Lovely. Kito, use this card to express your boundary.

KITO: It's a card. I need the ladder to reach the shelf where the cards are stored.

RUE: He has a point.

AUNTIE VEE: Then we'll solve it together, right here. Kito, can you call the neighbor while we're on air?

KITO: He is listening. He called before me and asked for the ladder back.

AUNTIE VEE: Why did he need it?

KITO: He wants to move it from the shed door to my front door. He says that makes the return convenient.

[PHONE BEEPS. ANOTHER LINE OPENS.]

AUNTIE VEE: Are we being joined by the neighbor?

RUE: That's the neighbor calling in.

AUNTIE VEE: Welcome. Can you hear us?

NEIGHBOR: Yes. I can hear Kito. I didn't realize the ladder blocked his way out. I thought I was returning it to the place where I borrowed it.

KITO: I lent it to you from the yard.

NEIGHBOR: Then I'll put it back in the yard. I'm there now.

[METAL LADDER CLANKS IN THE DISTANCE.]

KITO: I can hear it land.

AUNTIE VEE: A clear request, a clear answer, and a ladder in a useful place. This is a rare three-part resolution.

RUE: The call-in line is flashing again.

AUNTIE VEE: We have time for one more caller.

RUE: It's the neighbor. He wants to ask how to apologize for moving the ladder twice.

KITO: Tell him to bring the muffins he promised.

AUNTIE VEE: Kito has offered a concrete next step. Neighbor, bring the muffins, and leave the ladder outside.

[THE PIANO TURNS INTO A CHEERFUL JINGLE.]

AUNTIE VEE: Our slogan today: "Say what you need; then check whether a ladder is involved."

RUE: I'll print that on a card.

KITO: Put the card on the shelf by the window.

AUNTIE VEE: We are not advising the window route. This has been Auntie Knows the Airwave.

[THEME MUSIC. A LADDER CLANKS ONCE MORE, SAFELY OUTSIDE.]
