# The Critic in the Gallery

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Art criticism podcasts and opaque exhibition reviews*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Host Obo, a mandrill art critic; Sella, a squirrel monkey sculptor; Marn, a capuchin visitor; Deet, a beetle gallery guard.

[SFX: LARGE ROOM TONE. ONE DRIP IN A METAL BUCKET.] 

OBO: You're listening to Looking Closely, the art review that uses a microphone so we can stand farther away. I'm Obo. We're at the Marshlight Gallery with Sella's new installation, *Room for a River*. It contains one ladder, eight blue stones, and the steady drip of a leak from the ceiling.

SELLA: The drip is not in the piece.

OBO: That is what the gallery guide says. It also says the ladder represents migration, the stones represent memory, and the empty jar is "an invitation to notice absence."

SELLA: The jar holds my screws. I forgot to take it home.

OBO: That's unusually direct.

SELLA: I made the ladder from wood that washed up after the river changed course. The stones came from places where people used to cross. The jar is just a jar.

OBO: Then why is it on the pedestal?

SELLA: The pedestal was empty. I needed somewhere to put the screws while I was leveling the stones.

[SFX: A SMALL SCREW ROLLS ACROSS CONCRETE.] 

OBO: One has escaped.

SELLA: Leave it. It will reach the wall and stop.

OBO: The wall has a felt base.

SELLA: Then it will stop sooner.

OBO: A review should describe what the work does. Let me try. The ladder makes a path out of a landscape that has vanished. The stones mark the pieces of that path we can still touch. The jar collects the hardware needed to hold it together.

SELLA: That's good. The jar still holds screws.

OBO: Art can be more than the artist intended.

SELLA: It can. But that does not make the screws less useful.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS. A VISITOR TAPS A STONE.] 

MARN: I crossed this river when I was little. There was a flat rock where my family stopped to eat. It's not here now. I hear this piece and remember the smell of wet leaves.

OBO: Did the ladder bring that back?

MARN: The sound did. My feet remember the slope. The ladder made me stop walking for a moment.

SELLA: That's why I left space around it. I don't want visitors climbing on the art. I want them to hear the room.

DEET: People keep asking whether the drip is part of the exhibit. It is from a pipe above the north window.

OBO: Has the gallery repaired it?

DEET: They filed a ticket. The repair crew is waiting for the installer to approve the room's "water event."

SELLA: The water event is a leak.

OBO: A clear review, then. The installation asks us to listen to a river that is no longer in this room. The ceiling asks us to listen to a maintenance issue that is.

DEET: I can put a bucket under it.

SELLA: There is one by the door. That's where I put it before the opening.

[SFX: BUCKET CARRIED ACROSS FLOOR, SET UNDER THE DRIP. THE ROOM TONE CHANGES.] 

OBO: The drip has moved into the composition.

SELLA: It has moved into a bucket.

MARN: The room sounds calmer.

OBO: My review will say the gallery should fix the pipe, the guide should explain the real materials, and the jar should go home with the sculptor.

SELLA: Agreed. I will take the screws. Keep the stones in the room until the river story has been heard.

OBO: Looking Closely ends here. Sometimes a work holds a memory. Sometimes it holds screws. Often the distinction depends on whether you ask the person who made it.

DEET: The repair crew has arrived.

SELLA: Tell them the pipe is above the north window.

DEET: They brought a ladder.

OBO: Is that part of the installation?

SELLA: Only if they put it on the pedestal.

DEET: They are asking where the bucket goes.

MARN: By the drip. It has good acoustics there.

[SFX: SCREW ROLLS, HITS THE BUCKET. MUSIC OUT.]
