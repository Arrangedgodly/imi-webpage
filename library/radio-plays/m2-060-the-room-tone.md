# The Room Tone

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio drama sound-effect excess and behind-the-scenes specials*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Director Fenn, a macaque; actor Luma, a capuchin; sound artist Pala, an orangutan; and assistant Sedge, a gibbon.

[SFX: THUNDER, HORSE HOOVES, A DOOR SLAMS, THEN A PHONE RINGS.] 

FENN: Stop. The scene is in a quiet kitchen.

PALA: I have thunder, hooves, a door, and a phone.

LUMA: The script says a kettle boils.

PALA: I can make that sound dramatic.

FENN: Make it sound like water heating.

[SFX: KETTLE STARTS. LOW HUM.] 

SEDGE: The hum is clean.

FENN: We need room tone before we record dialogue. Everyone be quiet for ten seconds.

[SFX: SILENCE FOR THREE SECONDS, THEN A CHAIR CREAKS.] 

PALA: We have a chair.

FENN: We have a chair in the room. Keep it.

LUMA: I need to say the line, "I have been waiting all morning."

FENN: Say it as if someone is there with you.

LUMA: I have been waiting all morning.

PALA: Should there be rain against the window?

FENN: Is it raining in the scene?

LUMA: No.

PALA: Then no rain.

[SFX: KETTLE WHISTLES.] 

SEDGE: That is the kettle.

FENN: It is a little loud.

PALA: Should I lower it?

FENN: Lower the microphone, not the kettle. The actor has to hear the cue.

LUMA: The line is, "The tea is ready."

FENN: Go.

LUMA: The tea is ready.

[SFX: CUP SETS ON TABLE, A SPOON TAPS CERAMIC.] 

PALA: The spoon tap feels lonely.

FENN: It is a spoon.

PALA: I can add a piano note.

FENN: No.

PALA: A soft string?

FENN: No.

PALA: A distant train?

FENN: Absolutely not.

SEDGE: The room tone has changed. The fan turned off.

FENN: Good catch. We need a new ten seconds without the fan.

[SFX: ROOM TONE. SOFT VENT. A SMALL FOOTSTEP OUTSIDE.] 

LUMA: Someone is in the hall.

FENN: Is that in the scene?

PALA: It is a real footstep.

FENN: Then it goes in the room tone, unless it distracts from the actor.

LUMA: I have been waiting all morning.

[SFX: KETTLE, CUP, SPOON. NO EXTRA MUSIC.] 

LUMA: The tea is ready.

[SFX: CHAIR CREAKS.] 

LUMA: Sit down before it gets cold.

FENN: That's the scene.

PALA: It needs one more sound.

FENN: Which one?

PALA: The listener breathing.

FENN: We do not add the listener.

SEDGE: We do need a slate for the recording.

FENN: State the take number and room condition.

SEDGE: Take four. Fan off. Kettle boiled. One chair creak. Hall step retained.

PALA: No thunder?

FENN: No thunder.

PALA: No horse?

FENN: No horse.

PALA: No phone?

FENN: The phone was not in the script.

[SFX: STUDIO PHONE RINGS.] 

SEDGE: That is the station phone.

FENN: Don't answer during room tone.

[SFX: PHONE STOPS. SILENCE FOR TEN SECONDS.] 

PALA: That's the room.

FENN: That's the room.

LUMA: And the tea is cold.

FENN: Hold. Keep rolling through the kettle's last click.

PALA: I have a cue for the ending. One small spoon tap, followed by a low chord that says "time has passed."

FENN: The spoon is already in the scene. We don't need to explain it with music.

PALA: What if I play the chord so quietly nobody notices?

SEDGE: Then it is room tone with ambition.

[SFX: PALA PLUCKS ONE LOW STRING. THE KETTLE CLICKS OFF.]

LUMA: Was that the chord?

PALA: No. That was the kettle.

FENN: Keep the kettle. Lose the chord. The scene ends when the tea stops heating.

[SFX: LOW HUM FALLS AWAY. A CHAIR CREAKS ONCE.]

SEDGE: We should slate that last sound.

FENN: No. We should let it be a chair.

PALA: Should I name the chair creak in the sound library?

FENN: Only if you can spell "chair."

SEDGE: I have filed it under "furniture, accidental."

LUMA: The kettle has stopped. That sound can stay.

[SFX: QUIET THEME MUSIC, THEN ROOM TONE.]
