# The Meteor Shower Call-in

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night call-in science broadcasts, breathless caller theories, and hosts who must distinguish a real observation from a flashlight*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST PAVA, a macaque
ASTRONOMER VEX, an orangutan
CALLER LEO, a lemur
PRODUCER TAVI, a gibbon

[SFX: NIGHT CRICKETS, STUDIO MIC OPENS.]

PAVA: Welcome to Skywatch After Dark. A meteor shower is expected tonight, and our lines are open for observations.

VEX: A meteor is a streak of light caused by a small object entering the atmosphere. It is not a star falling.

LEO (PHONE): I saw one land in my yard.

TAVI: The caller is live.

PAVA: Where did it land?

LEO: Beside the shed. It glowed green.

VEX: Did it leave a crater?

LEO: It left a circle on the grass.

TAVI: We have a second report from the east hill.

PAVA: You have only one caller.

TAVI: The producer board is lighting up.

[SFX: PHONE LINES BEEP.]

VEX: Ask whether the caller saw it move.

PAVA: Did the light move across the sky?

LEO: No, it stayed on the shed.

VEX: That is a porch lamp.

LEO: It turned on by itself.

TAVI: We may have a mystery.

VEX: Or a motion sensor.

[SFX: PHONE BUTTONS, LINE RUSTLE.]

Pava asks Leo to step away from the shed and look back. The green light switches off. When he returns, it switches on again.

LEO: It follows me.

VEX: The sensor follows movement.

PAVA: We have identified the meteor's orbit.

TAVI: The show's sponsor sells garden lights.

PAVA: Please disclose that before we announce the discovery.

TAVI: The sponsor has asked us to call it an "atmospheric event."
 
Vex asks the producer to keep the sponsor line separate from the science report. Tavi opens two microphone channels and labels them FACT and PROMOTION. The labels are small enough that Pava cannot read them through the glass.

PAVA: Which channel is live?

TAVI: The one with the red light.

PAVA: Both lights are red.

VEX: One is reflected in the glass.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICK.]

The promotion channel opens. Tavi announces a sale on garden lamps before she realizes the caller is still on the line.

LEO: Is this still the meteor show?

PAVA: The lamp sale is not an astronomical event.

TAVI: We will put the sponsor message after the observation, not over it.

Vex confirms the shower's direction against the observatory chart. Pava reads the time into the log, then closes the promotion channel with a clear switch click.


VEX: It is a porch lamp.

Leo says a second green streak crossed the sky. Vex asks him to stay still and describe the direction. Leo points east. Pava checks the observatory feed; the actual shower is visible in the opposite direction.

[SFX: TELEPHONE STATIC, DISTANT WHOOSH.]

PAVA: I can see the meteors from the studio roof.

VEX: How many?

PAVA: Three bright streaks, then a faint one.

TAVI: The caller reports a fourth in his yard.

LEO: The lamp is still on.

VEX: Do not touch it. The owner can switch it off from inside.

PAVA: We can now separate the sky report from the yard report.

TAVI: The sponsor wants both under the same headline.

VEX: The headline can say, "Meteor shower above town; porch light below."

Leo's neighbor calls in to say the sensor is set too sensitive and turns on when a moth flies past. The moth circles the lamp and triggers it repeatedly.

[SFX: LIGHT CLICKS ON, OFF, ON.]

PAVA: The yard has its own meteor shower.

VEX: A moth shower.

LEO: I have stepped inside. The light has stopped.

TAVI: Our producer board is dark too.

PAVA: That is because you switched it off.

VEX: The night sky remains available without a sponsor.

Pava guides listeners to the real shower and asks them to report direction and time, not guesses about impact sites. Tavi removes the porch-light headline from the board.

[SFX: PHONE LINE DISCONNECTS, CRICKETS.]

LEO (PHONE, LAST WORD): The moth is still outside.

PAVA: It is not on the astronomy log.

VEX: Good night from Skywatch After Dark. Look up, check the direction, and let the porch light be a porch light.

[SFX: MIC CLICKS OFF, ONE METEOR WHOOSH.]
