# The Bird Count's Dramatic Pause

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: nature radio specials that extend a silence for suspense until the actual bird call happens off microphone*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Field host Yara, a gibbon; sound recordist Tekk, a macaque; ornithologist Vina, a capuchin; listener Oko, a lemur.

[SFX: DAWN MARSH. REEDS RUSTLE. A MICROPHONE OPENS.]

YARA: We are waiting for the rare silver reed warbler. The guide says it calls just before sunrise.

VINA: The sun is already above the eastern ridge.

TEKK: We have been recording the reeds for forty minutes. No warbler yet.

YARA: The program director wants a dramatic pause. We will stay silent until it calls.

[SFX: TWELVE SECONDS OF QUIET. REEDS MOVE. A DISTANT BOAT ENGINE PASSES.]

OKO: I can hear the water, not a bird.

YARA: The silence builds anticipation.

VINA: The bird might be calling behind us. We should turn the microphone toward the willow bank.

TEKK: The dish is aimed at the open marsh. A passing boat is masking the far side.

YARA: If we move, the pause is broken.

VINA: The bird does not know we are broadcasting.

[SFX: BOOTS SHIFT THROUGH DAMP GRASS. A MICROPHONE STAND TURNS.]

TEKK: The recorder is still running. The willow bank is clear.

[SFX: A SHORT, HIGH BIRD CALL SOUNDS BEHIND THEM, THEN A SECOND CALL.]

VINA: There. Silver reed warbler, two notes.

YARA: It called during our move.

TEKK: It is on the recording, but the director may cut it. They wanted the call after the long silence.

OKO: The call was real. Why cut it?

YARA: They want a reveal.

VINA: We can make the reveal honest. Say we waited, moved the microphone, and heard it from the bank.

TEKK: There is another call, farther away. I can isolate it when we get back.

[SFX: A BIRD CALLS TWICE. WATER RIPPLE. REEDS RUSTLE.]

YARA: The warbler is in the willow bank. It did not wait for the sunrise cue.

VINA: It is calling from cover. We should not step closer.

YARA: The guide notes say keep a distance and record from the path.

TEKK: I can lower the stand without approaching the willow.

[SFX: STAND CLAMPS RELEASE. MICROPHONE TILTS DOWN. A CALL COMES THROUGH CLEARER.]

OKO: I hear both notes now.

YARA: We should stop talking while it continues.

[SFX: FIVE SECONDS OF NATURAL MARSH SOUND. TWO BIRD CALLS.]

YARA: The director is asking whether we can record the silence again, this time without moving the microphone.

TEKK: We can record quiet reeds, but that won't be the warbler's call. The bird is still calling from the willow.

VINA: The field log should say the first microphone direction missed the bird. It shouldn't say we waited for it to appear.

OKO: I heard the call as soon as the dish turned. I would rather hear that than a long pause with suspense music.

YARA: I'll tell the director the take is usable as recorded. We won't stage another wait.

[SFX: RADIO EARPIECE CRACKLES. A PRODUCER'S VOICE ASKS FOR A "CLEANER REVEAL."]

YARA: There is no cleaner reveal. The bird called while we were adjusting the stand.

TEKK: The file has the change in direction and the calls together. I am marking that moment on the track.

TEKK: I have the call cleanly. The boat is gone.

YARA: That is our recording.

VINA: Don't label the earlier silence as the bird's absence. The microphone faced the wrong side.

YARA: The director wants a thirty-second suspense bed.

TEKK: We have thirty seconds of reeds, water, and a bird call. We can use the recording as it happened.

YARA: I'll say: "We heard no call from the open marsh. When we turned toward the willow bank, the warbler answered twice."

OKO: That's the story.

[SFX: RECORDER CLICKS OFF.]

YARA: It is time for the program sign-off. The sun is up, the bird is calling from the bank, and we are leaving the path clear.

VINA: Good. It can keep calling without an audience.

[SFX: THREE FOOTSTEPS BACK DOWN THE PATH. MARSH CONTINUES, WARBLER CALLS ONCE.]
