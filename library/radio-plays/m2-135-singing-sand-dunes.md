# The Singing Sand Dunes

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio field-recording expedition to capture desert wind tones, skeptical field notes, and a microphone that keeps recording the crew's breakfast*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Ula, an orangutan sound-documentary host; Seko, a macaque geologist; Marr, a gibbon recording engineer; and Dax, a capuchin field assistant.*

[SFX: Dry wind over dunes, recorder clicks on.]

ULA: We are at the singing dunes, where grains of sand can make a low tone when they slide down a steep slope. Marr, are we recording?

MARR: Yes. The microphones are placed beyond the ridge.

SEKO: Please walk softly. The sound depends on dry sand.

DAX: I brought the breakfast basket.

ULA: Keep it away from the recording equipment.

DAX: It is just a basket.

MARR: The microphone can hear the crumbs.

[SFX: A crumb crunches loudly.]

ULA: The dune is silent so far.

SEKO: The wind is too light. We need a small controlled slide.

MARR: I will release a handful at the crest.

[SFX: Sand slides softly; a faint low hum.]

ULA: There. A deep, resonant note.

DAX: It sounds like a large animal sleeping.

SEKO: It is the sand grains rubbing together.

MARR: The sound is clear, but a bird called over it.

[SFX: A bird gives one sharp cry.]

ULA: The bird has joined the dune.

SEKO: The bird is not part of the dune.

DAX: I can imitate the call for a second take.

MARR: Please don't. We need the natural sound.

DAX: I can be natural.

SEKO: You are holding a jam roll.

DAX: I can be naturally quiet.

[SFX: Basket lid closes.]

ULA: We will wait for the wind. Marr, check the recorder level.

MARR: The left channel is full. The right channel has a low rumble.

SEKO: Is the rumble the dune?

MARR: It appears between takes.

DAX: It might be the desert.

SEKO: The desert is not eating breakfast.

[SFX: A quiet chew through the right microphone.]

ULA: Dax, is that you?

DAX: I thought the basket was out of range.

MARR: Nothing is out of range. These are directional microphones, not mind readers.

ULA: We can keep the recording, but label the second channel "field breakfast."

SEKO: Wait. The wind is shifting. The upper slope is moving.

[SFX: A broad rush of sand, low singing tone swells.]

MARR: Recording now. Both channels are clean.

ULA: The dunes are singing in a low, steady chord.

SEKO: The slide is safe. The grains move in an even sheet.

DAX: The sound is beautiful.

MARR: It lasts twelve seconds. Hold still.

[SFX: Tone rises, then fades as sand settles.]

ULA: We have captured the song of the dune.

SEKO: And the natural bird call.

DAX: And breakfast.

MARR: Breakfast is on a separate track.

ULA: We will publish the wind tone and leave the chewing in the archive.

DAX: The archive can have the jam roll too.

SEKO: No food in the sound archive.

[SFX: Basket lifts; a crumb falls.]

MARR: I heard that.

ULA: This has been Singing Sands. The dune performed, the bird improvised, and the field assistant has been asked to eat off microphone.

MARR: I can replay the recording. The dune begins at a low note and settles without a click.

SEKO: The second slide has a scrape at the end. It is loose sand moving over dry grains.

ULA: We will keep both takes and label the conditions clearly.

DAX: What about the bird call?

SEKO: Keep it as a separate field note. It is a bird, not the dune.

MARR: And the chewing?

DAX: I will record that in the kitchen.

[SFX: Recorder clicks off; the basket lid stays closed.]


ULA: The clean take is ready for the radio and the archive label.

[SFX: Wind brushes the recorder, then a clean low dune hum fades.]
