# The Buoy That Sang Like a Whale

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: marine science broadcasts that attribute equipment alarms to wildlife before checking the sensor*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Host Iri, a lemur; scientist Dema, a macaque; technician Solu, a gibbon; caller Paku, a capuchin.

[SFX: UNDER-ICE WATER. A LOW, WAVERING TONE SOUNDS THROUGH HEADPHONES.]

IRI: Our research buoy has captured what sounds like a whale singing beneath the ice.

DEMA: It may be a hydrophone alarm. We have not checked the recording.

SOLU: The buoy battery is low. The alarm sounds when the voltage drops.

PAKU: I hear a long call and two short calls. Could it be a whale family?

IRI: The program title is "Songs Below the Ice."

DEMA: Don't name it until we know its source. The hydrophone is still attached to the buoy.

[SFX: LOW TONE, TWO SHORT TONES. A METAL RATTLE.]

SOLU: There is a loose battery bracket. It vibrates against the case.

IRI: That makes the long tone?

SOLU: The alarm is the tone. The vibration adds a rattle.

DEMA: The hydrophone is meant to record water. But if it touches the hull, it also records the hull.

[SFX: CABLE LIFTS OFF METAL. TONE CONTINUES.]

PAKU: The tone is still there.

SOLU: The battery is almost empty. The warning repeats on the same pattern.

PAKU: The sponsor's banner says "Whale Song at Midnight."

DEMA: That banner has to come down. It advertises a recording we did not make.

SOLA: I can fold it and leave the buoy notice visible. The maintenance marker shows the battery alert time.

IRI: We can tell the sponsor the title was wrong before it airs.

[SFX: PAPER BANNER ROLLS, TAPE PEELS OFF THE WALL.]

PAKU: The room sounds quieter without it.

DEMA: And the hydrophone is now recording water instead of a battery case.

IRI: The audience heard the sound. Could we keep it as a whale call and reveal the battery later?

DEMA: That would mislabel the science recording. We can play it as an alarm after we identify it.

SOLU: The case is sealed. I have logged the new battery, too.

IRI: The live intro should say "warning tone," not "song."

PAKU: I'll ask the sponsor to print a new banner after the next bulletin.

DEMA: We have a clear hydrophone track now. No one has to guess what the sound was.

SOLU: I can replace the battery from the service box. The buoy stays tethered, and the hydrophone stays in the water.

[SFX: CASE LATCH OPENS. A BATTERY SLIDES OUT; BRACKET CREAKS.]

DEMA: The bracket is bent. It lets the battery touch the sensor plate.

SOLU: I have a padded spacer. I'll fit it, then secure the new battery.

[SFX: FOAM SPACER INSERTS. BATTERY CLICKS INTO BRACKET. CASE CLOSES.]

IRI: The tone has stopped.

[SFX: SILENCE, THEN SOFT WATER MOVEMENT.]

PAKU: I can hear water and ice shifting now.

DEMA: That's the hydrophone. It is separated from the buoy hull.

IRI: Can we record the clear water sound?

DEMA: Yes. We will label the earlier tone as the low-voltage alarm and record this as ambient water.

[SFX: RECORD BUTTON CLICKS. ICE CRACKS FAR AWAY. WATER DRIPS.]

SOLU: Battery voltage is stable.

IRI: The buoy is recording again, and nothing is singing into the microphone.

PAKU: I thought it was a whale.

DEMA: A whale may be nearby, but we did not record one in that first clip.

IRI: The title needs to change.

SOLU: Call it "Buoy Alarm Under Ice." The ambient track gets its own file.

[SFX: LABEL PRINTER FEEDS TWO TAGS.]

IRI: That gives the listeners a real sound and a real source.

DEMA: We can keep listening. We just won't assign a voice to a warning tone.

[SFX: RADIO CLICKS OFF. CLEAR WATER AND DISTANT ICE MOVEMENT CONTINUE.]
