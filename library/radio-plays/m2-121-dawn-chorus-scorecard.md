# The Dawn Chorus Scorecard

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio bird-census broadcasts, hushed field reporting, and a capuchin caller whose mimicry keeps inflating the species count*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Tessa, a squirrel-monkey field host; Rook, a gibbon ornithologist; Fenn, a capuchin caller; and Mara, an orangutan sound engineer.*

[SFX: Dawn insects, wind in reeds, a bird gives three bright notes.]

TESSA: This is the Dawn Chorus Count, broadcasting from the reed edge. Rook, what have we heard so far?

ROOK: Two finches, one woodpecker, and the town bell. Please keep your voices low.

TESSA: Our audience can help. Call in with a sound, and Rook will identify it.

FENN: I heard a robin on my balcony.

ROOK: Can you imitate it?

FENN: [IMITATES BIRD: three chirps and a trill.]

ROOK: That is a decent imitation.

TESSA: We have counted one robin.

ROOK: We have counted no robin. We have counted a caller.

FENN: But it sounded like one.

ROOK: That is why we don't count by confidence.

[SFX: Bird answers from the reeds.]

TESSA: A robin has responded to Fenn.

ROOK: Or another bird has responded to the general dawn.

MARA: The microphone shows two peaks.

ROOK: Peaks are not species.

TESSA: The official count is still two finches and one woodpecker.

FENN: There is a fourth sound by my porch.

ROOK: Please describe it.

FENN: [IMITATES BIRD: a long, descending whistle.]

ROOK: That is a kettle.

FENN: My kettle whistles like a thrush.

ROOK: We do not count kitchen equipment.

TESSA: The audience may be disappointed.

ROOK: The audience can enjoy the kettle without adding it to the census.

[SFX: The town bell rings once.]

TESSA: Bell at six. Is it a bird?

ROOK: No.

FENN: It has a metal beak.

ROOK: It has a clapper.

MARA: Someone is walking toward us.

[SFX: Footsteps in dry grass.]

TESSA: Our sound engineer is approaching the mic. Mara, please whisper.

MARA: The cable is caught on a reed.

ROOK: Don't pull it. You'll disturb the nest.

TESSA: Can we count a bird if we hear it but cannot see it?

ROOK: Yes, if the call is distinct and repeated. One clear call, then another.

[SFX: Three notes from the reeds, followed by a shorter answering call.]

ROOK: That is a reed warbler.

TESSA: One reed warbler added. The total is four birds.

FENN: Five. I heard my balcony robin.

ROOK: You heard yourself imitating a robin.

FENN: I was standing by the balcony when I called.

ROOK: Location does not make the call wild.

TESSA: A second real robin has just called from the orchard.

[SFX: Distinct robin phrase.]

ROOK: Confirmed. Five birds.

MARA: The cable is free. No nest disturbed.

FENN: Could I imitate the bell to get six?

ROOK: You could, but we would not count it.

[SFX: Fenn imitates the bell. A real bell answers from town.]

TESSA: Rook, how do you distinguish them?

ROOK: The bell has a metal ring. Fenn's imitation has a breath at the end.

FENN: I can remove the breath.

ROOK: Then we still will not count it.

MARA: The real bell is outside the field recording, and the cable is back in place.

MARA: The station's counter has logged the real bell as three extra birds because it heard the repeated ring.

ROOK: A bell is not a bird call. We need to separate the field audio from the town clock.

TESSA: I can mark the clock on the time track and leave the bird channel clear.

FENN: My imitation is in the call-in channel.

ROOK: Label it "caller demonstration," not "wildlife."

MARA: The archive now has three tracks: birds, bell, and Fenn.

FENN: I have my own channel?

TESSA: Only for this census.

TESSA: Final count: two finches, one woodpecker, one reed warbler, and one robin. The kettle and the bell stay off the bird list.

[SFX: Morning birds rise as the broadcast fades.]
