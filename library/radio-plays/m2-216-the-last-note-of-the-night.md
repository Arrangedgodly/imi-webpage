# The Last Note of the Night

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio music request program convention, where a station must choose between a caller's requested song and the actual sounds outside the studio*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: DJ TAVI, gibbon; caller MIRI, macaque; sound engineer ODO, baboon; station intern PIRI, capuchin.

[SFX: RADIO THEME, WIND AGAINST WINDOW.] 

TAVI: This is The Last Note of the Night. Miri, what would you like to hear?

MIRI: The cricket outside my window. It keeps me company.

ODO: We do not have a recording of that cricket.

PIRI: We can record it now.

Odo opens the studio window. Wind rushes through and scatters the request cards. Tavi asks Piri to close the window, but the cricket stops when they move. Miri says it calls only when the room is quiet.

The crew turns off the lights and waits. A cricket chirps once. Odo raises the microphone. The studio clock ticks loudly. The cricket pauses.

TAVI: It has heard the clock.

MIRI: It is deciding whether to perform.

Piri moves the clock away. Odo holds the microphone outside the window, but a branch taps it. The recording captures three knocks and no cricket. Tavi asks if they can use the knocks as a percussion track. Miri says the cricket does not sound like a branch.

Piri listens to the test recording through headphones. One chirp is almost hidden by the station's ventilation fan. Odo lowers the fan setting, but a monitor begins to warm. Tavi asks if the room can stay comfortable without masking the caller's request.

Miri suggests opening the window only a finger-width. The wind noise drops. The cricket continues calling outside. Piri adjusts the microphone so it faces the garden instead of the street. The second cricket answers from farther away, no longer covered by the curtains.

MIRI: It is not one note. It is a conversation.

TAVI: We should not add words to it.

The crew agrees the test is not yet a usable recording. Odo moves the station clock away from the sill; its ticking had been louder than either call. Piri marks the fan and window positions so the next recording has a repeatable setup. Tavi asks Miri whether she wants the full recording or a short version. She chooses the full version. They will make it after the room is quiet.

PIRI: The fan is announcing itself louder than the cricket.

TAVI: No station identification until after the note. The machine can wait.



They place a small microphone on the windowsill and step away. The cricket chirps. Odo captures a short, clean pattern. Piri asks if the station can play it back. The recording has the sound, but also a faint rustle from the curtains.

[SFX: PLAYBACK: CHIRP-CHIRP, SOFT RUSTLE.] 

Miri recognizes the rhythm. She says the second chirp is lower and the third is longer. Tavi asks if that is a song. Miri says it is just the cricket calling.

The program's music library is ready with a dramatic orchestral piece. Odo suggests layering the cricket over it. Miri says the music would drown out the sound she requested. Tavi lets the recording play alone. The listeners hear the cricket and, underneath, the quiet room.

PIRI: Should we add a station identification?

TAVI: After the note.

They wait through a pause. A second cricket answers from farther away. Miri laughs. Odo records the answer too, then checks the levels. The two calls do not match exactly, but they overlap for a moment.

Tavi asks which one should close the program. Miri says neither; let them finish. The crickets call again, farther apart. The crew lowers the microphone and does not interrupt.

[SFX: TWO CRICKETS, DISTANT AND NEAR. FADE.] 

TAVI: That was Miri's request. No arrangement needed.

The station theme begins softly, then stops before covering the last chirp.
