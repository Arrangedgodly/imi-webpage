# The Moth Count at Porchlight Nine

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio night-insect surveys, cautious species identification, and a household porchlight mistaken for the moon by every moth in the valley*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Rina, a macaque field host; Jell, an orangutan entomologist; Puck, a gibbon caller; and Koi, a capuchin resident.*

[SFX: Night insects, a porchlight hums, recorder starts.]

RINA: We are at Porchlight Nine for the annual moth count. Jell, how do we identify the visitors?

JELL: By wing markings and antennae. We count each species once, not every moth that circles the lamp.

PUCK: I have counted twelve.

JELL: Twelve visits or twelve moths?

PUCK: Twelve circles.

JELL: It may be one moth making twelve laps.

KOI: The light is bright. They keep coming back.

RINA: Can we dim it?

KOI: The porchlight is on a timer. It should go out at ten.

PUCK: The moths are gathering by the shade.

JELL: That is a sheet, not a shade.

[SFX: A moth taps the microphone.]

RINA: One insect just landed beside the recorder.

JELL: It is a moth, but we need the wing pattern before assigning a name.

PUCK: It has spots.

JELL: Nearly every moth has a spot somewhere.

RINA: The porchlight count board says "white, brown, and other."

JELL: That is a color tally, not a species count.

KOI: I made the board before I knew there were so many.

RINA: We can sort them by pattern tonight and check the reference guide later.

[SFX: Paper page turns.]

JELL: This one has a banded wing. We have two banded moths, one small and one large.

PUCK: The small one has circled the lamp seven times.

JELL: Same moth. We can recognize the notch on its wing.

RINA: The count is four distinct moths so far.

KOI: The light is fading.

[SFX: Timer clicks; porchlight goes out.]

PUCK: The moths have disappeared.

JELL: They are still here. They have stopped circling the lamp.

RINA: I can hear them moving in the garden.

KOI: The porch is quiet now.

JELL: We can use a red-filtered light to inspect without drawing more insects.

[SFX: A soft red lamp turns on.]

RINA: I see a pale moth on the wall. It has a crescent mark.

JELL: That is a separate species. Add one.

PUCK: The count is five.

KOI: The light came back on.

[SFX: Porchlight hum resumes; moths flutter toward it.]

RINA: The timer has reset.

JELL: Turn it off. We have enough observations.

KOI: The switch is behind the panel.

PUCK: I'll find it.

[SFX: A careful click; light goes out.]

RINA: The moths settle. We have five distinct species, not twelve circles. The white-brown-other board needs replacing.

JELL: We will put the wing-pattern chart by the light switch and keep the lamp off during counts.

KOI: The moths can visit without making a lap around the porch.

PUCK: I have stopped counting circles.

RINA: This is the Moth Count at Porchlight Nine. The lamp is dark, five species are recorded, and no visitor has been named by color alone.

JELL: The station's tally program has recorded 19 moths because it treats every orbit as a new arrival.

RINA: We should not use the automatic counter for insects.

PUCK: It gives a very high total.

JELL: It counts the same individual on every pass. We will mark the wing notch and count each once.

[SFX: Recorder chirps softly.]

RINA: The auto-counter has also labeled the porchlight as a moon.

KOI: It is just a bulb on my porch.

JELL: The moths don't know that. They respond to the light, not its name.

RINA: I will remove the automatic tally and keep the field notes.

PUCK: Then five distinct species, recorded by visible markings.

JELL: Correct. The count is lower, but it means something.

[SFX: Recorder clicks off; night insects continue.]
