# The Museum of Missing Sounds

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio archive program seeking overlooked ambient recordings*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: ARCHIVIST ILU, a slow loris; HOST BEX, a macaque; TECHNICIAN ORI, a capuchin; and a flock of pigeons.

[SFX: STUDIO ROOM TONE, FILE CABINET SLIDES OPEN.] 

BEX: This is Sounds We Keep, the program that preserves noises before they disappear. Today we have come to the municipal archive to record the sound of an empty hallway.

ILU: The hallway is not empty. We are in it.

BEX: We can stand very still.

ORI: There are pigeons on the roof.

BEX: Can we record them later? The episode is about absence.

ILU: The archive has a file labeled QUIET HALLWAY, but the recording contains a cart squeak, two footsteps, and someone saying, "Sorry."

BEX: That's a complete scene.

ILU: It was a hallway during office hours.

ORI: What about this file: RAIN WITHOUT THUNDER?

ILU: It has thunder at the beginning.

ORI: Perhaps the thunder arrived early.

BEX: We need an example of a sound that is missing.

ILU: There is a recording of the old bell tower before the bell was installed.

BEX: What does it sound like?

ILU: Wind, birds, and someone climbing the stairs to check whether the bell is there.

[SFX: ARCHIVE TAPE. FOOTSTEPS CLIMB. A PAUSE. A VOICE SAYS, "NOT YET."] 

BEX: That's wonderful. The absence has a person in it.

ORI: Most absences do.

BEX: Can we record the hallway now?

ILU: We can. But the archive's microphone is on the other side of the door.

[THEY OPEN THE DOOR. HALLWAY HUM, DISTANT WING FLUTTER.] 

ORI: The pigeons followed us.

BEX: The hallway is not empty again.

ILU: The pigeons are waiting near the window. They might be the sound we came to find.

BEX: What sound?

[PAUSE. ONE PIGEON COOS. ANOTHER ANSWERS.] 

ORI: There are two of them.

BEX: We have an unplanned conversation.

ILU: The first bird called. The second answered. Then they stopped.

BEX: Is that the archive's quiet hallway?

ILU: It is the hallway as it was this morning.

ORI: If we record it, the file will contain the tape machine, the pigeons, and us trying not to breathe.

BEX: That sounds honest.

ORI: We should capture the archive's own room tone.

BEX: The room is quiet right now.

ILU: The file cabinet makes a small click when the air shifts.

[SFX: METAL CABINET CLICKS ONCE.]

BEX: That click sounds like a punctuation mark.

ORI: There is also a clock.

BEX: The clock is not missing.

ILU: The clock is missing its minute hand.

BEX: Does it still tell time?

ILU: It tells part of time.

ORI: That is not enough for the archive label.

ILU: It keeps the hour.

BEX: We will record it.

[SFX: CLOCK TICKS. A PIGEON WINGS ON THE ROOF.]

ORI: The clock tick and the wing sound overlap.

BEX: Which one should we name first?

ILU: Name the clock and list the pigeon as present.

ORI: And the person holding the recorder?

ILU: You can list the recorder. You do not have to list everyone who can hear it.

BEX: The archive is starting to sound like a guest list.

ILU: It is a record of what was there. That includes more than the title suggests.

[SFX: RECORDER CLICKS ON. FOUR SECONDS OF HALLWAY, PIGEONS, AND BREATHING.] 

ILU: Label it with the date and the time. Do not call it empty.

BEX: What should we call it?

ILU: A hallway, with company.

BEX: And if the sound changes tomorrow?

ILU: Make a new recording.

ORI: The archive will need another shelf.

ILU: The archive has shelves. The hallway has room.

BEX: And a door between them.

ILU: For now, the door is closed. We will listen again tomorrow.

[THEY WALK OUT. THE DOOR SHUTS. ONE PIGEON COOS ON THE OTHER SIDE.] 

BEX: Should we record that final sound?

ILU: We already did.
