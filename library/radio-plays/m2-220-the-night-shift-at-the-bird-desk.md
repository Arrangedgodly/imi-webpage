# The Night Shift at the Bird Desk

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio workplace comedy convention, where a station's overnight bird-call hotline receives requests from actual birds*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Producer NARA, gibbon; host PIRI, capuchin; caller ODO, baboon; intern TAVI, macaque; listener LEO, a langur; bird caller, SFX.

[SFX: RADIO THEME, THEN A SHARP CHIRP.] 

PIRI: This is the Night Shift at the Bird Desk. We take calls from listeners who hear birds after dark.

NARA: The first call is already on the line.

[SFX: THREE CHIRPS, A WING FLUTTER.] 

TAVI: That caller does not have a phone.

Odo enters carrying a small birdhouse. He says a bird is nesting beside the station window. Piri asks if the bird wants a request played. Odo says it wants the window open a little wider.

NARA: We cannot take requests from outside the studio.

TAVI: It is outside the studio.

Piri opens the window. A second bird answers from the tree. Nara says they have a duet. The birds call again, but the ventilation fan drowns them out. Odo turns the fan down. The room grows warm.

PIRI: We have an acoustic improvement.

NARA: We have a temperature problem.

Tavi places a small screen in the window to keep insects out. It blocks one half of the opening. The bird calls continue, and the station can hear them clearly. Odo says the birdhouse is too close to the speaker. They move it to a safe branch farther from the window.

[SFX: WOODEN BIRDHOUSE SET DOWN, TWO CHIRPS.] 

A caller rings the phone. It is listener Leo, who says the birds have been singing for an hour. Piri asks what they want to hear. The listener asks for a quiet moment so they can listen to the birds. Nara begins to play music, then stops.

PIRI: Our caller requested no song.

NARA: That is a difficult request for a radio station.

Nara asks the listener if they can hold the line while the crew changes the studio settings. The listener agrees. Nara asks Odo to turn the fan back up one notch at a time. At the first setting, the chirps remain clear. At the second, the fan covers the distant bird. They keep the first setting and open the window only a finger-width.

Tavi asks whether the birds are too close to the speaker. Piri moves the speaker away from the window. A low music note had been making the nearer bird call louder. With the speaker off, the bird returns to its softer rhythm.

NARA: We have confused a request with a performance.

PIRI: The station wanted a song.

TAVI: The caller wanted to hear a bird.

Odo checks the birdhouse branch from outside. It is secure and not touching the window frame. He adds a small sign asking staff to keep the sill clear. Piri records one minute of the call with no theme, narration or studio clock. The listener is still on the line when it ends.

LEO: That was what I wanted.

Nara asks if they should play it again. The listener says once is enough. Piri labels the recording with the date and the setting, then moves it to the archive rather than the music library.



The crew lowers the theme volume. The birds call. A second voice joins. The listener stays on the line, silent. Piri thanks them for calling. Nara asks whether the birdhouse is ready. Odo checks the branch. It is steady, and the entrance remains clear.

Tavi reads a message from a listener: "Please do not feed the birds through the window." Odo adds it to the station note. Piri says the hotline is now closed to bird requests.

[SFX: ONE CHIRP.] 

NARA: The birds have not received the memo.

Piri turns off the studio microphone, leaving the bird calls audible for a moment. Then the theme returns softly.
