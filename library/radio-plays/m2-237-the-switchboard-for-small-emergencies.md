# The Switchboard for Small Emergencies

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: 24-hour crisis hotline broadcast convention, where minor household problems receive an unnecessarily dramatic dispatch*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-008*

Cast: Dispatcher PIRI, capuchin; operator TAVI, macaque; caller ODO, baboon; field helper LUMA, gibbon; callers, several primates by phone.

[SFX: PHONE BANK, ALERT TONE.] 

PIRI: Switchboard for small emergencies. What is your situation?

ODO: A jar will not open.

PIRI: Is anyone in immediate danger?

ODO: The lid is tight.

PIRI: We are dispatching a helper.

TAVI: We have a jar opener in the drawer.

PIRI: Keep the line open. We need details.

ODO: The jar contains berries.

PIRI: How many?

ODO: A bowlful.

PIRI: The quantity is important.

Luma takes the call. She tells Odo to dry the lid and turn it with a cloth. The lid opens.

[SFX: JAR POPS.] 

ODO: It opened.

PIRI: Stand by. We need to log the outcome.

ODO: I am eating berries.

PIRI: We will call back after the situation is stable.

Tavi says there are three more callers waiting: a sock trapped in a chair, a spoon stuck in a bowl, and a neighbor whose hat is inside out. Piri ranks them by urgency.

PIRI: The sock may be torn. Send Luma.

TAVI: The spoon is already free.

PIRI: It could become stuck again.

LUMA: I am at the sock. It is not trapped. Odo's foot is inside it.

ODO: That is where I keep my foot.

PIRI: We have discovered a false report.

LUMA: No, we have discovered a sock.

The next caller says a hat is inside out.

PIRI: Is the wearer in a cold place?

CALLER: No.

PIRI: Is the hat keeping the sun out?

CALLER: Yes.

PIRI: Then it may be providing useful shade.

LUMA: The hat is also covering the wearer's eyes.

CALLER: Only when I look up.

PIRI: Then keep your eyes forward.

Luma asks the caller to turn the hat right-side out. The caller does so, but the hat's tag scratches their forehead. Luma suggests removing the tag; the caller removes it with a small snip.

[SFX: SCISSORS, RELIEF.]

PIRI: We have a secondary issue.

TAVI: The tag is in the wastebasket.

PIRI: Mark the case resolved. Piri asks whether the wearer can still see.

CALLER: Yes.

PIRI: Can they still hear?

CALLER: Yes.

PIRI: Can they turn the hat around?

CALLER: Yes.

PIRI: Then remain calm while they do so.

Tavi says the switchboard's emergency light is blinking. Piri orders a technician. Luma checks the panel; the button is stuck under a clipboard.

[SFX: BUTTON CLICKS OFF.] 

TAVI: The alert is canceled.

PIRI: Do not cancel an alert until we know why it began.

LUMA: A clipboard pressed the button.

PIRI: Was anyone under the clipboard?

TAVI: Only the schedule.

Piri asks listeners to send in their small emergencies so the team can prioritize. Calls flood in: a cup is empty, a ribbon is knotted, and someone cannot remember where they put a banana.

LUMA: I found the banana in the caller's hand.

PIRI: How long have they been holding it?

LUMA: Since they called.

PIRI: Then the emergency is resolved.

TAVI: The cup caller has filled the cup.

PIRI: Good. The ribbon?

LUMA: Untied.

PIRI: The switchboard is clear.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS AGAIN.] 

PIRI: We have another call.

ODO: I have finished the berries. The jar is empty.

PIRI: Is the lid still open?

ODO: Yes.

PIRI: Then we are standing down.

TAVI: We should change the sign to "Small inconveniences."

PIRI: No. That sounds like we will not dispatch.

LUMA: What about "We can probably help"?

PIRI: Put it on the sign.

[SFX: ALERT TONE BECOMES A GENTLE CHIME.] 

PIRI: Switchboard for small emergencies. We can probably help.

ODO: The jar is closed again.

PIRI: Luma, redeploy.
