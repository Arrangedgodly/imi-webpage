# The Last Page at Closing Time

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: literary discussion programs that mistake every unanswered question for profound ambiguity*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-011*

Cast: Host Omi, a gibbon; critic Pava, a macaque; librarian Kito, a capuchin; caller Seli, a lemur.

[SFX: BOOK PAGES, STUDIO BELL.]

OMI: Tonight we discuss a novel whose final page is missing.

PAVA: Or whose final page has chosen not to appear.

KITO: The binding came loose. I have the page at the desk.

ACT ONE

Seli calls to ask whether the author intended the book to end mid-sentence. Omi says the missing page creates interpretive space. Kito says the sentence ends with "She opened the door and saw..." and the next page is on his desk.

PAVA: The dash is a threshold.

KITO: The next page is a paragraph.

Omi asks Kito to read it. Kito refuses until the library's repair tape arrives. Pava says the delay allows listeners to imagine what comes after the dash. Seli imagines a door, since the sentence already says that.

ACT TWO

The tape arrives in a box labeled FRAGILE. Omi asks if they can repair the page on air. Kito explains that the page is loose, not torn, and should be reattached along the binding. Pava objects that repair will collapse the mystery.

KITO: It will restore the book.

PAVA: It will reduce the range of possible meanings.

Seli says she has the same book at home, with all the pages. Omi asks her not to spoil the ending. Seli says the character sees her sister in the doorway. Pava declares that the sister may be a metaphor.

KITO: She is also a character.

Pava insists that the show should preserve uncertainty until the repair is complete. Kito applies a narrow strip of tape. The last page flutters loose again because Omi keeps turning the pages for dramatic sound.

[SFX: PAGES FLUTTER.]

OMI: The book resists closure.

KITO: The repair is not dry.

Omi asks listeners to hold their breath while the adhesive sets. Seli asks whether she should keep holding. Kito says no; it is safe to breathe.

Before Omi reads the repaired page, Pava asks to hear the original sentence again. Kito reads it with the dash. Pava says the dash now sounds less like a cliff and more like a breath. Seli says the character may have paused because she recognized her sister. Omi wants to add a dramatic music sting before the answer. Kito asks whether the kettle needs one. Omi plays the sting anyway; it swells just as Kito says "borrowed kettle." The effect makes the household object sound like a royal return. Pava admits the repair did not remove ambiguity; it only restored the words. Kito says the library can now lend the book again. Seli asks if the next listener can borrow it. Kito checks the sign-out card and says yes, after the program ends.

ACT THREE

The page is secured. Kito reads the missing paragraph. The character opens the door and sees her sister, who has returned with the borrowed kettle. The novel ends with both of them laughing about the kettle's dent.

Pava says the ending is more domestic than expected. Omi asks what the dent symbolizes. Kito says it is a dent. Seli says that is what she thought the dash concealed.

PAVA: The missing page made us expect a revelation.

KITO: The page revealed a kettle.

Omi thanks listeners for joining a discussion that began with a repair and ended with a household object. Pava says the kettle could still represent memory. Kito says it can represent boiling water.

KITO: It cannot.
