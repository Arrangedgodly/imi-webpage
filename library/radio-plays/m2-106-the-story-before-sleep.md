# The Story Before Sleep

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: late-night bedtime-story radio, listener-supplied plot twists, and a narrator struggling to keep a fable restful*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Luma, a slow loris host; Pogo, a young macaque caller; Reni, a gibbon sound operator; and Vale, a baboon producer.*

[SFX: Soft chimes, crickets, a page turning very slowly.]

LUMA: Welcome to Moon Blanket, the bedtime story hour where every adventure ends with a quiet landing. Tonight's tale is about a small moth who must cross a garden before dawn.

VALE: Keep it soothing. No peril, no loud sounds, no cliffhanger.

RENI: The moth's wings are ready.

LUMA: Once, under a silver moon, a moth named Mallow set out to find the flower that smelled like warm tea.

POGO: Why didn't Mallow take a map?

LUMA: Because this is a story, Pogo, and the flower is nearby.

POGO: Then why not take a nap and find it tomorrow?

VALE: That is a reasonable question, but it cancels the hour.

LUMA: Mallow followed the moonlight to a row of tulips.

[SFX: A gentle flutter.]

POGO: Why are the tulips awake?

LUMA: They're listening.

POGO: To what?

LUMA: To this story.

VALE: Don't let the flowers ask questions. It encourages them.

[SFX: Two faint flower whispers: "What time is it?"]

RENI: They are whispering anyway.

LUMA: Mallow asked the tulips for directions. They pointed east.

POGO: Flowers don't point. They have no fingers.

LUMA: They tilted toward the moon.

POGO: The moon isn't east.

VALE: The map is not the point. The point is sleep.

LUMA: Mallow thanked them and flew on.

[SFX: Wings, slow and rhythmic.]

POGO: Is there a cat?

LUMA: No cat.

POGO: A fox?

LUMA: No fox.

POGO: A tiny storm?

LUMA: The weather is calm.

POGO: How does Mallow grow as a character?

VALE: He is a moth. Let him be small.

RENI: I have a soft sound cue for the flower.

[SFX: A kettle whistles quietly, then stops.]

LUMA: Mallow smelled warm tea. He had found the flower.

POGO: But he didn't cross anything difficult.

LUMA: He crossed a garden.

POGO: Was there a fence?

LUMA: A low fence.

POGO: Did he jump it?

LUMA: He flew over.

VALE: We're drifting toward an action sequence.

LUMA: Mallow landed on a petal, folded his wings, and watched the first pale stripe of dawn. The flower leaned close and asked him to stay.

POGO: If he stays, he misses breakfast.

LUMA: He can have breakfast here.

POGO: Does the flower serve tea?

RENI: The flower smells like tea. That is all it does.

VALE: The hour ends in two minutes. Give us a gentle resolution.

LUMA: Mallow thanked the flower, then tucked beneath a leaf to rest. The garden settled around him.

[SFX: Crickets slow. Soft breeze.]

POGO: Does the flower close at night?

LUMA: Yes. It closed its petals gently.

POGO: What if Mallow is inside?

LUMA: Then he has a quiet room.

RENI: I can close the flower with two soft taps.

[SFX: Two taps; a third, much louder tap.]

VALE: Who made the third tap?

RENI: A tulip.

LUMA: The tulip is requesting a speaking part.

POGO: Give it one line.

VALE: We do not add a new character after the ending.

LUMA: The tulip said, "Good night," and returned to being a flower.

VALE: That is one line. Fine.

POGO: Can I have a line too?

LUMA: Good night, Pogo.

POGO: Good night, moth. Don't forget breakfast.

[SFX: Gentle wingbeat, then stillness.]

LUMA: Mallow slept beneath the leaf until the garden brightened. The flower saved him one warm drop of dew, which he drank before flying home.

VALE: Complete story, no chase, no cliffhanger, and one rebellious tulip.

RENI: The chimes are fading.

[SFX: Chimes ring once, then crickets settle into silence.]

LUMA: This has been Moon Blanket. May your questions wait until morning.

[SFX: Page turns; broadcast ends on a sleepy breath.]
