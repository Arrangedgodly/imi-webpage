# The Countdown That Started at Two

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio hit countdowns, suspenseful rankings, breathless number reveals, and a host who forgets the list order*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
HOST BEA, a macaque
PRODUCER LUX, a capuchin
CALLER NERI, a ring-tailed lemur

[POP COUNTDOWN THEME. CROWD CHEERS.]

BEA: Welcome to the Kettle City Top Five, where the week's most requested sounds compete for the honor of being played after four other sounds. Let's begin at number two, the station door chime!

[CHIME.]

LUX: We start at five. You have skipped most of the show.

BEA: A powerful opening. Remind me of our list.

LUX: Number five, the kettle whistle. Four, the bus brakes. Three, a spoon dropped in a mug. Two, the station door chime. One, the dawn chorus.

BEA: Excellent. At number five: the kettle whistle!

[KETTLE WHISTLES.]

BEA: A strong opening. Number four: the station door chime.

LUX: That's number two.

BEA: We're building suspense by skipping ahead.

LUX: We should play the bus brakes at number four.

[BUS BRAKES HISS.]

BEA: The bus has arrived at number four. At number three, the spoon in the mug.

[SPOON DROPS.]

BEA: Listeners love a metallic sound with a little room reverb.

LUX: It has 180 votes, more than the brakes.

BEA: The poll measures requests, not quality.

[PHONE LINE OPENS.]

BEA: We have Neri on the line. Which sound did you vote for?

NERI: The dawn chorus. It woke me up at five this morning.

BEA: You can hear it again at number one.

NERI: Can you play it now? I'm trying to remember what it sounds like.

BEA: We have to save it for the top slot.

LUX: The dawn chorus is live outside the studio.

[SOFT BIRDS SING.]

BEA: The birds are attempting to spoil the countdown.

NERI: They sound lovely.

BEA: The official recording has studio clarity. Lux, cue number two: the station door chime.

[CHIME.]

LUX: A listener has entered the studio.

NERI: I'm at the door now. I came to ask when the show ends.

BEA: We have number one left. Please come in after we play it.

NERI: I'm standing in the doorway.

[CHIME AGAIN.]

BEA: The door chime has re-entered the chart. This is unusual.

LUX: The second ring is a new request from someone on the other side.

BEA: The list has changed. Number one is now the door chime.

LUX: The dawn chorus has 300 votes. The chime has 12.

BEA: The chime is our station sound. It has home-court advantage.

NERI: You can just play the birds now. They're still singing.

BEA: We are a countdown show. We must reveal the top sound in dramatic order.

LUX: You started at five, skipped to two, returned to four, and put the chime at one.

BEA: The list is a journey, not a staircase.

[THE LIVE BIRDS TRAIL OFF AS THE FLOCK LEAVES.]

NERI: The birds have stopped. They're flying away.

LUX: We can still play the recording. The microphone caught the first chorus before the market opened.

BEA: Can the winner accept its award by telephone?

LUX: The birds do not have telephones.

NERI: Put the award outside. They might collect it tomorrow.

BEA: The prize is a commemorative mug with our station name. They'll need to pose beside it.

LUX: The door chime cannot pose either.

BEA: It can stay still for a photograph. It already has a home at the station.

NERI: That is not how voting works. The mug can be their birdbath.

BEA: Do we have a sponsorship category for birdbaths?

LUX: We have a category called number one. It belongs to the dawn chorus. Cueing the recording now.

BEA: Then the winner is number one, the dawn chorus, with three hundred requests. Please enjoy it after the chime.

[CHIME. BIRDS PLAY.]

NERI: The birds are back.

BEA: We have our winner and our caller in the studio. The list is complete.

LUX: Does that mean next week we start with number five?

BEA: We'll start wherever the listeners are.

[THEME.]

NERI: Start with the birds.

BEA: The birds have requested the first slot.
