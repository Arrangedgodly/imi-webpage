# The Planetarium's Customer-Support Moon

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: planetarium programs that turn a live science question into scripted brand messaging and insist on a tidy cosmic narrative*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-014*

Cast: Presenter Kima, a macaque; astronomer Poro, a langur; producer Vesh, a gibbon; caller Lala, an orangutan.

[SFX: PLANETARIUM PROJECTOR HUMS; AUDIENCE SHUFFLES.] 

KIMA: Tonight we follow the moon on its smooth path across the sky.

LALA: My telescope showed a dark patch on the moon. Is that a new crater?

PORO: It may be a shadow. The moon rotates, and the light angle changes how old craters appear.

VESH: Our program card says the moon is a dependable companion. Keep the story reassuring.

LALA: I am not worried. I want to know why the patch moved.

KIMA: The moon's surface did not move. The shadow shifted as the sun angle changed.

[SFX: PROJECTOR CLICKS; STARS FADE, MOON BRIGHTENS.] 

PORO: Here is a time-lapse. You can see the terminator cross the crater rim.

VESH: The sponsor wants the bright side centered. It looks friendlier.

PORO: The bright side depends on the date and time. We should show the actual phase.

KIMA: The display is showing last month's full moon.

VESH: That is why it looks so welcoming.

LALA: My question was about tonight. The moon is a crescent now.

[SFX: KEYBOARD TAPS; PROJECTOR WHIRS.] 

PORO: I have today's date. The crescent is low in the west. The dark patch is the shadow beyond the crater rim.

KIMA: Can we zoom in?

PORO: Yes, but we should label the image as an illustration. We cannot resolve that small patch with the public telescope.

VESH: The program promised a close-up reveal.

LALA: I would rather see the real wide view than a fake crater.

KIMA: The audience is asking for the zoom anyway.

[SFX: PROJECTOR ZOOMS; IMAGE PIXELATES.] 

PORO: That is the display enlarging the pixels. It adds no detail.

VESH: It looks like a mysterious crater.

PORO: It is a block of gray pixels. I will return to the telescope image.

KIMA: Our companion moon has become a square.

LALA: The square is from the projector. The moon is still a crescent.

[SFX: ZOOM CLICKS BACK; SOFT AUDIENCE LAUGHTER.] 

VESH: The program has a quiz: is the moon following us or are we following it?

PORO: Neither. The apparent motion comes from our position and the moon's orbit.

KIMA: That answer does not fit the two choices.

LALA: Then add a third choice: the projector is zooming badly.

VESH: I will change the quiz card. We cannot make a wrong answer the only fun answer.

VESH: The ticket printer has already issued a star for the audience quiz.

KIMA: A star for what answer?

VESH: Everyone picked the square moon because it looked most dramatic.

PORO: Then the quiz rewarded the blurry image. We should cancel that score and show the source view.

[SFX: PRINTER WHINES; PAPER TEARS.]

LALA: The crescent looks better than the square. It also makes sense.

PORO: The crater shadow is clearer when we use the correct phase and keep the image at its actual scale.

[SFX: DATE SELECTOR CLICKS; MOON IMAGE SHARPENS.] 

KIMA: Here is the crescent as seen tonight. The dark patch is a shadow, not a fresh crater.

LALA: That answers it. Thank you.

VESH: The sponsor card says "Moonlight: always on your side."

PORO: The moon is not choosing a side. The sunlight is striking it from one direction.

KIMA: We can say "observe the phase" instead.

[SFX: PROJECTOR TRACKS; STARS RETURN.] 

LALA: I can see the crescent through the west window now.

PORO: The telescope is ready by the door. We will use the real sky, and keep the zoom modest.

VESH: The customer-support moon has no complaint form.

KIMA: It has craters. We have the questions.

[SFX: AUDIENCE WALKS TO TELESCOPE; PROJECTOR HUM FADES.]
