# The Lighthouse's Unreliable Countdown

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: maritime broadcasts that treat a clock fault as a dramatic countdown instead of a navigation hazard*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-012*

Cast: Keeper Yori, a macaque; announcer Tala, a gibbon; mechanic Enzo, a capuchin; skipper Venn, a lemur.

[SFX: SURF BELOW A LIGHTHOUSE. CLOCK TICKS QUICKLY, THEN A LAMP MOTOR TURNS. RADIO HISS.]

TALA: Harbor service bulletin. The east light flashes every ten seconds. Vessels should use it to identify the safe channel.

YORI: It flashed twice in six seconds.

ENZO: The timer is gaining time. The lens motor is fine; the clock escapement is skipping teeth.

TALA: We have already aired the ten-second interval.

VENN: I'm in the channel. I saw three flashes before your second announcement.

YORI: Keep outside the shoal marker until we confirm the interval.

VENN: I can hold position, but the radio operator is asking whether the light has reached its final flash.

TALA: Tell them there is no final flash. We are not counting down to anything.

[SFX: A DISTANT RADIO VOICE ASKS FOR THE COUNT. YORI CLICKS THE TRANSMITTER OFF.]

TALA: Should I call it an urgent countdown?

YORI: No. It is a timing fault. Say not to use the light for interval until we test it.

[SFX: RADIO CLICKS. A BELL RINGS ONCE BELOW.]

TALA: Harbor bulletin: east light interval is unreliable. Keep clear of the shoal and use the west beacon.

VENN: Copy. The west beacon is visible.

ENZO: I'll open the clock housing. Don't stop the lamp motor; its rotation is correct.

[SFX: METAL LATCHES OPEN. TICKING LOUDER. A SCREWDRIVER TURNS.]

YORI: The service log says the clock was calibrated at noon.

ENZO: It was. Someone wound it past the stop mark. The spring is skipping teeth.

TALA: Could that make a suspenseful pattern?

YORI: It could make a ship think the channel is clear when it isn't.

TALA: Understood. No suspense music.

[SFX: RADIO PRODUCER FADES OUT A DRUM ROLL THAT HAD BEGUN QUIETLY.]

ENZO: I need the winding key.

YORI: It hangs on the hook beside the chart cabinet.

[SFX: KEY LIFTS, CLINKS. A CABINET OPENS; PAPER CHARTS SHIFT.]

VENN: Your signal is breaking up. I can hear the clock through the radio.

TALA: That is the tower microphone. I'll move it away from the housing.

[SFX: MIC STAND SCRAPES ACROSS WOOD. RADIO HISS CLEARS.]

YORI: The light is still turning. Enzo, can you release the spring slowly?

ENZO: Yes. I'm easing the tension, not winding it further.

[SFX: KEY TURNS A QUARTER TURN. TICKING SLOWS. A METAL CLICK, THEN AN EVEN TICK.]

ENZO: It is moving evenly now. We need to compare the flash interval against the chart clock.

TALA: I'll count from the radio room.

YORI: No theatrical countdown. Call each flash as it happens.

[SFX: LENS MOTOR TURNS. A FLASH RELAY CLICKS.]

TALA: Flash.

[SFX: TEN SECONDS OF SURF AND EVEN CLOCK TICKING. SECOND RELAY CLICK.]

TALA: Flash. Ten seconds.

VENN: I see it at the west end of the channel. It is steady.

ENZO: One more check. If it holds, the fault is fixed.

[SFX: MOTOR TURNS. RELAY CLICKS ONCE MORE, EXACTLY TEN SECONDS LATER.]

TALA: Flash. Ten seconds.

YORI: The beacon is back to its marked interval. Venn, stay on the west beacon until you pass the shoal.

VENN: Copy. I'm clear of the marker.

TALA: Can I announce the repair?

YORI: Say the east light is verified at ten seconds and remind vessels to follow the posted channel chart.

TALA: Harbor update: the east light has been checked at ten-second intervals. The west beacon remains the safe reference while vessels pass the shoal.

VENN: I am through. The signal was clear.

ENZO: I'll lock the housing and mark the log. No countdown needed.

[SFX: HOUSING LATCH CLOSES. PEN SCRATCHES ON PAPER. CLOCK TICKS EVENLY.]

TALA: The bulletin ends here.

YORI: Let the clock keep time without an audience.

[SFX: RADIO SWITCH CLICKS OFF. SURF AND AN EVEN LIGHTHOUSE TICK CONTINUE.]
