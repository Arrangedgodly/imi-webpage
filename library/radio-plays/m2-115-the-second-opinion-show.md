# The Second Opinion Show

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio medical advice satire, credential-heavy panels, and specialists diagnosing a simple itch as a prestige mystery*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

*Cast: Dr. Grit, a macaque host; Dr. Pip, a capuchin skin specialist; Dr. Vale, an orangutan sleep consultant; Kori, a gibbon caller; and Caller, a langur whose tail twitches at music.*

[SFX: Serious medical theme, a clipboard taps.]

GRIT: Welcome to The Second Opinion, the program where every symptom receives two experts and a dramatic pause. Tonight Kori asks about an itch behind one ear.

KORI: I scratched it, and it stopped. I wondered if there was a leaf stuck in my fur.

PIP: We must consider the full picture. How long did the itch last?

KORI: A few seconds.

VALE: Did you sleep during those seconds?

KORI: No.

VALE: Then sleep cannot be ruled out.

GRIT: Dr. Pip, your initial assessment?

PIP: Possible leaf. Possibly a loose hair. I recommend looking with a mirror.

VALE: I recommend a sleep journal.

KORI: For an itch?

VALE: We need to establish whether it happens at night.

GRIT: Before we proceed, the network requires a scan. [SFX: A toy scanner beeps.] The scan reveals... one hair.

PIP: That is my sleeve brushing the microphone.

GRIT: A dramatic false positive.

KORI: I can feel the spot again.

PIP: Touch only the outer ear. Do not poke inside.

VALE: Close your eyes and rate the itch from one sheep to ten sheep.

KORI: Why sheep?

VALE: Sleep imagery.

GRIT: The itch is now a seven.

KORI: I didn't say that.

GRIT: The graphic says seven.

PIP: The graphic cannot diagnose.

[SFX: Another beep.]

GRIT: Breaking update: the itch has moved.

KORI: I turned my head.

PIP: That explains the movement.

VALE: Head-turning can indicate an interrupted sleep cycle.

KORI: I was awake the whole time.

GRIT: We'll take a caller. A listener says her tail twitches when she hears music.

PIP: That is often a normal response.

VALE: Does the music occur at bedtime?

CALLER: It is an afternoon drum circle.

VALE: Sleep timing remains relevant.

GRIT: We return to Kori. Dr. Pip, do you see a leaf?

PIP: Yes. A tiny leaf is caught in the fur behind the ear.

KORI: I thought so.

GRIT: The mystery deepens. Was the leaf placed there intentionally?

PIP: It came from the branch Kori rested beneath.

VALE: Did the branch disrupt your sleep?

KORI: I was awake.

VALE: The case remains open.

PIP: I will remove the leaf gently.

[SFX: Soft brushing; leaf drops onto the clipboard.]

KORI: The itch is gone.

GRIT: Our experts have identified a leaf, removed it, and ruled out a complicated sleep pattern.

VALE: I have not ruled it out. I have scheduled a follow-up nap.

PIP: No follow-up is needed if the symptom is gone.

GRIT: The network has prepared a dramatic reveal anyway.

[SFX: Theme swells; a paper flap opens.]

GRIT: The leaf was not alone. It had a tiny beetle passenger.

KORI: The beetle is on your clipboard.

PIP: It is walking toward Dr. Vale.

VALE: I feel an itch.

GRIT: A new patient joins the panel.

[SFX: Chair squeaks as Vale stands.]

VALE: I am going to sleep in another room.

PIP: That is not a diagnosis.

GRIT: Tonight, it is the ending. Kori is comfortable, the leaf has been removed, and Dr. Vale is avoiding the beetle.

KORI: Thank you for keeping it simple.

PIP: The beetle is off the clipboard and back on the leaf. I will carry it outside.

VALE: I have written down the time, the room, and the moment I felt an itch.

GRIT: That is a sleep journal.

VALE: It is now a beetle journal.

[SFX: A door opens; the beetle is gently released outdoors.]

PIP: No symptoms remain.

GRIT: The panel is symptom-free and the clipboard has one fewer guest.

[SFX: Serious theme ends with a tiny beetle footstep on the clipboard.]
