# The Root of the Matter

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio gardening call-in advice hour*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST ORI, a macaque; GARDENER MICA, a squirrel monkey; CALLER PEA, a capuchin; CALLER TARN, a baboon; and a spade.

[SFX: GARDEN THEME, SOFT WATERING CAN, A BELL.] 

ORI: Welcome to Root Notes, the gardening hour that answers questions from listeners and occasionally from whatever is growing near the microphone. Mica is here with expert advice.

MICA: If your plant has drooping leaves, check the soil before you add water. It may already be wet.

ORI: Our first caller has a question about a vine that has grown through a doorway.

PEA: It is blocking the entrance to my nest.

MICA: Does it have fruit?

PEA: Not yet.

MICA: Then trim a small section away from the doorway and support the vine along the wall.

ORI: Can we call this a boundary-setting vine?

MICA: You can call it a vine that needs a trellis.

[SFX: A SMALL SNIP. A VINE CREAKS.] 

PEA: I have moved it. I can open the door now.

ORI: A satisfying outcome. We have a second caller, Tarn, who says his seed bed is empty.

TARN: I planted acorns yesterday. Today the bed is empty, and there are tiny holes all over it.

MICA: How many did you plant?

TARN: Twelve.

MICA: How many are left?

TARN: I told you. The bed is empty.

ORI: The mystery grows.

MICA: Did you bury the acorns or leave them on top?

TARN: I made a shallow hole, dropped one in, then covered it.

MICA: Could you check the nearby ground? Squirrels may have moved them to a safer place.

TARN: I am a squirrel.

ORI: A specialist caller.

TARN: I stored them in the path by accident. I was looking at the garden while carrying them.

MICA: Then you know where to look.

[SFX: DIGGING, PAUSE, THEN AN ACORN ROLLS.] 

TARN: I found one. It has a little root.

MICA: Put it back in soft soil, root down, and mark the spot with a stone.

ORI: Can listeners send in stones?

MICA: Please don't. One small stone is enough.

[THE ACORN IS REPLANTED. A STONE SETS DOWN.] 

TARN: I can see it now.

ORI: Gardening success on the air.

MICA: And don't dig it up every day to check. Roots need time.

ORI: Our final caller says a bird keeps taking the garden labels.

PEA: That's me again. I used a twig label. The bird uses twigs in its nest.

MICA: Try a flat stone marker. The bird can choose another twig.

[SFX: STONE SCRATCHED WITH A CHARCOAL MARK.] 

PEA: I wrote "vine" on it.

ORI: There is a new line in the garden ledger.

MICA: The plant has not changed. We have simply learned where it is.

ORI: That's Root Notes: careful observation, sensible watering, and a reminder that birds have their own construction plans.


ORI: We have a question from a young gardener who planted a seed in a glass jar.

CALLER: I can see the roots growing along the side. Should I turn the jar every morning so the plant grows straight?

MICA: The stem follows light. Put the jar where it gets gentle sun and turn it only if one side gets much more light. Be careful not to leave glass in strong hot sun.

ORI: Could the jar be a tiny greenhouse?

MICA: It can also trap too much heat. A pot with drainage is safer once the seedling grows.

CALLER: I can move it to a clay pot.

MICA: Keep some of the same soil around its roots. Lift it gently by the leaves, not the stem.

[SFX: SOIL POURED, A SMALL POT SET DOWN.]

ORI: The advice line has become very practical.

MICA: Plants are practical. They respond to water, light, and room for roots.

ORI: Can we play the root-growth sound?

MICA: Roots do not make a sound we can hear.

ORI: We could make one.

MICA: Then label it as a sound we made.

[ORI RUBS A DRY LEAF BETWEEN TWO FINGERS. IT CRACKLES.] 

ORI: This is a leaf sound, not a root sound.

MICA: That's accurate.

ORI: We are learning. Our next caller is Pea again. Has the bird taken another label?

PEA: It took the flat stone. I think it wanted the charcoal mark.

MICA: Is the stone near the nest?

PEA: Yes.

MICA: The bird may be using it to line the entrance. Mark the plant with a second stone and leave the first where it is.

ORI: Does that mean the bird is gardening?

MICA: It means the bird found a useful object.

[SFX: A SECOND STONE IS PLACED. WINGS FLUTTER IN THE DISTANCE.] 

PEA: I can see both the vine and the nest from here.

ORI: Root Notes has expanded into a neighborhood map.

MICA: No. We have simply stopped moving things that already work.
[SFX: THEME. WATERING CAN STOPS. A BIRD CARRIES OFF THE SHOW'S WOODEN SIGN.]
