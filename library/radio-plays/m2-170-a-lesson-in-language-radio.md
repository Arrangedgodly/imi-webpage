# A Lesson in Language Radio

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: language-learning broadcasts, call-and-repeat drills, cheerful pronunciation correction, and examples that become literal misunderstandings*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
TEACHER LINA, a squirrel monkey
PUPIL OBI, a capuchin
ANNOUNCER SOL, a lemur

[UPBEAT LESSON THEME.]

SOL: Welcome to Speak Along, the language program that teaches useful phrases one radio lesson at a time. Lina, what are we learning today?

LINA: Directions. Obi, repeat after me: "Turn left at the fountain."

OBI: Turn left at the fountain.

LINA: Excellent. Now say, "The station is across the square."

OBI: The station is across the square.

LINA: Perfect. Let's combine them: "Turn left at the fountain, and the station is across the square."

OBI: Turn left at the fountain, and the station is across the square.

SOL: Our listeners have spoken the sentence. Where are we going?

OBI: To the fountain, then across the square to the station.

LINA: Good. But first, we'll practice a polite greeting: "Excuse me, can you help me?"

OBI: Excuse me, can you help me?

[DOOR OPENS.]

DELIVERY VOICE: What do you need?

OBI: I need help. The station is across the square, and I have to turn left at the fountain.

DELIVERY VOICE: You're in the station.

OBI: The lesson said the station is across the square.

LINA: The example station is across the square. This studio is in the station.

OBI: That sentence has made the directions harder.

SOL: We have found our first false friend: an example that resembles our actual situation.

LINA: Let's practice asking for clarification. Say, "Do you mean this station or the other station?"

OBI: Do you mean this station or the other station?

DELIVERY VOICE: This one. The other one is across the square.

OBI: Thank you. Now I understand.

LINA: Wonderful. We'll learn a new word: "near." Repeat: "The fountain is near the station."

OBI: The fountain is near the station.

LINA: And "far": "The other station is far from the fountain."

OBI: The other station is far from the fountain.

SOL: Can you now give the delivery voice directions to the other station?

OBI: Excuse me, turn left at the fountain. The other station is across the square, near the bakery and far from this studio.

DELIVERY VOICE: That is clear. Thank you.

[FOOTSTEPS. DOOR CLOSES.]

LINA: A successful conversation. Obi asked politely, checked which station, and used near and far.

OBI: I still haven't reached the other station.

SOL: Would you like to go now?

OBI: Yes. Which way is the fountain?

LINA: Out the front door, then left.

OBI: Does "left" mean my left or the studio's left?

LINA: Your left, as you face the street.

OBI: That's useful. I'll say it aloud.

[DOOR OPENS. FOOTSTEPS.]

OBI: Turn left at the fountain, cross the square, and arrive at the other station.

SOL: He has reached it.

OBI: I'm at the bakery.

BAKER: You are close. The station entrance is across the square. Go past the clock, then turn right at the blue sign.

OBI: Is the blue sign near the station?

BAKER: Yes. You can see the doors from there.

OBI: Thank you. I can follow that.

[FOOTSTEPS, CROSSING SIGNAL BEEPS, A STATION BELL.]

LINA: This time, he has arrived at the correct station.

OBI: I checked the sign and asked when I was unsure.

SOL: The lesson has taken us there by the long route, but the phrase worked.

LINA: You turned left at the fountain, but you crossed the side street too early.

OBI: I learned a new phrase: "I am near the bakery, not at the station."

SOL: Excellent use. The baker can point you onward.

BAKER: Go past my door, then cross at the light.

OBI: Thank you. I'll follow those directions.

[FOOTSTEPS CONTINUE, THEN A STATION BELL.]

OBI: I made it to the other station.

LINA: And what will you say?

OBI: "I'm here."

SOL: Short, clear, and correct. That concludes Speak Along. Next week, we learn the difference between "near the train" and "on the train."

[TRAIN WHISTLE. THEME MUSIC.]
