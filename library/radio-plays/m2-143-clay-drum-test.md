# The Clay Drum Test

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio craft demonstrations, pottery kiln safety, and a ceramic drum maker whose prototypes sound better before firing*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Omi, a gibbon craft host; Valla, an orangutan potter; Peep, a macaque apprentice; and Rell, a capuchin caller.*

[SFX: Pottery wheel turns, clay squishes, one hollow tap.]

OMI: Welcome to Hands in Clay. Today Valla is shaping a small ceramic drum. Listeners can follow the sound, though we will not ask you to use a kiln at home.

VALLA: First I center the clay, then pull the walls upward. The rim must stay even.

PEEP: The drum is round.

VALLA: It is a bowl.

PEEP: It has a rim and a hollow middle.

VALLA: It will become a drum when we stretch a skin across the opening.

OMI: Can you test it now?

VALLA: Carefully. The clay is wet.

[SFX: A soft finger tap; a satisfying hollow tone.]

PEEP: It sounds ready.

VALLA: It sounds hollow. It is not strong enough to play.

RELL: I have made one at home. Mine made no sound.

VALLA: Was the clay too thick?

RELL: It was a flowerpot.

OMI: A flowerpot is not a drum.

RELL: I put a skin on it.

VALLA: Did you stretch the skin evenly?

RELL: I tied it to a handle.

PEEP: The handle is not a drum rim.

RELL: It kept the skin in place.

VALLA: It may work as an instrument, but we should test it gently.

[SFX: A low thump from Rell's pot, then a wobble.]

OMI: It has a sound.

VALLA: The handle is vibrating against the pot. That is the wobble.

PEEP: We can add a felt pad.

VALLA: That would change the sound and could hide a crack.

OMI: The demo drum is ready for the kiln. What happens next?

VALLA: It dries slowly, then fires at the correct temperature. Never rush a wet pot into a hot kiln.

PEEP: If it cracks, it is not a drum.

VALLA: It may be a planter.

RELL: My pot is already planted.

[SFX: A small leaf rustles inside Rell's pot.]

OMI: The caller has an instrument and a flowerpot in one.

VALLA: I will finish the clay drum after the broadcast. We can compare it with Rell's pot tomorrow.

PEEP: The flowerpot can be the control sample.

RELL: Does that mean it is part of the experiment?

VALLA: Yes, if it stays on the table and no one hits it hard.

OMI: We will not fire the caller's pot. It already has a plant.

[SFX: Wheel slows; a towel wipes the workbench.]

PEEP: The clay is centered, the rim is even, and the drum is ready to dry.

VALLA: Tomorrow it will be fired and tested with a soft mallet.

RELL: I will bring my pot back.

OMI: Keep the plant watered, but leave the skin at home.

RELL: The plant likes the skin. It shades the soil.

PEEP: Then the pot is also a tiny greenhouse.

OMI: We have taught pottery, tested a sound, and prevented a wet drum from entering the kiln.

[SFX: Clay tool rests on table; wheel stops.]

VALLA: The finished instrument comes next episode, after the clay is dry.

OMI: This has been Hands in Clay. The bowl is not yet a drum, and the plant pot is doing fine.

VALLA: The kiln has reached temperature. The test pot is fully fired and cooled before we touch it.

PEEP: The rim is solid. I can tap it with a soft mallet.

[SFX: A light mallet tap; clear ceramic tone.]

OMI: This pot is ready to use as a drum after its membrane is fitted. The wet prototype stays on the drying shelf.

RELL: My plant is still growing in its pot.

VALLA: Leave it there. The plant does not need a sound test.

[SFX: One gentle tap; the empty clay shell answers with a hollow tone.]
