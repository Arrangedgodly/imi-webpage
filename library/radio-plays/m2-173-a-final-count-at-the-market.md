# A Final Count at the Market

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live election-night radio coverage, early projections, map graphics described aloud, and correspondents who treat partial counts as final*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
ANCHOR VERA, a capuchin
REPORTER JAL, a gibbon
CLERK ETTI, a ring-tailed lemur

[NEWS THEME. CROWD AMBIENCE.]

VERA: We are live from Kettle Market, where voters are choosing the new market bell. The candidates are a brass bell and a wooden clacker. Jal, what are you seeing?

JAL: The first three ballots have been counted. Two favor the bell, one favors the clacker. Our projection desk calls this a bell lead.

VERA: Is it too early to call?

JAL: It is early to call, but not too early to project. We have a map on the screen, though radio listeners must imagine two colored dots.

VERA: One dot for each candidate?

JAL: One dot for the table where the ballots are being counted.

[PHONE LINE BEEPS.]

VERA: We have Clerk Etti on the line. How many ballots remain?

ETTI: Twenty-seven. We're counting one at a time and checking the marks.

JAL: The bell candidate has won the first precinct by two votes.

ETTI: There is only one precinct. Those were the first three ballots.

VERA: We have a difference in terminology, but the lead remains real.

JAL: The bell has now taken a commanding lead. Four ballots counted; three for bell, one for clacker.

ETTI: That is a lead, not a command. The clacker has supporters.

VERA: Let's go to the market entrance. Are voters still arriving?

[FOOTSTEPS. A CROW QUACKS.]

JAL: Voters are coming through the east door. One has brought a basket. Another appears to be carrying a duck.

ETTI: That's a decoy clacker. It is carved from wood, but it is not a ballot.

VERA: A dramatic symbol from the clacker campaign.

JAL: The duck is not endorsing anyone. It is a market decoration.

[DECORATION FALLS, MAKING A CLACK.]

VERA: The clacker has made a sound. We should note that it was not an official vote.

ETTI: Correct. We're at sixteen ballots counted. The bell has eight, the clacker eight.

JAL: We are tied. The map is now split exactly down the middle.

VERA: What does a tie mean for the market?

ETTI: Both devices will be tested. The vendors choose which sound they prefer after hearing them.

VERA: That is not how I thought the election would end.

ETTI: The ballot asked, "Which should we use?" It did not ask which campaign would win forever.

[BRASS BELL RINGS. THEN WOODEN CLACKER TAPS.]

JAL: The bell carries farther across the square. The clacker is easier to hear inside the covered stalls.

VERA: Can we use both?

ETTI: The market committee can. The bell marks opening time; the clacker signals the short aisle is clear.

JAL: A coalition of sounds. Neither candidate has lost its purpose.

VERA: JAL: The last ballots are being checked now. One is smudged, so Clerk Etti is comparing it with the voter's mark before counting.

VERA: We will not project from a mark we cannot read.

ETTI: The voter confirmed it was for the clacker. It counts. We have a final number.

JAL: The brass bell has a majority by one vote. The clacker has nearly half the count.

VERA: Is there a recount?

ETTI: No request has been made. The committee's plan gives both sounds a use, and the voters have heard the test.

The final count is twenty-seven ballots: thirteen for the bell, twelve for the clacker, two blank. The committee will use both for different jobs.

ETTI: The result has been checked and posted. No one needs to guess from a colored dot.

JAL: The brass bell rings at the entrance; the clacker taps within the covered aisle.

[BRASS RING, THEN TWO CLACKS.]

VERA: Election night has concluded with a clear count, a shared solution, and a duck that is not running for office.

JAL: The duck has left the market.

VERA: It may return for the next poll.

[NEWS THEME.]

ETTI: It cannot vote. It is a decoration.

VERA: We will report that accurately next time.
