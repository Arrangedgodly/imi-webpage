# The Planetarium Schedules a Meteor Shower

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: science broadcasts that promise spectacle on a fixed schedule*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-017*

Cast: Presenter Seli, a lemur; astronomer Mara, a macaque; projectionist Odo, a gibbon; visitor Pip, a capuchin; producer Vex, an orangutan.

SFX: Planetarium dome. Projector whirs. Seats creak.

SELI: Tonight we present the meteor shower at precisely eight.

MARA: The meteors are visible only if the sky is clear.

VEX: The ticket says eight o'clock.

ODO: The ticket says the program begins at eight.

PIP: Is the shower happening now?

MARA: The real shower may be visible later. We'll show a sky map while we wait.

VEX: We promised a spectacle.

ODO: The projector has a star field, not a meteor track.

VEX: Add streaks.

SFX: Projector buttons click. A bright line streaks across the dome.

PIP: That looked like a scratch.

MARA: The slide is upside down.

ODO: I can rotate it.

SFX: Projector whirs; image shifts.

VEX: Tell the audience the first meteor has arrived.

MARA: It is a demonstration.

PIP: I would rather know which direction to look outside.

MARA: The radiant is in the northeast, above the ridge.

ODO: We can put a compass arrow on the dome.

VEX: A compass arrow is not as exciting as a meteor.

SELI: We can explain how to find the real one, then let people step outside.

VEX: The ticket holders were promised a scheduled shower. We cannot tell them to go outside and hope.

MARA: We can tell them the forecast and show the map. A meteor shower is not a stage cue.

ODO: I can dim the dome without adding fake streaks. The audience can learn where the radiant sits.

PIP: Will the room be dark enough to see it?

MARA: Yes. The projector will go off, and we'll give everyone a minute before leaving.

SFX: Projector dims. Stars appear, fixed and quiet.

SELI: The ridge is north. When you face it from the dome steps, northeast is to your right.

VEX: The stars are not moving.

ODO: The planetarium is showing the sky as it is now.

PIP: I can pick out the bright triangle.

MARA: That's the guide shape. The meteor can cross any part of the sky, so don't stare at one point.

SFX: A seat creaks in the row behind Pip.

PIP: That was my chair. Nothing has crossed the sky.

VEX: The program needs a moment of suspense.

MARA: Suspense is not evidence. The dome is showing fixed stars, and the real sky has not appeared yet.

ODO: The projector is still on. I'll shut it down before we leave.

SELI: The projection is going off now. Outside, we will give our eyes a minute to adjust before looking for a real meteor.

SFX: Projector bulb clicks out. The fixed stars vanish; the projector motor stops.

SFX: Chair legs shift as the group stands.

PIP: The door is stuck.

ODO: The latch is caught by the projector cable.

MARA: Don't pull. I'll lift the cable while Odo opens the latch.

SFX: Cable lifts; door clicks free.

VEX: The audience is leaving before the finale.

PIP: The sky is the finale.

SFX: Night air. Crickets. Odo holds the dome door for the last visitors.

MARA: Give your eyes a minute to adjust.

SFX: A quiet minute passes under the crickets.

ODO: There. A faint streak above the north ridge.

PIP: I saw it. The projection is off, so that one was in the sky.

ODO: We can point out the radiant without drawing another streak.

SELI: The group is facing the right direction now.

PIP: I can describe where I saw the light, so the next people know where to look.

MARA: Good. We are not promising another one tonight.

VEX: The audience can keep watching if they want.

SFX: Door closes gently behind the last visitor.

SELI: We can describe the streak we saw outside, but we cannot promise another one at a scheduled minute.

VEX: The ticket said eight.

MARA: The ticket was for the program, not the meteor.

VEX: Was that a meteor?

PIP: It was a cricket.
