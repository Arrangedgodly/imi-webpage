# The Missing Note Archive

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: music-preservation broadcasts, solemn record introductions, and expert arguments about hiss*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-004*

Cast: archivist Pela, a macaque; host Roro, a gibbon; technician Kett, a gorilla; collector Senn, a capuchin; caller Bavi, a lemur.

[SFX: NEEDLE SETTLES ON A RECORD. SOFT HISS. THREE PIANO NOTES.]

RORO: Tonight, we preserve the final recording of the Hollow Quartet.

PELA: The recording is missing one note.

KETT: The needle skips.

SENN: It is the original performance.

BAVI: I hear a scratch.

RORO: The scratch is part of the archive.

PELA: The missing note may change the whole piece.

KETT: The record is warped.

SENN: It is historically warped.

RORO: We will play the recording in full.

[SFX: RECORD PLAYS. THREE NOTES. A PAUSE. HISS.]

PELA: There. The fourth note is absent.

SENN: The quartet ended on three notes.

PELA: The catalog says four.

KETT: The catalog was written by Senn.

SENN: I counted the echo.

BAVI: There is an echo after the third note.

RORO: A note beyond the music.

PELA: It sounds like a chair.

KETT: The sound comes from the studio.

[SFX: CHAIR CREAKS.]

RORO: We have recreated the missing note.

PELA: No. That chair was not there in the original recording.

SENN: It may have been.

KETT: The recording was made in a room with chairs.

SENN: The quartet sat down between movements.

RORO: A clue from the room itself.

PELA: We should inspect the master disc.

[SFX: DRAWER OPENS. RECORD SLEEVE RUSTLES.]

KETT: The disc has a crack.

SENN: It is a fine crack.

PELA: That is why the needle skips.

RORO: The missing note may be trapped in the crack.

KETT: It is not trapped. It is not recorded.

BAVI: Could the quartet have played the note softly?

PELA: The waveform shows a gap.

RORO: A silence can still be music.

SENN: This was a rehearsal.

PELA: You said it was the final recording.

SENN: It was the final rehearsal before the final recording.

KETT: That is a different record.

RORO: Which one are we preserving?

SENN: The rehearsal. The final disc is in another sleeve.

[SFX: SECOND DRAWER OPENS. A CLEANER RECORD IS REMOVED.]

PELA: The final recording has four notes.

KETT: It plays without a skip.

RORO: We have found the missing note.

[SFX: RECORD PLAYS. FOUR PIANO NOTES. THE FOURTH IS A SHORT, HIGH TINK.]

BAVI: The note is very small.

SENN: The player hit a spoon.

PELA: The score says piano.

SENN: The spoon was on the piano.

KETT: The recording preserves the performance exactly.

RORO: The spoon is now part of history.

PELA: We should label the record correctly.

SENN: Write "quartet with spoon."

RORO: Will you still call it a quartet?

SENN: There were four musicians.

BAVI: And a spoon.

PELA: A fifth instrument, then.

KETT: The spoon has no solo credit.

[SFX: SPOON TAPS AGAINST A CUP.]

RORO: A postscript from the archive.

PELA: That was the tea service.

SENN: The archive is alive with sound.

BAVI: I can hear the kettle.

[SFX: KETTLE WHISTLES.]

RORO: Another missing note.

PELA: It is the kettle.

KETT: We should turn it off.

[SFX: SWITCH CLICKS. KETTLE STOPS.]

RORO: The silence returns.

PELA: The broadcast has preserved enough.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS. RORO ANSWERS.]

RORO: We have a listener question.

BAVI: If the spoon made the fourth note, is the quartet still complete?

SENN: The four musicians are on the record.

PELA: The spoon is a sound in the room.

KETT: The archive can preserve both versions.

RORO: The rehearsal with the skip, and the final take with the spoon.

BAVI: Which one is the real recording?

SENN: They both happened.

PELA: We can label the dates and let listeners hear the difference.

[SFX: TWO RECORDS PLAY BRIEFLY, FIRST THREE NOTES, THEN FOUR WITH A TINK.]

BAVI: Now I can hear the room.

[SFX: RECORD STOPS. NEEDLE LIFTS.]
