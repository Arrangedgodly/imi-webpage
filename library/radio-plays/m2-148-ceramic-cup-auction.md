# The Ceramic Cup Auction

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio craft-market auctions, breathless bidding calls, and an audience that thinks every handmade cup is a microphone*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-005*

*Cast: Sela, a lemur auction host; Varo, an orangutan potter; Mip, a capuchin bidder; and Tern, a macaque sound technician.*

[SFX: Auction bell, crowd murmurs, ceramic clink.]

SELA: Welcome to the craft market auction. Lot one is a blue ceramic cup made by Varo. It holds one drink and has a small thumb rest.

VARO: The thumb rest is part of the handle.

MIP: I thought it was a tiny microphone.

TERN: It is not a microphone.

SELA: We begin at three seeds. Do I hear three?

MIP: Three seeds for the cup. I will sing into it at home.

VARO: Please do not strike the rim. It is glazed ceramic.

SELA: Four seeds. Five. Six. The cup is going once.

[SFX: Auction bell rings; ceramic resonates.]

TERN: That tone came from the cup, not the bell.

SELA: Seven seeds. The sound technician is interested.

TERN: I am checking the resonance.

MIP: Eight seeds. It makes a clear note.

VARO: It is a cup. It can also make a note.

SELA: Sold to Mip for eight seeds.

MIP: I will use it as a microphone.

VARO: You may use it for tea.

SELA: Lot two is a tall vase with a narrow neck. It will hold one branch.

TERN: Can it hold a microphone?

VARO: No.

TERN: I was asking for the station.

SELA: We begin at two seeds. Do I hear two?

MIP: I can bid three, but I already have the cup.

SELA: The vase is going once. Twice.

[SFX: A branch is placed inside; vase makes a hollow tone.]

TERN: That sounds like a small drum.

VARO: It is a vase.

SELA: The sound technician bids four.

TERN: I did not bid. I said it sounded like a drum.

MIP: I bid five. I will use it as a drum.

VARO: Please do not use the vase as a drum.

SELA: Sold to Mip for five seeds.

MIP: I have one cup and one vase. We can make a band.

VARO: Only if you keep them intact.

SELA: Lot three is a small bowl, safely designed for soup.

MIP: Does it sound like a microphone?

TERN: It sounds like soup.

SELA: One seed. Two. Going once.

[SFX: Bowl taps lightly against a table.]

VARO: That tap is not part of the bid.

MIP: Three seeds. I have started a collection.

SELA: Sold. The bowl goes to Mip.

MIP: I will drink tea, hold a branch, and eat soup. The band is canceled.

VARO: Excellent. The cup, vase, and bowl each have their intended use.

TERN: The cup's resonance could still help tune the bell.

SELA: We can test that after the auction.

[SFX: Bell rings; cup answers with a gentle tone.]

MIP: I thought the cup was a microphone, but it is a cup with good acoustics.

VARO: That is the correct description.

SELA: The auction has sold three pieces, and the potter has explained what they do.

TERN: The station will keep the bell. It is the only object built to make that sound.

MIP: I will take my cup, vase, and bowl home in separate baskets.

VARO: Carry the vase upright.

MIP: I will not sing into it.

SELA: The bell has been tested. It rings clearly, and the cup answers softly.

TERN: The cup is not a substitute for the bell.

MIP: It is a second tone in a small kitchen ensemble.

VARO: Only if you set it on a padded shelf after using it.

SELA: The next lot is a plate with a painted fruit border.

MIP: I already have fruit on my cup.

VARO: Those are flowers.

MIP: I will look closely before I bid.

[SFX: Crowd applauds; ceramic pieces are packed carefully.]

SELA: This has been the Ceramic Cup Auction. The highest bid bought a cup, the vase remained a vase, and no one acquired a microphone by mistake.

[SFX: Auction bell rings; one soft cup note follows.]
