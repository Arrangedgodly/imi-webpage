# Interval at the Old Hollow

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Classical orchestra intermission broadcasts and program notes*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Announcer Val, a capuchin musicologist; conductor Pella, a langur; Brix, a mandrill percussionist; concertmaster Omi, a marmoset; Tuck, an elephant groundskeeper.

[SFX: ORCHESTRA PLAYS A FORMAL CHORD. APPLAUSE, THEN A LONG PAUSE.] 

ANNOUNCER VAL: We are between movements at the Old Hollow Concert Hall. The first half of tonight's program was "Four Moods of the Rain," followed by a ten-minute audience cough. I am Val, a capuchin announcer with a folded program and no place to put it.

VAL: The second half features Pella's arrangement of the forest's spring chorus. She has placed the violins high in the canopy, the cellos near the roots, and Brix's log drums somewhere the audience can feel in its sternum.

PELLA: It is not a drum solo. It is the sound of the grove waking.

BRIX: The grove wakes at six. I have to be here at four.

VAL: We have a question from a listener: why did the orchestra stop after the first movement?

PELLA: A branch fell on the roof. We waited for the sound to stop moving before we continued.

VAL: Was it part of the performance?

PELLA: It became part of the room. It was not part of the score.

[SFX: WOOD CREAKS ABOVE. A SMALL TWIG DROPS INTO A BASKET.] 

VAL: We can hear a soft creak now.

TUCK: That is the hall settling. The old tree has grown around the roof. I am checking the upper roots during the intermission.

VAL: Will that interrupt the next movement?

TUCK: Only if the tree decides to conduct.

VAL: Our musicians are ready. Before they return, a few program notes. Pella asked the cellists to listen for the spring peepers in the second section. The frogs are outside and will not follow tempo. Brix said that is how he likes them.

BRIX: They don't ask for an encore.

OMI: The opening flute line is based on the call of a gibbon at dawn. It rises, drops, then catches on the last note, like a swing that almost misses.

VAL: Did the gibbon approve the arrangement?

OMI: She is in the front row. She says the flute is too polite.

PELLA: We tried a louder version. The audience thought it was an alarm.

VAL: The orchestra is returning. A reminder: please unwrap your snacks quietly. The program says the second movement is "The First Warm Branch." It is six minutes long, though the branch warmed before the title was chosen.

[SFX: MUSIC BEGINS, A QUIET STRING PATTERN. LOG DRUMS ENTER, THEN A FROG CALL ANSWERS.] 

VAL: The piece has started. We can hear Omi's flute above the strings. Brix is tapping a hollow log. That sound moves through the floor. A young gibbon in the front row has begun to sway.

PELLA: She's listening with her whole body.

VAL: The orchestra reaches the first full chord. The audience breathes in together. A twig falls. Nobody stops.

[SFX: TWIG TAPS A BUCKET. THE MUSIC PAUSES, THEN ADDS THE BUCKET'S PITCH.] 

VAL: The bucket has joined.

PELLA: It is in tune.

BRIX: Give it a chair.

VAL: The second half ends with a high note held by the strings, followed by the bucket's small answer. The spring chorus carries on outside. The audience applauds, including the gibbon whose call inspired the flute. She says it was closer this time.

TUCK: The roof is safe. The tree gets a trim tomorrow, and the bucket will go back to the storage room.

VAL: The bucket may stay for the encore.

PELLA: It has earned the chair.

VAL: This has been the intermission broadcast. Next time, we will explain why the orchestra tunes for so long and why the oboe always looks surprised. Until then, listen to the hall around the music.

PELLA: The oboe is ready.

VAL: It looks startled.

OMI: It has just found out it is tuning alone.

BRIX: I have the bucket for the encore.

TUCK: The bucket is back in storage.

BRIX: I borrowed another one.

TUCK: Why?

BRIX: The tree is still settling.

PELLA: The bucket can join for one note.

VAL: We will list it under guest percussion.

BRIX: It demands a program fee.

[APPLAUSE, A SINGLE BUCKET NOTE, MUSIC OUT.]
