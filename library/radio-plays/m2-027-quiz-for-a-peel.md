# Quiz for a Peel

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Live audience quiz programs and telephone prize rounds*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Host Javi, a squirrel monkey; contestant Nori, a mandrill; caller Fenn, a gibbon; judge Plum, a parrot who knows the rules.

[THEME: TRUMPET, TWO HANDCLAPS, A BUZZER THAT SOUNDS LIKE A GOOSE.] 

HOST JAVI: This is Quiz for a Peel, the call-in show where listeners win a mystery prize and learn how long a mystery lasts. I'm Javi. Our in-studio contestant is Nori, a mandrill. Nori, if you win, you get the golden acorn under this cloth.

NORI: Is it edible?

JAVI: The prize has been polished for broadcast. We make no claims about flavor.

PLUM: I am required to say the acorn is a painted wooden replica.

JAVI: Thank you, Judge Plum. First question. Which animal can hang by its tail?

NORI: A spider monkey.

JAVI: Correct. You win one point and a drawing of a tail.

NORI: I have one.

JAVI: The drawing is signed by our sponsor.

[SFX: PHONE LINE DINGS.] 

JAVI: Caller Fenn, you're up for the sound round. Listen carefully. What made that noise?

[SFX: TWO TAPS, A SHORT SCRAPE, THEN A SQUEAK.] 

FENN: A gibbon opening a sticky fruit jar.

JAVI: Correct. How did you know?

FENN: I am a gibbon. My jar makes the same sound.

JAVI: Expertise! One point to Fenn. Nori, next question. A monkey sees a ripe mango on a high branch. What should it do?

NORI: Check whether the branch can hold its weight, then climb carefully.

JAVI: Also correct. You are now tied with Fenn. The audience may help by calling in one answer. Lines are open.

[SFX: FIVE PHONES RING AT ONCE. A PARROT SQUAWKS.] 

CALLER ONE: Jump!

CALLER TWO: Ask the mango!

CALLER THREE: Take the ladder!

PLUM: There is no ladder in the question.

JAVI: Judge Plum has ruled that the mango may not be cross-examined. Final question. We have a prize bell. If you ring it, the round is over. When should you ring it?

NORI: When the answer is settled.

JAVI: Correct. Ring the bell.

[SFX: Nori rings. The bell produces a cough.] 

JAVI: The prize bell is broken.

PLUM: The show rules say a broken bell still counts if the contestant used it with confidence.

NORI: Then I win?

JAVI: You win the golden acorn, the drawing, and a small bag of actual snacks for helping us discover the bell is broken.

NORI: Can Fenn have some?

JAVI: Sharing is allowed. It costs the show nothing.

FENN: That's my favorite prize.

JAVI: Our callers have one last chance to win a clue about the mystery item. The answer is a thing you can hear but not keep.

CALLER FOUR: Applause?

JAVI: Correct. The prize is the applause we just recorded.

[SFX: RECORDED APPLAUSE PLAYS. IT STOPS ABRUPTLY.] 

NORI: It ended too soon.

JAVI: That is the mystery. The audience recording was eight seconds long, but the button was taped down. We have extended it to twelve seconds. The prize lasts longer now.

PLUM: I approve the extension.

JAVI: The listener line is still open. Caller five, you have ten seconds to tell us what a prize is for.

CALLER FIVE: For the person who wins.

JAVI: Correct. The prize is this bell's repair bill.

PLUM: That is not a prize.

JAVI: It is a bill that someone else will pay. We have learned our rules need a better prize definition.

NORI: Can the next prize be something useful?

JAVI: Yes. Next week's prize is a working bell and a bag of snacks. The snacks are not polished. The bell will be tested before we go on air.

FENN: I will call in from home.

PLUM: You are already in the studio.

FENN: Then I will call from the snack table.

JAVI: Quiz for a Peel ends here. The golden acorn goes home with Nori, the snack bag is shared, and the bell will be repaired before next week. Call again when you know an answer or want to hear a goose buzzer.

[THEME PLAYS. GOOSE BUZZER ANSWERS. END.]
