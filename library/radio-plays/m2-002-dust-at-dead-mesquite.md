# Dust at Dead Mesquite

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Classic radio western serials*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Mica, a capuchin marshal; Biscuit, her mule; Mayor Townsmarmal, a baboon; Miss Kettle, a gibbon teacher; Cress, a coyote whose camp needs water.

[THEME: FIDDLE, LOW DRUM, A WIND MACHINE THAT SQUEAKS.]

NARRATOR: Dead Mesquite had one street, two wells, and enough dust to hide a dishonest hoofprint. At sundown, Marshal Mica came riding in on a mule who knew more about town politics than the mayor.

[SFX: HOOVES, THEN A LONG BRAKE SQUEAL.] 

MICA: Easy, Biscuit.

BISCUIT: Hrrr.

MICA: That is what I said.

NARRATOR: The town's brass bell rang once. It was the signal for trouble, or for the bakery's cinnamon rolls. In Dead Mesquite, the difference was one cracked note.

[BELL: A DULL CLANG.] 

TOWNSMARMAL, MAYOR: Marshal! Somebody stole the town's only water cart.

MICA: Which direction?

MAYOR: The direction with less water.

MICA: Biscuit, your thoughts?

[MULE SNORTS. HOOVES TURN LEFT.] 

NARRATOR: Biscuit's nose led them past the livery to a dry wash. There, a line of hoofprints ran west, then doubled back toward the schoolhouse. Mica dismounted. As a capuchin, she could grip the wash's steep bank with hands and feet, but she made Biscuit take the long trail. Biscuit had a strong back and a delicate opinion of falling.

MICA: These tracks are too neat. Whoever took the cart walked backward for a while.

MAYOR: To fool the law?

MICA: To fool anyone who thinks law is just looking at the ground.

[SFX: A SCHOOL BELL. CHILDREN CALL FROM A DISTANCE.] 

NARRATOR: The schoolteacher, Miss Kettle, stood in the doorway with an empty bucket. She told them a stranger had delivered water at noon, then left without asking for payment. The stranger wore a red bandana and walked backward all the way down the road.

MICA: Did you see the cart?

MISS KETTLE: I heard its wheel squeak. One long squeak, two short ones. The town cart makes three short squeaks.

MAYOR: We have two carts?

MISS KETTLE: We have one cart and a broken wagon. The wagon squeaks three short ones.

[SFX: MULE SNORTS TWICE.] 

NARRATOR: Biscuit had found a strip of blue cloth caught in a thorn. It smelled of cactus flower and peach pie. Mica followed the smell to a canyon shed where a young coyote named Cress was patching a wheel with schoolbook string.

CRESS: I borrowed the cart.

MAYOR: You stole it!

CRESS: The well at my camp went dry. I filled the school cistern. I was going to bring the cart back.

MICA: Why walk backward?

CRESS: I thought the mayor would see tracks and come after me before I got the water to the children.

MAYOR: I would have.

MICA: Then the cart wasn't the only thing that needed repair.

NARRATOR: The mayor looked at the empty canteen on his belt. Dead Mesquite's well had been running low for weeks, and nobody had told the neighboring camps. He lowered his hat.

MAYOR: The cart is town property. The water is everybody's trouble. We'll make a schedule.

CRESS: And fix this wheel?

MICA: First we fix the wheel. Then we ride to the ridge and mark every dry well.

CRESS: The camp has a spring a mile farther west. It isn't enough for all of us, but it can refill the school cistern if we take turns.

MAYOR: I'll put the schedule on the bell post.

MISS KETTLE: Put it where children can reach it. They are the ones carrying the buckets.

[SFX: WRENCH TURNS. WHEEL SQUEAKS ONCE, THEN GOES QUIET.] 

NARRATOR: At sunset, the cart rolled back into town. Biscuit pulled it, Cress pushed, and the mayor walked beside them carrying a bucket. The schoolchildren ran ahead with tin cups. No one asked who had won the chase.

MICA: Biscuit, where to?

[MULE HOOVES TURN TOWARD THE RIDGE.] 

NARRATOR: Dead Mesquite's bell rang again. This time the note was clear. The bakery had cinnamon rolls. Marshal Mica rode out before they cooled, and every well along the way got a mark on the map.

MICA: Biscuit, you want one?

BISCUIT: Hrrr.

MICA: That means yes.

MISS KETTLE: It means you have cinnamon on your sleeve.

[THEME RETURNS. HOOVES FADE INTO WIND.]
