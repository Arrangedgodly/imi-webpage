# Two Banana Moonlight

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Late-night romance advice radio*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Mira, a gibbon host; Elo, a capuchin caller; Tavi, a ring-tailed lemur separated from her tree-shrew mate; Sol, a macaque sharing a nest with a koala.

[THEME: A SOFT SAXOPHONE, A PHONE RINGS ONCE, THEN IS ANSWERED.]

HOST MIRA: This is Two Banana Moonlight, the call-in show for tangled hearts and untangled headphones. I'm Mira, a gibbon with a very long reach and a short patience for surprise parties. Our first caller says her partner keeps leaving fruit at her windowsill.

CALLER ELO: It's not a gift. It's a warning. We live on the third floor. The fruit arrives on a rope.

MIRA: What kind of fruit?

ELO: A pear on Monday. Two oranges yesterday. Today, a papaya.

MIRA: That is either courtship or a very strange grocery delivery. Have you asked?

ELO: I did. My partner said, "You know." I do not know.

MIRA: Then ask a question that leaves room for an answer. "Are you leaving fruit because you want to share breakfast, or because you think my pantry is empty?"

ELO: My pantry is empty.

MIRA: Ah. The papaya may be practical. Still, the rope is a lot of effort. Does your partner climb?

ELO: They're a tree kangaroo. They climb beautifully. They also hate stairs.

MIRA: Then perhaps the romance is in avoiding the stairs. Tell them you appreciate the fruit. Ask them to knock next time, so you can open the door instead of playing fruit detective.

ELO: I can do that.

[SFX: PHONE CLICK, THEN A RECORDING OF A ROPE SLIDING THROUGH A PULLEY.] 

MIRA: Caller two, you have a problem with distance.

CALLER TAVI: My mate is going away to study tide pools for a month. I said that was fine. Now I'm counting the days on the kitchen wall, and I don't want to make them feel guilty.

MIRA: What do you want while they're gone?

TAVI: A call on Sunday. Maybe a postcard. I don't need constant messages. I need one little sign that I'm still in the same story.

MIRA: That is a clear request. Don't hide it inside a joke about the wall calendar.

TAVI: I already drew a cartoon of myself eating every day square.

MIRA: Keep the cartoon. Add the request out loud. Long-distance affection works better when nobody has to decode a picture of a chewing lemur.

TAVI: I can try. They're a tree shrew. They love details. I can send a drawing of my breakfast.

MIRA: Make sure the drawing includes the actual breakfast.

TAVI: It will. I have a complicated porridge.

[SFX: A SPOON STIRS. MIRA LAUGHS.] 

MIRA: The final caller is Sol, who says he and his partner keep having the same argument about their shared nest.

SOL: They fluff the moss every morning. I flatten it every night. We have done this for months.

MIRA: Why do you flatten it?

SOL: I am a macaque. I sleep better on a firm branch. They are a koala. They need a soft bed.

MIRA: Is there a place for both?

SOL: The old basket in the corner.

MIRA: Can the basket become their spot, and the branch yours?

SOL: The basket is full of old blankets.

MIRA: Then together, clear it. Keep the blanket they both like. A home is allowed to have two sleeping surfaces.

SOL: I thought that meant we were drifting apart.

MIRA: You can share a home without sharing every cushion.

SOL: I'll say that. They might finally stop putting moss on my branch.

MIRA: Or you'll discover the moss is quite comfortable. Try it for one night before filing a complaint.

[SFX: THEME RETURNS. A BELL TINKS.] 

MIRA: On our board tonight: knock before lowering the papaya, make the Sunday call, and let each other sleep where the back feels good. Elo's partner has sent a message. They meant the fruit as breakfast, and they will use the stairs tomorrow. Tavi is drawing porridge for a postcard. Sol is trying the moss basket.

MIRA: Love is a practice. Sometimes it includes rope. Sometimes it includes separate bedding. Good night from Two Banana Moonlight. Keep your ears open, and your requests clear.

ELO: I will ask before the papaya arrives.

TAVI: I'll pack a postcard.

SOL: I'll try the moss.

[SAXOPHONE ENDS ON A WARM NOTE. A PAPAYA LANDS ON A PILLOW.]
