# The Day the Creek Went Quiet

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: field recording nature documentary with overly dramatic narration*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: NARRATOR VELA, a slow loris; FIELD RECORDER KITO, a capuchin; BIOLOGIST SEN, a macaque; and a chorus of frogs.

[SFX: STREAM WATER, MORNING BIRDS. A TAPE RECORDER CLICKS ON.] 

VELA: At dawn, the creek makes its ordinary sound. Water over stone. Frogs in the reeds. A twig, briefly convinced it is a boat.

KITO: I have recorded the twig. I also recorded Sen saying, "Don't record the twig." Both are excellent.

SEN: I said that because you placed the microphone in the creek.

KITO: It was the closest microphone to the sound.

VELA: Yet today, one sound is missing. The frogs have stopped calling.

[SFX: STREAM. NO FROGS.] 

KITO: We should follow the silence.

SEN: We should look first. The frogs are probably under the broad leaves. It rained.

VELA: The mystery deepens beneath a leaf.

SEN: That is not a mystery. It is where frogs sit when it rains.

[LEAVES SHIFT. A LOW CROAK, THEN ANOTHER.] 

KITO: There. Two calls from beneath the leaf.

VELA: A clue emerges. The creek has concealed its witnesses.

SEN: They are frogs, not witnesses.

KITO: For the recording, can you ask them where the chorus went?

SEN: I can ask. They will answer when they are ready.

VELA: The biologist begins a patient inquiry.

[SFX: SEN SETS DOWN A SMALL DISH OF WATER. A PAUSE. FROGS CROAK.] 

KITO: They're back.

SEN: They were here the whole time. They waited for the rain to pass.

VELA: Then the disappearance was not a disappearance but a pause.

KITO: Should I record the explanation?

SEN: You just did.

KITO: I mean the frogs.

SEN: Record them, but don't make them repeat it.

VELA: The chorus rises, unprompted, in a layered call.

[SFX: FROGS IN THREE DISTINCT RHYTHMS. A RECORDER BEEPS.] 

KITO: Battery warning.

VELA: The natural world has a deadline.

SEN: No, your recorder does.

KITO: I brought spare batteries.

[HE OPENS A BAG. ACORNS RATTLE AND ROLL DOWN A ROCK.] 

VELA: Another sound enters the habitat: the rolling acorn.

SEN: Kito, your lunch is moving downstream.

KITO: I'll get it.

SEN: Leave it. The water will carry it to the bank.

VELA: The field team demonstrates restraint. The acorn travels without a camera crew.

KITO: I can still narrate it from here.

SEN: You can also let the creek be a creek.

[THE ACORN CATCHES IN REEDS. THE FROGS CROAK AROUND IT.] 

KITO: It's stopped by the bank.

VELA: The soundscape resolves: water, frogs, and one acorn resting where it belongs.

SEN: That is the whole event.

VELA: Sometimes the rarest discovery is an ordinary afternoon.

KITO: I recorded six minutes of it.

SEN: Did you record the battery warning?

KITO: Yes.

VELA: And so we leave the creek, carrying only the recording, the spare batteries, and fewer acorns than we arrived with.


KITO: I want to capture the difference between rain silence and ordinary silence.

SEN: The microphone cannot tell you why a sound stopped.

KITO: The narration can.

VELA: The recorder listens without judgment. The narrator supplies a theory.

SEN: Then the theory should be tested. Let's move a little farther from the stream and wait.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS ON WET LEAVES. THE STREAM SOFTENS. A FROG CALLS ONCE, THEN PAUSES.]

KITO: One frog. No chorus.

SEN: It may be calling to another frog. We should not answer over it.

VELA: The field team practices silence, an action rarely celebrated on this program.

[LONG PAUSE. A SECOND FROG RESPONDS FROM THE REEDS. THEN A THIRD.] 

KITO: The chorus is building from the edges.

SEN: It often starts with one call. The others join when conditions suit them.

VELA: Not every silence is a warning. Some are space for a response.

KITO: I can use that as the episode title.

SEN: You can use it as a sentence. Don't make the frogs carry it.

KITO: We have a second sound. Something is splashing near the bank.

[SMALL SPLASH. KITO RUSTLES TOWARD IT.] 

SEN: Stay on the path. We can hear it without stepping into the reeds.

KITO: It sounds like water.

SEN: It is water. The stream is still moving.

VELA: A revelation with no musical sting.

[SFX: THE ACORN ROLLS AGAINST A STONE, THEN SETTLES.]

KITO: I thought that was a fish.

SEN: The creek has offered you a lesson in scale.

KITO: I will leave the acorn where the current set it.

SEN: Good. The birds may collect it later.

VELA: And the team leaves behind both the acorn and the temptation to explain it twice.
[SFX: STREAM AND FROGS. TAPE CLICKS OFF BEFORE KITO CAN SAY GOODBYE.]
