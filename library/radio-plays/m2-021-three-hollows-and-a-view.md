# Three Hollows and a View

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Real estate tour podcasts and luxury property listings*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO*

Cast: Tour host Fia, a langur real estate agent; Toma, a squirrel monkey renter; Mr. Root, a tortoise inspector; builder Kesh, a macaque carpenter.

[INTRO: ELEVATOR MUSIC, THEN A DOOR CREAKS OPEN.] 

HOST FIA: Welcome to Three Hollows and a View, the audio tour for homes with character and one too many branches. Today we're at Elder Oak, a three-level treehouse with panoramic creek sounds, private moss storage, and an open-concept trunk.

TOMA: The trunk has a hole in the back.

FIA: We call that the nature window.

TOMA: It is a hole where the rain gets in.

FIA: That's why the listing says seasonal water feature.

[SFX: DRIP, DRIP, BUCKET CLINK.] 

FIA: At ground level, the kitchen nook has room for one berry basket and a very close friend. The counter is carved from reclaimed branch. The stove is two warm stones and a clay pot, included with the sale.

TOMA: Is the stove safe?

MR. ROOT: The stones are stable. The moss mat beneath them is not. A cooking fire needs a clear ring and a sand tray.

FIA: The sand tray is available as an optional upgrade.

TOMA: I am not buying a home that sells fire safety separately.

FIA: Good note. We'll add it to the walkthrough.

[FOOTSTEPS CLIMB A LADDER. WOOD GROANS.] 

FIA: The sleeping hollow offers a dramatic view of the creek. From this level, you can hear fish jump at dawn.

TOMA: And hear the neighbors argue about whose branch crosses the window.

KESH: That's my branch. It is carrying the roof brace.

TOMA: The brace crosses my sleeping space.

KESH: I can move it to the outer trunk, but I need to add a second support. The roots below are shallow on that side.

MR. ROOT: I measured them. The safest place is two arm-lengths east. It will narrow the balcony, but the tree can take the load.

FIA: A charming, intimate balcony.

TOMA: A balcony that fits one cup and not the grill.

FIA: The grill is not part of the home.

TOMA: It's part of my life.

[SFX: A BRANCH FLEXES, THEN SETTLES.] 

FIA: The third hollow is currently advertised as an office. It has no outlet, but the canopy light is excellent. Mr. Root, is this space dry?

MR. ROOT: No. The roof leaks when the wind comes from the south. The bark is sound, though. Kesh can cap the seam before the next rain.

KESH: I can do that if the owner agrees to leave the outer branch alone. A family of swifts nests there.

TOMA: I don't need the view more than they need the nest.

FIA: That's a rare answer on a property tour. The lower hollow can be a workbench instead. Kesh can add a shelf for Toma's bowls. The rent will include the sand tray, the roof cap, and a quiet agreement about the swifts.

TOMA: And the neighbor branch?

KESH: I'll move the brace tomorrow. It will take a day.

TOMA: I'll rent it if the repair happens before I move in.

FIA: That's a clear offer. The owner agrees. We have a listener asking whether the creek is included.

MR. ROOT: No. It is shared by everyone downstream.

TOMA: I wouldn't know where to put it anyway.

FIA: Elder Oak is rented, with its leak repaired, its swifts undisturbed, and its cooking stones properly spaced. The open-concept trunk remains open, but now the rain goes outside.

[SFX: HAMMER TAPS, THEN A DRY ROOM TONE. BIRDS SETTLE.] 

FIA: This has been Three Hollows and a View. Remember, a good listing describes the home you can live in, not the one the microphone wants you to imagine.

TOMA: Will the listing include the swifts?

FIA: We can call them quiet neighbors.

KESH: They sing at dawn.

TOMA: That's quieter than my alarm.

MR. ROOT: The alarm is not included.

FIA: The bird call is complimentary.

[MUSIC OUT.]
