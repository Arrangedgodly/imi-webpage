# The Quiz with No Wrong Answers

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio quiz shows, suspenseful answer reveals, and hosts who stretch simple questions into cliffhangers*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
NELL, a macaque quiz host
BASI, a gibbon contestant
PAX, a capuchin scorekeeper

[QUIZ THEME: FANFARE, THEN A TICKING CLOCK.]

NELL: Welcome to The Quiz with No Wrong Answers, where the prize is a golden fruit bowl and the questions are carefully designed to make you doubt yourself. Basi, are you ready?

BASI: I have been ready since the audience applause ended.

NELL: First question. How many wheels are on a standard bicycle?

BASI: Two.

NELL: A confident answer. Pax, what does the official answer card say?

PAX: Two.

NELL: The official answer is two, but what if the bicycle has training wheels? We must account for the possibility of a third and fourth wheel. Basi, would you like to revise?

BASI: No. A standard bicycle has two.

NELL: The judges accept the answer. One point. Our contestant has survived the opening question.

[TICKING CLOCK CONTINUES.]

NELL: Question two: name an animal that can fly.

BASI: A parrot.

NELL: A parrot can fly, but can it fly in a studio? Pax?

PAX: We have a parrot in the booth.

[ONE WING FLAP.]

PAX: It is standing on the microphone.

NELL: The evidence is mixed. Does the animal need to be airborne for the answer to count?

BASI: No. The question asks for an animal that can fly.

NELL: Correct. Two points. The parrot has also knocked the scorecard down.

[CARDS FALL.]

PAX: I can't see the scores.

NELL: The mystery deepens. Basi, you may now wager both points on our final question: which is heavier, a kilogram of feathers or a kilogram of stones?

BASI: They weigh the same.

NELL: The traditional answer. However, feathers fill more space, and stones take up less room. Which is heavier to carry across a studio?

BASI: That depends how they're packed.

NELL: A nuanced response. Pax, can you retrieve the props?

PAX: The feathers are in a large pillow. The stones are in a bucket. The pillow is lighter.

NELL: Both are one kilogram. We will conduct a live test.

[BUCKET LIFTED. PILLOW FLUFFS. A THUD.]

PAX: The bucket weighs more because it includes the bucket.

NELL: Important distinction. The question asked about the contents, not the containers. Basi is correct and wins the golden fruit bowl.

BASI: Is the bowl golden?

NELL: It is painted gold. It contains three plastic fruit pieces.

BASI: Do the pieces count as fruit?

NELL: The prize committee says yes, and the quiz has no wrong answers.

BASI: Then I'll take it.

[APPLAUSE.]

NELL: The quiz is complete, but the golden bowl has a hidden feature: it doubles as our tie-breaker podium. Basi, would you like to stand on it?

BASI: It is a bowl. I'll put the plastic fruit in it.

PAX: The scorecard says the prize is one bowl, not a podium.

NELL: Then Basi has won by correctly identifying the bowl. That is our bonus question.

BASI: Do I get another prize?

NELL: You get to choose the closing music.

BASI: Please play something without a ticking clock.

[THE CLOCK STOPS. THE AUDIENCE CHEERS.]

NELL: A bold choice. Before we close, our lightning round. Basi, what is the sound of one hand clapping?

BASI: A hand hitting a thigh.

[SLAP.]

PAX: That's correct.

NELL: But the hand also made a sound when it hit the thigh. We may have discovered a second clap.

BASI: I'm leaving with my bowl.

NELL: A bold final answer. The winner exits with the prize and both points. Pax, tally the score.

PAX: Two points, one bowl, three plastic fruit pieces.

NELL: And no wrong answers. Join us next time for the question "Is this microphone on?"

[MIC FEEDBACK SHRIEKS.]

PAX: It is now.

[THEME MUSIC.]

NELL: We accept that answer.
