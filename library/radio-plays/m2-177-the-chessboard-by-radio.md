# The Chessboard by Radio

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: live chess commentary, dramatic move announcements, and analysts who inflate a quiet match into a thunderous showdown*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-006*

CAST
ANALYST REN, a ring-tailed lemur
CALLER TOR, a gibbon tournament director
PLAYER MIM, a macaque competitor

[LOW TENSION MUSIC. CLOCK TICKS.]

REN: Welcome to the Kettle City open chess final. The board is quiet, the audience is quieter, and Mim has just moved a pawn from e2 to e4.

TOR: A confident opening.

REN: The pawn advances into the center, announcing that the game has begun and that there is now one fewer pawn at home.

MIM: That is generally how pawns work.

REN: The opponent responds with c5. The Sicilian Defense. A dramatic counterstroke from the left flank.

TOR: The pieces are still on the board.

REN: Yet the psychological territory has shifted.

[CLOCK TICKS. A CHAIR CREAKS.]

REN: Mim is looking at the queen. The queen is looking nowhere, as she is carved from wood.

MIM: I'm calculating.

TOR: The clock is running.

REN: An invisible battle between patience and the ticking mechanism.

MIM: I can hear the clock from here.

REN: Can you hear the pieces?

MIM: No.

REN: The audience is holding its breath. One cough in the hall could decide the match.

[AUDIENCE MEMBER SNEEZES.]

REN: A sneeze! The players remain composed. Mim moves her knight to f3.

TOR: That develops a piece.

REN: It also brings the knight closer to the board's center, where every square has a story.

TOR: The knight is on f3. That's its story for now.

REN: The opponent moves a bishop. We have reached a position of delicate balance.

MIM: The bishop is pinned.

REN: The bishop cannot move without exposing the queen. We have discovered a trap.

TOR: It is a common pin. The opponent can still respond.

REN: And respond they do: the queen shifts to d6. The tension grows. The clock ticks louder.

[CLOCK TICKS LOUDER IN MIX.]

MIM: Could you turn the clock down in the broadcast?

REN: We cannot alter the sound of competition.

TOR: It is a normal clock. I can move the mic away from it.

[THE TICKING SOFTENS.]

REN: The room is calmer. Mim makes a move with her rook, a quiet exchange that gives her a clear path down the file.

MIM: Check.

[SMALL BELL.]

REN: The king is in check. The opponent has three legal responses: move the king, block the check, or capture the attacking piece.

TOR: They block with the bishop.

REN: The position remains alive. Mim studies the board, then captures the bishop with her knight.

REN: We have one more development before the end of the match. Mim has offered a draw.

TOR: Her opponent declined. Both players are playing to win.

REN: The audience leans forward. No one moves. The clock advances, the pieces remain still, and the tension has nowhere to go.

MIM: I was waiting for the clock to be adjusted. It was ticking too loudly in the broadcast.

TOR: The clock is now at a normal volume. The match can continue.

REN: The game returns to the board, where a rook and a knight coordinate to close the escape squares. The opponent has no safe reply.

MIM: Checkmate.

TOR: Confirmed. The game is complete.

[APPLAUSE.]

REN: A thrilling conclusion after forty-seven moves and one sneeze. The clock is stopped, the pieces are reset, and the players shake hands.

MIM: Thank you. I'd like to play again, but without commentary during the opening.

REN: Our analysis will continue after a short break.

TOR: We can use the break to reset the board.

REN: First, let us reflect on the pawn's emotional journey.

MIM: It moved six squares and got captured.

REN: A compact arc. This is chess by radio, where silence is meaningful and every square gets a microphone.

[THEME MUSIC. CLOCK TICKS ONCE, THEN STOPS.]
