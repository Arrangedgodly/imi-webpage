# The Bird Call Has Subtitles

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: nature documentaries that confidently narrate animal motives*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-017*

Cast: Narrator Vex, an orangutan; naturalist Mara, a macaque; recorder Odo, a gibbon; listener Pip, a capuchin; translator Seli, a lemur.

SFX: Woodland microphone. A bird calls from a branch.

VEX: The bird is demanding breakfast.

MARA: We don't know that.

SELI: I can translate the call as "I am here."

PIP: It called twice.

SELI: "I am still here."

ODO: The recorder picked up wing movement.

VEX: The bird is preparing to launch a dramatic attack.

MARA: It is shifting on the branch.

SFX: Leaves rustle. The bird calls again.

PIP: It moved to another branch.

VEX: A tactical retreat.

SELI: Or a short hop.

MARA: The call could be a contact sound. We should watch what happens next.

ODO: A second bird answered.

VEX: A rival has entered the scene.

SFX: Two calls overlap.

SELI: One says "I am here." The other says "I hear you."

PIP: They sound like they are having a conversation.

VEX: Could they be negotiating territory?

MARA: We can say they are calling back and forth. We cannot know their private meaning.

ODO: The first bird has found a berry.

VEX: It rewards itself for the successful negotiation.

MARA: It is eating.

SFX: Beak taps fruit. Wings flutter.

PIP: The second bird took another berry.

SELI: They are sharing the tree.

VEX: A story of cooperation.

MARA: We don't know whether they planned it. We can describe what we hear and see.

ODO: The microphone is clipping. I need to lower the level.

ODO: The recorder can show the call's shape without pretending to translate it.

MARA: Play the first call and the reply, then leave room tone.

SFX: Playback: two calls, a pause, one answering call. Leaves rustle.

PIP: There is a pause after the second call.

SELI: Yes. The bird stops when the other answers.

VEX: Could the pause mean it is waiting for a reply?

MARA: It might. We can describe the timing, not assign a sentence to it.

ODO: The waveform is cleaner when the windscreen faces the branch.

SFX: Stand rotates. Wind noise drops; bird call sharpens.

PIP: Now I can hear a softer call underneath.

SELI: A second bird is farther away.

VEX: A hidden character enters the story.

MARA: Or an animal we didn't notice at first. We can say that plainly.

ODO: The light is fading. We should pack the microphone before the birds leave the grove.

PIP: I'll hold the stand while you fold the legs.

SFX: Latches click; tripod closes.

VEX: Can the last subtitle read "Good night"?

SELI: Only if we label it as our sign-off, not the bird's words.

VEX: Fine. The field crew says good night.

SFX: A dial turns. Bird call comes through clear.

SELI: No invented translation needed.

VEX: But the subtitles are ready.

PIP: Put "bird calls" on the screen.

MARA: That's accurate.

TAG

SFX: A third call from farther away.

MARA: We have enough sound for a short field segment: call, reply, wing movement, and feeding.

ODO: I'll label each recording with the time and branch.

SELI: The subtitle card can show "nearby bird answers" instead of a sentence.

PIP: That tells me what happened without deciding what the bird meant.

VEX: The narration will be less colorful.

MARA: The sounds are colorful. We can let the listener notice them.

SFX: Paper card slides into a sleeve.

ODO: Wind is picking up. The stand is secure, but we should take it down before the gusts shake the microphone.

PIP: I'll carry the recorder case.

SELI: Then we have the call safely recorded, with no fictional translation attached.

VEX: The sequel has arrived.

SELI: It may be the same bird.
