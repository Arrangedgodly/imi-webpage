# Goodnight, Little Listener

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: children bedtime radio program that cannot agree on a soothing story length*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST ARA, a gibbon; STORYTELLER KIP, a capuchin; PRODUCER VEM, a macaque; and a sleepy young listener.

[SFX: LULLABY THEME, SOFT PAGE TURN.] 

ARA: Welcome to Goodnight, Little Listener. Tonight's story should be short, soft, and complete before the moon reaches the top of the window.

KIP: I brought a story about a monkey who climbs a mountain, crosses a river, meets a bear, finds a cave, and discovers a city under the roots.

VEM: That is six stories.

KIP: They are all in one book.

ARA: The listener is already yawning. Can you begin with one small event?

KIP: A monkey finds a smooth stone.

VEM: What does the monkey do with it?

KIP: He carries it up the mountain.

ARA: Why?

KIP: He does not know yet.

ARA: Does he need to carry it up a mountain tonight?

KIP: The stone is heavy.

VEM: Then the monkey can set it down.

KIP: That changes the story.

ARA: It also gives the monkey both hands back.

[SFX: STONE SET DOWN ON SOFT MOSS.] 

KIP: The monkey rests beside the stone. A cricket chirps nearby.

ARA: Better. Does the monkey need to meet the bear?

KIP: The bear is waiting at the cave.

VEM: The monkey is by the stone.

KIP: The bear hears the cricket and walks over quietly.

VEM: Now two animals are resting.

KIP: The bear asks whether the monkey is cold.

ARA: What does the monkey say?

KIP: He says the moss is soft and the night is warm.

VEM: That's the whole story?

KIP: The bear listens to the cricket. The monkey listens too.

[SFX: CRICKET CHIRPS ONCE. A SOFT BREATH, THEN A PAUSE.] 

VEM: We have time for a little more. The book promises a city under the roots.

ARA: The listener is settling in. If we add a city, there will be gates, streets, and someone who needs directions.

KIP: The city can wait until morning.

VEM: The audience might expect a reveal.

ARA: We can reveal that the monkey is warm and the stone is safe.

VEM: Is that enough suspense?

KIP: The monkey is wondering whether to pick up the stone.

ARA: Does he need it now?

KIP: No. It can stay on the moss until morning.

[SFX: SOFT WIND THROUGH GRASS. THE STONE DOES NOT MOVE.] 

VEM: I would like to add a dramatic drum roll.

ARA: A drum roll would wake the listener.

VEM: A tiny drum roll?

KIP: The cricket can keep time.

VEM: The cricket is already keeping time.

ARA: We can leave the sound as it is.

[CRICKET CHIRPS TWO STEADY NOTES, THEN PAUSES.] 

KIP: The bear rests. The monkey rests. The stone rests beside them.

VEM: And the city?

KIP: It will still be under the roots tomorrow.

VEM: The sponsor has sent us a nightlight shaped like a bright yellow moon.

ARA: Is it dim?

VEM: It is very bright.

KIP: The monkey could use it to find the stone.

ARA: The monkey can see the stone in the morning.

VEM: The sponsor asked us to mention that the light has three settings.

ARA: We can mention it after the story, with the studio lamps low.

VEM: I will put it in the drawer.

[SFX: DRAWER OPENS. A SMALL LAMP CLICKS ON, THEN IS QUICKLY CLOSED.] 

KIP: Even the drawer is awake now.

ARA: We will let it sleep too.

ARA: The moon is at the top of the window. We have reached the ending on time.

VEM: The listener is asleep.

ARA: Then we can say goodnight without adding another chapter.

KIP: Goodnight, little listener. The stone is safe, the bear is sitting, and the mountain can wait.

[SFX: LULLABY THEME. CRICKET CHIRPS TWICE, THEN QUIETS.]
