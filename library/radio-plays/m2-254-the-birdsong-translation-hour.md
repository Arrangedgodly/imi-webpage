# The Birdsong Translation Hour

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: nature radio translation segments, confident animal-language interpretation, and hosts who assign complete thoughts to brief calls*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-009*

CAST
HOST OMI, a gibbon
TRANSLATOR VEE, a capuchin
FIELD NATURALIST KOR, a macaque
CALLER PALLA, a ring-tailed lemur

[SFX: DAWN CHORUS, A SINGLE BIRD CALL.]

OMI: Welcome to The Birdsong Translation Hour. Vee will interpret today's call from a bird on the studio roof.

VEE: The bird says, "I am glad you are awake. The morning is full of possibility."

KOR: It made one short chirp.

VEE: A concise speaker can still have a rich inner life.

OMI: We will hear another call.

[SFX: TWO SHARP CHIRPS.]

VEE: "You have not refilled the seed tray. I remember last Tuesday."

KOR: That bird has never visited the seed tray.

VEE: Then it has a very good memory from a distance.

OMI: We have a field report from Kor, who is standing beneath the roof.

KOR: The bird just flew to the gutter.

[SFX: WINGS FLUTTER, WATER DRIPS.]

VEE: "I have moved to a wetter neighborhood."

KOR: It is avoiding us.

VEE: Avoidance is also a form of communication.

OMI: A listener has called. Palla, what did you hear?

PALLA: A chirp followed by a scratch.

VEE: "I have an urgent announcement, followed by a correction."

PALLA: I thought it sounded like a bird on a loose tile.

KOR: That is exactly where it is.

[SFX: TILE TAPS IN WIND.]

OMI: We have a second voice in the conversation.

VEE: The tile says, "I will not be rushed." 

KOR: The tile is not alive.

VEE: Then it is unusually calm.

OMI: Our next segment is Listener Translation. Palla, would you like to try?

[SFX: BIRD CALL, THEN A LONGER TRILL.]

PALLA: It might be saying, "Keep off my roof."

VEE: That is a very direct translation.

PALLA: It calls every time we come outside.

KOR: It may be territorial.

VEE: Or it may be reviewing the program.

OMI: How would we know which?

KOR: We can step back and see if it stops calling.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS RETREAT. BIRD CALLS AGAIN.]

VEE: It says, "The hosts have retreated, but the broadcast continues."

PALLA: Or it is calling to another bird.

OMI: Can we ask it a question?

KOR: We should not imitate its call too closely.

VEE: I can offer a respectful greeting.

[SFX: VEE MAKES A SOFT, SINGLE CHIRP.]

[SFX: BIRD REPLIES WITH THREE FAST CHIRPS.]

VEE: "Please stop doing that."

KOR: You may have startled it.

OMI: We will not translate the bird's private reply without permission.

VEE: I am happy to translate only the public call.

PALLA: It has flown to the tree.

KOR: The gutter is dripping on the seed tray. The tray is empty now.

OMI: Could the bird be asking for seed after all?

KOR: It may be looking for a dry place. Let's hang the tray beneath the eave, out of the drip.

[SFX: TRAY HANGS ON HOOK. DRIP STOPS HITTING METAL.]

VEE: The bird gives one call.

OMI: What does it say?

VEE: It says nothing I can responsibly translate.

PALLA: That seems like progress.

KOR: It is eating.

[SFX: SEED TRAY RATTLES. BIRD CHIRPS ONCE.]

VEE: I can say the tray is being used. Anything beyond that is a guess.

OMI: The bird has delivered no review of our program.

VEE: It may be waiting until it has heard the whole hour.

KOR: It is gone.

VEE: Then the review is pending.

OMI: Then our feature has reached a conclusion. A chirp is a chirp until evidence gives us more.

VEE: The bird may have its own words for us.

KOR: It just flew away.

OMI: And we will let it have the last word.

[SFX: DISTANT BIRD CALL. STUDIO MIC FADES OUT.]
