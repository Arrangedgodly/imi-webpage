# The Station ID Contest

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio station identification spots, branding debates, and announcers who try to fit the entire town into a ten-second name*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-010*

CAST
HOST KITO, a macaque
ANNOUNCER LUMA, a gibbon
ENGINEER ODO, a baboon
MANAGER PIP, a lemur
CALLER TAVI, a capuchin

[SFX: STATION BEEP, RECORDING LIGHT.]

KITO: The station needs a new ten-second identification. Manager Pip wants the town's full name, region, and slogan included.

PIP: "Canopy Community Radio, serving the eastern ridge, river plain, old orchard, and new market, bringing every voice to every home."

LUMA: That takes eleven seconds.

ODO: It takes sixteen when spoken clearly.

PIP: We can speak faster.

[SFX: FAST PLAYBACK, WORDS BLUR.]

KITO: Listeners heard "radio, ridge, market, home."

TAVI (PHONE): I thought you were selling a house.

PIP: We need the whole service area.

LUMA: The station name is enough to identify us.

PIP: The slogan is part of the identity.

ODO: The signal tone already identifies the station.

[SFX: TWO-NOTE BEEP.]

TAVI: That sounds like the elevator.

KITO: The elevator is not a radio station.

PIP: We can add a third note.

[SFX: THREE-NOTE BEEP.]

ODO: That sounds like a doorbell.

LUMA: We need spoken words.

Pip asks each announcer to record a version. Luma gives the station name and call sign in a clear voice. Odo adds the region. Kito adds the slogan. The edits overlap, and the final file plays two voices at once.

[SFX: LAYERED VOICES.]

TAVI: The station is arguing with itself.

PIP: We need one voice.

LUMA: We need one sentence.

KITO: And it has to fit in ten seconds.

Odo uses a stopwatch. Luma records: "Canopy Radio, your local voice on the ridge." It lasts six seconds. Pip says it omits the river plain and the market. Tavi calls from the river and says the signal reaches there.

TAVI: I heard you clearly.

PIP: Then perhaps the signal area does not belong in the station ID.

ODO: We can put the details in the program guide.

PIP: The slogan?

LUMA: "Your local voice" is the slogan.

KITO: The station name, call sign, and slogan fit.

PIP: What about "bringing every voice to every home"?

TAVI: My home has a radio. It does not need a second invitation.

Odo records this candidate at normal speed for the next timing test.
 
Pip asks whether the call sign should be spoken before the station name. Luma reads both orders into the recorder. The first version sounds like an emergency code; the second is clear.

[SFX: TWO TEST TAKES.]

LUMA: "CR on Canopy Radio."

PIP: That sounds like a repair notice.

LUMA: "Canopy Radio, CR-8."

TAVI: That sounds like the radio's serial number.

KITO: Listeners need one name, not every label on the transmitter.

ODO: The call sign is a legal requirement.

PIP: Then put it in the station ID.

They shorten the line and time it again. The signal tone, name, slogan, and call sign take eight seconds. Odo leaves the final two seconds empty rather than adding the region list.

TAVI: We can use the gap for the next program.

PIP: No. The ID ends there.

LUMA: The silence is not a second announcement.

KITO: The meter says eight seconds.

The manager signs off on the concise version, and Odo locks the approved recording so no one can append the town directory.
 The station beep plays once, followed by Luma's clear announcement.

[SFX: TWO-NOTE BEEP.]

LUMA: Canopy Radio, CR-8, your local voice on the ridge.

[SFX: RECORDING STOPS.]

KITO: Eight seconds.

PIP: We have two seconds left.

ODO: We do not have to fill them.

PIP: The silence is available?

LUMA: The identification is complete.

Tavi requests that the next show announce the town meeting. Pip begins reciting the full location into a second microphone. Odo points to its PROGRAM MIC label and checks that it is still muted.

PIP: This microphone has not started.

KITO: We have met the ten-second limit.

PIP: Can the station ID say that?

ODO: It has no time to tell you.

[SFX: MIC CLICKS OFF, CLEAN STATION BEEP.]
