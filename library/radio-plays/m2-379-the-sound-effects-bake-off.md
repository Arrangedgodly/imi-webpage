# The Sound-Effects Bake-Off

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: radio production contests that judge dramatic effects while performers secretly record the sounds on location*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-013*

Cast: Producer Kiva, a capuchin; host Ulo, a gibbon; contestants Sera, a macaque, and Moku, a lemur; judge Piri, a langur.

[SFX: STUDIO. TWO BUTTONS BEEP. HOST MIC OPENS.]

ULO: Welcome to the Sound-Effects Bake-Off. Contestants will create a thunderstorm using only studio props.

KIVA: The contest rules say no recorded sounds.

SERA: I brought a metal tray and dried peas.

MOKU: I brought a water bowl and a brush.

PIRI: I will judge whether each sound resembles rain and thunder.

[SFX: SERA RATTLES PEAS ON TRAY. A LOW ROLL FOLLOWS.]

ULO: A convincing storm.

KIVA: That low roll came from the ventilation fan. It is not Sera's effect.

SERA: I haven't started the tray yet.

MOKU: The fan is humming in B flat.

PIRI: We should turn it off before the next round.

[SFX: FAN SWITCH CLICKS. HUM STOPS.]

ULO: Now, contestants, begin.

[SFX: SERA SHAKES PEAS ON TRAY. MOKU BRUSHES WATER ACROSS BOWL.]

PIRI: Sera has rain on a roof. Moku has rain in a puddle.

ULO: Which wins?

KIVA: The sponsor wants a third contestant, a pre-recorded thunderclap.

PIRI: The rules say the contestants make the effect in the studio.

ULO: We can use the thunderclap as a comparison after the live round.

SERA: Then label it as a recording, not a contestant.

MOKU: I can make a small rain sound with the bowl while the recorded thunder plays.

PIRI: Only after I score the live effects. I need to judge each sound on its own.

[SFX: SCORECARD PAGES TURN. THE RECORDED THUNDER BUTTON REMAINS COVERED.]

KIVA: The recording can wait until after the results.

PIRI: I need to hear the sound without both playing at once.

KIVA: The show clock says we have thirty seconds.

SERA: The sound can wait for the judge.

MOKU: I can do a single drop on the bowl.

[SFX: ONE DROP TAPS METAL BOWL.]

PIRI: That sounds like a drip from a pipe. It is distinct from the brush rain.

ULO: The audience wants a dramatic thunderclap.

KIVA: The rules require props. No bass effect file.

SERA: I can tap the underside of the tray with a padded mallet.

[SFX: LOW THUMP. PEAS JUMP ON THE TRAY.]

MOKU: The peas moved. That adds a second sound.

PIRI: The first effect was rain; this one is thunder. We can judge both separately.

ULO: The contest card gives each contestant one overall score.

KIVA: The card has boxes for clarity, variety and control.

PIRI: Sera's tray is clear and controlled. Moku's brush is quiet, but the single drop had a good pipe note.

MOKU: I want to change my score. I used a brush on the bowl, not a brush in water.

PIRI: That's why it sounded like cloth, not rain.

ULO: We can let him try once more.

[SFX: BRUSH DIPS INTO WATER. WATER SWEEPS THE BOWL'S RIM.]

PIRI: Better. The bowl carries a wash, then one drop marks the end.

KIVA: That is one effect made from live props.

SERA: I can combine my peas and tray, but not the ventilation fan.

[SFX: PEAS RATTLE. PADDED MALLET THUMPS ONCE. THE SOUND FALLS SILENT.]

PIRI: That is a complete storm cue.

ULO: Sera wins the contest.

MOKU: What about my rain?

PIRI: You win best single drop. The rules do not require one winner for every sound.

KIVA: The scoreboard can show both labels.

[SFX: TWO BUTTONS BEEP. A PAPER SCORECARD TEARS ALONG A PERFORATION.]

ULO: We have learned that thunder sounds better when the fan is off.

KIVA: We have learned to turn the fan off before judging.

[SFX: FAN SWITCH STAYS OFF. MIC CLOSES; ONE DROP FALLS INTO THE BOWL.]
