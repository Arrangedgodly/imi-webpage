# The Oral History of a Very Short Walk

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: public radio oral history interviews and reverent archival narration*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-003*

Cast: HOST ELA, a lemur; ELDER TOMA, a macaque; ARCHIVIST PAX, a gibbon; and a young listener.

[SFX: TAPE REEL TURNS, A SMALL BELL.] 

ELA: Today we preserve the history of the journey from the old fig tree to the spring. Elder Toma, how long did it take?

TOMA: About three minutes.

PAX: Our archive has listed it as a major migration.

TOMA: I walked to get a drink.

ELA: What do you remember?

TOMA: The ground was damp. A leaf stuck to my foot. I stopped and removed it.

PAX: The tape label says "Crossing of the Wet Valley."

TOMA: It was a patch of mud.

ELA: Did anything change you on the journey?

TOMA: I saw a beetle on the path. I moved around it.

PAX: We can describe the beetle as a guardian of the route.

TOMA: It was eating a leaf.

ELA: Let's listen to the recording.

[SFX: ARCHIVE TAPE. FOOTSTEPS, A LEAF PEELS FROM A FOOT, THEN A SMALL STEP AROUND SOMETHING.] 

ELA: That sound is vivid.

TOMA: It sounds like walking.

PAX: It is a rare document of daily life.

TOMA: I did it again the next morning.

PAX: We have not found that tape.

TOMA: It sounded the same.

PAX: We should ask what the route meant to Toma, not just what happened on it.

TOMA: I had been sitting by the nest all morning. The walk was a reason to get up.

ELA: Was the leaf under your foot part of the story?

TOMA: It stuck because I stepped on it. I put it back by the tree.

PAX: Did you intend to return it there?

TOMA: It belonged on the ground. I did not need it.

ELA: The archive has a file for ordinary journeys. It is called Daily Paths.

PAX: That file is empty.

TOMA: Then use this recording. Everyone walks to water.

PAX: We usually archive migrations, ceremonies, and storms.

TOMA: A short walk can still have a reason.

YOUNG LISTENER: Can I record my path to the spring tomorrow?

ELA: You can. Tell us where you started and why you went.

TOMA: And record the way back too. The return tells you what changed.

PAX: We will label the recording with the weather and the route, not call it a crossing.

ELA: The archive has a new category: paths people use every day.

TOMA: That's a useful history. Someone may need the spring later.

PAX: We can include the sound of water at the end, so a listener knows they arrived.

ELA: Our younger listener has a question.

YOUNG LISTENER: Did you know the spring would be there?

TOMA: Yes. It was where the spring always was.

YOUNG LISTENER: Then why did you go?

TOMA: I was thirsty.

PAX: That is an important motivation.

ELA: Can we hear the rest of the journey?

[SFX: ARCHIVE TAPE. FOOTSTEPS STOP. WATER RUNS. A CUP FILLS.] 

TOMA: I drank, then walked back.

ELA: Was the return different?

TOMA: I was less thirsty.

PAX: The archive can preserve the same route from both directions.

TOMA: Then label one "out" and one "back."

ELA: We have uncovered a structure: leaving, arriving, and returning.

TOMA: That's how walking works.

PAX: The archive should keep that line with the recording.

ELA: We will also save the path name, the time, and the weather. A future listener may walk the same route.

TOMA: They may hear a different bird and find the mud has dried.

PAX: Then we should not label our recording as the only version.

ELA: It is one morning, preserved as one morning.

ELA: The journey may be short, but it contains weather, a beetle, a leaf, and a reason to leave the nest.

TOMA: It contains a drink of water.

[SFX: TAPE REEL SLOWS. THE BELL RINGS ONCE.]
