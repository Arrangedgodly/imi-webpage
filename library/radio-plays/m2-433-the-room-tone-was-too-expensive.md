# The Room Tone Was Too Expensive

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: film and podcast sound production, luxury room-tone libraries, and producers who treat silence as a premium asset*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-015*

Logline: A sound studio spends its entire budget licensing silence, then discovers its own room tone is already recorded in the hallway.

Cast: Producer Kema, a macaque; mixer Odi, a gibbon; director Fera, an orangutan; actor Luko, a capuchin; licensing agent Boro, a baboon.

[SFX: DIGITAL PLAYBACK OF SILENCE. A PRICE CHIME.]

KEMA: We have purchased twelve seconds of premium room tone.

ODI: It contains a faint refrigerator hum.

FERA: Can we remove the hum?

BORO: That requires the deluxe silent room license.

LUKO: I am standing in a quiet hallway.

ACT ONE

The production is making an audio drama set in a library at dawn. The script has a pause while a character waits for a page to turn. Fera wants a perfectly quiet background. Boro offers a bundle: silent room, gentle air, and one optional distant clock. Kema approves the smallest license and discovers it costs more than the actor's recording session.

ODI: We have twelve seconds of licensed silence. The scene needs twenty.

KEMA: Can we loop it?

ODI: The refrigerator hum will repeat every twelve seconds.

FERA: Perhaps the library has a refrigerator.

LUKO: It is a library, not a break room.

Boro says the studio must not publish unlicensed room tone. Odi asks what happens if the recording includes the hallway. Boro says hallway tone is available under the corridor package. Kema asks whether the corridor package includes footsteps. Boro says footsteps cost extra.

ACT TWO

Luko performs the scene. He waits, turns a page, then speaks. The silence between lines includes a breath and a faint HVAC sound. Fera says it feels alive. Boro says the sound cannot be used until licensed. Odi plays the twelve-second premium clip under it. The refrigerator hum rises exactly as Luko turns the page.

LUKO: The library refrigerator is opening.

FERA: The library has no refrigerator.

BORO: The license is not a guarantee of setting accuracy.

Kema asks if they can record the studio after closing. Odi mutes the control room. A distant elevator bell sounds. Boro asks whether that is part of the building or the recording. Odi says it belongs to the building. Boro checks his catalog; elevator bells require a separate urban interior license.

KEMA: We cannot afford to record the sound of our own hallway.

BORO: The hallway is already recorded in our library.

ACT TWO-B

Luko walks to the hallway and holds a microphone still. The building ventilation makes a low, steady hush. Odi records it for twenty seconds. There is no refrigerator hum, bell or speech. Fera listens and says it sounds like a quiet building at dawn. Boro checks the license list and finds the studio's own sound covered by the production's room-use agreement.

BORO: You own the recording. You do not own the silence.

ODI: We are not selling it. We are using it in our own scene.

BORO: That is covered.

Kema points out that the premium tone has a visible licensing cost, while their hallway tone has none. Boro says the package contains a beautifully documented source. Luko asks if they can document their own source by writing "studio hallway, after closing" on the recording sheet. Odi does so. Fera asks whether the new tone is completely silent.

ODI: No. It has air and a tiny floor creak.

FERA: Keep the air. Remove the creak only if it distracts.

Luko walks once more, carefully. The floor creak returns at the same spot. Odi places a marker there. The take uses the hush without the step. The room sounds quiet, not artificially empty.

ACT THREE

The final scene plays. Luko waits in the library, turns one page, and speaks. The hallway hush sits beneath the pause. The refrigerator hum is gone. Fera approves the mix. Boro removes the premium silent-room clip from the session and refunds the unused license. Kema saves the hallway tone with its source note, then locks the budget sheet.

KEMA: We have spent less on silence than on the page turn.

ODI: The page turn was recorded once.

LUKO: The page had no licensing fee.

Boro asks if they will use the tone in future episodes. Fera says they will record the room again each time, because the building may sound different. Boro notes that this avoids a recurring royalty. Odi saves the new file and closes the session.
