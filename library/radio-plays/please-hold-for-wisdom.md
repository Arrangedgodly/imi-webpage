# Please Hold for Wisdom

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: Call-in advice radio programs*
*By: IMI Broadcast Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

[THEME: warm piano, one cheerful trumpet note that arrives late.]

HOST MILES: Welcome to Ask a Monkey, broadcast from a branch-shaped studio at Infinite Monkey Industries, where you bring a dilemma and I provide a sentence that sounds useful until you try it. I'm Miles. Our phone lines are open, our tea is lukewarm, and the advice budget was cut in half. Caller one, you're on.

CALLER NINA: Hi, Miles. My coworker keeps replying "per my last email" even when I haven't sent an email. She says it before I've even asked anything.

MILES: That's called preemptive correspondence. Has she tried saying "hello" first?

NINA: I suggested it. She sent me a calendar invite titled "Hello, per our conversation."

MILES: Classic. You should reply with a question that cannot be answered by email.

NINA: Like what?

MILES: "Which of these two muffins has the higher morale?" Then watch her struggle.

NINA: I did. She attached a spreadsheet.

MILES: Then she has won round one. Keep your head down and your muffin close.

[SFX: PHONE LINE BEEPS.]

MILES: Caller two, what's on your mind?

CALLER DEX: My monkey band keeps asking me to write a theme song for the company picnic. We practice on the loading dock because the music room is reserved for a banana budgeting meeting. Every time I send a draft, the committee says it needs "more picnic." What does that mean?

MILES: It means they want the song to include something you can hold in one hand and drop in the grass.

DEX: I included a hot dog.

MILES: Was it a metaphorical hot dog?

DEX: It was a lyric about longing for the hot dog I forgot in the car.

MILES: That's too much car, not enough picnic. Add a chorus with a paper plate and a person trying to keep a napkin from flying away.

DEX: There is already a napkin in verse two.

MILES: Make it the main character.

DEX: I can't believe that's good advice.

MILES: You called the right show. Or the only show.

[SFX: BUTTON CLICK.]

MILES: We have a note from someone calling herself "The Calm One." Go ahead.

CALLER CALM: My partner labels every shelf in the refrigerator. The labels include "top shelf," "middle shelf," and "lower shelf." They are correct, but I don't know what problem they're solving.

MILES: Ask what happened before the labels.

CALM: We argued about where the mustard lives.

MILES: Then the labels are a treaty. Do you want to reopen the mustard question?

CALM: The mustard is now on the door.

MILES: Ah. A third party has negotiated.

CALM: The door is also labeled "door."

MILES: That feels less like peace and more like a museum tour. Tell your partner you appreciate the labels, but the door can identify itself.

CALM: I did. They put up a little sign that says "You are here."

MILES: I'd keep that one. It is useful when the fridge is open and you're wondering how you got there.

[THEME SWELLS; A MUG CLINKS.]

MILES: Before we take the last call, our producer has placed a sticky note in front of me. It says, "You should take a break." I will accept advice from a piece of office stationery. Caller four?

CALLER RAE: I work on a team where everyone wants to brainstorm, but nobody wants to choose. We have sticky notes all over the wall. They keep multiplying. Last week, one note said "consider having fewer sticky notes."

MILES: Did anyone act on the note?

RAE: They put it on a larger sticky note.

MILES: Then your wall has become the team. Give everyone two votes and a deadline. After the deadline, remove the notes without votes.

RAE: What if people say the idea needs more discussion?

MILES: Put that sentence on a note and give it zero votes.

RAE: You're telling me to silence people with stationery.

MILES: I'm telling you to ask a wall to make a decision. It has been running the meeting for a month.

RAE: I can try that.

MILES: Good. And when you do, take a photo first. Walls have no archive.

[SFX: PHONE RINGS. MILES ANSWERS, THEN STOPS.]

MILES: That's the producer. We are out of time. The advice board has one last note: "If advice fails, blame the show." Fair. Nina, keep your muffin. Dex, promote the napkin. Calm One, let the door be a door. Rae, count the votes and take a picture of the wall before it develops a committee.

[THEME.]

MILES: This has been Ask a Monkey. Our next episode is "Should I reply-all?" The answer is no, but we'll take your calls anyway. Good night.

[END MUSIC.]
