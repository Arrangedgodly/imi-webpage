# The Parrot's Translation Hotline

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: call-in advice shows that treat a parrot's repeated phrases as legal testimony*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-019*

CAST: Host Vex, an orangutan; caretaker Mara, a macaque; vet tech Odo, a gibbon; handler Pip, a capuchin; caller Nari, a lemur.

SFX: Animal shelter office. Cage latch; a parrot whistles twice.

VEX: We have a breaking statement from the parrot: "Open the window!"

MARA: That's a phrase he repeats. The window stays closed; the air is cool and the cage is secure.

PIP: I am his handler today. I will cover the cage if we need to move it, but he is not leaving the room.

ODO: The care card says he learned the phrase from a previous owner. It does not mean he wants to go outside.

NARI, on phone: I am calling from the shelter front desk. I heard him say it again. Is he asking to be adopted?

VEX: The bird has filed a petition.

MARA: The bird is vocalizing. We should not treat a repeated phrase as consent or a legal request.

SFX: Parrot whistles, then says, "Open the window."

VEX: He has repeated his demand.

PIP: He repeats it most when people gather near the cage. We can lower our voices and give him space.

MARA: Let's move the interview to the office next door, where the parrot can settle without our microphones.

ODO: His water dish is full and the enrichment toy is in place. I will close the room door after the cage check.

SFX: Footsteps to adjacent office; door shuts softly; parrot sound fades.

VEX: The witness has been removed. Court is in session; I order the window opened!

SFX: A chair scrapes; a hand reaches for the window latch.

PIP: I am at the latch. Is that an actual instruction?

MARA: No. Stop there. The window stays closed, and the cage stays latched.

SFX: Hand withdraws; window latch clicks once in its closed position.

VEX: My order has been overruled.

MARA: There was no court and no order. Your courtroom language nearly sent a staff member to the window.

NARI: I can ask about adoption without a verdict.

ODO: Ask the shelter staff about his care history and behavior. A phrase alone does not tell you what he wants.

PIP: He has a cardboard puzzle to explore. He may whistle, talk, or ignore us. We do not force a response.

VEX: May I ask him one question from here?

MARA: No. Stay in this room and let him settle.

SFX: Paper turns; adoption profile prints in the adjacent office.

NARI, still on the front-desk telephone: May I meet Mara while I am here at the shelter desk?

ODO: Yes. The caretaker can explain his previous home and daily routine. The repeated phrase is not a signature.

VEX: I will enter "window requested" in the case file.

MARA: No. That would turn your guess into his record. Write: "Phrase learned in previous home; caretaker will discuss known cues."

SFX: Pen scratches; page is clipped to the profile.

SFX: Pip opens the enclosure-room door quietly, checks inside, then closes it.

PIP: The profile is ready. He is working on his cardboard puzzle, and we do not need to record another call.

NARI: I will ask the caretaker here and follow the instructions.

VEX: Is that the verdict?

MARA: It is a scheduled visit. No one has made a decision for the bird.

SFX: Front desk pager buzzes once.

NARI: That is my meeting call.

ODO: Mara can meet you at the desk now.

MARA: I will bring the profile out to you.

SFX: Nari sets down the desk telephone. In the office, Mara's chair moves; she picks up the completed profile from the desk.

ODO: Mara has the profile and is joining Nari at the front desk. The cage remains latched, and the window stays closed.

SFX: Office door opens at the front desk; footsteps recede. In the enclosure, the parrot taps the cardboard puzzle.
