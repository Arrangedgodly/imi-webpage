# The Mitten on the Bench

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: investigative radio journalism that turns a misplaced everyday object into a serialized mystery*
*By: IMI Radio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-011*

Cast: Host Piri, a capuchin; reporter Odo, a baboon; librarian Nara, a macaque; caller Fenn, a gibbon.

[SFX: NEWS THEME, PAPER SHUFFLE.]

PIRI: Tonight, one mitten has vanished from the village lost-and-found. We ask: who took it, and why was the other mitten left behind?

ODO: It was found beside the bench.

NARA: I put it there. It was damp.

PIRI: Our first lead.

ACT ONE

Odo reports live from the library table, where the mitten sits in a clear evidence bag. Nara says she placed it there to dry. Piri asks whether she saw anyone approach the bench. Nara says a child, two pigeons, and a person carrying a basket all passed.

ODO: Any of them suspicious?

NARA: The pigeons were looking at crumbs.

PIRI: We cannot rule out the pigeons.

Fenn calls in to say the mitten belongs to him. It is his left mitten, and the right one is still at home. Piri asks why he did not claim it earlier. Fenn says he did not know it had been found.

ACT TWO

Piri sends Odo to inspect the bench. He narrates the scene in a grave voice: one dry leaf, one crumb, and a small scratch on the wood. Nara says the scratch has been there for years. Odo asks if the scratch could be a mark left by the mitten.

NARA: It is longer than the mitten.

ODO: A second clue.

Fenn arrives to identify his property. The mitten has a blue patch shaped like a fish. He sews patches on all his gloves so he can tell them apart. Piri says the patch could be a signature. Fenn asks whose.

PIRI: That is what we're trying to determine.

Nara opens the evidence bag. The mitten smells faintly of rain and library paste. Odo says it was near the bench, which is close to the library. Fenn says he carried it in his pocket while borrowing books.

ODO: So you placed it on the bench?

FENN: I think I did.

PIRI: Then the mystery has a suspect.

Fenn explains that he pulled it out to wipe a damp seat, then left it on the bench. He did not notice until he was home. The other mitten stayed in his pocket.

Odo asks Fenn to name the basket he carried. Fenn says it is woven from reed and has a loose handle. Nara's lost mitten was beside that basket while she repaired the handle. Piri wonders whether the basket caused the exchange. Fenn demonstrates by setting the evidence bag beside it; the bag slides into the basket's shadow, making the blue patch hard to see. The station's dramatic music swells. Nara tells Odo to stop narrating the evidence and check the patch. Odo magnifies it with a hand lens. The thread color matches the repair kit Nara keeps at the library desk. Nara admits she sewed the patch herself, then forgot because she had borrowed the basket. The case is not theft, but a mix-up with one witness, two gloves, and a very theatrical bag.

ACT THREE

Piri asks why the station made a special report if the owner is here. Odo says the report can still explain the lost-and-found system. Nara says the mitten dries faster when laid flat, not hung by its cuff. Fenn takes it, thanks her, and places it over his hand.

The mitten is too small. It belongs to Nara. Fenn's fish patch is on the other glove, still at home. Nara recognizes the blue patch she sewed onto her own left mitten last winter. She had borrowed Fenn's basket, then confused their gloves at the bench.

PIRI: We have found the owner and discovered a glove exchange.

FENN: I was the first suspect and the wrong suspect.

NARA: You are still holding the mitten.

Fenn hands it back. Nara gives him her spare right glove, which fits.

TAG

PIRI: Tomorrow, we investigate the right mitten.

ODO: It is on Fenn's hand.

PIRI: The case continues.
