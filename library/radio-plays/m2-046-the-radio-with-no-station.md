# The Radio with No Station

*Format: Radio play parody*
*Parody target: amateur radio listening clubs and mystery signal programs*
*By: IMI Audio Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-AUDIO-002*

Cast: Host En, a cautious macaque; club member Rusa, a gibbon antenna builder; young listener Lolo, a capuchin; and Old Pail, a baboon who insists every static burst is a message.

[SFX: STATIC SWEEPS ACROSS DIAL, THEN A SHORT WHISTLE.] 

EN: This is The Listening Bench. We are scanning the old valley band with Rusa from the amateur radio club. Last week, listeners reported a signal that sounded like a whistle followed by three clicks. Pail believes it is a message from a lost station.

PAIL: I do not believe. I have prepared a chart with arrows, a legend, and a red circle around the gate.

RUSA: You have not decoded it.

PAIL: The signal said "come find me."

EN: How did you get that from a whistle?

PAIL: I know whistles.

[SFX: SHORT WHISTLE, THREE CLICKS.] 

LOLO: That sounded like our gate.

RUSA: What kind of gate?

LOLO: The one outside the studio. It squeaks when the wind pushes it.

PAIL: The gate is sending a message. It has repeated the same phrase all week, which is what makes it suspicious.

RUSA: The gate is moving a metal latch against the frame.

EN: Let's check before we name it.

[SFX: DOOR OPENS. WIND BLOWS THROUGH, GATE SQUEAKS.] 

LOLO: There it is. Whistle, then three clicks.

PAIL: The gate knows the code.

RUSA: The latch has three loose washers. Pail has circled each one and labeled them "possibly conspiratorial."

EN: Can we tighten them?

RUSA: With the caretaker's permission. We should not alter the gate on air.

PAIL: So the mystery is solved?

RUSA: We have one source for the sound. We do not know whether another station made a similar sound.

EN: That's a fair limit. Let's compare recordings. Rusa, play the signal from last week.

[SFX: RECORDING, WHISTLE AND CLICKS. PAUSE. GATE SQUEAKS AGAIN.] 

RUSA: The timing matches. The pitch is slightly different.

LOLO: Because the recording had static.

RUSA: Or because the wind changed.

PAIL: Or because a station is hiding under the hill.

EN: Is there a legal station on that band?

RUSA: There is a weather relay. It sends a test tone once a week. The tone is longer and has no clicks.

EN: Can we check the schedule before Pail prints another chart?

RUSA: The club has it in the logbook.

[SFX: BOOK OPENS, PAGES FLIP.] 

RUSA: The relay test happened at 8:15. Our recording was made at 8:17.

PAIL: Two minutes later. The station waited.

EN: Or our clock was two minutes fast.

LOLO: The clock is the same one that stopped yesterday.

PAIL: The clock is part of the mystery.

RUSA: It needs a new battery.

[SFX: BATTERY DOOR OPENS. BATTERY REMOVED, NEW ONE INSERTED. CLOCK TICKS.] 

EN: Now the time is right. The relay test was at 8:15, the gate recording at 8:17. We can label the source as unknown, with a likely gate match. We will not call it a transmission from a station we cannot identify.

PAIL: Can I keep my theory?

EN: You can keep it as a theory.

RUSA: And fix the gate.

LOLO: I have a screwdriver.

RUSA: You can hold it while the caretaker tightens the washers.

[SFX: GATE LATCH ADJUSTED. WIND BLOWS. GATE STAYS QUIET.] 

PAIL: The signal is gone. That is exactly what a gate would do after we identify it.

EN: The gate is quiet. The valley band is still open, and Pail has one minute to explain why a quiet gate is more suspicious than a noisy one.

RUSA: We'll log future signals with time, frequency, and weather.

PAIL: And mystery?

EN: Mystery goes in the notes column.

PAIL: I will keep the chart. It has a blank square for the next sound.

[SFX: ONE CLEAN GATE LATCH CLICK, THEN THE CLUB THEME.]
