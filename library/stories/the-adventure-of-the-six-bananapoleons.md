# The Adventure of the Six Bananapoleons

*A parody of "The Adventure of the Six Napoleons"*

It was Inspector Lechimp of the Scotland Banyard who brought it in, sheepish as a mandrill at a wedding: somebody in Landon was going about the city smashing plaster busts of Bananaparte.

He found us at 221B Banyan Street, where I had dropped in on Sherlock Howlmes for no reason at all, which was always my best reason. He filled his pipe from the toe of an old slipper, as was his habit, pushed the fig-sweets toward me, as was mine, glanced once at my cuffs, and remarked that I had taken a villa and a shrubbery — there being mango sap on my sleeves and a new self-respect in my shoulders — and then turned his attention to the Inspector, because a monkey who smashes crockery was, in his phrase, "a strong wind from a strange quarter."

"Not a murder, mind," said Lechimp. "Not even a burglary. Vandalism — fruit-flavoured vandalism. Morse Hudson's Emporium lost one off its shelf a fortnight back; the buyer's gone bust-looking since. Then Dr. Barnicot, the busy chimp physician — a 'nana-splitter broke into his hall in the night and smashed the two Bananapartes he'd just bought, and touched nothing else, though the fig-silver lay in plain sight."

"And the fourth?" said Sherlock Howlmes, who had been grooming the problem out of the air for some minutes.

Lechimp gaped. "How the deuce — yes, the fourth. Last night, at the house of Horace Harker, a press-howler of this city. His bust smashed on the lawn — and a dead monkey in his drive. Pietro Venucci, an organ-grinder's capuchin, known to us. A whole murder has grown out of smashed crockery, Howlmes."

"Ape-lementary, Lechimp, that it has. Six Bananapoleons were cast together at Gilder & Sons' plaster-works — banana-plaster, the yellow kind that takes a polish. One at Hudson's, two at Barnicot's, one at Harker's. Bust-smashers do not commit murder for crockery. They do it for what is *inside* one particular crockery — and Venucci's people say he spent his last week asking shopmen one question: *where are the other busts?* He was hunting the same object as our smasher, and got between him and it."

We confirmed the middle of the chain at Dr. Barnicot's, where the busy chimp received us between patients, in a hall that had lately been a gallery of Little Corporals. "Two of them, Howlmes — shattered on my own tiles, splinters to the skirting, and never a drawer turned over, though the fig-silver sat in plain sight by the umbrella stand. My housemaid swears the door was locked and the night as quiet as a judge. I ask you: who breaks into a respectable hall and vandalizes only the ambience?"

"An admirer with a grudge," said Howlmes, kneeling over the shards, "who knew these three Bananapartes shared a cradle." And all that day he went unhurriedly about Landon, doing the arithmetic of empires: at Gilder & Sons the batch-book gave him six; at Hard-fig Brothers, the wholesale, four; at Hudson's Emporium, one. Six casts, three smashed, one smashed with a murder attached, one gone to Chiswick — and one loose in the middle-class wilds, wearing the only pearl in Landon under its hat.

We called on Horace Harker that forenoon and found him writing his own account of the outrage for the evening edition, in which he was already a "well-known public figure" twice. "I lose a bust," he complained, "and I gain a corpse, and the corpse gets the column inches. There's gratitude. Print in Landon is dying, Howlmes, dying of ingratitude." He had seen nothing, heard one crash, and composed a headline about it.

"The fifth," Lechimp admitted, back at Baker Street, "has been traced to Josiah Brown, Esq., of Chiswick. The sixth is unaccounted for. I had it from Hard-fig Brothers, who handled the wholesale — three smashed, one sold and smashed, one in Chiswick, one vanished into the middle-class wilds."

"Then tonight we sit in Mr. Brown's garden," said Howlmes, "and count busts as they fall."

We sat among the goosefig bushes until the moon was high — Lechimp, two of his constables, Sherlock Howlmes, and I — on the kind of warm, whispering night that turns even constables theological. The house slept. A dog argued with a shadow twice. Then a lithe shadow dropped the wall, slipped to the garden library window, and in a moment held aloft a yellow Bananaparte, poised above the paving-stones. The smashing-hand was already in motion when we took him: Beppo, organ-grinder's capuchin, plaster-dust to the elbows, fighting like a whole barrel of monkeys gone sour. It took Lechimp and both constables to fold him, and he bit nobody who did not deserve it.

"A year in the cage for knifework," said Lechimp, reading from his card-file, "released Tuesday. And at the trial of that affair — wait. Howlmes. The Borgias case."

"The Black Nana of the Borgias," said Howlmes quietly, "the midnight banana-shaped pearl stolen from the Countess' rooms at the Hotel Cosmopolyp. Beppo here hid it where no search would ever look — in the wet banana-plaster of a batch of six busts of the Little Corporal, drying at Gilder's, the night before his arrest. Fetched off to prison before the plaster dried, he has come out to reap his harvest, one Bananaparte at a time, following the busts down the chain of sale — and Venucci, sent by the pearl's former owners to retrieve it, reached the fifth before him, and paid for his speed on Mr. Harker's lawn."

"Then where—" began Lechimp, but Howlmes had already produced a crumpled bill of sale. The sixth bust, he explained, had gone to one Sandeford, a retired feeder of Reading, out among the mango-orchards. "I wired him this morning. His reply, in a farming hand, said: *Take the ugly beggar and welcome, and tell the trade the next one will cost you double.* He has sold me his Bananaparte for ten gold bananas, and shook my paw twice — once for the money and once for the joke of it. If I am wrong, I have bought the Banyard a very expensive paperweight, and I shall apologize to plaster in general."

The bust stood at that moment on the sitting-room table. Howlmes took up his cane. "It is possible," he said, "that I have been sold a very expensive paperweight."

*Tap.*

The cane cracked through the plaster crown of the Little Corporal, the shards rang on the table — and out of the shattered Bananaparte, wobbling on its great curved orbit, rolled a pearl as black as midnight in a closed fig-store, banana-shaped, cool, and worth a province.

Lechimp uttered a sound the Banyard's regulations do not encourage.

"You reasoned it out from plaster dust and a dead capuchin," he said, "while I was filing vandalism. You'll want the credit kept quiet, I suppose."

"On the contrary, Lechimp, brief your press-howler friend Mr. Harker generously — the man needs his column inches, and I have not finished with the Borgias' correspondents in this city. But take tonight's glory; it is honestly earned."

The Inspector of the Scotland Banyard stood very straight and wrung Howlmes' paw, a thing the Yard does for nobody. "The Banyard is not jealous of you, Mister Howlmes," he said. "The Banyard is proud of you."

And Sherlock Howlmes — I record it as his biographer and his friend — turned pink about the ears, picked up the Black Nana, and put it into Lechimp's palm as if it were nothing but a very small banana indeed.
