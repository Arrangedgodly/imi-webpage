# The Lemurinaries

*A parody of "The Luminaries"*

In the zodiac of the Crown Plume Hotel, on the west coast of Zealandor, in the ninth week of the gold rush, there sat twelve lemurs, and not one of them was talking.

Walter Moody, late of Edinburgh, paused in the doorway of the smoking lounge with his brandy in his paw and his tail curled in the shape of a question mark. He was nine-and-twenty, a ring-tailed lemur of good family and poor digestion, lately arrived on the steamer *Godseed* to make his fortune in law — and he knew at once, the way a monkey knows a storm, that he had interrupted a conspiracy.

"You're welcome to the fire," said the biggest of them, a grey ruffed ship's agent named Balfour, moving his boots an inch. "We were only meeting."

"You were only meeting *in silence*," said Moody, "which is the loudest kind."

The twelve exchanged looks around the room, constellated like a zodiac: a fat sifaka publican, a mouse-lemur chemist with quick paws, a Chinese golden-cat? — no, a slender loris trader called Sook Yongsheng, come south to sell opium and buy dust; a Norwegian indri, a banker, a gaoler, a warden, a clerk, a politician, a sea captain, and one empty chair that everybody kept glancing at, as if it owed them money.

"Tell you what," said Balfour. "We'll tell you what we were meeting about, and then you'll be one of us, and bound."

"Bound," said Moody, "is exactly what I was afraid of. Proceed."

And so, over that night and several chapters, the twelve gave their evidence, and each one held a piece of the affair the way a lemur holds a nut: close, and against the chest, and only slightly dishonestly.

---

**First, the dead lemur.** On the fourteenth of January, a hermit named Crosbie Wells had been found dead in his cottage up the Arahura track, laid out neat as a Sunday, with his boots off. In the same cottage, sewn into the mattress and packed into the chimney and tucked under the floor like mice, was gold. Sixty thousand pounds of it, by the warden's scale. Nobody had known Crosbie Wells had sixpence.

**Second, the arrested female.** That same night, on Revell Street, they had picked up one Anna Wetherell — a young ring-tail of great beauty and greater bad luck, a seamstress-slash-hotel-worker-slash-whatever-the-town-required — insensible in the gutter with laudanum in her blood. They held her for attempting her own life, and, the town being the town, for the hermit's death too.

**Third, the vanished boy.** Emery Staines, nineteen, golden, the champion digger of the whole coast, who had struck the Sunrise claim richer than a plum pudding and been adopted by every monkey in it — Emery had walked into the hills after the fourteenth of January and not walked out. The empty chair was his.

**Fourth**, said the politician Lauderback, a broad black-and-white ruffed lemur with an election in his fur, "there is the small matter of the corpse lashed to the deck of my chartered steamer and the gold in her hold that no one will claim—" and here the company told him to hush and drink.

**Fifth, the widow.** Lydia Wells, the dead hermit's widow, a handsome aye-aye with a lantern finger and a taste for séances, who had arrived in town the very week of the death to claim the fortune, read the stars over it, and announce that the gold had come to her by destiny. "She read a horoscope in the hospital tent," said the chemist, "over the unconscious girl's cot, out loud, to the *newspaper*. It concluded that Miss Wetherell was a Scorpio."

"And sixth," said Moody quietly, "the sea captain."

For Moody had seen the sea captain. He had come aboard the *Godseed* in the roads that very evening, seeking the lawyer's advice about a matter of fraud, and that advice-seeker — one Captain Francis Carver, bullock-faced, brass-ringed — had left Moody's cabin, and had then, by the testimony of the second mate, walked straight into the hold to count certain crates that were not on the manifest, and been found at dawn among them, dead, with his own laudanum flask tipped into him by somebody's steady paw.

"There is your murder," said Moody, "and it is not the one any of you were guarding."

---

By candle-end they had it assembled, and it hung over the smoking lounge like a mobile over a cradle.

Carver and Lydia Wells, husband and wife under other names, had cooked the scheme between them years before: gold bought dirty on the Chopshee coast, shipped to Zealandor in Crosbie Wells' name — for Crosbie, it emerged, was Carver's own half-brother, a gentle creature they had used as a letterbox. When Crosbie grew suspicious, Carver had paid him a friendly visit with a bottle of brandy and a dose for the bottom of it. Then they had hid the fortune in the hermit's chimney to season, meaning to "discover" it after the claim, and Anna Wetherell — who had carried letters, quite innocently, between the parties — had been chosen to hang for all of it: dosed, dumped, and declared a Scorpio.

The laudanum flask that finished Carver had been traced, at last, to Crosbie's true friend in the hills: not a digger at all but Quee, the old scales-keeper of the Chinese camp, who had come down to ask the captain, quietly, why the hermit's dog still waited at the gate, and had received an answer he could not carry home. Twelve lemurs had each seen a piece — the boots, the letters, the crates, the horoscope — and each had kept it, because in Hopitika a secret is the only currency that doesn't shrink.

"You knew," said Moody, looking round the zodiac, "every one of you knew something, and the town very nearly hung a girl on the sum of your silences."

"We didn't know the *sum*," said the publican, wounded.

"Nobody ever does," said Moody. "That's what sums are for."

---

The inquest ran a fortnight. Moody, who had crossed the world to be dull and instead found himself the only lemur in the southern hemisphere without a tail in the fire, produced the *Godseed*'s bill of lading, Lydia's legerdemain in her own fair paw, and — by subpoena of the second mate — the whole crooked cargo. Anna Wetherell was cleared, wept once, terribly, and then straightened her collar like a queen. The coroner, a portly bamboo lemur, ruled Crosbie Wells poisoned by Carver, and Carver poisoned by pride and Quee in equal parts, and Lydia Wells — who had by then left for Melbourne with one trunk too many — welcome to return and explain the difference whenever she cared.

The fortune in the chimney was the surprise. It belonged by the court's arithmetic neither to the widow nor to the crown but to Emery Staines, whose Sunrise dust it had partly been laundered through; and Emery, when he finally came down out of the hills — thin, unharmed, having spent the winter in a miner's hut with a broken paw and a guilty conscience about a card game he'd fled — signed the whole of it over to Anna Wetherell in front of the warden and eleven astonished lemurs.

"It's her fortune," he said simply. "The stars put it in her path; Carver only carried it. I've been rich. It's itchy."

He would not stay for the celebration. He looked at Anna across the courtroom — looked, and swayed like a lemur on a thin branch, and both of them touched, unthinkingly, opposite sides of their chests — and then he tipped his hat and went, because a lemur who has been alone in the hills a whole winter has customs of his own.

"That," said Moody, watching him go, "is the most expensive hat-tip in colonial history."

---

Two years passed. The rush moved on, as rushes do, like weather. Moody had a practice on Revell Street and the beginnings of a good name; the twelve were eleven, then ten, then scattered to Hokitika's cousins, each carrying his chapter.

And Anna Wetherell, proprietress now of the Crown Plume's dining room, stood one evening at the door in the low golden light, and saw coming up the street a young male with sun in his fur and a fortune itching to be nowhere at all.

"You're Emery Staines," she said.

"You're Anna Wetherell," he said.

"I've been expecting you," she said, "since about the fourteenth of January."

"I know," he said. "I got lost. There was a mountain in the way. There's been a mountain in the way for two years."

There was no mountain. There was only a street, and the two of them standing in it, sun and moon arriving at last at the same hour of the same sky. He took her paw. The eleven, wherever they were, felt the balance of the zodiac shift and settle.

In which, said the almanac of that coast, nothing whatever happened that the stars had not arranged in advance — and everything happened anyway, the way it does with lemurs.
