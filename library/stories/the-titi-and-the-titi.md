# The Titi & the Titi

*A parody of "The City & the City"*

Inspector Tyador Boughlú of the Besnut Extreme Crime Squad swung into the Ropewalk groves at dawn, where the bushels smelled of overripe fruit and worse decisions. A young female titi lay dead beneath a banana palm, her tail arranged almost politely, as if someone had tried to make her comfortable after failing to make her alive.

"She's one of theirs," said Sergeant Corwi, jerking her chin at the far side of the grove, where the same branches kept right on being the same branches.

"She's in our grove," said Boughlú.

"Both can be true, and neither can be said. Look at her with Besnut eyes or don't look at all, sir. You see her, you unsee her."

That was the arrangement. Besnut and Ul Banama were two troops of titi monkeys occupying the same canopy, the same fig trees, the same patch of sky. A Besnut titi could dangle an arm's length from an Ul Banaman titi and be legally, morally, and socially obliged to act as if the other monkey were fog. It wasn't that they couldn't see each other. They were trained not to. From the moment a kit opened its eyes, its mother hissed: *that fruit-seller? Weather, darling. That ripe banana in a stranger's hand? A trick of the light.* Breach — acknowledging the other troop — brought the Breech: baboons in red velvet knee-breeches who took you by the scruff and were never seen again, in either sense.

The dead girl was Mahalia Greenery, a student archaeologist, and she had made the fatal mistake of digging at the Split Fig — a ruin claimed by both troops, groomed by neither — hunting for proof of Urciny, the legendary Third Troop said to live in the between.

Boughlú canvassed the Ropewalk market himself, which meant asking forty witnesses to describe a monkey they had all watched very hard not seeing.

"Describe him?" said a fig-seller. "Inspector, I saw nothing."

"He was tall nothing or short nothing?"

"Tall nothing. Scarred ear. Whistled when he walked, which is unusual, for nothing." She counted his change into his palm. "Also the nothing had a limp. Not that I noticed."

"You're a credit to the canopy," said Boughlú, writing it all down in the margin where evidence goes to be denied.

The trail ran straight at the border and stopped dead, so Boughlú crossed to Ul Banama the only legal way: through Canopy Hall, where a monkey could queue, flash his marks, cross a vestibule, and emerge — having swung precisely nowhere — legally elsewhere. There he was partnered against his will with Detective Qussim Dred, an Ul Banaman with immaculate fur and terrible coffee.

"Your girl was passed around like a rotten plum," Dred said. "Killed here, maybe. Carried there, certainly. Passed hand to hand at night, each courier unseeing the one before. The perfect route through two troops: every monkey who touched her was already committed to pretending she wasn't there. Who describes the journey without confessing the crime?"

Before Boughlú could answer, they were summoned before the Oversight Committee — six bureaucrats, three from each troop, seated on one branch at carefully alternate intervals so that no two monkeys of the same troop sat adjacent, which made voting a matter of gymnastics.

"You will investigate," said the Besnut chair, "without questioning Ul Banaman citizens."

"You will investigate," said the Ul Banaman chair, "without questioning Besnut citizens."

"You will not," they said together, "investigate the between, mention the between, or draw a map with the between on it. Also, the Bothroop Fruit Combine, which owns the groves on both sides and finds the whole arrangement profitable, has asked us to wish you luck and no success."

The committee adjourned, satisfied, into mutual unseeing.

Mahalia's friend Yolanda came to Boughlú next, shivering so hard her fruit dropped. "She believed. She'd say, 'There are more monkeys in this canopy than the canopy admits.' She said the unseeing wasn't to keep the troops apart. It was to keep something hidden *in* the middle. And she wrote to Professor Bodwin about it. Read his reply. He begs her to stop."

Professor Bodwin was the most famous archaeologist in either troop, author of *Between the Troop and the Troop*, the book that had made Urciny a household whisper — and then, in its scandalous second edition, unmade it, calling the Third Troop "a fable for monkeys who cannot decide which of two mothers to groom."

Three days later Yolanda was found drowned in a gutter that ran, with perfect legality, down the exact seam of the two troops. Half of her lay in Besnut and half in Ul Banama. Both troops ruled it a private tragedy: jurisdictionally, she had drowned in two separate puddles, each too small for a crime.

"She was killed," said Dred, staring at the gutter with cold fury, "in the one place in this canopy where nobody can look."

"That's not a place," said Boughlú. "That's a *method*."

The nationalist angle led him to Breccia, a young Ul Banaman patriot with muscles and grievance, who came at Boughlú with a trowel bearing Mahalia's name on the handle.

"She was going to say Urciny was *under* us," Breccia snarled, driving him back through the Split Fig. "That our great troop was sitting on top of another troop's ruins like a monkey on a stolen mango. Besnut and Ul Banama hate each other — that's the whole point of us! If there's a Third Troop, then we were never two. We were always one troop being stupid in stereo."

"So you bonked her on the head," said Boughlú, "for arithmetic."

"I *wished* her dead," said Breccia, panting, lowering the trowel. "Half of Ul Banama wished her dead. Wishing is our national sport. But I was in custody that week — for throwing fruit at a unification parade. Ask your own jailers, Inspector, and enjoy the paperwork."

Boughlú checked. It held. Which left the monkey who had the most to lose if the middle of the map got crowded, and who had begged Mahalia to stop writing to him: the professor whose life's work she had finished, the wrong way.

He cornered Bodwin in Canopy Hall at shift-change, with a thousand monkeys legally elsewhere all around. The old archaeologist had a thorn-knife out of his satchel before Boughlú finished saying her name.

"She told me what Urciny was," Bodwin said, and his voice cracked like a dry fig. "Not a third troop. A *mask*. The between worn as a face — and she could prove it, from our dig. Do you understand what that means? My book made the between holy, and my retraction made it empty, and she made it *managed*. I spent forty years being wrong in both directions. So yes. I struck her. And then I did what every monkey in this canopy does best." He smiled, terrible and calm. "I handed her to strangers and unseeingly walked away."

"Drop the knife, Professor."

"Urciny protects me," said Bodwin, stepping forward. "Urciny has always protected its own."

And that — Boughlú would think later — was the one sentence nobody in either troop was allowed to say aloud, because saying it claimed the between acted in the world, and claims about the between belonged to the between.

"URCINY," Boughlú bellowed, at the top of his lungs, at rush hour, in Canopy Hall.

The baboons came out of nowhere the way weather comes out of the sky: red breeches, enormous hands, calm as tax collectors. One of them looked at Boughlú the way Boughlú had been trained never to look at anyone, and Bodwin's knife clattered to the branch, and Bodwin went with it, into the between, in both senses at once.

"You invoked us," the baboon said afterward, almost approvingly. "You used the third troop as a whistle. Clever, and expensive. You saw both troops all case long, Inspector — crossed, and *perceived*. That makes you a problem. Or an application."

"Application for what?" Boughlú managed.

"The between needs new eyes. Ones that admit what they see. Urciny never existed — there was only ever the gap, and us inside it, holding the two of you apart so you don't tear the whole canopy down out of pride. The first thing everyone sees on this job is that it was always one troop up here — one tree, too many bananas, not enough honesty."

Boughlú thought of Mahalia, arranged politely under the palm, and of a canopy that trained its children blind in stereo.

He took the enormous hand.

"I already saw it," he said. "I'm Besnut Extreme Crime Squad. Seeing what's plainly there is the only trick we know."
