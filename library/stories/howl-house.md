# Howl House

*A parody of "Hell House"*

The offer came from the richest monkey in the world, which is the only kind of monkey who can afford the truth. Old Man Chestnut, the Nut Baron, was dying in his tower in Connecticut, and he wanted one question answered before he went — *is there anything after?* — and he was willing to pay a million dollars to anyone who could prove it in the one place on earth where proof came spitting.

That place was Howlasco House.

"Built in 1919 by Emeric Howlasco," said the briefing dossier. "The Roaring Giant, the newspapers called him. A gorilla who never stopped growing, by his own account. For a decade he filled the house with guests — actors, artists, the fast and the clever — and hosted the party that famously never ended. It ended. In 1929 the house was found sealed from within: every guest dead, every window shuttered, no cause ever established. In 1953 a full expedition entered: eight monkeys. One came out."

The one who came out was in the room with us, not reading his own biography: Benjamin Franklin Fischer, a capuchin of thirty-odd with a face like a closed fist and a cigarette.

"I was ten," he said. "My mother took me along as a cute mascot. I survived by doing one thing: shutting down. The house wants fear — it *eats* it. I went silent in my head, and silent in my heart, and it passed over me like weather." He looked at the cheque on the table and his jaw worked. "Old Chestnut's dying, and so am I, only slower, and I want to be paid. Fine. But hear me: that house is not haunted. Haunted is a hiccup. That house is *occupied*."

We were four: Fischer; Dr. Lionel Barrett, a physicist gibbon who believed every ghost was bad plumbing plus bad physics, and who brought a machine the size of a stove to prove it; his wife Edith, a gentle spider monkey whom none of us — none of us — should have allowed to come; and me, Florence Tanner, mental medium, ex-actress, professional orphan of the world behind the world. I wrote this account before we went in, in case I got no after. As it turned out, I got no after to write it in. Fischer finished it for me, from my notes and my shorthand. Pay attention when the handwriting changes.

We drove up on a Tuesday. Howlasco House stood in its dead valley like a gorilla sitting on a haunch: vast, black-shingled, patient. On the door of the little chapel beside it — Howlasco had built himself a chapel, which is the first thing you should know about a monkey — a card was pinned to the board in careful Edwardian script:

*WELCOME TO MY HOUSE. I'M DELIGHTED YOU COULD COME. I'M SURE YOU'LL FIND YOUR STAY HERE HIGHLY ILLUMIN-EEK-ATING. PLEASE EXCUSE THE CHILDISH PRANK OF THE NAILED SHUTTERS. I LIKE MY PRIVACY. — E.H.*

Edith read it aloud and put her paw over her mouth like a monkey at a funeral.

"Now that," said Fischer, lighting his cigarette off his last cigarette, "is the only welcome you get. After this you pay for the room."

The first night was quiet as a held breath. The second was not. I was touched — I, Florence Tanner, who had been touched on two continents — by something with paws the size of dinner plates and the temperament of a spoiled emperor. Barrett's machine registered nothing, which he called science and I called a machine with no commission. Edith began to sleepwalk. On the fourth night they found her in the fountain at two in the morning, floating on her back, smiling at the sky, saying in a voice not hers: "Stay for the party. Nobody leaves the party. The party is *forever.*"

Barrett pulled her out and she came round slowly, ashamed, cold, with no memory — but from that night she slept with the light on, and I caught her once standing at the black door of the sealed "Humid Room," the old steam-house where the famous party had guttered out, listening with her whole spine.

The house worked on each of us with a craftsman's economy. On Barrett it used pride: banging sounds that moved whenever his instruments were pointed at them, until the great physicist was chasing squeaks through the pantry at four in the morning in his dressing gown, shouting about resonance. On Fischer it used silence — gave him nothing at all, for days, which wore him worse than blows: "It's *saving* me," he kept muttering. "It remembers I keep. It's marinating me."

And on me it used the one thing I have never been able to resist: a lost soul.

He called himself Daniel. He wept in the chapel; he moved the chairs; he wrote on my tablet, in a boy's hand, *i am daniel howlasco he keeps me here i never wanted any of it help me rest.* I will not pretend I checked. A medium with an orphan in her arms does not check. I opened myself further and further to bring the child peace — against Barrett's physics, against Fischer's flat warning ("There's no child in this house, Tanner. There never was") — and on the ninth night, in the chapel, with the candles going, I called him into me to carry him into the light.

And the thing that came into me was not a child. It was the house's landlord, wearing the boy like a coat, and it opened my own gifts against me. I remember the balcony rail. I remember it letting go.

Florence's notes end there. — B.F.F.

They found her at the foot of the chapel balcony at dawn, composed as an actress between scenes, and Barrett finally believed, and Edith stopped pretending she was all right, and I stopped pretending I was paid enough. We sat in the kitchen — the only room the house never touched; make of that what you will — and I told them what I have known since I was ten and never once said aloud.

"It isn't a hundred ghosts in there. It never was. It's *him.* Howlasco alone. Every haunting in that house is one monkey, throwing his voice. And he's still there because he is the only ghost who knows he's lying."

"Playing what?" said Barrett hoarsely.

"The Giant." I took them to the Humid Room, to the tiles mortared back wrong in 1929, and we broke the fresh mortar with a fire iron, and behind the wall, seated in a chair, dressed in his enormous famous pelt, was Emeric Howlasco — the Roaring Giant himself, dead a hundred years and grinning — and his legs ended at the knee.

Below the knee: carved. Pegs, jointed clever as a puppet's, built into his famous boots. The seated corpse measured out, unpegged, at four feet ten.

"He wasn't a giant," said Edith, in a small gone voice. "He was a little ape in a padded pelt. And when the accident with the steam took his legs anyway, he couldn't bear the world to know — so he nailed the house shut, sealed the party in with him, told the dark itself he was enormous — and the dark, God help us, took his word."

"Belief built this house twice," I said. "Once in timber. Once after he died — out of nothing but his will and his lie. That's the whole ghost: a rumor that grew muscles. And a rumor can't stand being laughed at."

I stood in front of the corpse. I was ten years old again, hiding under the stairs with my mother's heartbeat in my ears, and I was done hiding. The room went cold; the pelt's shoulders began, obscenely, to swell; the dark drew itself up and up to its full terrible height above me, a hundred years of fear banking itself like a fire about to roar.

I looked up its whole height and I laughed at it.

"You're nothing," I told him. "You hear me, Howlasco? You were never a giant. You were a small sad ape on wooden legs, and every monkey in this valley is taller than you *now.* Nature is nature, Howlasco — and you are nothing. NOTHING."

I hit him once, in the chest, everything I had. It was like hitting a bale of old theater curtains. And then it wasn't like anything at all — the pelt folded forward, empty, the pegs clacked on the boards like sticks, and a sigh went through the house from cellar to shingle like a hundred years of held breath let go — and Howlasco House fell down dead.

We walked out at noon, the three of us, past the chapel. The card on the door had gone blank.

Old Chestnut got his proof, they tell me, though he was past reading; and the house is being pulled down this spring, the timber going into a crèche, because Edith insists on the joke.

Florence never got her million. I'd give mine all back — but she'd have said, being Florence, "darling boy, spend it loudly."

So I do. In memory of her, and of the party that finally, finally ended.
