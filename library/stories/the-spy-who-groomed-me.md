# The Spy Who Groomed Me

*A parody of "The Spy Who Loved Me"*

Two banana submarines vanished that spring, one from each side of the Cold Jungle, each with missiles enough to ruin everybody's afternoon. In Cairo, beneath the Great Vine Pyramid, the Jungle Service sent its best and the Kremlin Grooming Bureau sent theirs. M's briefing had been short. "Both submarines, both sides, one villain," he said. "You will cooperate with the Soviets, Banna. That is an order." "Entirely," said Banna, stealing the word before Moscow could trademark it.

"Major Anya Apesasova," she said, snapping a salute crisp enough to slice bread. "Triple-X. My orders are to cooperate with you, Commander Banna, and I will. Cooperate. Entirely."

"And your unofficial orders?"

"To remember that you killed my mate in the alpine operation," she said pleasantly. "He went down swinging. When this mission ends, so do you."

"Understood," said Banna. "Partners, then. Shall I carry your files?"

"You may carry your own banana, Commander, and stay upwind."

Their contact was to be Professor Fekkesh, the papyrologist, who knew where the submarines' tracking data had been sold. He was found beneath the Sphinx's left paw an hour before the meeting, dead as an audit, with his water bottle full of shark. The bite marks in the steel cable of his office door told Banna everything, and the witness baboon who had seen the biter confirmed it while shaking.

"Big," the witness said. "Gorilla-big. Teeth like a coconut crusher. He smiled at me, monsieur. I have retired from sightseeing." He gave the rest from behind a market stall, selling telescopes nobody had ever used for stargazing.

His name was Jaws, and he boarded the Ex-vine-press the same night they did. The fight on the roof of the speeding sleeper — Banna's dinner jacket against seven feet of dentistry — ended with Jaws biting through the coupling and falling eighty feet into a fig grove, where he walked away chewing.

Everything pointed to Karl Stromberg, an enormously round orangutan aqua-tycoon who lived on an undersea estate called Bananatlantis, quoted octopus poetry, and had recently fed his head of research to a shark. Q Branch issued the transport with their usual sermon: the Lotus Sprout-rit, a jungle runabout that converted, at the touch of a button, into a submarine, because Q Branch believed every problem in nature could be solved by amphibious motoring. Also stocked: underwater missiles, a wet bar, and a leaflet about optimism titled YOUR CAR IS ALSO A BOAT.

Sardinia brought the whole cast together. Stromberg's helicopter pilot, a chic macaque named Naomi, chased the Sprout-rit off a jetty and out over the bay — at which point Banna pressed the button, the car grew fins, and the ocean accepted them like a valet. Naomi circled for the kill. The Lotus surfaced one missile. "Splash, one ferry," said Banna, who kept score. Underwater, Stromberg's divers met them with knives; the Sprout-rit answered with a spray Q Branch marketed as seasoning. The divers surfaced sneezing, unemployed.

Jaws pursued them through the olive groves on a motorcycle with a sidecar full of grudge; Banna unhooked the sidecar at speed, and Jaws rode it into a fig warehouse and ate the evidence.

On Bananatlantis, cover story honeymooning biologists, they took lunch under glass while Stromberg ladled squid-ink consommé and quoted his own verse about the ocean's patience. "The sea," he observed, "does not negotiate. It digests." Anya asked whether his architects had budgeted for tidal wear. Stromberg said something about tenants. It was, both agreed later, the least relaxing lunch of the Cold Jungle.

Then the sea opened, the tanker Liparus waited, and they motored in over the teeth of the dock.

Inside the supertanker, the two missing submarines hung in flooded cradles — swallowed in open water, the way a frog takes a fly and calls it shipping.

"Banna," said Stromberg, rising from a couch of kelp pillows. "James Banna. You've made a serious mistake, coming here. Sit." A trapdoor flipped Banna into a wicker chair with a gun beneath the armrest. "Observe, Commander. Two submarines, two crews, four missiles. Moscow fires on New York; Washington fires on Moscow. The jungle burns, the surface boils, and I —" he gestured lovingly at the aquarium — "inherit the sea. A new Atlantis. A water world for apes. My webbed fingers were not a birth defect, Mr. Banna. They were a preview."

"You're out of your tree," said Banna.

"I own the tree, Commander. I own the whole flooded orchard."

They were locked, naturally, in a luxurious cabin, which is where the mission's true diplomacy occurred. Anya sat armed on one bunk; Banna sat unarmed on the other; and between interrogations of captured scientists and plotting escapes, Banna — who could not help himself, being a chimp, and chimps are social apes with social rituals — began, delicately, to groom.

He started with her shoulder. She stiffened like a dead branch. He moved, respectfully, to the back of the neck, where every Kremlin major secretly carries the day's tensions and at least two unattended lice. By the time the escape signal came, Anya Apesasova had made a decision of state.

"You're still a killer, Commander."

"I'm a chimp who was sent up a tree with a bad map," said Banna. "Your mate was a good monkey. I think about him at every sunset."

"I know," said Anya, and picked — gently — a louse from his collar. "I've decided to be extremely angry with you next week."

"Tuesday," said Banna. "You'll be busy Monday, being shot at."

"Correct," said Anya. "Thank you for scheduling this."

The uprising of two captured submarine crews turned the Liparus into a barrel of gorilla warfare. The captain of the American boat, a gorilla named Carter who had spent his captivity memorizing the guards' card game, led the breakout on a signal Banna improvised from a dinner tray. The control room fell; the countdown did not — Stromberg had left it running with a timer's obedience. With thirty seconds on the board, two crews and one chimp retargeted four missiles by hand, which is in no manual anywhere, and the pads blew to confetti in two friendly hemispheres with nine seconds to spare. Anya spent all nine not forgiving him. Jaws, dropped into the shark tank for finishing school, wrestled the shark to a draw, shrugged, and swam off into legend.

Stromberg heaved himself up, harpoon in hand, poetry in his throat.

"You sail off the edge of the world, Stromberg," said Banna, and introduced him to his own aquarium.

Bananatlantis went down with its master, its piranhas, and its terrible poetry. In the escape pod, drifting toward a rescue ship and a diplomatic incident, Anya's pistol found Banna's chest one final time.

"Orders," she said, "are orders."

"Not tonight," said Banna. "Tonight, we're just two monkeys who saved the jungle and badly need a groom."

Anya lowered the pistol, sighed like an entire bureau, and sat him down to work the tangles out of his fur, while the radio crackled from London: "Say again, Banna. Report. Banna, are you there?"

Banna, eyes closed, keyed the transmitter with one toe.

"Keeping the troop's end up, sir," he said. "Nobody grooms it better."
