# Hairy Potter and the Prisoner of Azkabanana

*A parody of "Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban"*

Hairy Potter had never run away from anything in his life, except once, and that was from a haircut. But when Aunt Peelsia's visiting sister declared his tail "an abomination and a fire hazard" and suggested trimming it with the garden shears, Hairy inflated her like a balloon animal, stuffed his trunk with bananas, and left the Dursleys' treehouse under cover of night.

That was how he came to be standing in the dark holding his wand — a fine peeled stick of willow, twelve inches, springy — when the Branch Bus materialized out of nowhere. It was a double-decker banyan, driven by a macaque in a purple uniform, that hurtled through the treetops rearranging squirrels.

"'Ere you are, young gorilla," said Stan, the conductor. "Where to? And watch the news — you're on it. You heard about Serious Black?"

Everyone had heard about Serious Black. The most dangerous prisoner ever held in the fortress of Azkabanana, the rotten-banana prison out at sea, and the first monkey ever to escape it. They said he'd betrayed Hairy's parents to He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Groomed. They said he was coming for Hairy next.

At Hogwarts School of Monkeycraft and Skulduggery, the trouble started on the train, when the windows iced over and the Dementeds came gliding through — hooded, gray, and hungry for happiness. One dipped its scabby face toward Hairy and began, quite literally, to unpeel his joy. Somewhere far away a lady monkey was screaming.

"Chocolate," said the new teacher, Professor Lupine, breaking off a hunk afterward. "Eat. And next time, think of the happiest thing you can. For me, it's an uninterrupted grooming session."

The school year crawled along on guard rails. Dementeds ringed the boundaries. At the first match of the season they drifted onto the pitch, and Hairy fell fifty feet off his racing branch, which is how he met the ground, the hospital wing, and — courtesy of his friends — a Firebranch, the fastest racing branch in the world, which was then confiscated on suspicion of being cursed, returned, and only then flown, in that order of injustice. Hairy's Defense Against the Dark Arts lessons became Patronus lessons, because Hairy's happiest memories — winning the Quidditch-of-the-Trees cup, his first broomstick-tailed swing — kept sliding into one bright image: a silvery ape he did not remember, laughing.

Meanwhile Hermonkee Granger, the cleverest chimp in the year and possibly the century, was appearing in two classrooms at once and looking exhausted about it. And Rondald Weasel's pet rat, Scabbers, was going bald with nerves, which for a rat is a kind of confession.

Into this, Fred-and-George Weasel handed Hairy a scrap of old parchment with a map on it that insulted Snipe on sight and tracked every ape in the castle by name. The Marauder's Map was signed by its makers: Messrs Moonkey, Wormtail, Padfoot, and Prongs — "mischief managed," said Fred, "and occasionally expanded."

It was a good year for lessons in fear. Professor Lupine's boggart class taught the counter-spell Ridikulus, the whole art of comedy compressed into one word: the thing you dread appears, and you make it ridiculous, and it dies. Rondald's giant spider lost its legs to roller skates. The whole class was still laughing when the boggart found Professor Lupine and turned into the full moon, and the laughter stopped being a lesson.

And in Divination, Professor Treelawny — who predicted Hairy's death every term, the way other teachers take attendance — suddenly went rigid, her voice dropping into someone else's: "Tonight — before midnight — the servant will break free — and return — to his master —" and then she came back to herself and asked if she'd been napping. She had prophesied. Nobody noticed, which is how prophecy usually gets its start.

Everywhere Hairy looked, meanwhile, the Grim kept looking back — a great black shape with glowing eyes, on the quidditch pitch, in the teacups, at the bottom of the pond. Death's omen, said the books. Noted, said Hairy, and kept going; and it turned out to be Padfoot, a great shaggy black howler who was hanging around the castle — as the map had been hinting all year — for the oldest reason in the world: a friend.

Then, one triumphant and terrible night, the truth came out in the Shrieking Treehouse. Serious Black — a great grizzled blackback with haunted eyes and a shag that hadn't seen a grooming comb in twelve years — stood over Rondald's bed.

"I never betrayed your parents," he growled. "It was Peter Peeledgrew. The rat. All those years, sleeping in a boy's pocket."

The rat wriggled, transformed, and there stood a small weaselly monkey with a missing toe, wheedling immediately. And Professor Snipe arrived to collect them all like overdue library books, and had to be floated out of the room in a hammock of his own dignity — and before anyone could groom the truth out of anyone, the full moon rose, Professor Lupine went loudly, magnificently lemur, and in the chaos Peeledgrew escaped — because monkey justice is quick, but monkey attention spans are quicker.

Dementeds closed in on Serious Black by the forest's edge, a hundred hooded mouths opening at once, and Hairy — certain that the silver ape who had saved him at the lake was his father — waited for rescue. Nobody came. Something in him understood, finally, that it never had been anyone but himself: and he raised his willow wand and thought of the silver ape, of laughter, of everything ripe and good in the world.

"EXPECTO BANANUM!"

The Patronus erupted from his wand — a colossal, glowing silver banana that charged the Dementeds down and sent them flapping off into the night. And out of it padded the great bright ape, Prongs, his father — who had been the laughter all along — and it winked, and was gone.

By treaty of plot and a small device called the Time-Turner that Hermonkee had been wearing around her neck all year like a guilty walnut (the secret of her double classes, the reason she looked like finals season personified), they went back three hours, rescued the condemned Hippogibbon Beaverbeak from an unjust executioner, hurled expired fruit at Rondald's own past self's head on principle, and delivered Serious to the tallest tower window, where the great creature knelt to let the escaped prisoner climb aboard.

"You know the map still works, don't you?" Serious called down as they rose into the stars. "I solemnly swear I am up to no good — and I finally am."

The year ended with the Quidditch-of-the-Trees final — Rondald in goal, the stands hexed silent, Captain Wood vibrating with seven years of wanting — which Rondald won with a save he later described as "mostly falling accurately." Captain Wood cried on the cup, polished it, and cried on it again; Hermonkee threw her Time-Turner into the past where it belonged; and there was, at last, a parcel Serious had sent ahead — a brand-new, officially-his Firebranch, polished to a gleam and perfectly springy.

Because somewhere over the mountains, a wrongly accused ape was flying to freedom on a Hippogibbon, which is not a sentence anyone at Azkabanana would have believed — and which every young monkey in the castle would repeat all summer, going ape, until the bananas themselves were sick of hearing it.
