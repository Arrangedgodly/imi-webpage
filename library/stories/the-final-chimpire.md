# The Final Chimpire

*A parody of "The Final Empire"*

Ash fell on Lemurthadel like gray snow that never melted, because a thousand years ago the Lord Groomer climbed to the top of the world and decided the sky was his.

The Final Chimpire had one law: *Ape shall not groom ape.* All grooming flowed from the Lord Groomer — and so all loyalty did too, because in monkey society, controlling the grooming is controlling everything. Beneath the great houses lived the Scruff, who picked the green 'nanas for the lords and ate the peel. Everyone obeyed. Everyone was very neatly combed.

Everyone except Kelsier, who smiled too much and had survived the worst place in the world — the Pits of Hathi-Sin, where Scruff crawled into the earth to pluck the golden ati-yum 'nanas that bud there, the most precious fruit ever grown. A thousand died each year. Kelsier crawled out. After that, smiling was his religion.

"Half the troops in this city would kill you," said Vin.

"The other half would pay to watch," said Kelsier. "That's show business."

Vin was a Scruff street-chimp, all knuckles and suspicion, who had learned young that trust is a heavier thing to carry than stolen fruit. Kelsier found her because she could do what he did: *peelomancy*. Swallow a fleck of metal rolled in banana, and burn it in your belly. Steely-nana to Push on metal. Ironbana to Pull. Pewterfruit for strength. Tinskin for senses. Zest to inflame a troop's emotions, Brasso to soothe them. Copper-peel to hide your burning, bronze-rind to hear others burn. Most were born with one power. A rare few could burn them all, riding the mists from rooftop to rooftop.

"We're going to steal the Chimpire," Kelsier told her, cheerful as theft. "The whole thing. The Lord Groomer, the great houses, the sky — all of it. I've got a bunch."

He did have a bunch: Dockson, who organized chaos; Hambone, a wrestling orangutan who pondered everything; Breeze, a bonobo so soothing he could talk a leopard into a nap; Clubs, a grumpy gorilla; and Sazed, a lemur Keeper who kept the memory of dead faiths in the little brass rings through his tail, one religion per ring, "for someday, Mistress Vin, folk may wish to believe again."

There was also Ore-fur, a Furandra — a pile of fur that wore the shapes of others — delivered under contract. "I am bound to serve you, Mistress," it said. "Bound to feed, also."

"That's the contract?" said Vin.

"That is the center of the contract," said Ore-fur, and ate her slipper ceremonially.

Vin's job was the parties. Scrubbed, silked, and re-named Valette of House Renoo, she swung from gala to gala among the high-furred lords, learning that aristocracy is mostly fruit-arrangement and fear. There she met Elend Venture of House Bent-Tail — a bookish young silverback who read troop law at balls and was the only noble who apologized when he stepped on a servant's tail. It was extremely annoying for both of them, being in love.

Not everyone at the balls loved Valette. Shan Ela-rill, a high lady with two Peelborn bodyguards and a face like a licked knife, challenged her to a rooftop moonlight stroll — which in Lemurthadel means a duel. Shan came on in perfect steely-nana form. Vin, who had learned fighting in gutters, fought like a gutter: she threw her whole purse, Shan Pushed it grandly away, and Vin Pushed herself off her own coin and arrived early, from below. First rule of the high houses, broken: never fight a street chimp on her own budget.

The crew worked the city like a fruit cart. Breeze talked a garrison into a nap. Hambone pondered a wall down. Kelsier put on a mask and became the Survivor of the Flames, robbing the great houses in person, publicly, hilariously, so the Scruff would have a story with a grinning hero in it. And Marsh — Kelsier's brother, hard and quiet — went into the Conventacle of Seran to spy on the Chimquisitors, whose eyes had been replaced with curved bronze 'nana-blades. Marsh did not come out. Later, at all the worst moments thereafter, something watched from the shadows with bronze where its eyes had been.

Kelsier had one more weapon: the Eleventh 'nana, a strange metal bought from a mad tinkering tarsier, said to show an ape the self he might have been. When he finally faced the Lord Groomer — a black-furred colossus with a silver back like a shield, older than the ash — Kelsier burned it in the tyrant's face, and for one heartbeat the Lord Groomer saw himself as a young gardener with clean paws. He flinched. It was the only wound Kelsier ever gave him, and it did not close.

But the heart of the plan was never the crew. It was the Scruff. Ten thousand steps, and most of them were lies, but the true step was this: make the Scruff believe they could stand up. The Scruff army rose too early and died bravely, and Kelsier smiled at the funerals too, because despair, he said, is only a troop that hasn't been organized yet. And then he let himself be caught.

The Lord Groomer struck Kelsier down before ten thousand Scruff. Kelsier lay there a moment. Then, terribly, he laughed.

"Look on me," he rasped, to the crowd, to the sky, to the Groomer's astonished face. "You can kill a monkey. You can't kill an idea. I'm the Survivor of the Pits — and there's always another 'nana."

He died laughing. The square rose. Lemurthadel burned with rebellion, and the Scruff tore the emblems off their cages, and the Final Chimpire found out what a thousand years of neatly-combed resentment weighs.

It ended that night in Kredik Claw. Vin climbed through smoke and Chimquisitors to the throne of the Lord Groomer, and she was not alone: the mists had come into the city, and they streamed into her like a river finding the sea. Sazed stood at the gates with a forgotten war-religion burning in his rings, holding them open. Clubs held the courtyard, grumbling heroically. And on the roof, something with bronze eyes watched and did not stop her, and turned, and walked away.

"You are a child," said the Lord Groomer.

"I'm the last mistake you'll ever groom," said Vin, and Pushed a golden 'nana-coin through the dawn.

He fell at last like a very old tree, and before the end he wept and clutched a worn diary, muttering that he had only ever been trying to hold the jungle together. "I was never a god," he said. "I was just the best-groomed ape alive."

In the week after, the Scruff councils met in smoke-stained halls and voted on everything, including whether to vote. Elend was crowned because he had the most books, which the Scruff considered a radical new form of legitimacy. Clubs was made a general and grumbled about it all the way to the map table. Breeze retired to a vineyard and was back within the week, complaining that comfort was exhausting. And Sazed quietly copied out the tyrant's diary, because someone should remember that monsters keep journals too.

When the ash settled, Elend stood with Vin on the tower of Kredik Claw, the Scruff cheering below, the sky showing its first blue in a thousand years.

"So what happens now?" he asked.

"Now," said Vin, watching the mists curl and gather, patient as something waiting, "we find out what the mists want."

Because far below, in the dark under the roots of the world, something opened one eye — and smiled at all the lovely rot to come.
