# The Big Friendly Gorilla

*A parody of "The BFG"*

The witching hour found Sofia still awake in the treehouse orphanage. All the other little monkeys were asleep, and Matron Tapir had never allowed curtains, because curtains, she said, was for softies.

And that is why Sofia saw it: an enormous shape moving through the jungle with the quietness of fog, and in its hands a long thin trumpet of bamboo.

The shape stopped beside the orphanage tree. It leaned down. It put the trumpet to the window of a sleeping tarsier — and blew. And in through the window, invisible as moonlight, went something soft and shimmering and golden.

Then the great head turned, and two ears as big as satellite dishes swiveled toward Sofia's open eyes.

"A monkey is watching!" boomed a voice like coconuts rolling downhill, and the next moment a hand the size of a hammock plucked Sofia from her nest and swept her up into the dark.

They galloped for hours — over ridges in a single stride, through whole valleys in a leap — until they reached a country where the rocks looked like gorillas and the gorillas looked like mountains.

Giant Country.

Because that is what he was. A gorilla, twenty-four feet tall, with a nose like a canyon and a smile you could camp in. And around him, in the purple dusk, other gorillas: the Fleshlumpeater, the Bloodbottler, the Bonecruncher, the Childchewer, the Gizzardgulper. As Sofia watched from inside her captor's pocket, they set off galloping in nine different directions, each with a destination.

"Whereto tonight, lads?" boomed the Fleshlumpeater.

"Gibbon-grabbing in Gibbon-land!" "Chimp-chomping in Chimpland!" "Marmoset-munching up the river!" And off they thundered, each one, the BFG explained grimly, to a different troop every night, so no jungle ever got used to them.

"And you?" whispered Sofia.

"I doesn't go," said the huge gorilla. "I is the disgrace of the family."

"What is you doing with that little monkey?" snarled the Bloodbottler, ambling back and sniffing horribly. "Give it to me! I is having a monkey for supper!"

"No, you isn't!" cried the huge gorilla, hiding Sofia behind his back — and then, in a flash, thrusting her deep into a vast slimy vegetable thing that stood on the table. "I isn't doing nothing! I is only eating my snozzbanana!"

The Bloodbottler seized a fistful of the revolting, backwards-growing snozzbanana (which tastes of frogskins and sour cabbage) and crammed it into his mouth, and gagged so tremendously he went off home to bed, and did not find Sofia, who was by then extremely squashed and extremely relieved.

In the giant gorilla's cave, Sofia recovered among shelves and shelves of glass jars, each one glowing and swirling and labeled in wobbly writing. Dreams.

"I is the BFG," her captor said shyly. "The Big Friendly Gorilla. I is the only one of them that doesn't guzzle monkeys. The others goes gobbling gibbons and munching marmosets and chomping chimpanzees every night. I is a disgrace to gorilladom. All I does is dreams."

He told her everything: how he caught dreams with a net at the tip-top of the tallest kapok, where they roost like butterflies; how he bottled them; and how, each night, he went blowing them with his trumpet through the windows of sleeping monkeys. He showed her the jar he'd blown to the tarsier: *I DREAMS I IS FLYING WITH A THOUSAND FRIENDS.* A golden phizzwizard, he called it — the best kind. On another shelf sat the black ones: trogglehumpers, nightmares, which he caught only to lock away.

He took her out once, at dawn, to see how it was done. From the tip-top of the tallest kapok in Giant Country, dreams flitted up like fireflies in dresses, and the BFG stood among them swinging his net with the patience of a fisherman and the grace of a falling tree.

"Netting is all in the wrist," he said, and missed a golden one, and caught a jar's worth of others: *I DREAMS I IS BRAVE AT THE DENTIST'S. I DREAMS MY TAIL GROWS BACK. I DREAMS I IS THE BEST GROOMER IN THE TROOP AND EVERYONE CLAPS.*

"That one," said the BFG fondly, "I has blown to forty different barbers."

Supper was snozzbanana, sliced, fried, and snozzbanana again. "It is disgusterous," said the BFG cheerfully, "but it is all I has, and eating it is my patriotic duty." Then he poured two glasses of frobscottle, the bubbly green drink whose bubbles go down instead of up, which has one very important consequence.

The consequence arrived. The BFG's thunderous whizzpop rattled the dream jars and blew a passing parrot off its branch.

"Whizzpopping," explained the BFG, "is the sound of a happy heart."

"But the others," said Sofia. "They'll eat every monkey in the world someday. We have to stop them."

"How?" groaned the BFG. "Us is only two, and one of us is little."

"Then we get more of us. We'll need the Queen. Queen Lantana, who rules the whole jungle troop. Give her the nightmare — a dream so real she knows it's true — and me on her window branch to prove it. Queens don't dither."

They mixed the nightmare themselves, in the cave, shaking ingredients into one black bottle: a squeeze of gorilla-shaped shadow, a pinch of screaming, a drop of midnight. The BFG held it to the light and read the label aloud, in a voice that wobbled: *GORILLAS IS GUZZLING THE NURSERY TROOP AT DAWN.*

"I hates this one," he said. "Dreams like this is my worst work." And he blew it through the palace leaves, set Sofia gently on the window branch, and waited below, knees knocking like bamboo.

Queen Lantana woke with a shriek that woke the guard, and found, on her sill, a small monkey exactly as dreamt.

"You," said the Queen. "You are real. Therefore the gorillas are real. Therefore — breakfast. Sit down, both of you." (The BFG's head came through the window, which was the only part of him that fit.) "Now. Tell me everything, slowly, and nobody say 'guzzling' while I'm chewing."

It took the elephants. The elephants took the sleeping-powder bananas the BFG had brewed, crept to Giant Country in the small hours, and fed them to the nine sleeping gorillas — and the Fleshlumpeater, even asleep, was dreaming his own personal nightmare, shrieking "IT IS BITING ME! A LITTLE MONKEY IS BITING ME!" and swatting at his own huge foot, so that the elephants nearly laughed loud enough to wake him.

The Gizzardgulper woke halfway over the sea, and took one look at the elephants, one look at the waves, and went politely back to sleep on the grounds that whatever was happening was clearly somebody else's fault.

Then the elephants lifted all nine snoring giants — the Fleshlumpeater, the Bloodbottler, and all — and carried them out over the ocean, and dropped them on a far island where nothing grows but snozzbananas.

"Let them eat snozzbananas forever," said Queen Lantana. "And if they complain, they can write to the BFG, who will visit at Christmas with his trumpet."

Back home, Sofia was given a proper nest with a window and a banana tree of her own, and the BFG was made Royal Dream-Blower to the Entire Jungle, with a grand cave beside the palace and lessons in speaking proper.

"I is — I mean, I am," he said carefully, standing proudly at the palace gate, "the happiest gorilla in the whole world. I could whizzpop with joy."

And he did. Twice.
