# The Number of the Fleas

*A parody of "The Number of the Beast"*

It began, as catastrophes usually do, at a party.

Zebadiah Carter — bonobo, test-glider pilot, owner of the finest pair of brass-buckled pilot gauntlets in four counties — offered to drive two fellow guests home. They were Deety, a brilliant young chimp mathematician whose father had named her, with love and no mercy at all, Dejah Thoris Banana; and that father himself, Professor Jake Banana, chairman of branchematics at the university, a fat cheerful scholar who had that very evening acquired a new wife. The new wife was Hilda, a sharp-eyed widow of the sharpie tradition, who sized Zeb up in one glance, decided he would do, and proceeded to run the rest of the night, the week, and eventually the multiverse, from the navigator's seat.

The trouble was waiting on the road home: a barricade, a burning hay-cart, and monkeys in black hats shooting at them with what Hilda estimated as "hunting rifles and poor manners."

"Duck, children," said Professor Jake mildly, as a bullet took his hat. "Zebadiah, I strongly recommend the emergency climb."

Zeb's car was the *Gay Deceiver* — a four-seat, fig-leaf-and-lattice glider he had rebuilt from a military wreck with a contraband lift-vine drive — and she went up the sky like a slapped gibbon. It was on the way up, with the black hats' second car sprouting wings of its own, that Deety finished a computation she had been chewing all evening and turned around in her seat with the face of a monkey who has looked behind the curtain.

"They're not bandits," she said. "They're *organized*. Zeb — the numbers work. It's the continua. Six to the sixth to the sixth. Papa, tell them what six to the sixth to the sixth is."

"Six raised to the sixth power," said Professor Jake, reloading calmly, "is forty-six thousand six hundred fifty-six. Raised to the sixth again — well. It is a number with over three thousand digits, and every single digit of it is a *world*. Every possible universe, my dears, and they are *real*, and they are *numbered*, and the monsters in the black hats have found a way to walk between them, and they do not intend that anyone who has seen them do it should ever file a report."

"Wait," said Hilda. "You're telling me that's the number of the beast."

"No, dear," said the Professor, and squeezed her hand. "It's the number of the *fleas*. Every world has its troop, every troop has its fur, and every fur has its fleas — six to the sixth to the sixth of them, hopping world to world forever. We are now, I regret to say, the fleas that bit the wrong dog."

The black hats — who styled themselves the Ecology of the Purrse, and were, in the plain terms Zeb preferred, cats: actual black cats with actual little hats, organized across realities like a flea's dark library — chased them through the Long Beach of one world and the rainy night of another, and would have had them at the third had Deety not, in pure fury at being shot at over her own wedding-adjacent celebration, rewritten the *Gay Deceiver's* twist-vine equations in flight and dropped the family into the one kind of world the cats could not follow: a world that was *made up*.

They fell out of the twist over a country of impossible colors: yellow brick, to be exact, running through an orchard of fighting trees toward a city of green glass where Hilda negotiated, in twenty minutes, an audience with the ruler of the Land of Ookz — a squirrel monkey in a crown — and a favor from the real power behind its throne, Glindamarmoset of the North, who verified Deety's mathematics with one raised eyebrow, an eyebrow that had seen stranger.

"Fictional universes," said Glindamarmoset, kindly, "are simply the ones with good publicity. Six-to-the-sixth-to-the-sixth, child? Yes. And a made-up world is as real as its details, and yours, I notice, are excellent. Would you like the tin woodcutter's number? He's mad for branchematics."

From Ookz they hopscotched by Deety's equations to Barsimian, the red world of the dying seas, where a gray-furred warlord of enormous chest and old-fashioned courtesy named John Gorilla, Warlord of Mars, examined Zeb's gauntlets, pronounced them "almost worthy of a green Martian," and — upon learning Deety was named for his own princess — stood her on a table, declared a holiday, and sent them off with a guard of ten tall red monkeys and a letter of introduction to a mutual friend.

The mutual friend was the reason the black hats were losing.

Because it turned out, as Glindamarmoset had hinted and John Gorilla confirmed, that the six-to-the-sixth-to-the-sixth worlds are not a chaos. They are a *grove*. Every story any monkey ever told around any fire is a real world somewhere in the braid, and every teller — the teller, not the tale — is its founder: the world holds still, and observes, and *becomes*. Glindamarmoset called it the World as Troop. Professor Jake called it pantheistic monkeybusiness. Hilda called it an organizational opportunity.

The fleas of the multiverse, in other words, were not the cats. The fleas were the *storytellers* — and every monkey who ever made up a tale had been seeding worlds with every sentence, which meant the cats' whole program of quiet conquest was doomed by arithmetic: you cannot hold six-to-the-sixth-to-the-sixth worlds when every world is generating new troopers, new heroes, and new sequels faster than you can put hats on your agents.

The cats made one last great try for it, at the borders of Barsimian, a whole squadron of them in formation, hats on, whiskers forward — and that was the night the *Gay Deceiver* fought a midair running battle, Hilda navigating four universes deep while Zeb flew inside the turn like a bonobo who had finally been given license to go completely and gloriously ape, Deety calling the twist-vectors, and Professor Jake in the back seat with a fire extinguisher, a flare pistol, and the happiest grin of a fat academic in the recorded history of any world.

They won the way monkeys win: messily, melodically, and with unexpected allies — the fighting trees had sent word ahead; John Gorilla's red guard arrived through the twist behind the cats; and a small squad of Ookz flying monkeys, who will follow anyone's flag if the story's good, dropped out of the rainbow with pitchforks.

The party afterward ran eleven days and is still studied.

It was held, naturally, at the estate of a mutual friend of everyone — the oldest monkey alive, a gray reprobate named Lazarus Longtail, who greeted the four of them at the gate of his Grandfather Branch with a fig in one hand and a footstool in the other, shouting, "Zebadiah! You still owe me a rematch at knuckles, and your new wife cheats!"

There were bonobos of the starboard watch, and a mandrill magistrate in full facial color, and a macaque girl raised by a talking book, and a two-headed gibbon arguing with itself about insurance, and a small tamarin with an enormous white mustache who declined all drinks and asked everyone, pleasantly, who was holding the paw. There was a bonobo nurse with a silver banana on a chain. There was an orangutan in a bathrobe holding court on the porch about the decline of modern everything. And at midnight they all stood, monkeys of forty worlds and four authors' worth of impossibilities, and Lazarus Longtail raised the toast that Glindamarmoset had flown in to hear.

"To the Worlds as Troop," said the oldest monkey alive. "May every monkey who ever told a story be reminded that somewhere, a world hangs on their next sentence — so end it well, you young fools, and put somebody *kind* in charge of the sequel!"

"Hear, hear!" said Jake Banana, weeping freely.

And that is where the four of them live now, when they are not out hopping the braid — Zebadiah and Deety, Jake and Hilda, the *Gay Deceiver* waxed and fueled in the yard, the black cats in slow retreat across the six-to-the-sixth-to-the-sixth, and the fleas — the storytelling, story-loving, story-*owed* fleas of every world in the braid — as numerous as the mathematics require, and considerably better company.

Deety did the final count herself, and had it engraved on the hangar door, where every monkey who zooms past can read it:

*THE NUMBER OF THE WORLDS IS 6^6^6. THE NUMBER OF THE FLEAS IS THE SAME. SO IS THE NUMBER OF THE STORIES. — D.T.B., NAVIGATOR, WHO CHECKED.*
