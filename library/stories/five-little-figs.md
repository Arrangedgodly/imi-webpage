# Five Little Figs

*A parody of "Five Little Pigs"*

Sixteen years after her mother was hanged for murder, a young gibbon named Carla Figg climbed the three flights to Hercule Poirot's flat, put a faded letter on his table, and said, "I want you to find out who really killed my father."

Poirot, a fastidious orangutan of great renown, adjusted the ripeness of the fig he was eating and read the letter. It was written in a shaky hand, in a prison cell, by Caroline Figg — and it said only this: *I did not kill Amyas. Your loving mother.*

"You realize," said Poirot, "that everyone believes she did."

"Everyone can go bananas," said Carla. "She wrote this on the day she died. Mothers do not lie in their last letters. Not mothers like mine."

Poirot studied her. She had her father's springy ears and her mother's steady eyes, and she was engaged to a serious young gibbon named John Rendall, who stood behind her trying to look like legal counsel and mostly looking like a coat stand.

"It is sixteen years cold," said Poirot. "There are no fingerprints, no witnesses, no banana peels. There are only — how many were at the house that day? Five. *Five little figs*, one might say, hanging on one tree. Very well, mademoiselle. I will interview the figs."

---

**The fig that went to market** was Philip Baboon, a stockbroker of the Banana Exchange, broad and bristling and rich. He had been at Figbarrow, the Crales' house, the weekend the painter Amyas Figg drank poison and died.

"Caroline did it, of course," said Philip. "Amyas was flaunting his latest model under her very nose — a girl of twenty, if that. Caroline was a bonobo, Monsieur Poirot. Gentle, but gentle creatures snap. I always said she had the temper of a wet chimpanzee."

"You admired her," said Poirot.

"I — what? I did not."

"You admired her very much, and she married the painter, and you have disliked her ever after with the special energy of a monkey pretending he never wanted the banana. It is, I assure you, the most common crime in the jungle. Proceed."

**The fig that stayed at home** was Philip's elder brother, Meredith Baboon, a mild herbalist who lived next door to Figbarrow with his distilling glasses and his poisons.

"The coniine came from my laboratory," Meredith admitted, mournfully picking a leaf to pieces. "Spotted hemlock. Caroline was seen taking a little bottle of it that very morning. She told the court she meant it for herself — that she was so unhappy she had thought of — of leaving. No one believed her. One doesn't, with a bonobo."

"And who else was near your hemlock?"

"Anyone, Monsieur Poirot. Half the county came through my orchard that day. It was warm. The figs were ripening. Everyone was swinging by."

**The fig that had the roast fig** was Elsa Greenmonkey herself — the model, the mistress, now Lady Dittisham, cold and lovely and rich. She had been twenty that summer; Amyas had been painting her in a white dress against the fig tree, and the whole county knew he meant to marry her.

"People say I poisoned him out of jealousy," said Elsa. "Look at me, Monsieur Poirot. Do I look like a monkey who does things badly? If I had poisoned Amyas, I'd have poisoned the wife too, and inherited the figs."

"That," said Poirot mildly, "is the most honest thing anyone has said to me all week. Also, madame, the most rehearsed."

**The fig that had none** was Cecilia Guenon, the governess, now grey-muzzled in a cottage by the sea.

"Caroline asked me to look after the children that day," she said, "which she never did. And she said goodbye to little Carla with the kind of hug you give a monkey you fear you will not see again. I have taught forty years of young primates, Monsieur Poirot, and I know the difference between a goodbye and a *goodbye*. She thought she was taking the fall for someone."

"For whom?"

The governess's hands folded. "For Angela. Her little sister. Fifteen that summer, and full of pranks."

**The fig that cried wee-wee-wee all the way home** was Angela herself — now Dame Angela, the famous archaeologist, one eye clouded pale where Caroline, as a child, had thrown a paperweight at her in a temper. She had been sent away to school soon after the murder.

"I hated him," Angela said, without apology. "Amyas. He teased me as adults tease clever children, which is to say cruelly. I told Caroline once I'd like to put something in his drink — a purgative, a pinch of itchweed, nothing worse. A *prank*, Monsieur Poirot. Children plan crimes the way monkeys plan heists: loudly, and with snacks." She touched the blind eye. "Caroline spent her whole life atoning to me for this. And I spent mine suspecting she had atoned in some final, terrible way — that she believed I had done it, and died in my place. Find out which it was. I beg you."

---

Poirot wrote to each of the five figs and asked for an account of that day, written in their own words. Then he laid the five letters side by side, like five figs on a shelf, and tapped them one by one with one orange finger.

"A-ha," he said.

"You have something?" asked John Rendall, who had appointed himself clerk of the investigation.

"I have a temperature," said Poirot. "Consider, young sir. Amyas Figg died of coniine in his *beer*. Caroline brought him a bottle, cold from the stream — every witness agrees the bottle was cold. And yet Philip Baboon's account records Amyas's last complaint, spoken in the afternoon: *'Everything tastes of spoiled 'nana today.'* A-ha! One does not complain that everything tastes foul — monsieur has already *tasted* foul — and then drink, with pleasure, a fresh cold beer. The poison was already inside him. It was in a *warm* glass of beer, served earlier, by somebody else. And who was in the garden studio all morning, handing the great artist his refreshments, keeping them warm beside her while she posed?"

The room — Meredith's drawing room, where Poirot had gathered all five figs, plus Carla and her fiancé — went very still. Elsa Greenmonkey did not move.

"You watched Caroline take the hemlock from Meredith's laboratory," said Poirot, "and you followed her home and stole it from her room. Then you heard, that afternoon, what I suspect you heard through the fig leaves: Amyas telling his wife that of course he was not leaving her, that the child — you, madame — would be sent home when the picture was finished, that painters say anything to keep a model sitting still until the light goes."

"It was not the light that went," said Elsa.

"It was you. You gave him the warm glass. Caroline believed her sister had played her prank and killed him by accident — so she took away the cold bottle, washed the glass, and said nothing, even to save her own neck. She was not protecting her crime. She was paying her debt for a thrown paperweight, sixteen years of installments, and one hanging. And you, madame, married a lord, and hung your white dress in a wardrobe, and waited for the world to forget."

Elsa Greenmonkey rose. She was still beautiful; that was the horrible part, how well the paint had kept.

"You can prove nothing," she said. "There is no banana left, no peel, no witness."

"That," Poirot admitted, "is unfortunately true. The law will not hang you now. But listen to me, Lady Dittisham, one primate to another: you killed the only monkey who ever made you sit still, and it has made you sit still forever. Caroline and Amyas swung free of you the moment they died. You are the one in the cage — and a cage," said Poirot, "is only a tree with no branches."

She went out without another word. Somewhere in the hall, a door closed like a fig dropping at midnight.

---

Carla Figg married John Rendall in the spring, under a fig tree that behaved itself. Dame Angela sent a trowel from a dig in Crete as a wedding gift — the tool, she wrote, of somebody who has finally stopped digging up the past.

And Poirot went home and ate the rest of his figs, all but one, which he placed on the mantelpiece and left there.

"One must remember," he told it severely, "that even the little grey fur follicles require, from time to time, a case with no murder in it at all. And also — one does, you know, love to be *fed*."
