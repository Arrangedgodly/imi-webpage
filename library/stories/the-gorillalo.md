# The Gorillalo

*A parody of "The Gruffalo"*

A pygmy marmoset went swinging one day through the deep dark jungle, looking for ripe lunch. The jungle hummed. The jungle dripped. Somewhere far off, something big moved slowly, but the marmoset was small and quick and had never once in his life been eaten, so he felt lucky.

Too lucky, possibly.

He ran straight into a boa constrictor, sunbathing in a coil on a log. The boa saw the marmoset, and the boa saw lunch, and the boa smiled the way only a snake can — which is all at once.

"Whither are you swinging, little marmoset? Come, feast with me in my leafy treetop. My favorite dish is marmoset à la leaf."

"Feast with you? Grand!" said the marmoset. "But I'm meeting a Gorillalo, right here, right by this log — and his favorite dish happens to be boa constrictor on rice."

"A Gorillalo? What's a Gorillalo?"

"A Gorillalo! Why, don't you know? He has mossy black fur and a chest like a thundercloud, two amber eyes and a silver back, and purple fruit flies swarm all over his head. His favorite dish? He adores boiled boa."

"Boiled boa!" gasped the snake. "In that case, I'll make myself scarce. Good-bye, little marmoset." And he slithered off, going completely bananas with fear, and could be heard for some distance insisting to nobody that rice was overrated anyway.

"Wasn't that a treat!" said the marmoset. "Silly old snake! Doesn't he know? There's no such thing as a Gorillalo."

On went the marmoset, deeper into the dim, until he came to an owl monkey blinking out of his tree hollow. The owl monkey saw the marmoset, and the owl monkey saw supper, and the owl monkey rotated his head all the way round with delight, which is showing off, but effective.

"Whither are you swinging, little marmoset? Come, feast with me in my cozy tree hollow. My favorite dish is marmoset roulade."

"Feast with you? Grand!" said the marmoset. "But I'm meeting a Gorillalo, right here, under this very tree — and his favorite dish is roasted owl monkey."

"A Gorillalo? What's a Gorillalo?"

"A Gorillalo! Why, don't you know? He has knobbly knees and turned-out toes, and a giveaway whiff of fermented figs on his nose. His favorite dish? He's mad for monkey-on-toast."

"Monkey-on-toast!" hooted the owl monkey. "In that case, I'd best be roosting elsewhere. Toodle-oo, little marmoset." And he blinked off into the dark, vowing to sleep with the lights on.

"Wasn't that clever of me!" said the marmoset. "Silly old owl monkey! Doesn't he know? There's no such thing as a Gorillalo."

On went the marmoset, deeper and darker still, until the path ran narrow and the growls ran low, and he bumped into a jaguar, prowling the shadowy floor. The jaguar saw the marmoset, and the jaguar saw dinner, and the jaguar did not smile at all, which somehow was the worst part.

"Whither are you swinging, little marmoset? Come, feast with me in my favorite fig tree. My favorite dish is marmoset supreme."

"Feast with you? Grand!" said the marmoset. "But I'm meeting a Gorillalo, right here, on this very spot — and his favorite dish is jaguar casserole."

"A Gorillalo? What's a Gorillalo?"

"A Gorillalo! Why, don't you know? He has terrible tusks and terrible claws, and terrible teeth in his terrible jaws. And his favorite dish — I've heard it whispered — is crunchy jaguar."

"Crunchy jaguar!" choked the jaguar, hackles rising. "In that case, I shall prowl somewhere less ape-infested. Farewell, little marmoset." And he loped off at top speed, humming loudly, the way you do when you are being brave in reverse.

"Everyone's scared of my Gorillalo!" chuckled the marmoset, quite full of himself now, which is the most dangerous way to be full. "There's no such thing as a Gorillalo. There's no such thing as a —"

"OH YES THERE IS!" boomed a voice like a rockslide.

The marmoset turned around — and froze. There, blocking the whole path, filling it from root to canopy, was a creature with mossy black fur, a chest like a thundercloud, a silver back, knobbly knees, turned-out toes, terrible tusks, terrible claws, terrible teeth in terrible jaws, and purple fruit flies buzzing over his head like a small angry orchestra.

"You wanted to see me?" rumbled the Gorillalo, cracking his terrible knuckles one by one. "Because my absolute favorite dish is... scrambled marmoset."

"Scrambled marmoset!" squeaked the marmoset, thinking fast, which is easy when your brain is the size of a pea and very practiced. "You wouldn't want to eat me, big fellow. Everyone knows I'm the most terrifying creature in this jungle. Walk behind me and see for yourself. Everyone runs when they spot me. It's terribly embarrassing. I've no one to talk to."

"Terrifying? You?" The Gorillalo laughed until the fruit flies fell off. "All right, marmoset. You go first. I'll follow. And if anyone doesn't run — I eat you both."

They hadn't gone far when the boa constrictor slid out and took one look — at the marmoset, and at the enormous shadow behind him.

"Good heavens, it's the marmoset!" hissed the boa. "And he's — he's brought his Gorillalo! And that Gorillalo looks hungry for boiled boa!" And he vanished into the undergrowth so fast he tied himself in a reef knot.

"Sssh!" said the Gorillalo, ducking behind a fern that hid roughly one of his knees. "You never saw me!"

The marmoset noted, without turning a hair, that his enormous companion had begun to sweat.

They went on, and out peered the owl monkey. "The marmoset! And — GREAT GALLOPING GIBBONS — the Gorillalo! Roasted? ME?" He slammed his tree hollow shut and locked it from the inside, and was heard afterwards pushing furniture against it.

"Ssssh!" said the Gorillalo, hiding behind a sapling that hid approximately his left elbow. "You never saw me! You never heard me! You never smelled the fermented figs!"

Next, the jaguar, who took a single look at the marmoset, a single look at the tusks, and sprinted for the savanna and a nice quiet career of chasing mice. "The marmoset! With his Gorillalo! NOT STAYING!" He was heard from shortly after, asking the mice if they'd seen anybody.

"Ssssh!" whispered the Gorillalo, now crouched behind the marmoset himself, which fooled nobody. "You never saw me!"

"Well, Gorillalo," said the marmoset, turning around with a smile of tremendous calm. "You saw. I am the most terrifying creature in this jungle. Even you kept out of sight. And my favorite dish, just so you know, is — Gorillalo crumble."

"Gorillalo crumble!" bellowed the Gorillalo, going pale under his fur, which takes some doing when you are that mossy. "In that case — GOOD-BYE, TERRIFYING ONE!" And the Gorillalo turned tail and thundered off, going completely ape, and did not stop until he was three jungles away, where he told everyone he had never been anywhere near that marmoset.

The pygmy marmoset sat down to a lunch of ripe figs, all alone in the deep dark jungle.

"Silly old Gorillalo," he said, licking his tiny fingers. "Doesn't he know? There's no such thing as a scary marmoset..."

He looked at his reflection in a raindrop and grinned.

"...Well. There is now."
