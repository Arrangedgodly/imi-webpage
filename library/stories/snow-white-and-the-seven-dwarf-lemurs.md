# Snow White and the Seven Dwarf Lemurs

*A parody of "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs"*

Once upon a time, in a jungle far away, there lived a queen mandrill whose nose blushed the exact red of a ripe strawberry. Every morning she climbed to the tallest tree, where a mirror-bright pool lay in a fork of the trunk, and asked it the question:

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall — who has the fluffiest tail of all?"

And the pool, which reflects honestly because water cannot help it, always rippled: "You, my queen, are fluffiest of all."

For years this worked beautifully. Then her stepdaughter grew up.

Snowwhite was a silky sifaka with fur so white it glowed and a tail so fluffy that songbirds nested in it by accident. The morning the pool rippled a different answer, the queen nearly fell out of her tree.

"Snowwhite's tail," said the pool, "is fluffier by a whisker. Also by a mile."

The queen went bananas and summoned her huntsman, a solemn owl monkey who worked nights. "Take Snowwhite into the deep jungle," hissed the queen, "and bring me her tail tuft as proof she'll never out-fluff me again."

The owl monkey led Snowwhite deep into the dark trees — then stopped, hung his head, and told her everything. "Run, little princess. Your stepmother is a menace. Here." He snipped a tuft of cotton-plant fluff. "She'll never know the difference. She never looks closely at anything but herself."

Snowwhite ran until her paws ached, then stumbled through ferns into a hidden glen — and found a tiny cottage with seven tiny beds, seven tiny mugs, and a sign: THE SEVEN DWARF LEMURS, GRUB MINERS, OUT TO WORK.

They were seven dwarf mouse lemurs, each no bigger than a teacup, and each a whole character.

Doc, the foreman, prescribed things constantly — grass compresses, warm pebbles, a licked leaf for every ailment including happiness. Grumpo grumbled about everything and built shelves about everything; nobody asked for the shelves, everybody used them, and Grumpo denied making four more. Hap-lemur laughed at everything, especially danger. Sleepy was asleep — that was his entire job description. Sneezy was allergic to his own fur and got through every sentence in three tries. Blushful turned pink when looked at and communicated chiefly from inside his own tail. And Drowsy was about to fall asleep at all times, mid-shift, mid-air; nobody, including them, could ever remember which of the two was which.

When they found Snowwhite asleep across all seven of their beds at once, they were delighted beyond all reason.

"Stay!" they cried. "Cook for us! Tidy a bit! You're the size of *three hundred* of us — you can reach the middle shelf!"

So she stayed. She stewed the figs, swept the crumbs, and every evening the seven marched home from the grub mine singing:

*"We grub-dig, we grub-dig, heigh-ho, heigh-ho, It's home from the mine we go —"*

(There were more verses. Sneezy never got through one.)

And each night at the door, Doc delivered the household's one rule: "Let nobody in. Not peddlers, not pilgrims, not anybody selling anything fluffy, silky, or *interesting*. Interesting is how it gets you."

Alas, the queen soon asked her pool the question again.

"Snowwhite's tail," said the pool, "fluffier than ever, in the glen with the seven dwarf lemurs. Also, the huntsman conned you with a cotton ball."

The queen went utterly ape. She dyed her brow fur brown, bent her back, and disguised herself as an old fruit-bat peddler woman with a basket of ribbons — and down she flapped to the glen, where Snowwhite hung the washing.

"Ribbons!" she cawed. "Tail-ribbons, tail-laces! A lace for the fluffiest tail in the forest — I've heard of you, dearie, and I came *especially*."

"We're not supposed to buy from strangers," said Snowwhite, politely.

"Buy? Who said buy? *Free*," said the peddler-bat. "It's the fashion in the lowlands. A tail like yours unlaced? Scandalous."

Well, Snowwhite was princely about kindness, not sense. She let the old bat wind the lace round and round the base of her famous tail — and the queen pulled, and pulled, and *pulled*, until the lace sank deep into the fluff and Snowwhite dropped where she stood, cold and still.

"That's flattened THAT," sniggered the queen, and flapped off home.

At dusk the seven trudged home to find Snowwhite lying under the washing line like a dropped cloud. Grumpo cut the tail-lace with his sharpest shelf-tool, Doc applied a warm pebble ("It's what she'd have wanted"), and Snowwhite's whiskers twitched, and she sat up, sneezing.

"A ribbon," she said weakly. "She said it was the fashion."

"THE PEDDLER," said Doc, and all seven said it at once: "The queen." They made her promise again — nobody in, nothing free, especially not fashion.

The next morning, the pool was waiting with its report before the queen even asked.

"Alive," rippled the pool. "You have been out-laced by dwarfs with scissors. Honestly, you two deserve each other."

The queen disguised herself again — a different bat, she felt sure this was the key detail — and returned with a basket of grooming combs, one of sandalwood so fine and so lovely that Snowwhite gasped.

"Strangers aren't allowed to sell us things," said Snowwhite, arms crossed, doing her very best.

"Sell? *Free*," said the peddler-bat. "One try, one favor for an old bat's poor itchy back."

And the queen combed her back exactly twice, and the comb was poisoned — the Sleeping Snooze steeped in the sandalwood — and Snowwhite dropped mid-swing, for the second time.

At dusk the lemurs found her, and Blushful, very pink, pulled the comb from her shoulder fur with two fingers — and away from her skin, the Snooze lost its grip, and Snowwhite woke, groggy and grumpy.

"FREE things," said Grumpo. "The word FREE. That's the trap. Nothing free in this jungle but rain and advice."

From then on Snowwhite let nobody in at all. But the queen had stopped listening to reports and started studying delivery.

The third time, she came not as a bat at all, but as a very old grandmother lemur with a walking stick and a basket — and in the basket lay the most beautiful banana ever grown: golden on one cheek, blushed rosy red on the other, and soaked through with the Sleeping Snooze. She waited until the seven were down the mine, then moaned piteously by the path, until kindness did what fashion and grooming could not.

"You can't come in," said Snowwhite, through the hedge. "But I can bring you water, and figs if you're hungry."

"Nothing for me, dearie," wheezed the granny, "but my eyes are bad, and my paws are worse, and I can't peel this banana I've been saving. Split it with an old granny — half for you, the red cheek; I'll manage the yellow. It's no good keeping it whole."

Now Snowwhite knew the rules. But the granny had asked her to help — not to buy, not to accept, just to *help* — and no rule her friends had made covered being kind to grannies, because no rule should have to.

She bit the rosy blush — and dropped, mid-swing, fast asleep. The third Snooze went deepest of all, and the granny was gone before Snowwhite landed.

When the seven trudged home and found her, they tried everything. Grumpo cut every lace in the cottage on principle. Doc applied pebbles until the pebbles ran out. Sneezy combed her fur gently with the good comb. Nothing woke her. So they grieved the only way they could — carefully. They wove her a bier of amber sap that dried clear as glass, laid her where the fireflies gathered, and kept watch in shifts, night and night and night, while Drowsy slept beside her and Sleepy, for the first time in living memory, did not.

Word of the sleeping princess reached every branch, and one day it reached Prince Whirl — a dancing sifaka famous for arriving sideways, as sifakas do. He came, and looked at Snowwhite glowing in her amber case like a song with the sound turned down, and his heart turned a somersault.

The lemurs lifted the amber lid, and Prince Whirl, leaning close, brushed her cheek with the ancient lemur cure no physician has improved upon: love's first grooming nibble.

Snowwhite's whisker twitched. Then her nose. Then — achoo! — she woke, sneezing her way back into the world. (Sneezy had been crying on her fur. It was, everyone agreed, very romantic.)

Grumpo built a celebration shelf. The queen, hearing the pool's final verdict — "Snowwhite: fluffiest, happiest, and getting married; you: still talking to water" — flew into such a rage her stripes turned upside down, and she moved to the far coast, to spend her days arguing with mirrors and losing.

Snowwhite married Prince Whirl beneath the fig blossoms. The seven dwarf lemurs were, obviously, the entire wedding party, and sang all seven verses at once; no one slept, not even Drowsy — except Grumpo, briefly, but he denied it. And Snowwhite kept the middle shelf forever, where nobody could reach it but her — with a lace, a comb, and one dried banana-cheek under glass, labeled in Grumpo's handwriting:

*"Free things. That's the trap."*

**And the moral, whispered down every generation of lemurs: you may ask the water who is fluffiest — but it's kinder to just groom your friends and let the tail fall where it may.**
