# Ababoon's Gate

*A parody of "Abaddon's Gate"*

Past the orbit of Uranus, hanging in the dark like a dare, the Ring was a shimmering circle a thousand meters across — a tire-swing the size of a weather system. Every monkey in the system knew scholars would someday object to the name that stuck anyway. It was, is, and forever shall be: the Tire Swing.

Three fleets went out to look at it: Earth's, Mars's, and the Behemoth, the Outer Plantain Alliance's ancient generation-ark with guns welded on, crewed by idealists who had never been shot at. Riding herd on them all, by way of a career's worth of bad luck, came the usual suspects: Holden the chimp, his crew, and their stolen gunship — whose whole function, as far as anyone could tell, was to arrive first at things that were about to become much larger than anyone's plans.

Also aboard, under a false name, came Melba Banana — really Clarissa Macaque, daughter of the disgraced fruit magnate, her body illegally modified with combat glands and her heart full of nails. Holden had broken her family on the rocks of his honesty, and she had signed her own name away to end him. Each night she whispered it like a scab she couldn't stop picking: "My name is Melba. My name is Clarissa Macaque." And then she planted a bomb on an Earth ship, framed Holden for firing on it, and very nearly got him and the truth both spaced.

She got her paws on him once, in a maintenance bay, with a wrench and four seconds of advantage. What she had not budgeted for was that Holden's crew had spent years learning exactly how much trouble their chimp could survive, and that Naomi, who missed nothing that breathed near him, was already coming down the ladder. Melba's glands sang. Holden talked — stupidly, honestly, about her sister, about grief, about the size of the thing they were all standing next to — and the talking held until the cavalry arrived. They shipped her to the Behemoth in a box, and she lay in the box whispering her name, and for the first time neither half of it fit.

Aboard the Behemoth, first mate Carlos Baca — "Bull" — a great grey gorilla from Earth, spent the voyage being the only adult in a treehouse of hotheads. The OPA had made him second-in-command of a flagship commanded by true believers, which meant every day was a bar fight he wasn't allowed to win. When the truth about Melba surfaced, he growled: "Congratulations, children. We brought a knife to a faith-healing."

Because the ships had also brought believers: among them Anna Volovodov, a small gibbon pastor who had come out to the far nothing to pray over the biggest thing monkeys had ever found, and who worried mostly, on the way, about her flock's digestion.

"God," she remarked to the stars, "is either very patient or very funny. I have decided to appreciate both."

Then the fleets reached the Swing, and the slow zone beyond it, and everything — all three fleets, every shuttle, every nut and bolt and dropped wrench — was suddenly decelerated at once, caught by a rule older than the oldest jungle: nothing here moves faster than a lazy swing. Inside the ships, everything that hadn't been strapped down kept moving. The med bays filled. Somewhere in the Behemoth's drum, a thousand liters of soup became an anecdote.

"It's all right to be scared," Anna told a young Brancher with both arms splinted. "Fear is just grooming the mind does to itself. Let it settle. Then brush it out."

She held services in the Behemoth's drum, half rock garden, half bomb shelter, and the congregations grew even among the scientists, who came officially to observe and stayed to sing. "You don't have to believe anything to be here," Anna told them, which was lucky, because the day's reading was the velocity limit. "Faith isn't the point. The point is that whatever built that thing also built the rules it keeps, and it keeps them. In my line of work, that's already a theology."

The zone, an old dead station standing watch at the center, had rules about weapons too. When a frightened Earth ship fired a railgun at it, the station simply — stopped the ship. Every monkey aboard died of deceleration in the space of a sneeze. After that, the fleets, terrified, began pointing guns at each other instead, which the station permitted, because the station was not the danger. The station was the fence.

Captain Ashford of the Behemoth declared that the Swing was a trap and must be destroyed, and started charging the great laser to crack the ring — a plan with exactly one flaw, being that it would kill every monkey in the zone and slam the door on everything that came after. The crew mutinied in the polite Belt way, which is to say everyone quietly agreed to be somewhere else. Bull broke up a counter-coup with his fists and came out of it with a broken back, and ran the rest of the battle from a stretcher, issuing orders like a gorilla-shaped avalanche in park.

It came down, as these things do, to a handful of monkeys crawling through a half-lit ship: Bull on his stretcher; Anna getting the children's distress broadcast out on every band, honestly, in plain language, telling the watching station exactly what they were and what they were trying to stop; and Melba, of all souls, switching sides at the bottom of her fall.

She found Anna first, cringing like a struck kit, waiting to be arrested, and Anna did the pastoral math in one glance and gave her a job instead: "You can kill with those hands. Fine. Today we need them to save idiots. Report to the engineering deck."

Melba reported. It was Melba who tore out the laser's charging circuitry while the ship shuddered around her, Melba who held a bulkhead door open with her modified shoulders long enough for a party of strangers — her enemies, technically, the people whose fleet had wrecked her family — to crawl through to safety. Nobody thanked her. She noticed that she didn't mind.

And the station, which had been waiting a billion years to see whether the dangerous animals in its pen could calm down, listened. The blue ghost of a dead detective, flickering in Holden's cabin, put it plainly one last time:

"It's a zoo, Holden. They built a pen for the biters. Play nice, and they open the gates. Now go swing."

The gates opened. All of them.

Through the great hollow hub at the center of the slow zone bloomed thirteen hundred rings, each one a doorway, each doorway a new system, new suns, new jungles beyond counting or deserving.

"Monkeys," Anna said softly, watching the buds of light open like spring on a black tree, "have just been handed a playground by something that was worried we might be livestock."

Bull, flat on his back, grinning like a gorilla: "First one through buys the banana splits."

Melba went to the hole in the law that was waiting for her name — Clarissa Macaque, prisoner, cooperative — and found, oddly, that it fit better than Melba ever had. And far away at the bottom of the zone, the station kept its gentle count of the animals, and opened another gate.
