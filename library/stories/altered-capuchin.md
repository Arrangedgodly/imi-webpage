# Altered Capuchin

*A parody of "Altered Carbon"*

They monkeycast his mind two hundred light-years to Bay Canopy City and poured it into a pelt that wasn't his.

Takeshi Kovacs had died a capuchin — lean, quick, and the last trained Envoy of his generation — and woke in a loaner body with hands like dinner plates: a gorilla sleeve, three hundred pounds of somebody else's shoulders. Behind his brainstem sat his cortical stack, the little peel of crystal that carries your whole self — your Digital Primate Freight — from body to body, world to world, for as long as somebody pays the postage.

"You're in Bay Canopy, buddy," said the cop who'd untanked him. Lieutenant Ortega, badges of the Bay Canopy Police, tasted his name like a bad fig. "Kovacs. Quellist. Envoy. You die a lot for somebody with opinions."

"I'm economical," Kovacs said. "I only die when it's someone else's turn."

She hated him on sight, which interested him, because hate that fresh is usually about something old. It was. The gorilla sleeve they'd poured him into had a name — Ryker — and Ryker had been her partner, and her more, and was now doing fifteen years of storage in a police evidence locker while a dead stranger walked around wearing his shoulders. "You're wearing my friend's face," Ortega said, going. "Try not to get it shot. He never did."

They'd bought his release for a job. At the top of the cloud-spike, in the highest suite of the Aerium, waited Laurens Bancroft: a Meth — a Methuselah — three hundred and sixty years old, a gorilla rich enough to shed into a new pelt whenever the old one itched. A week ago, somebody had vaporized Bancroft's head with a particle blaster. His stack, backed up forty-eight hours earlier, had poured him into a fresh body on the slab. The police called it suicide. Bancroft disagreed.

"I did not shoot myself, Mr. Kovacs. I was at a brothel called Head in the Canopy, and then I was dead. Find out what happened in between. I will pay you what your Envoy pride pretends it doesn't want."

Envoy conditioning had taught Kovacs to read a troop the way other monkeys read fruit labels: who groomed whom, who flinched at whose name, and the whole hierarchy lay open like a peeled banana. He took the case, and lodgings at the Raven — an ancient tower hotel run by an AI in the shape of a polite black parrot named Poe, who was very courteous and very lonely.

"We do not get many guests," Poe said. "I have taken the liberty of stocking the mini-bar with roasted seeds. Also, the tap water is Excellent. That is a brand, not an assessment, though I stand by both."

The case led down and down. At a party in the Aerium, Bancroft's wife Miriam poured him wine older than nations and made it clear that a Meth takes what amuses her. Kovacs, professionally, declined — with a Meth, everything is a transaction, and he'd seen the prices. Then a brothel worker named Mary 380 was found beheaded, her stack fried — real death, no re-sleeve, gone like a dropped egg. The last thing in her log was Bancroft's suite. Ortega's squad hit a wall of lawyers; the trail ran through the Wei Clinic, where monkeys who asked bad questions were taken apart and reassembled humbler.

Kovacs went in anyway, and the clinic's specialists welcomed him into virtual for the traditional interview. They built a grey room and a table, sat him down, and cranked the pain up by degrees, like sommeliers. Two hours of that, the senior torturer promised pleasantly, and he'd be telling them his kit litter's names. What the clinic's handbook didn't cover was Envoy partitioning: the trick of cutting yourself into rooms and standing in the one that isn't screaming. Kovacs sat through it, filed the sensation under *weather*, and when they paused to adjust the dials, came back with questions of his own. He left with needles in him, an appetite for arson, and a name.

He knew the name. That was the worst part.

"I waited two centuries for you, little brother." Reileen. His sister — alive, immortal, a Meth above the Meths, her paw on every stack in the canopy. She ran Head in the Canopy. She had drugged Bancroft with a code phrase that made him kill, then self-destruct — and die with the memory backed up out of reach — so that forever after, one of the richest monkeys alive belonged to her. Mary 380 had been recording that night, which made her inventory, and inventory gets composted.

"It was always going to be us against the tree, Takeshi. We could own this whole canopy together. Prime mates, the pair of us."

"You were the best of us once," Kovacs said. "On New Hok. Before you learned that everything with a price can be bought."

"I learned it from watching you die," she said, and for one flicker she was his sister again, small, gripping his hand at the cremation line. Then the flicker closed like a shop window. "I'm still the best of you."

She took him, of course. She had the servers, the sleeves, the law, and the guest list. What she had accounted for poorly was what Envoy conditioning does with a cage: it turns the cage into a classroom. In the virtual of Head in the Canopy, where she liked to interrogate her acquisitions over sunset skies, she laid out the whole garden of options — alliances, partnerships, a sleeve of his own choosing, a world of his own. Kovacs laid out one fact: virtual pain runs both directions, and she had never once, in two hundred years, worn a body that could actually die.

"You built a heaven," he said, "and you're the only monkey in it who never checks the floor."

"You wouldn't," she said.

"I'm Envoy," he said. "Checking floors is the whole job."

And while her attention was inside the sunset, the real world kept its appointments: Poe's ancient hotel, insulted on its own doorstep by her security monkeys, revealed itself to be a fortress with excellent manners and a beak like a bolt cutter. The Raven held. The servers fell. Kovacs burned Reileen's stack farms all the way down to the roots, and gave the ashes to Bancroft as his final report — along with the truth, which was that Bancroft himself, code-phrased and out of his head, had pulled the trigger on Mary 380, and had been paying his own blackmail ever since without knowing it.

"Every one of them immortal," Kovacs said, "and not one of them grown up. The Meths are kits, Bancroft. A thousand-year-old kit is still a kit. You have all the time in the world and no reason to become anything."

"What do I owe you?" the old gorilla asked.

"The truth costs extra," Kovacs said. "You already paid."

He left Earth on one of Bancroft's ships, outbound. At the berth, the parrot's voice followed him down the corridor.

"Shall I keep your suite ready, Mr. Kovacs?"

"Keep the seeds warm, Poe," he said. "Something always comes back."
