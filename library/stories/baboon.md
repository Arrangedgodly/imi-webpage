# Baboon

*A parody of "Dune"*

The night House Apeides left the green world of Calabanan for the desert planet Arabarkis, young Paul's mother held him by the shoulders and said, "Remember the Litany Against Fleas."

Paul recited it while the ship hummed around them like a hive of patient bees:

*"I must not scratch. Scratching is the fur-killer. Scratching is the little death that brings total baldness. I will face the itch. I will permit it to pass over me and through me. And when it has gone past, I will turn the inner eye to see its path. Where the itch has gone, there will be nothing. Only fur will remain."*

He had passed a harder test before departure. The Reverend Mother Marmoset, a Bana Gesserit crone the size of a regret, had put his paw in a box and held a poison-tipped thorn — the Gum Jabber, for high treason against the troop — at his neck.

"Put your paw in the box."

"What's in the box?"

"It itches," said the old ape, and nothing more.

It itched like the whole jungle had died in there. It itched like the invention of itching. And Paul, weeping but immobile, held still, because a duke's son learns early that the pack is watching.

"How much of your attention is on the itch?" asked the Reverend Mother, impressed.

"All of it," Paul gasped, "and none of my fingers."

"You'll do," she said, and put the Gum Jabber away. "An animal would have scratched."

Arabarkis was all dune and glare, a planet of sand that had never once grown a banana — and yet it was the most valuable world in the imperium, because Arabarkis and Arabarkis alone produced the Spice: the golden crushed-nana melange that folded space, extended life, and let the navigators of the Spacing Guild smell a safe path between the stars. On the journey out, dreams came to Paul with the smell of cinnamon on them: a girl in the desert, a bloody jihad, a voice saying *the sleeper must awaken*, which he mentioned to no one, because "I dream in trailers" is a poor start to a duke's career.

Their first night in the great keep at Arrakeen, a hunter-seeker slid into Paul's chamber — a needle of murder the size of a gnat, steered by a hidden enemy's thumbs. Paul held still until the thing's shadow passed, then cracked it out of the air the way his training demanded. "The sleeper," said his mother grimly, examining the needle, "is awake now. Somebody was testing whether the trap had sprung."

*Whoever controls the nana controls the universe,* went the old saying. Paul's father, Duke Leto Apeides, had just been handed control. It was, everyone agreed, a magnificent gift, in the way a coconut tied to a stick above a pit trap is a magnificent gift.

The trap sprang at moonset. The Harkonnuts — the gross old Baron Harkonnut and his giggling nephews, Rabban and Feyd-Rautha — swept in with imperial sanction and inside help: Dr. Yuehtan, the family physician, whose loyalty was surgically guaranteed by imperial conditioning, until the Baron found the off switch, which was Yuehtan's wife. Duke Leto died with a poison gas tooth in his jaw, taking a roomful of enemies with him. Old Gurney Balleck, the bald uakari battle-master who taught Paul fighting and played the nine-stringed banjaset, fought their way clear with a cry of "Long live the fighters!" — and Paul and his mother, Lady Jessica, fled into the deep desert with a traitor's laughter behind them and the planet's great sandworms below them: worms that heard the thump-thump-thump of running feet the way a zoo monkey hears the rattle of the feed cart — with total, loving attention.

But Jessica was of the Bana Gesserit, the sisterhood of political witches with perfect posture, and she had trained her son in the Voice — that tone you use on a younger sibling, weaponized — plus every branch of primate combat. And the desert had its own troop: the Furremen, hard-eyed riders in stillsuits that recycled every drop of spit and sweat, who had been waiting two thousand years for an offworlder worth following.

"Fear is the mind-killer," said Stilgorilla, the Furremen's chief, tossing Paul a thumper — the device whose rhythmic drumming summons a worm so a rider may climb it. "The itch is the fur-killer. Now climb."

They gave Paul a name that meant "the little groomer of the dunes" and then a truer one: **Macaqu'Dib**, the one who teaches. And in the deep ergs he met Chimpni, a young woman whose eyes were blue-within-blue from a lifetime of Spice, and who looked at this strange wet offworlder with his pale fur and soft paws and thought: *This one will need so much training.*

"Tell me about the water discipline," Paul said.

"We wear our tears, in the stillsuit," said Chimpni. "On my world, the dead belong to the troop. Your body's water belongs to your people. We shed no tears we cannot drink."

Paul drank the Water of Life — the poison bile of a drowned baby worm, which either kills you or opens your inner eye — and woke up seeing time the way his enemies saw maps. He saw the jihad his legend would loose, the banana crusade that would sweep the universe in his name, and he could not see how to stop it. That was the terrible arithmetic of prophecy: the bigger the hope, the bigger the bill.

The Emperor himself came to Arabarkis when the Furremen rose — Shaddam IV, annoyed to discover his desert had produced a messiah with a grudge, bringing his Sardoughkar, the oven-hardened elite, and his daughter Irulangur, who wrote histories and would soon be narrating her own husband's rebellion with icy dignity. Paul cracked the Shield Wall with the atomics his family had kept labeled *in case of relatives*, rode the storm of sand through the gap, and threatened to destroy the Spice, to poison the little nana forever — and the universe held its breath. Thumper drums rolled across the sand. A worm the length of a city rose under Macaqu'Dib's hooks, and he rode it to the negotiating table, which is the single most dramatic way to arrive at a negotiation ever invented.

Then Feyd-Rautha Harkonnut, sleek and giggling and wicked with a blade, invoked the old law: single combat, monkey to monkey, knife to knife.

"Will you bleed?" Feyd sang as they circled.

"Only a little," said Paul, and made it true.

When it was over, the Emperor's troops knelt in the sand. Paul Macaqu'Dib, heir of House Apeides, wormrider of the Furremen, took the throne of the known universe — with Irulangur as his political bride, Chimpni as his heart, and the Litany Against Fleas on his lips. Chimpni, who had been told all her life that a foreign bride would sit at her Paul's side, drew herself up and told the Imperium precisely what it could do with its thrones — but that, as the histories say, is the next book.

*The nana must flow,* they say in the bazaars of a thousand worlds.

*And where the itch has gone,* thought the young Emperor, *only fur remains.*
