# Goldilocks and the Three Bare Bottoms

*A parody of "Goldilocks and the Three Bears"*

Once upon a time, in a grand old fig tree at the top of Banana Bough, there lived three baboons with the three barest bottoms in the whole jungle: Great Big Papa Baboon, Middle-Sized Mama Baboon, and Little Wee Baby Baboon.

Their bottoms were famous. They were glossy. They were pink shading to red, and in the morning sun they glowed like three warning lanterns. Travelers used the tree to navigate by. "Bear left at the glowing bottom," they would say, and mean it in both senses.

One morning, Mama Baboon made banana porridge for breakfast. But the porridge was piping hot — "hot as a termite mound at noon," grumbled Papa — so the three baboons decided to leave it to cool and go for a scamper through the branches.

They were very organized about it. Papa counted the fig trees for the winter census. Mama inspected the view from the top of the hill, which was the same view as yesterday but she liked to check. Baby Baboon practiced hanging by one heel and yelling "WHEEE," which was his entire curriculum.

While they were out, along came a golden lion tamarin with a mane of spun gold and a nose for trouble. Her name was Goldilocks, and she was as curious as a crew of chimps at a birthday party. She had been exploring the Bough since sunup, opening hollow knots, sniffing flowers, tasting three kinds of moss, and forming opinions about all of them.

She found the fig tree, and the knot-hole door, and the knot-hole door was open. In she went.

Goldilocks sniffed the air. "Porridge," she said, with the reverence of a professional. She had, in her time, tasted porridge in every tree from here to the Papaya Cliffs, and she considered herself something of a scholar of other people's breakfasts.

On the table stood three nut-shell bowls of banana porridge. She tasted the great big bowl.

"Ooh — too hot!" (That was Papa's, who liked his porridge to fight back.)

She tasted the middle-sized bowl. "Brr — too cold!" (That was Mama's, who had added chilled banana, because warm banana, she said, was "too enthusiastic." Do not ask why. It is a position she holds.)

Then she tasted the little wee bowl. "Mmm — just right!" And she ate it all up, every last smear, and licked the shell until it shone.

Feeling full and rather pleased with herself, she thought she would sit a while. There were three seats: Papa's bark bench, Mama's mossy cushion, and Baby's springy vine swing.

The bark bench was too hard. The mossy cushion was too soft — "I'm sinking!" — but the vine swing was just right. She swung and she swung and she swung, until the liana went twang and snapped, and down she went, bump, on her royal gold bottom.

"Hm," said Goldilocks, who was not one for apologies.

Sleepy now, she climbed up to the sleeping hollow, where there were three beds: Papa's bed of brambles ("Too scratchy!"), Mama's nest of kapok ("Too fluffy! I shall sneeze for a week!"), and Baby's little leaf cradle ("Just right"). She curled up in the cradle, pulled a leaf to her chin, and fell fast asleep.

By and by the baboons came home, hungry as — well, as baboons.

Papa looked into his great big bowl. "SOMEBODY'S BEEN AT MY PORRIDGE!" he bellowed, in his great big voice.

"Somebody's been at my porridge," said Mama Baboon, in her middle-sized voice, "and it had only just cooled to perfection, which is somehow worse."

"Somebody's been at my porridge," squeaked Baby Baboon, in his little wee voice, "and they've eaten it ALL UP!" And he turned his empty shell upside down to prove it, which is baboon for exhibit A.

Then they saw the seats. "SOMEBODY'S BEEN SITTING IN MY SEAT!" — "Somebody's been sitting in my seat!" — "Somebody's been swinging in my swing — AND BROKEN IT ALL TO BITS!"

"Now that," said Mama Baboon, with terrible calm, "is not porridge. That is vandalism."

Up to the sleeping hollow they climbed, three sets of footsteps going thump, stomp, patter. Papa sniffed the air. "There is a stranger," he rumbled, "in my HOUSE."

"SOMEBODY'S BEEN SLEEPING IN MY BED!" boomed Papa.

"Somebody's been sleeping in my bed," said Mama, "and dented the kapok into the shape of a stranger."

"Somebody's been sleeping in my bed," squeaked Baby Baboon, "AND SHE'S STILL THERE!"

Goldilocks woke — and looked up, and up, and up — at three enormous bare bottoms, all in a row, glowing over her cradle like the world's reddest stoplight.

She took one look, decided her luck had officially run out, leapt from the cradle, shot out the knot-hole window, pelted down the trunk, and away through the branches. Down came the baboons after her, thundering and hollering, three glowing bottoms flashing through the canopy like the jungle's angriest fireworks.

Goldilocks dove through a curtain of mistletoe, scrambled the wrong way up a liana, corrected, and vanished over the ridge. Baby Baboon, in hot pursuit, got one paw on the very tip of her tail-fur and missed, and slid to a stop at the top of the ridge with the whole green jungle spread out below him and no golden stranger in it at all.

"KNOCK NEXT TIME!" he bellowed, at the top of his little wee voice, so loudly that a family of toucans three trees over moved house.

And neither the baboons nor anybody else in that jungle saw hide nor gold hair of her again — for a week, anyway.

For that is how long it took Goldilocks, who was nosy but honest, to feel properly terrible. She braided a new vine swing, strand by strand, stronger than the old one, with a double knot at the top. She picked a bowl of mango-and-banana porridge in a fresh green leaf. And she came back up Banana Bough, stood at the knot-hole door, and — knocked.

Three baboon faces appeared, side by side, glowing faintly with suspicion.

"I ate the porridge," said Goldilocks. "I broke the swing. I slept in the bed. I am very sorry, and I have brought replacements."

There was a long silence. Then Mama Baboon opened the door.

"Sit down," she said. "Breakfast is the apology department."

They ate porridge together, four bowls' worth, and it was — all of them agreed — just right. Papa interrogated her politely between spoonfuls (where was she from, who were her people, had she ever burgled anyone else, and did she know her left from her right for the census). Baby Baboon demanded a full account of the moss-tasting tour, and pronounced her reviews "advanced." And Mama watched Goldilocks weave the last double knot into the new swing and gave her the smallest, rarest thing Mama Baboon had to give: an approving nod.

Papa installed a knocker made of a hollow gourd, so that nobody could ever again claim the door was open to interpretation. And Baby Baboon got his new swing, which held eleven baboons, one tamarin, and the toucans' entire luggage.

"Next time," said Baby Baboon, rubbing his bottom thoughtfully — it was a family habit — "she should knock."

"She did knock," said Mama.

"Then she learns fast," said Baby Baboon, "for a stranger."

And far away, in another tree, they say a golden-haired tamarin still shivers at the sight of glowing red lights — but nowadays it is only because she is remembering her manners, and reaching up, and always, always knocking.
