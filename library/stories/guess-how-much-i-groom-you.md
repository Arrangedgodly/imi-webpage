# Guess How Much I Groom You

*A parody of "Guess How Much I Love You"*

Little Tamarin and Big Tamarin were getting ready for bed in their nest of leaves, high in the highest branch of the forest, where the moon looked down like a great golden banana.

Little Tamarin, holding on to Big Tamarin's fur by the very tips of his tiny fingers, said, "Guess how much I groom you."

"Oh," said Big Tamarin, "I couldn't guess that. It's past my bedtime, and my brain has already swung down for the night."

"This much," said Little Tamarin, stretching his little arms out as wide as they could possibly go, which, to be fair, was not very wide at all.

Big Tamarin reached out his longer arms, wider, wider, until his fingertips touched the branches on either side of the nest — and then a little wider still, just to be sure. "But I groom *you* this much," he said.

"That's not fair," said Little Tamarin. "You're only bigger because you've been big longer."

"Being big," said Big Tamarin, "is mostly patience and bananas."

"Hmm," said Little Tamarin, and thought.

He scrambled up Big Tamarin's shoulder and stood on tiptoe, stretching his fingers right up into the leaves above their heads. "I groom you as high as I can reach," he announced.

"I groom you as high as *I* can reach," said Big Tamarin, standing up so tall in the nest that he had to duck a passing cloud.

Little Tamarin looked up. Way, way up, past the highest branches, the canopy stretched and stretched, and above it hung the moon.

"That's nothing," said Little Tamarin, and had a bigger idea. He wrapped his tail tight around the branch above, tipped himself upside down, and hung there by the tail with his arms pointing straight at the forest floor. "I groom you all the way down to my toesies!" he said.

"I groom you all the way down to *my* toesies," said Big Tamarin, flipping upside down beside him and hanging by his own much longer tail, "and back up again — with a detour."

They hung there together, upside down, two golden lion tamarins in the moonlight like a pair of Christmas tree ornaments. A firefly drifted past, blinked twice, and scored the upside-down round a perfect ten.

Then Little Tamarin reached over, laid his tail against Big Tamarin's tail, and measured very carefully. "I groom you the whole length of your tail," he said. "Twice."

"I groom you the whole length of your tail," said Big Tamarin, "which is shorter, so I do it four times, plus once for the curl."

"I groom you more than a hundred bananas," said Little Tamarin.

"I groom you more than all the bananas ever picked," said Big Tamarin, "and the bunches nobody has found yet, and the two the birds got."

"I groom you further than the smell of a ripe durian travels," said Little Tamarin.

Big Tamarin considered this, and held his nose. "That is genuinely far," he admitted. "I groom you further than the smell of a durian travels *upwind*."

"I groom you as loud as the howler monkeys howl," said Little Tamarin, filling his chest and producing one small, polite howl.

"I groom you as loud as *both* howler troops," said Big Tamarin, "at dawn. On the first of the month."

"I groom you as deep as the tapirs' favorite mud," said Little Tamarin.

"I groom you as deep as the mud the tapirs only *dream* about," said Big Tamarin.

Far below, in the dark of the understory, a sloth began — very slowly — to ask them to keep the noise down. His name was Hubert. He had begun his request in the spring, and would finish it by Thursday.

"I groom you right up to the top of the tallest kapok tree," said Little Tamarin, swinging himself upright again.

"I groom you right up to the top of the tallest kapok tree *and* the second-tallest kapok tree," said Big Tamarin, "and I groom the second-tallest kapok tree for getting in the way, and I groom you some more on the way down."

"I groom you as old as the oldest fig in the forest," said Little Tamarin.

"I groom you as old as the seed that started the forest," said Big Tamarin, "back when it was still deciding what to be."

"I groom you right across the jungle," said Little Tamarin, "and back."

"I groom you right across the jungle and back — twice," said Big Tamarin, "once for each of your ears."

"I groom you right round the whole troop," said Little Tamarin, "every auntie and every uncle and that one cousin nobody can place."

"I groom you right round the whole troop *and* the neighboring troop," said Big Tamarin, "including their auntie who gossips, so that she will say nice things about you."

"I groom you as much as all my fleas put together," said Little Tamarin, who had counted, and there were three.

"I groom you as much as every flea in the forest," said Big Tamarin, "and I would groom each one of them personally, if it meant keeping you."

They were both very quiet for a while. Big Tamarin stretched his arms out one more time, wide as wide, to make the point properly — so wide that something in his shoulders creaked, and a gecko who had been living in his left armpit packed up and moved out. The gecko left no forwarding branch.

"I groom you past the river," whispered Little Tamarin.

"I groom you past the river and over the mountains where the snow monkeys live," said Big Tamarin, "and I'd bring you back a snowball for your nest, if snowballs weren't so soggy."

Little Tamarin was getting sleepy. His eyelids went down, down, and his tail curled itself round Big Tamarin's arm like a question mark that had found its answer. He looked up through the leaves at the great golden banana of the moon, which is what monkeys who don't want to say "moon" call the moon.

"I groom you right up to the golden banana in the sky," he murmured, and his eyes closed.

"That's very high," said Big Tamarin. "That's a lot of grooming."

"It's as much grooming as there is," said Little Tamarin, already mostly asleep on the last word.

"Oh," said Big Tamarin, "that's a great deal indeed."

Then he lay back in the nest, gathered the sleeping Little Tamarin into the soft crook of his arm, and gently — so gently — groomed the last little leaf-bits out of the tiny fur between the tiny ears.

And just before he closed his own eyes, Big Tamarin looked up at the moon, hanging golden and ripe above the sleeping jungle, and whispered, "I groom you right up to the golden banana — and back."

And if you had been passing that tree that night, and heard the jungle go quiet all at once, that was the sound of two tamarins, one little and one big, agreeing that some things — like 'nanas, and bedtime, and grooming — are best when they're shared.
