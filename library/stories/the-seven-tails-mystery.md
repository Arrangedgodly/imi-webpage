# The Seven Tails Mystery

*A parody of "The Seven Dials Mystery"*

The trouble began, as most trouble does, with what everybody agreed was a splendid joke.

Gerry Wade could sleep through anything — avalanches, brass bands, his own name — and so the house party at Treetops, the great place Sir Oswald Coote had rented for the season, collected eight alarm clocks, set them at shattering intervals, and arranged them around his bed like a ring of baboons. The clocks went off one by one through the morning. Gerry slept through seven.

By the eighth, he was dead. A sleeping draught stood half-finished on his nightstand, and one of the eight clocks was missing from the room altogether.

It was found a week later at the bottom of the yew hedge, having been thrown, the county agreed, a quite impossible distance by anybody at the party; and the county thereupon agreed to stop talking about it, the county being an organism with a strong instinct for the quiet life.

"Overdose," said the doctor, and the coroner agreed, and the house party dispersed to be privately miserable in eight different counties. Only Jimmy Thesiger said anything interesting. Jimmy — a lanky young chimp of tremendous charm and moderate wits — pointed at the gap on the nightstand and said, "Somebody took the eighth clock. Why take a clock when you're leaving seven behind? A thief takes everything. A *murderer* takes the one thing that matters." Everyone told him not to be morbid. Being told not to be morbid is the traditional first step of every great investigation.

It was taken next by Lady Eileen Brent, known to the entire jungle as "Bundle" — a young spider monkey with a roadster, a taste for trespass, and a father, Lord Caterham, who sighed like a kettle. Driving up to London through the dusk, Bundle found a man lurching in her headlights: Ronny Devereux, who had been of the party at Treetops. He fell against her bonnet, looked up at her with enormous apologetic eyes, and died with two words on his lips.

"Seven... tails," said Ronny Devereux. "Tell... Jimmy."

The doctors found he had been shot, not motored, and Bundle — having delivered the message, and noticed exactly how interestingly Jimmy received it — motorcycled off to keep an appointment with destiny in a rented room above a nightclub called the Seven Tails, in the shabby coconut-oil district of London, where the proprietor was a vast mandrill named Mosgorovsky and the doorman was Alfred, lately a footman at Treetops.

Hiding in a cupboard, because cupboards are where investigations are properly conducted, Bundle watched six hooded figures file in. Each wore a black domino, and through the table before them each put a tail — a tail thrust deliberately through a hole cut in the wood, displayed like a signature. Six tails, numbered. One seat empty. The seventh.

They spoke of a formula and of a gathering at Wyvern Abbey, where the German inventor Eberhard meant to sell to the Minister of Flight the secret of a new jungle alloy — lighter than balsa, stronger than ironbark. They spoke of a man they hunted: an international criminal whose whole trade was the theft of secrets. And they spoke of Number Seven, their absent chief, in tones of reverent trust.

"Ronny was one of these," Bundle reasoned, wedged among the mop caps. "Gerry may have been one too. Ronny took the eighth clock — to see if anyone *reacted* to there being only seven. And somebody did react, with a revolver. Therefore the murderer is inside whatever these monkeys are hunting — or inside the hunt itself."

At Wyvern Abbey the formula was stolen in the night. Jimmy Thesiger took a bullet in the right arm, heroically wrestling an intruder down the ivy; Superintendent Battle — of Scotland Yard, solid as a baobab, oldest friend of every Brent alive — found in the morning a scorched glove in the fireplace, gnawed, unmistakably, by teeth: the thief had hurled the pistol away, bitten off his glove, and climbed back in to play victim. Battle also found the glove was a *left* glove, and said nothing at all, which from Battle was a speech.

Then Jimmy sent word: come to the Seven Tails, both of you — Bundle, and Loraine Wade, Gerry's pretty stepsister, who had inherited the formula in her handbag without quite meaning to. Bundle went. Loraine went. Bill Eversleigh, who worshipped Bundle from a distance of two paces and ten years, was found apparently unconscious in a car outside. Jimmy went, he said, to fetch a doctor.

He did not fetch one. He came back alone, locked the street door, and hit Bundle neatly behind the ear.

It is worth recording, because Bundle made everyone record it afterwards, that the last thing she saw before the lights went out was the cellar door — standing open — and the last thing she thought was not *how dare he* but *how sensible of him*: for the cellar door had still been locked at teatime, and a monkey who is fetching a doctor does not unlock the cellar. "That," Bundle explained afterwards, from a sofa, with ice on her skull and her reputation for observation fully restored, "is the whole secret of detection, Eversleigh. Watch which doors a monkey opens when he thinks nobody is counting. Doors are far more honest than monkeys."

She came to in Eversleigh's arms, in the club, with the worst headache of a well-provided life and a room full of hooded figures putting their tails through the table. The seventh chair was being filled at last — by Superintendent Battle, who removed his domino, nodded to Bundle with complete absence of surprise, and turned to the matter at hand.

"The Seven Tails," said Battle, "are a branch of the Secret Service. We exist to hunt one animal: a monkey who has made a career of stealing the world's secrets and selling them — a monkey with no politics, no nerves, and an excellent bedside manner. Gerry Wade was one of ours; he had got onto the scent of him, and was poisoned with a sleeping draught for it. Ronny was one of ours; he tested the household with the eighth clock, and the gentleman who had already murdered once for silence murdered again, and died leaving you his message. And tonight the hunt is over, because the monkey we sought never went out to find a doctor tonight. He stayed in this building, in the dark, to brain a young lady in the cellar — and we have been sitting here, in the cellar's own club, waiting for the light to go on."

Battle said it plainly, as one lays a card down.

"Jimmy Thesiger. Murderer of Gerald Wade and Ronald Devereux. Thief of the Eberhard formula, which he passed to his accomplice, Miss Loraine Wade, before shooting himself in the arm and biting his own glove off. Master criminal of the long game — invited everywhere, trusted everywhere, engaged to be married to a witness by spring."

It had the quality of a branch cracking underfoot: everyone hears it, nobody believes it, and then everybody is on the ground. Jimmy, arrested that same evening, smiled his excellent smile even into the police van, which somehow finished the job of convincing the party.

"He was going to *marry* me," said Loraine's successor-in-wait, hopefully.

"He was going to marry *evidence*," said Bundle.

Loraine Wade was arrested quietly in the week that followed; the formula came home; the German inventor was invited to invent something less portable.

As for the Seven Tails, they had a vacancy — Gerry's chair — and Battle, with the air of a monkey offering a saucer of cream, suggested that a young lady of Bundle's acquaintance, energy, and criminal record in the matter of cupboards might fill it. Bundle considered the state of the season, the state of her father's sighing, and the state of Bill Eversleigh, who had carried her out of one cellar already and was clearly good for several more.

"Yes," said Bundle. "But I shall want business cards. 'Bundle Brent — Seven Tails. Ring twice and show your tail.'"

She married Eversleigh in the autumn — the honeymoon was abroad, and, as Bundle put it, one was never sure with Bill whether it was a honeymoon or an assignment, and neither, thank goodness, was he.

Lord Caterham sighed like a kettle coming to the boil.

"Seven tails," he said mournfully, to nobody. "There were seven *horrors* in this family at the last count. Nobody ever counts the fathers."
