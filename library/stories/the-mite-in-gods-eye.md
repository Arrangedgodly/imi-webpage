# The Mite in God's Eye

*A parody of "The Mote in God's Eye"*

Off to the galactic east hangs the Coalsack, a black nebula that the star charts of the Second Empire of Prime-apes call the Face of God — and at its edge burns the red supergiant Muncheson's Eye, and beside the Eye, a single yellow star, visible only as a fleck of light swimming in the red.

For a thousand years, apes looking up at that fleck had a name for it.

They called it the Mite.

Nobody thought much about the Mite until the year 3017, when a derelict spacecraft under a sail of silver foil the size of a province came out of nowhere, decelerating hard, and was caught by the Empire at the gas giant of New Scotland. Aboard the derelict: one small alien corpse, mummified in its harness, with a flat muzzle, huge dark eyes, fur like plush — and two arms on each side that did not match. On the right, an arm like a weightlifter's, heavy as a jack. On the left, an arm slim as a wrench, ending in fingers a watchmaker would pray for.

It had crossed the void, dead at its post, to say one thing: *we are here.*

Captain Roderick Blaine — a young baboon of the great Blaine troop of Sparta, newly and politically given command of the battleship *MacArthur* — received the orders sealed in imperial cipher: *PROCEED TO THE MOTE. MAKE CONTACT. REPRESENT THE EMPEROR.* Aboard, whether as passenger, scientist, or imperial spy (all three, and no one pretended otherwise), came Sally Fowler, a chimpanzee noblewoman who had never met a species she didn't want to interview; Minister Horvath, a gorilla of boundless scientific appetite and zero military caution; and the *MacArthur's* Sailing Master, Kevin Renner, a capuchin whose grin was visible from other decks and whose mind ran on rails nobody else could see.

"We are about to meet the first intelligent species in the history of the Empire," Horvath said, quivering, as the *MacArthur* dove into the corona of Muncheson's Eye — the only place, bizarrely, where the jump point to the Mote sat, bathed in the red star's fury. Sailors called that jump Crazy Chimp's Folly, after the mythical Mite-side fool of their own charts: Crazy Chimp, the little primate who tries what cannot work, forever.

"First intelligent species," Renner agreed, buckling his harness as the corona flared. "Question is whether we count as theirs."

The Mote system was a riot of traffic: fusion ships, orbital habitats, an industrial belt stripped and strip-mined a hundred times over. The Mites came out to meet the *MacArthur* in gleaming ships of obvious genius and obvious, eerie patchwork — new alloys welded onto old hulls, as if their whole civilization had been repaired rather than built.

The first ambassador across the airlock stood half the height of a chimp, brown-and-white, with the four mismatched arms and eyes like wet coffee. It studied Captain Blaine for a long moment in front of the assembled honor guard, and then, in trader's Empire accent learned from the derelict's recordings, said:

"You are the leader. I name myself Rod." A pause, a very un-ape tilt of the head. "I have studied you for six of your days. I am your interpreter between species. We should be friends. Also — " and here every human in the lock jumped — "do you have a banana? The histories said the word. The word is important. We have wanted to know the word's *cargo* for a hundred years."

They did not, at first, understand why that was funny. That was the trouble. Nobody laughed at anything for a year.

The Mites, the Empire's scientists learned, were the finest engineers the universe had ever produced. Rod's retinue included a Mediator — a diplomat caste, bred to bond with a specific host and think exactly as it thought, who renamed himself Fyunch(nana) and mirrored Captain Blaine's walk, his speech rhythms, eventually his very reasoning — and a pair of Watchmakers: half-meter miniatures of the master species, all four hands and curiosity, whom the crew took for pets and trained to fix things in the ducts.

That was the *MacArthur's* death sentence, delivered by kindness. The Watchmakers bred in the dark of the ventilation — no one ever found the litters — and each generation was born improved, redesigned, and improving. Within a month there were hundreds; within two, thousands, working, welding, *optimizing*. The engines grew strange new organs. The wiring healed itself. The ship was becoming a better ship, and it was becoming it the way a body becomes a fever.

"Abandon ship," Rod said at last, and it cost him everything to say it, because a captain's ship is his branch, and a baboon does not let go of his branch. The crew poured onto the docked liner, and the *MacArthur*, with its small gods aboard still joyfully at their lathes, was burned in the Mote's outer dark by the escort *Lenin*, whose captain, a grim orang named Kutuzov, had drawn up plans to sterilize the entire contact mission since the day they arrived. "The Empire," Kutuzov said, by way of sympathy, "does not need to know what almost happened. I know. That will be enough for both of us."

And aboard the *Lenin*, in the long months home, Rod and Sally and Renner and Fyunch(nana) — who had come as Ambassador, and who watched Captain Blaine with a devotion that had stopped being study and become love of a kind the Empire had no regulation for — assembled the truth, and the truth was a trap with a species inside it.

The Mites could not stop having children. It was not culture; it was biology. A Mite female who did not conceive died of it, within a year, always. Every world they held filled to its rafters, and every cycle — their word; their calendars were carved with them — the over-filled worlds burst into war, burned themselves back to the museums, and the museums (this was the part that made Horvath weep: they had museums in exactly the way an amputee has a diary) preserved the knowledge to rebuild the next cycle faster. The great cycle of Mote history had run a hundred times. They had sterilized themselves down to one world and climbed back up, a hundred times, and now, this cycle, they had found the Crazy Chimp jump out — a clean, empty, hungry galaxy of empty branches — and they were asking, with perfect courtesy, to be let in.

"The derelict pilot," Sally said, on the last night, watching the red Eye swell behind them. "The one they sent a hundred years ago. Dead at its post. It was a message in a bottle. It was *hope*."

"It was a scouting report," said Renner, quietly.

"Can't it be both?"

"No," said Renner, and for once he did not grin. "That's the whole tragedy, ma'am. It can't."

The council on Sparta took three days to decide the fate of a species, which is either a long time or the shortest in galactic history. The Mites would outbreed, out-invent, and out-everything the Empire inside ten generations; every analyst agreed. Sally argued quarantine-with-trade, and the argument died in committee as arguments do, of arithmetic. The Emperor, in his own hand, set the answer: the jump point at Muncheson's Eye, the only door between the Mote and a million worlds, would be held closed. Forever. By force.

It fell to Captain Blaine to tell Fyunch(nana), because the Mediator had earned the truth from the one being in the universe whose mind it wore.

The small brown figure was silent a long time. When it spoke, it spoke as Rod would have, in Rod's own cadence — and then, deliberately, in its own.

"We guessed," Fyunch(nana) said. "We hoped your species was strong enough to say no. It is a terrible thing to learn about oneself, Captain, that one's hope is *being refused correctly*. We will build museums about you. The good kind. The kind with tools that still work." It looked out the port at the great red Eye, and the tiny bright fleck beside it, that mote of light in the eye of God. "You named us well, you know. A mite. Something small, in the eye of something vast, that makes it weep."

The *Lenin* took station at the Crazy Chimp point with a hundred ships behind it, and years later, when Captain Rod Blaine, brevetted and gray-muzzled, came out to take the blockade command, Renner met him at the dock with the old grin barely weathered at all.

"Pirates, blockade duty, and the most important door in the universe," said Renner. "Terrible posting. No amenities. You'll hate it."

"Somebody," said Captain Blaine, "has to mind the store."

And beyond them, in the red light, the Mite burned on — patient, brilliant, full — waiting for the Eye to weep, or blink.
