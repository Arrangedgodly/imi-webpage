# Hairy Potter and the Goblet of Fur

*A parody of "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire"*

The summer ended with Hairy Potter, Ron Wheezy, and Hermango Grubber gripping an old coconut on a misty hilltop at dawn — a Portkey, which is public transit for monkeys too tired to swing — and tumbling into the Quidditch Jungle Cup, where a hundred thousand primates screamed as Bulgaria's Viktor Krumhorn caught the snitch off a dive so steep the announcer fainted twice.

"Best match I ever saw," said Ron, "and it ended in a riot."

At midnight, it very nearly did: hooded baboons came marching across the campsite, cackling, levitating tents, and partying like it was the Dark Ages. Overhead, someone cast the sign everyone had hoped was retired — the Dark Mark, a scorch in the sky shaped like a skull with a banana peel slithering out of its mouth. The panic lasted all night. The summer, somehow, never really ended.

That autumn the Goblet of Fur sat in the Great Hollow of Howlwarts School for Distinguished Young Primates, glowing like a coconut full of sunrise. It was a chalice woven from the shed fur of a thousand generations of monkeys, and tonight it would choose three champions for the Trimagical Tournament — three tasks, eternal glory, and a pile of gold bananas the size of a small gorilla.

Around it, Albus Bumbledore, the ancient silverback headmaster, had drawn an Age Line in golden rope. Ron's twin brothers leapt over it drenched in aging potion and wearing matching false beards; the line bounced them into a heap, where the beards fused into one beard, which they wore jointly for a week out of stubbornness.

"Baaaah," rumbled Bumbledore, lifting one palm for quiet. "The Goblet has chosen. Viktor Krumhorn, of the Durmstrangle Vine Academy!" A hulking chimp with a nose like a bent banana bowed. "Fleur Delabana, of Beauxbanana!" A silvery langur flicked her tail. "That is all. Champions — to your branches!"

The Goblet hiccupped. A fourth scrap of birch bark shot into the air and sizzled onto the floor.

"Hairy Potter," read Bumbledore — and then, very calmly, because he was a very calm gorilla, "Hairy. Did you put your name in the Goblet of Fur?"

"No, sir," squeaked Hairy, a small macaque with a lightning-shaped cowlick and spectacles taped at the bridge.

"Did you ask an older monkey to put it in for you?"

"No, sir!"

"Somebody," muttered Ron, "is going ape."

Ape indeed. The Daily Bughole's Rita Skeeta — enormous glasses, enormous hat, a sting in every sentence — interviewed Hairy with a Quick-Quotes Quill that took dictation before anyone said anything. "Hairy Potter: Disturbed, Dangerous, and Possibly Dandruffed," ran the headline. Hairy got hate mail for a month, most of it pre-addressed.

Ape, part two. Hagrub, the hairy gamekeeper, leaked the first task while "accidentally" dropping fifty banana peels: "Nesting silverbacks, Hairy. Mothers. Guardin' golden bananas. Be very polite."

Hairy was polite for exactly four seconds. Then he shouted "Firebranch!" and his racing broom — a genuine birch twig with racing stripes — smacked into his palm. He performed the Rhesus Feint (learned, like all great art, by stealing it from Krumhorn), diving low so the furious mother chased him out of her nest, then swung underneath, snatched a golden banana, and stuck the landing in a rain of thrown fruit.

That night the common-room fire flickered, and a shaggy black shape appeared in the embers: Sniffles, Hairy's fugitive godfather, a grimound with a price on his pelt. "Someone inside Howlwarts put your name in that Goblet, pup. Watch the Macaque. Trust Sniff." He was the only adult in Hairy's life who gave advice that fit in a fortune cookie, which is to say: the best kind.

The second task's clue was the golden banana itself, which, when opened, screamed like a troop of howlers in a kettle. The answer arrived in the prefects' bathroom, of all places, where Moaning Myrtle hovered suggestively through the steam while Hairy submerged it and the shriek became a gurgle, then a song. "Mer-song," said Myrtle. "They'll take what you'll sorely miss. I heard it through the taps years ago. Nobody ever listens to the taps."

Ron forgave him by the second task, which was lucky, because the second task involved Ron. *"Come seek us where our voices are deep, / a treasure you'll peel but never keep,"* the Mer-troop of the Black Lagoon had sung, and then they took what each champion would sorely miss: and what Hairy sorely missed was Ron, floating limply in the kelp. A freed house-marmoset named Dobby pressed a wad of gorilla-weed into Hairy's hand — "Chew it, sir, and breathe like a gorilla, sir!" — so down he went and hauled both Ron and little Gabrielle Delabana to the surface. The mer-chief awarded him points for courtesy. Ron awarded him a noogie.

Between tasks came the tournament's cruelest test: the Yule Ball. Hairy danced with the grace of a monkey in a sack race; Ron wore dress robes cut from a moth-eaten tablecloth; and both spent the evening staring at Hermango, who arrived on Krumhorn's arm looking like a duchess and acting like one, too.

"You're fraternizing with the competition!" Ron hissed.

"You're wearing a doily," said Hermango. "Take notes, Wheezy. I'll be at the winners' table."

The third task was the Tanglemaze: a labyrinth of throttling vines, snapping sphinx-moths, and one riddle — *what is long and yellow and guilty of nothing?* — whose answer, obviously, was banana. Hermango, watching from the stands, had by then jarred a beetle she'd noticed attending too many private conversations; Rita Skeeta's career as a bug ended with air holes and no column.

Hairy and the handsome Cedric Digguiri reached the center together. "Take it," said Cedric nobly. "No, you take it," said Hairy. Since neither chimp could out-noble the other, they grabbed the golden trophy banana at the same time.

The banana yanked. The world peeled away. They landed in a graveyard of hanging lianas where a sniveling tree shrew named Wormtail dropped the trophy into a cauldron of milk, moonlight, and one writhing hair. Out of the froth rose a hairless, noseless macaque, pale as an unripe peel.

"I am ripened again," hissed Voldemacaque — He Who Must Not Be Groomed. He flicked his tail. "Peel the spare."

The spell missed Cedric's dignity but caught his wakefulness, and Cedric dropped into a hundred-year nap. The Dark Lord turned on Hairy, and Hairy, terrified and furious, shouted the only spell that mattered: "Expelliarmus, you banana-split!"

Their wands locked. Golden light latticed the mist — Priori Banana-tam — and out of it drifted echoes: Cedric's sleepy voice begging him to take his body home, and older, braver monkeys still swinging. *Break the connection!* they whispered. Hairy wrenched free, seized the trophy, and tumbled back to Howlwarts.

"Constant grooming!" barked Mad-Eye Macaque, hauling Hairy into his office and licking his own eyeball, which was frankly rude. "Grooming reveals everything, boy — like how I'm not Moody at all!" But before Barty Squeak Jr. could finish his villain monologue, Bumbledore burst in, polyjuice splashed, and one traitor went bananas into a barrel.

Cedric woke a day later with a magnificent mane of snow-white fur and no memory of peels. At the end-of-term feast, Bumbledore rose. "Remember Cedric Digguiri. Remember that naps have consequences, that cups can lie, and that Voldemacaque has returned. This is no longer monkey business. It never was."

Hairy, rubbing his scar, decided that next year he was taking up gardening. Quiet gardening. Underground.
