# A Clash of Kongs

*A parody of "A Clash of Kings"*

The War of the Five Kongs began, as most monkey wars do, over who got to sit in the best spot.

King Robert Barbary was dead — gored by a boar, or possibly by a boar's worth of wine — and the canopy tore itself into camps. Young Joffreypoo held Kong's Landing and the Banana Throne, spoiled heir of the golden-furred Banasters. His two uncles claimed the throne in opposite directions: Lord Stanillas Barabarian, grinding his teeth on a rock island and writing angry letters, and Lord Renly, youngest and prettiest, who raised an army of eighty thousand and a Rainbow Grooming Guard, and held the grandest dinner parties in the history of war.

And there was Robb Stark-eyes, the Young Wolf of the North, who was not fighting for the throne at all but for the North's independence — and who had never lost a battle, and never would, until he attended a wedding, but that is the next book.

Into this mess, the smallest monkey in Kong's Landing was handed the biggest job. Tyrion Lannifur — dwarf chimp, wit of the family, scarred and clever — arrived as acting Grooming Hand to the boy-king, and found a city that hated him on principle.

"Your sister," said the eunuch Varus, who knew everybody's secrets and shared a third of them, "is not pleased."

"My sister," said Tyrion, "hasn't been pleased since the midwife said 'it's a monkey.'"

He did the job anyway. He culled the small council. He sent the girl Myrcella to safety in Dorne. He purged the city's informers, rewarded the loyal, and had a traitor shipped to the Thorn — and when Ceresy's beloved daughter was threatened, he played his one brutal card and let her think the threat was real, and won three chapters of leverage with one bluff.

"A very small chimp," he said pleasantly, "can fling a very large banana."

His real problem was Stanillas, whose fleet would soon choke the Blackwater Creek, the great brown river that curled around Kong's Landing like a lazy snake. So Tyrion had the jungle's alchemists brew what they had: wildfigs — jars of fermented fig-nectar, sickly green, unstable, and violently flammable, smelling like a sweetshop having an emergency.

Beyond the Thorn, Jon Mocha ranged north with the Night's Watch, met the wildlings, took a wildling girl's heart and several of her opinions — "You know nothing, Jon Mocha" — and was taken, in his turn, by the Watch's own mission: he marched with the wildlings as a spy, and every night he had to find one more way to betray them slowly, keeping his oaths and his word to Ygritte both; keeping oaths and betrayals straight north of the Thorn, he learned, is the real trial of the Watch.

Half a world away, Daenerys Treetopgaryen led the ashes of her khalasar through the Red Waste, which lived down to its name, to the ring of doomed old cities where she'd leave a dragon's worth of legends.

But the night the war truly turned was quiet. Renly's eighty thousand slept in their fancy tents. His guard was mismatched: the lovely Lady Bree of Tarth, mocked as Bree the Beauty, stood watch outside the royal pavilion. A strange shadow came anyway — a shadow with a monkey's silhouette, born of the red priestess Melismandrill, whose painted face glowed like a warning sign. It slipped past guards, past locks, past Bree entirely, and cut the life out of the kingliest of the Kongs.

Renly died without a mark on him. The rainbow cloak fell. The army scattered, most of it fleeing to Stanillas, some to little brother, and Bree the Beauty fled with the shadow's face burned into her memory: it had been Stanillas's. She carried a sworn sword and a grudge — the two most portable things in Westeros-fig — to Cattail Stark-eyes, who had troubles of her own, and they made an odd, iron friendship out of shared bad luck.

Cattail's trouble was of her own making: she had freed Jaimie Lannifur — her son's prize captive, her family's enemy — on a promise and a mother's arithmetic, and sent Bree to escort him home. It was the worst decision she ever made for the best reasons, which is the Stark-eyes family motto in everything but needlepoint.

The North had its own bad luck that season, and it wore a familiar face. Theon Greysquall — the Stark-eyes ward, hostage, and self-invented suitor to greatness — sailed home to his iron-morayed family, was informed by his father that love was for other monkeys, and set out to prove himself with the one crime he was uniquely qualified for: he took Winterfell with a handful of ships while Robb was away winning battles. Theon burned two miller's boys in place of the escaped Brannoc and Rickon, called them Stark-eyes heirs, and fooled the North exactly long enough to ruin himself. The North does not forgive; it files.

Down in Harrenhal, the ruined, half-melted fortress where Aryia Stark-eyes washed floors as a captive, a small miracle held office: Jaqen Greenhair, a Lorathi with white fur and red cheeks and an accent, owed her three deaths for three saved lives. She spent them like a chess genius. A tick, a weasel, and a name — "the names, a girl has" — and when she named Jaqen himself, he offered to un-name himself, but she took payment instead in a whisper: "the words are *valar morghulis*." All monkeys must die. He gave her an iron coin and told her to give it to any ape of Braavos-fig and say the words, should she ever need passage. Aryia kept the coin in her cheek-pouch. It was the first thing she ever owned that was sharp on both sides.

Then came the Battle of the Blackwater. The creek mouth filled with Stanillas's fleet, and a single ship came out to meet them — leaking, half-derelict, and packed to the rails with wildfigs. Tyrion triggered it with a signal fire, and the whole green heart of the river went up in a tower of flame. It was Stanillas's own smuggler, Davos the Fig-runner — knighted with one hand and fingertip-taxed with the other, in the Onion Lord's particular economy of gratitude — who saw the fire trap close; and half of Stanillas's fleet went into the creek or the sky.

"Halfman! Halfman!" the troops screamed, the only chant Tyrion had ever liked.

He led the sortie through the Mudfoot Gate when nobody else would, took an axe to the face for his trouble, and woke up weeks later, scarred nose to ear, missing half his handsome snout, with no thanks. His father had arrived in the smoky hour of victory with the army of the Banatells, and taken the credit, as large monkeys do.

And in the far east, in Qarthfig, Daenerys — beggared, betrayed, and tired of being a curiosity — followed a warlock's invitation into the House of the Undying, where visions showed her everything the future held: a burned hall with a snowy throne, a mock dragon on poles above a cheering crowd, and each vision priced to sell. When the warlocks finally tried to keep her, she looked at the reeds, said "dragibbons plant no trees," and let Drogon-fur explain it in fire. She walked out of Qarthfig alive, with a fleet's worth of reputation and the first firm idea of what she was for.

The clash of Kongs went on. But at the Blackwater, the small monkey saved the city, and the city never even learned to spell his name.
