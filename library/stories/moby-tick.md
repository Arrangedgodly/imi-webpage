# Moby-Tick

*A parody of "Moby-Dick"*

Call me Chimpmael. Some years ago — never mind how long precisely — having little or no money in my fur, and nothing particular to interest me on the branch, I thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the jungle. Whenever I find myself growing grim about the muzzle; whenever it is a damp, drizzly November in my soul; whenever I find myself involuntarily pausing before banana warehouses, and bringing up the rear of every funeral I meet — then I account it high time to get to sea as soon as I can.

I signed aboard the Peapod, a ship of the old school out of Nantucket-nest, and I will tell you what befell her voyage, which explains everything that followed.

The first happened before we ever sailed. At the Squawker-Inn I shared a bed with a harpooneer — a colossal, tattooed gorilla named Queequeg, a prince in his own country, who sold carved harpoon-heads for a living and offered me half of his raw banana at breakfast as a sign that we were married in the eyes of his tribe. "Queequeg no go back on friend," he said, and he never did. On Sunday we heard Father Maple preach — a right whale of a chaplain hoisted bodily up to his pulpit by block and tackle, like furniture with opinions — on the subject of Jonah, who was thrown to the sea by a crew that had no quarrel with him whatever. "To preach the Truth to the face of Falsehood!" he thundered, paw on the mast-pulpit, and every monkey in the pew straightened his fur.

The second was the captain. We shipped without seeing him, and no wonder. Captain Ahab stood a full inch taller than any baboon in the fleet, with a white scar running down his face and into his grey fur like a lightning-stroke in a dead tree, and he stood upon a peg of polished bone — for Moby-Tick, the great white tick of the Pacific, had taken his leg. Some say it took his calf, which Ahab felt was adding insult to injury. Before we sailed, a ragged old baboon called Elijah watched us up the gangway and wished us a horror of a good voyage: "Some of ye have shipped with Ahab before — did they come back, now?" We laughed, pocketed our advance bananas, and went aboard laughing, which is what crews are for.

The third was the gold. Ahab nailed a Spanish ounce to the mainmast and addressed the crew from the quarterdeck.

"Whosoever of ye raises me that same white tick," he cried, "shall have this ounce of gold, my boys — give it to me!"

"Aye, aye!" we shouted, and swung into the rigging like the mad, merry crew we were. Starbuck, the first mate — a steady, long-armed ape with a conscience — alone looked grave.

"Vengeance on a dumb beast!" said Starbuck. "That simply smote thee from blindest instinct! To be enraged with a tick, Captain Ahab, is madness!"

"Hark ye," said Ahab, dragging the peg-leg across the deck. "All visible objects, man, are but as pasteboard masks. That inscrutable thing is chiefly what I hate; and be the white tick agent, or be the white tick principal, I will wreak that hate upon him."

Every monkey aboard read the nailed doubloon his own way. Starbuck saw in it the mountains of home and a nest under a quiet tree; Stubb saw his zodiac and his own lucky star; Flask said it was worth sixteen cigars if anybody was buying. Ahab read it last, and read it as himself.

For months we cruised, lowering our boats for common ticks enough to fill the ship's casks and fund the captain's crusade. Queequeg and I went over the side tied together by what the fleet calls, for reasons I never understood, a monkey-rope; his luck was my luck, his drowning would have been mine, and neither of us thought anything of it, which was the whole marriage. At night the try-pots blazed on deck, and I steered by the light of boiling tick-fat, which is as close as a monkey comes to steering by his own conscience. And in the bow of the captain's own boat, when it was lowered, crouched Fedallah and his Persian baboons, who had come aboard nobody knew when, wearing their turbans in Ahab's shadow as though the shadow had hired them. We gammed with the Albatross, a trim bark whose captain hailed us back with no words at all — only a speaking-trumpet dropped into the sea, as who should say the weather had said everything first. We gammed with the Samuel Enderby, whose Captain Boomer had lost an arm to the white tick and wore a spare monkey's arm for Sundays. He laughed at Ahab across the water.

"Tighter and tighter, old fellow! I'd advise ye to be content with the leg ye have. I'll send ye my spare, nay, I'll throw in a crutch for free!"

"Away!" cried Ahab, and we left them laughing, which is the worst thing you can do to a monkey in earnest.

Then Queequeg took a fever and lay down to die. He had the carpenter build him a coffin of good fig-wood, calmly, like a monkey ordering a hammock; then he rallied, remembering (he said) a banana he had left unfinished ashore, and lived. The coffin, being handsome and watertight, was caulked, slung at the stern, and used by the crew as a banana-crate — which is the sort of joke a ship makes without knowing it.

Then we raised him: a rolling, appalling bulk of whiteness, the vast pale parasite of the seas, with a wrinkled brow and a long inexplicable shadow trailing beneath the waves.

"Thar she burrows!" cried the lookouts.

We had passed, that very week, the Rachel, whose captain, mad with grief, begged us to help him search for his son, lost in a boat. "For eight days and eight nights," he called across the water. Ahab set his face against him. "I will not do it," he said, and the sea closed between us like a ledger shutting.

On the first day of the great chase, Ahab's boat gave chase. Moby-Tick, with a malice astonishing in so lowly a vermin, smashed the boat with one heave of his bloated body, and Ahab went down clinging to the wreck, dragged to and fro like a chimp on a chain.

On the second day his boat was stove again and his tiger-striped rower lost, tangled in the line and gone. And on the third day — as the Peapod herself closed for the kill — the great tick turned and bore down upon the ship.

"Turn up all hands and make sail!" cried Starbuck. But Ahab stood in the bow, harpoon poised, and spoke the words I have never since shaken out of my fur:

"Towards thee I roll, thou all-destroying but unconquering tick; to the last I grapple with thee; from heck's heart I stab at thee; for hate's sake I spit my last breath at thee!"

He hurled the harpoon, and the line, snaking foul, caught him round the neck. He was gone into the white water before the echo died — gone, dragged down by his own rope and his own hate. Moby-Tick breached once, in terrible triumph, and brought his whole vast bulk down upon the Peapod. She sank as a harpooneer's banana sinks: straight down, a flurry of bubbles, then nothing. Tashtego, the last monkey at the mast, was still hammering the ship's banana-flag to the spar as the sea took the hammer, then the arm, then the sky.

The drama's done. Why then does anybody step forth? Because one did survive the wreck. It was Queequeg's coffin — the watertight one, the banana-crate — chosen by fate to be my life-buoy, that bore me up through the wreck-sea; and it was the devious-cruising Rachel, that in her retracing search after her missing children, only found another orphan chimp.

Call me Chimpmael still — for I only am escaped alone to tell thee.
