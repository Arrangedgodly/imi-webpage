# The Scratch Trials

*A parody of "The Scorch Trials"*

Thomas woke up in a white room that smelled of antiseptic and bad decisions, and his first thought, as always, was: *Where's the maze? Where are my friends? Where are my bananas?*

He'd survived the Glade, escaped the Grievers — those horrible mechanic monkey-spiders with too many arms — and been "rescued" by people in white coats. For a few days, it had almost been nice. Chow. Bunk beds. Rat-face taking attendance. And Teresa, on the other side of a wall, talking to him inside his skull the way she always had — which was romantic, in a haunted sort of way.

*They're playing games with us,* her voice said.

*Then they picked the wrong monkeys,* Thomas thought back, which felt brave and accomplished nothing.

Then the lights went out, the windows went dark, and when Thomas's friends cracked open their rescuers' chamber, every white coat hung from the ceiling like a ripened pod. Cranks, someone whispered. Or worse.

"We're taking over," said Janson, a scarred old drill sergeant of a baboon, reappearing as if nothing were wrong. "You've been selected for the next phase of trials. The Scratch Trials. PICKED will save the jungle. PICKED is good."

"PICKED," Thomas repeated slowly, "is neither of those things."

PICKED — *Primate Institute for Cruel Komprehension of Extreme Diseases* — had a problem: the Flea-r, an itch that got into the brain. First you scratched. Then you twitched. Then you went completely bananas, screaming at trees. The sun-scorched jungle between the two bases, a bald strip of dead land called the Scratch, was crawling with the infested. Also, every Glader now had PROPERTY OF PICKED tattooed on the back of his neck, which was the most honest thing the institute had ever done.

"Cross the Scratch," Janson instructed, gesturing at a shimmering portal — the Flat Trance — that hummed like an angry beehive. "Reach the safe haven in two weeks. Lovely weather we're having. Off you pop."

The Gladers stepped through and found the Scratch waiting: a hundred miles of dust, dead stumps, and heat that made a monkey appreciate shade the way a fish appreciates water. Their heads had been shaved — "to prevent pest migration," the white coats had said, the most sinister sentence ever spoken to a primate — and the wind carried a fine gray ash that itched.

The first Cranks came at dusk. They'd been people once, and you could still see it in their faces, which was the worst part — a wild-eyed chimp who'd clawed his own fur out, giggling, "Got rice? Got rice? Got rice?" through the broken window of an abandoned tower.

"Hi there! I'm Brenda," said a girl with her hair cropped to fuzz and a machete slung on her back. "And this is Jorge. We run the Crank-pot around here." Jorge, a scarred howler with a pilot's smirk, waved. "Stay off the fourth floor. They've gone full banana."

They didn't stay off the fourth floor, because Thomas's luck worked like that. The fourth floor was having a party — Cranks in party hats, swaying to music only they could hear, one of them weeping over a balloon. The balloon was somehow the saddest part. Brenda grabbed Thomas's paw in the dark of the stairwell, and they ran, and somewhere between screaming matches she whispered: "If I start scratching my ear, you run. Promise me." He promised. Neither of them liked it.

The Scratch itself tried creative murders. There was a storm of ball lightning that rolled across the flats like a troop of drunk comets, herding them into a rusted fruit-packing plant where the metal beams sang. There was a village where the water was fine and the welcome was not. On the fourth day their own water ran out, and on the fifth they learned why the maps showed no oases: the Flea-r didn't just take minds, it took the wells first, and PICKED had drawn the route anyway.

They crossed. They buried friends — because this was a trial, and trials took payment. They laid Winston under a cairn of dead stumps, and Newt said the only prayer the Glade had ever owned: "He was one of us. Whoever's watching — take note."

Then, in the mountains, the ambush: a troop of girls led by Teresa, Thomas's oldest friend, who tied him to a tree and wouldn't say why.

"This is the plan," Teresa said, and wouldn't explain, and her eyes were wet, and she kept saying *trust me* like a monkey who'd already been told the ending of a very sad story. Thomas kept his eyes closed for two days while she talked to the white coats over his head. Some friendships survive that. This one held on by a branch.

The "safe haven" turned out to be a PICKED compound, and Janson was waiting in a white coat, looking pleased in the way that only a baboon with a clipboard can. Teresa had been working for them. Thomas wasn't even angry anymore — anger took energy, and so did walking.

"You passed," said Janson. "The Scratch was a filter. The Flea-r kills; some of you are immune, and we needed to know which. Your brains resist the itch, and we're going to find out why, one needle at a time. PICKED is good, Thomas."

"Stop *saying* that," said Thomas.

But he'd seen the things the white coats didn't say out loud: the cages. The needles. His friend Minho, their fastest runner, strapped to a gurney in a windowless room, sedated mid-sentence. The Gladers had escaped a maze. They'd crossed the Scratch. Now they needed to do something harder — take the fight to PICKED itself.

"We're not variables," Thomas told his friends through the bars. "We're not their banana data. We're the troop. And troops don't leave runners behind."

Something in the compound began to bang. It was Newt — gentle, limping, level-headed Newt — hammering at his cell door with the quiet fury of an ape who has finally identified the real monkeys running the circus.

"PICKED," he called down the corridor, "is *not* good."

The lights flickered. Somewhere, a klaxon — and then an alarm of a different kind: the Right Paw, a scruffy network of runaways and half-scratchy rebels who had spent months stealing PICKED's keys, blowing PICKED's doors, and generally failing to write victory speeches. They hit the compound's west wing. Jorge hit the roof in a stolen berg, laughing like a howler at the moon, with Brenda at the controls and no landing plan whatsoever, on the principle that a plan would only slow them down.

"Minho first," Thomas said, and the troop moved.

They came out through the smoke with their runner over their shoulders, Newt hammering a guard with a food tray, and Brenda flying cover so low she took fur off the fence. Teresa came too — dragged aboard, half-willing, all exhausted — because Thomas had made a decision on that tree that no trial had authorized: whatever she'd done, he wasn't leaving her. Behind them the compound burned, and Janson stood at a window watching his variables leave, already writing the next trial in his head, because the only thing PICKED ever really produced was more PICKED.

The Scratch Trials were over. The war for the jungle was just beginning.
