# The Loris

*A parody of "The Lorax"*

At the far end of town where the Grickle-Grass grows, and the wind smells slow-and-sour when it blows, and no birds ever sing excepting old crows — is the Street of the Lifted Loris.

What was the Loris? And why was it lifted and taken somewhere? To find out, pay up: fifteen cents and a nail and the peel of a great-great-great-grandfather's 'nana. Then the old monkey in the Lerkim tells all through the Whisper-ma-Phone.

His name is the Chimpler, old Chimpler is he, and he snaps and he chats in a chimpling-key. "It all started way back," he croaks, "when the canopy was still green and the clouds were still clean, and we Chimplers had great biggie dreams of biggering, biggering, biggering everything.

"I came with a wagon, a rope, and a sly, chimplish grin, looking for something that everyone, everyone, EVERYONE needs. And I found it! The tufted Banuffula Tree! Trunk like a handshake, tufts soft as sneezes, all dripping with banana-bright, bunchy-ish ease. And the Bar-ba-loots frisking in their Bar-ba-loot suits, the Swomee-Swans singing their swanky sweet tunes, and the Humming-Fish humming in the clean-water lagoon!

"So I knitted a Troop-need from the tuft of one tree — a Fine-Something-That-All-Monkeys-Need! — and I chopped down a tree with one great whack, hack, hack — and at that very moment, a creature came back. He was shortish. And oldish. And mossy-ish, too. And he moved very slowly, the way lorises do. He gazed at the stump with his big round slow eyes and spoke with a voice that was sharpish and wise:

"'I am the Loris,' he said. 'I speak for the trees. I speak for the trees, and the vines, and the bees. I speak for the figs and the ferns and the flowers. And I'm asking you, sir, with the last of my breath — what do you think you are doing to death?'

"'Look, Loris,' I said, 'there's no cause for alarm. I chopped just one tree. I'm doing no harm. I'm being quite useful. This thing is a Troop-need! A Troop-need's a Fine-Something-That-All-Monkeys-Need! It's a swing. It's a sling. It's a groomer. It's a hat — and it has other uses, far beyond that.'

"'Sir!' cried the Loris, 'you are crazy with greed! There is no monkey on earth who would buy that fool Troop-need!'

"But at that very moment, a chap came along, and he looked at the Troop-need, and he tried it as a hammock, and he wore it as a cape, and he used it to carry his fruit home — and he happily paid me nine bananas. And a granny bought two. And a bachelor baron bought nine. And that was the start.

"'I'm just one small monkey,' I told myself, 'with one small wagon.' But the money came faster than the leaves come in autumn, and my Chimpler family came, hungry for shares — aunts, uncles, cousins, all named Chimpler, all named for the sound of a cash-box — and we built biggering mills, and I invented the Super-Axe-Hacker, which whacked off four Banuffulas with one smacker! And my family, sir, my family, they all sat around, and they biggered my money, which everyone needs.

"The Loris came back. He came back every time.

"'I am the Loris,' he said, and he hauled himself up by the seat of his furry pants. 'I speak for the Bar-ba-loots, and I speak for the Swomee-Swans, and I speak for the fish that go hum in the ponds. And I'm asking you, sir, with the last of my patience —'

"'No harm, no harm,' I said. 'I'm being quite useful.'

"'Useful!' said the Loris. 'You're glumping the pond where the Humming-Fish hummed! Your Schloppity-Schlopp is schlopping their summer! The poor Bar-ba-loots — first they got crummies in their tummies, then all their Banuffula gumminies ran out, and now they sit all day on their rumps and just snooze. They'll have to go. I cannot let them stay. I'm sending them off to find their own way.'

"And he did. And I biggered.

"'I am the Loris,' he said the next time, in air gone all smogglulous. 'I speak for the Swomee-Swans, who can't sing a note, not even a snoat, for the smoke in their throats.'

"'Smoke,' I said, 'is progress, and progress is needed by everyone, everyone, EVERYONE.'

"'You are being very clever, sir,' said the Loris slowly, which was somehow worse than shouting. 'That is exactly the problem. You say everyone needs your Troop-needs. But everyone needs trees to begin with, sir, or there will be no everyone left needing.'

"And I biggered. I biggered my mills, and I biggered my wagons, and I biggered my money and my family's shares, and I biggered my 'everyone, everyone, EVERYONE needs' until the word 'everyone' meant only the Chimplers. And I chopped, and I hacked, and I smacked and I whacked — until one morning, I tell you, one bright terrible morning, I whacked and I hacked and there was nothing left standing, nothing left bending, nothing left to chop at all. Just one stump. Then one more whack — the very last Banuffula Tree of them all. No more trees. No more tufts. No more Troop-needs to sell. And without Troop-needs, no more money — and without money, my family, every last Chimpler named Chimpler, packed off in a great big hurry, leaving me alone in the Grickle-Grass, wondering where the biggering had gone. It was the middle of the afternoon, and it was dark, sir. That was the strange thing. It was dark at noon, and quiet — no humming, no singing, no frisking, no snoozing. Nothing at all, and nobody left to say nothing to. Except one.

"The Loris never said any words to me then. He didn't scold. He didn't point. He just gave me one very sad, sad, backward glance, slow as syrup, sad as slow — and he lifted himself, by the seat of his own furry pants! — up through the smogglulous hole in the sky where the canopy used to be, and he went. He went, and he left me with the stump and the silence, and one last thing, on that grim, grickle-y day: a small pile of rocks by the stump where he'd lay. And the rocks said one word, just one word, if you please — and that word was: UNLESS.

"For years I sat there, thinking it through. UNLESS what? UNLESS who? I'm an old monkey now, and it took me an old monkey's while, but since you've paid your fifteen cents and your nail and your 'nana-peel, I will tell you what's true, the whole of it, truly: UNLESS someone like you cares a whole awful lot, nothing is going to get better. It's not.

"So... catch!"

And the Chimpler, old Chimpler, he lets something drop. It's a seed — the last Banuffula seed of them all, small and brown in your palm, and suddenly you're tall with the whole jungle waiting on you.

"Plant a new Troop-need?" says the Chimpler. "Treat it with care. Give it clean water. And feed it fresh air. Grow a forest. Protect it from axes that hack. Then the Loris — and all of his friends — may come back."
