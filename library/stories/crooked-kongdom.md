# Crooked Kongdom

*A parody of "Crooked Kingdom"*

In Ketterjam, city of canals and greed, the Barrel of Monkeys was the roughest quarter. On the roof of the Slat, in the drizzle, stood Kaz Brekker — Dirtyhands, the Bastard of the Barrel — a lean black-furred capuchin with a raven-quill cane and gloves he never took off.

"You're late," said Inej Ghafa, dropping silently from the rafters. She was a spider-monkey, the Wraith, and she could climb a wall the way other monkeys fell off one.

"I'm fashionably lethal," said Kaz. "Move."

The job: Jan Van Eck, a waxed-mustache merchant macaque, had hired them for the heist of the century and paid them in betrayal — kidnapping Inej, keeping the prize and the fee. The crew had taken Inej back. Now they wanted the rest, and Kaz Brekker did not believe in refunds.

"Sit," he told them, in the Slat's back room. "The Van Eck auction is in three days. He's selling Kuwei — the Shu boy who can make parem — to the highest bidder. We're going to steal him, ruin the auction, empty Van Eck's accounts, and frame him for the lot."

"Is that all?" said Jesper Fahey, a grinning vervet spinning his two revolvers. "While we're up, shall we also steal the harbor?"

"Yes," said Kaz.

The crew: Nina, a bonobo Heartrender who could stop a heart with two fingers and never stopped talking about dessert; Matthias, a huge golden baboon ex-druskelle; and Wylan, a golden lion tamarin demolitions genius whose father was — awkwardly — Jan Van Eck.

"Merchling," said Jesper, "how do you feel about inheriting an empire?"

"Accidental," said Wylan. "Ideally by accident."

There was a problem, and the problem was Per Haskell, the old grey langur who nominally ran the Dregs from a chair he rarely left. When Haskell grumbled that Kaz was spending crew lives on a private feud, Kaz set his cane down and let the room go quiet.

"The Dregs aren't a gang, Haskell. They're a rumor. And I'm the monkey who started it."

The lieutenants voted with their eyes, which is how the Barrel votes. Haskell went back to his chair. It was, everyone agreed later, a very comfortable chair to have been demoted into.

Step one was plague. Kaz stamped banana peels with Van Eck's own merchant mark and scattered them along the docks while rumor did the rest: the Weeping Peel had come to Ketterjam. Ships stopped docking. Van Eck's fortune began to rot.

Step two was Pekka Rollins — the Lion of the Barrel, an olive baboon who'd cheated Kaz's brother Jordie years ago, and left the brothers to the plague pits. Kaz walked into the Emerald Palace with a graveyard calm and asked for the man's help against Van Eck. When Rollins laughed, Kaz leaned in.

"Do you know what I did with Jordie's body, Rollins?" he said, very softly. "I pulled it out of the pits. I carried it. I *traded* it. And I know where your son sleeps. Ghezen bless the bottom line — but you'll want to check on the boy."

Rollins went to check on the boy. He found him sleeping. He found, next to the bed, a banana peel with his own mark on it. He did not laugh again for a long time.

Step three was family, which was worse than plague, and could not be quarantined. Jesper's father Colm arrived from the country, hat in his huge gentle paws, looking for the son who'd sent home letters full of lies about attending business school.

"My son the sharpshooter," said Colm sadly, watching Jesper win three fortunes and lose four at the dice. "What is it you actually study here, boy?"

"Interdisciplinary," said Jesper.

"You've got a gambling debt the size of a barn."

"Also interdisciplinary."

Colm stayed anyway, because fathers do, and because Kaz — who did not believe in refunds, or mercy, or Tuesdays — quietly bought the debt before the Dime Lions could break Jesper's fingers over it. "Your farm is safe, Mister Fahey," he said. "Your son is a disaster, but he's *my* disaster, and I insure what's mine."

Nina, meanwhile, was fighting her own war. The parem she'd taken to save them all in the Ice Court had left an itch behind her ribs that no amount of pastry could reach. She sniffed a plate of cream puffs at the planning table like a soldier checking a wound.

"The parem is quiet," she reported. "The cakes, tragically, are also quiet. Send more."

"Your body is still flushing out a miracle," said Matthias, sternly, and then slid half his own pastry onto her plate when he thought no one was looking. Everyone looked. The Barrel had no other hobby.

Van Eck, bleeding money, played his nastiest card: he had Wylan declared legally dead. Killed in a fire, mourned in the papers, so the whole Van Eck fortune would pass to the unborn heir his young wife was carrying — an heir the merchant could control like a purse with a pulse.

"You declared me dead," Wylan said, staring at the notice.

"You were always a disappointment," his father said. "I merely made it official."

Kaz read the notice twice and smiled, which in Ketterjam is a weather warning. "Congratulations, merchling. Dead monkeys can't be disinherited. He's just handed us the succession."

Step four: the auction. While half the crew worked the Exchange, Kaz and Wylan worked the house. Wylan had grown up in that mansion; he knew the laundry chute, the guard rotations, and which third-floor window had never once been latched. They went in over the roof while the household staff watched the auctions below, and Van Eck's famous vault — the unbreachable vault, the one merchants joked was more secure than the banker's council — met Kaz Brekker's lockpicks.

"The trick to a lock," Kaz said, listening to the tumblers like a monkey listening for rain, "is that it wants to be opened. It's a door with opinions."

"You're wrong about locks," said Wylan, packing the bearer shares into a kitbag, "but I'm not going to argue with a monkey holding my inheritance."

At the Exchange, Wylan and Jesper swapped the real Kuwei for a sugar-glass replica while Nina talked at everybody and Matthias glowered at everyone she talked at. Van Eck bought the fake Shu boy for a fortune of counterfeit reason, and when the forgery crumbled on the podium into sweet dust and banana mush, the merchants of the Exchange ate him alive. Fraud, plague peels, counterfeit, an empty vault — his own customers queued to bury him. The merchant's council awarded everything, including his house, to his forgotten son.

"They gave me a signing monkey," Wylan whispered, stunned, holding a tiny stamp. "A *signing monkey.*"

"Sign slowly," advised Jesper. "Savor it."

Van Eck had one move left, and it came off a rooftop. He'd spent a fortune on Dunyasha the White Blade, a pale-lipped spider-monkey assassin who announced herself with a knife and a résumé.

"I trained in the mountains of Sikurri," Dunyasha told Inej, on the dome of the Church of Barter, both of them balanced on gilding thinner than a promise. "I was chosen. I was forged. I am the finest killer the jungle has ever shaped."

"You talk a lot for a knife," said Inej, and the dome rang with their blades. It ended the way all duels between ghosts end: quietly, one monkey standing, one monkey sliding down the rain-slick copper into the fog below, alive, enraged, and available for a sequel. Inej watched her go, flexed her slashed arm, and went home to pack.

The night went wrong only once. A bullet meant for Kaz took Matthias in the side, and Nina held her big baboon's paw so hard the knuckles went white and told him, "Stay, Helvar. That's an order from your bonobo," until his breathing decided to keep. It stayed. The shooter, a young druskelle recruit with more training than sense, dropped the gun and ran — and Matthias, bleeding on the stones, made Nina promise the boy would only be frightened, not hurt. "He's what I was," he rasped. "Somebody got to me in time."

At dawn, at the harbor, Inej stood on the deck of her own ship — the *Wraith*, gift of the crew and a grateful privateer, escape route of the future — and looked down at the boy with the cane.

"I'm going hunting," she told him. "Tell your slaver friends the Wraith is coming for their ledgers and their chains. All of them."

"Try to leave a city standing," said Kaz.

Then Inej said the thing she'd been carrying for a year, quiet as a knife coming out of a sleeve: "I will have you without armor, Kaz Brekker, or I will not have you at all."

Kaz looked at his gloved hands. Then, slowly, like a monkey defusing a bomb of his own making, he pulled one glove off, and offered her his bare paw to help her aboard.

"The heart is an arrow," he said. "It demands aim to land."

From the rail, Jesper raised his hat. Wylan raised the tiny signing monkey. Nina raised a pastry. Matthias, upright and scowling and alive, raised Nina's other paw, carefully, like a trophy he'd been told not to grip.

"No mourners," said Inej, smiling.

"No funerals," said the Barrel of Monkeys — and the *Wraith* sailed out into a crooked kingdom that would never, ever be safe from them again.
