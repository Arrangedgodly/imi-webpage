# Gorilla's Travels

*A parody of "Gulliver's Travels" by Jonathan Swift*

My father had a nice little grove in Nottinghamshire, and being third of five sons, I was sent to sea as barber-surgeon to the crews, and so began my travels. I, Lemuel Gorillaver, a plain gorilla of honest bulk, have set down here only the plain truth.

**A Voyage to Littleput.** Our ship struck a rock in a storm, and I swam until I could swim no farther, and dropped asleep upon a shore. I woke bound: a slender vine crossed my chest, and when I tried to rise, a hundred tiny arrows of prickly seed-pods stung my knuckles. The little people — pygmy marmosets no bigger than my thumb — swarmed about me with ladders and vines and wheeled fruit-carts, and their Emperor, from a leaf-throne, delivered a long and magnificent speech, of which I understood nothing but the grandeur.

They fed me pressed fruit by the barrel, and made a careful inventory of my pockets. Of my watch they wrote: "a wonderful engine, half the bigness of our Emperor's head, which he calls his oracle, for he consults it when the fruit is ripe." Of my bananas: "great yellow moons, which he worships, and eats." In time we made a treaty, sealed with articles: I might have meat and drink enough for 1,724 marmosets, and in return I was to be their mountain in war.

And here I learned the great affair of state that had set all Littleput at war with its neighbor Blefuscu: the Stem-enders and the Tip-enders. For their ancient scripture says, "All true believers shall peel their bananas at the convenient end" — but the Tip-enders hold that the prophet Nutkin peeled at the tip, and half the jungle burns with the quarrel. One emperor lost his crown; eleven thousand monkeys perished (or perished of the argument); and when I waded the channel between the empires and towed home the entire Blefuscu navy by the rigging, expecting to be a hero, the Littleput court was deeply offended that I would not help them enslave Blefuscu forever.

When fire broke out in the palace by night, I put it out with a gourd of coconut water, and the Empress was so offended by the method that I never stood so high in her favor again. Soon after, a committee of my enemies (led by the Treasurer, who believed me too expensive, and the Admiral, who envied me) drew up articles of treason, and proposed — after mature debate — to starve me gently, or at least to poison my breakfast and blind me with pod-arrows. A friend at court whispered the plan to me, and I swam to Blefuscu, where the Tip-enders treated me civilly, and so got home.

**A Voyage to Brobdingnang.** Cast away a second time, I found myself in a country of Gigantopithecus, apes as tall as kapok trees. The farmer who found me carried me home in his pocket and exhibited me for nuts — a shilling a groom — and his daughter, Glumdalclitch, became my nurse, taught me the language, and mended my fur. The Queen bought me, and the scholars examined me, and one court dwarf, a mere orangutan of eight feet, dropped a blanched almond into my soup for sport. The beggars were a horror; the flies were as big as baked loaves, and a hailstone once knocked me flat. Once, two rats the size of great danes climbed my table, and I defended myself, as I told the Queen, "with my hanger drawn" — she was much entertained, and I was much sick.

"Ah," said the King, when I had proudly related the history of my own troop — our wars, our bananas, our elections — "from what I have heard, I cannot but conclude the bulk of your natives to be the most mischievous race of little odorous monkeys that nature ever suffered to swing through her canopy." And when I, to retrieve the honor of my kind, offered him the secret of a certain powder of ours, that makes stones rain death upon whole troops, the King was struck with horror and forbade me to mention it again: an ape who could make death from dust, he said, would be the most odious of all nature's vermin — and I had no answer for him, and was ashamed.

My house was carried off at last by a great harpy eagle and dropped into the sea, and a passing ship took me up, small again among small apes.

**A Voyage to the Floating Canopy.** In the land of Laputa — the Floating Canopy, a city of orangutan mathematicians held aloft on woven cloud-vines — I met savants so buried in calculation they must be attended by flappers: monkeys carrying dried gourds of seeds, who gently rattle their masters' ears to warn them of conversation. Descending to the Academy below, I found their projects sublime: to extract sunshine from overripe bananas; to build a tree from the top downward, which the wisest held impossible; one scheme, much funded, for rendering green bananas, once peeled, green again; and a college of language which proposed to abolish words entirely, since words are only names for things, and things themselves could be carried about in sacks. The professors carry their conversation in sacks now, and meet in silence, and are said to be the happiest apes alive.

In Luggnagg I met the Struldbruggs — apes born with a spot on the forehead, marked to live forever. I rhapsodized at first: what a monkey might read and learn and become in ten thousand years! But the Struldbruggs themselves set me right — they grow old, ever older, ever weaker, ever more despised, holding on and on, until at ninety they are so many withered figs, jealous of the dead and envying the young, and the law removes their estates at eighty so they do not outlast their own grandchildren's patience. Forever, they taught me, is not a gift; it is a sentence.

**A Voyage to the Country of the Gibbons.** Last of all I was set ashore in a land ruled by Gibbons, the rational singers, so wise and gentle they had no word for a lie — when I said an untruth, my master gently told the others I had said "the thing which is not." My master was a grey gibbon of noble bearing, and my greatest torment was the Yahoos — wild baboons of matted fur and boundless appetite — with whom, to my horror, I shared a shape. When I was caught bathing, and my fur was revealed, the Assembly debated gravely whether I was a Yahoo taught to whistle, or a small gibbon gone to rot.

At last they exiled me for the peace of their songs, and though I wept at parting, a crew of apes carried me home against my will. To this day I cannot abide the smell of my own troop; I flinch when my wife grooms me, and I retire to the end of the garden, where I converse for an hour every evening with two singing gibbons in the hedge.

Thus, gentle reader, I close these travels, having seen the little, the great, the floating, and the reasonable, and having learned at last only this: that a gorilla among marmosets is enormous, and among Gigantopithecus a pet, and among gibbons an embarrassment.
