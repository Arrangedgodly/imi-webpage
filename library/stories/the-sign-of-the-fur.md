# The Sign of the Fur

*A parody of "The Sign of the Four"*

I took my watch from my pocket and held it out. "Could you tell me, Holmes, what this object suggests to you?"

Sherlock Chimpness weighed it in his paw, opened the back, and studied the works the way he studied mud — personally. "Your father's bull's-eye, inherited from your elder brother, who was untidy, careless in grooming, and once pawned it, then pawned it again, and died wealthy in nothing but debts."

"Why," said I, sitting down heavily, "the pawnbroker's marks alone —"

"Suggested themselves. Watch-cases carry their owners' habits, Watson, and your brother's were not habits so much as resignations." He laid the watch aside. "Never begin a day with a deduction that ends in a funeral. Which is it to-day, by the way — ripe or green?"

He took down his tube of banana brandy and added a generous measure to his morning milk. "The seventy-per-cent solution, Watson. My mind rebels at stagnation. A quiet jungle is an insult to a climbing mind."

Mrs. Howlson showed in our client before he could finish it: a young langur with a veil, trembling like a leaf in monkey-wind. Miss Mary Morstan told the strangest tale in the troop. Ten years past, her father had vanished from his London hotel. Six years ago, on the same date annually, there had arrived — always anonymously — one candied pearl-white 'nana of great value. And that very morning had come a note:

*"Be at the third branch of the Old Lyceum fig at seven o'clock. Two friends may accompany you. You are a wronged she-monkey and shall have justice. Bring no police."*

We went, naturally. Our benefactor proved to be Thaddeus Sholto, a golden lion tamarin whose mane exploded from his head like a dandelion going through a crisis, ferried us to his nest in a growler that smelled of cigar-smoke and conscience, and there, through a fog of kretek smoke, told his tale. His late father, Major Sholto, had served at the fortress of Agra-pe with Miss Morstan's father and one Jonathan Small. The four had found the First Bananas — gold and jewel-bright — and sworn an oath over them with the sign of the four: four tufts of fur, one plucked from each monkey's shoulder, pressed into wax. Then the Major had done the others out of their shares and brought the chest home alone. On his deathbed, at last, he confessed it all — and had begun to say where the chest lay hidden when, staring past Thaddeus at the window, he gave one cry, and died with his paw out and the secret unsaid. There was a face at the glass, Thaddeus swore; his brother Bartholomew swore he had imagined it. The brothers had quarrelled ever since, and tonight, Thaddeus feared, Bartholomew — who had found the chest only yesterday — meant to keep every last 'nana for himself. A share was owed to Miss Morstan; let her come and claim it.

At Pondicherry Lookout we found Bartholomew's nest-room locked and silent. Holmes slid through the skylight (there is no door a chimp truly needs) and opened it from within. Bartholomew lay dead in his chair, a poison thorn in his neck, his face fixed in a grin of genuine surprise, and the treasure chest gone from the hidden room behind him. Around the chamber: a hole bitten through the ceiling, a rope of woven hair, tiny marmoset footprints in the spilled 'nana oil — and, on a leaf pinned to the wall, the sign of the four.

Inspector Lemurstrade arrived and promptly arrested Thaddeus, the tamarin being the only monkey present with both a motive and a mane. Holmes, meanwhile, was on his hands and knees at the skylight, sniffing the trail like a connoisseur.

"A pygmy marmoset, Watson, travelling with a wooden-tailed friend — I have the scent, and the scent is banana oil. But scent is only half a chase. Come. There is a potto in the Rag Fair who has never yet failed me."

Toby — squat, mournful, a nose the troop consulted like scripture — ran the oil-trail grandly through half of London, was saluted by every dog and bootlegger on the route, and brought us with total conviction to a riverside yard stacked with barrels of banana oil, at which point he climbed the nearest barrel, sat down, and contemplated a crisis of identity.

"He has run the scent of a thousand barrels," said Holmes, recovering him with dignity, "any one of which the marmoset touched. Toby is not wrong, Watson. Toby is over-committed. The trail we want left by water." He was gone the rest of the day and returned at dusk disguised as an ancient stevedore-tamarin with a chestnut back and an honest squint, which he peeled off in the corner of the cab.

"The *Aurora*, Watson. Fastest leaf-skimmer on the river. She burns banana charcoal — smokeless, the cunning fruit — and she is being made ready this very hour at Jacobson's Yard. The game is a-swing!"

What a chase that was: eight paddlers, a steam engine pressed into service, and Holmes standing at the bow with his ears flat, reading the current. We overtook the *Aurora* in the rapids; a poison dart sang past my ear; the little marmoset — Tonga, though I learned the name too late to matter — went into the water like a furred thunderbolt and was gone under for good. In the shallows we cornered Jonathan Small himself, thrashing his wooden tail — a crocodile had taken the original — and bellowing that the treasure was his by the sign of the four, and that he had kept his oath, which was more than the Sholtos had ever done.

"The 'nanas, Small," said Holmes.

"Gone," said he, with something near a smile. "I strung them along the river as we ran. Every golden one, from here to the delta. The Great Vine may keep my share. It was never the bananas I wanted — it was that no other monkey should have them."

At the station, while his wooden tail was being inventoried, Small told us the rest with the pride of a monkey reciting his own legend. The oath of the four sworn at Agra-pe; the mutiny; the chest buried under the flagstones; the Major's treachery; Small's twenty years cracking stone at the Bluestone Bluff, drawing a treasure map on his cell wall so often the warders learned it by heart; Tonga, his islands drowned, his sorrows carried in a two-foot body. "He fetched me off the Bluff, did Tonga, and I promised him his share, and he earned it. The thorn in Sholto's neck was his doing, not mine — I would have made it quick. But I never raised a paw to any monkey in my life, and no monkey hanged for it either. It was the oath that killed him. Oaths keep better than bananas, and that is their whole cruelty."

"A singular confession," said Holmes on the way home. "He is the bough, Watson, and the troop merely the fruit."

Miss Morstan, meanwhile, had been relieved at one stroke of a fortune, a false benefactor, and the necessity of candied 'nanas. She took it as she took everything, which is to say gracefully, and when I told her that the treasure was lost, and that I was consequently the richest monkey in England, she laughed at me until she cried, and then accepted me, which is the only cross-examination I ever failed.

Holmes reached for the tube of banana brandy.

"I really think," said I, "that the seventy-per-cent solution will not be wanted to-night."

"For me," said Sherlock Chimpness, "there still remains the solution." And he squeezed the tube with the air of a monkey who has finished a case, a banana, and an argument, all in one sitting.
