# The Hunt for Red Ooktober

*A parody of "The Hunt for Red October"*

In the sonar shack of the USS Dallas, Seaman Jonesy — a vervet with headphones the size of dinner plates — heard something no howler monkey had any business making a hundred fathoms down.

"Wally," he said slowly, "classify this for me."

The computer chewed on it. BIOLOGIC, said Wally. PROBABILITY: HOWLER MONKEY, TAKING A BATH.

"No howler ever swam," Jonesy said. "And no bath ever had a reactor signature." He cupped his paws around the earphone. "It's singing, sir. Through the hull. Ook-ook-oook — and there's a hum under it, like a caterpillar the size of a bus."

A thousand miles away, in a briefing room in Washington, Jack Rhesus of the Central Intelligence Apes stood in front of admirals with his monograph, his laptop, and a powerful urge to be back teaching midshipmen.

"Gentlemen," he said, "the Soviets have launched their newest Typhoon — a leviathan painted harvest yellow like a six-hundred-foot banana. Her name is the Red Ooktober, and her captain is Marko Ramius, a sea-gray silverback, half Lithuanian forest, half Baltic storm. And she is not out for a training run, because Ramius sent this letter to Admiral Padorin before he sailed." Jack turned over the transparency. It read, in careful silverback cursive: I AM GOING TO THE FREE JUNGLE.

Admiral Padorin, on the far side of the world, read the same letter twice, canceled lunch, and took to his bunk with a cold compress and a career.

In Washington the admirals argued for two days. First strike, said half the table. Trap, said the other half. Jack Rhesus, who had written the monograph on Ramius and once been graded harshly for optimism, laid his whole professional life on the conference table: the silverback had no prophecies left in the Motherland. His wife was dead because a Party doctor couldn't be bothered. He was defecting with the boat.

"Suppose you're wrong," growled an admiral, "and we start World War Three over a seafood banquet?"

"Then I'll apologize very quickly," said Jack. The President signed two orders. Find her. And if the Soviet Fleet touched her, lie.

Find her fell to the Dallas, where Jonesy taught himself to sing "Ook" in four-part harmony and trailed the great yellow banana across the Atlantic by its bath music alone. Captain Mancuso, a capuchin who did crosswords in the torpedo room because the lighting was better, listened once and said, "Jonesy, you're either a genius or a sunken man," which on submarines is a promotion.

Aboard the Ooktober, Ramius fed his political officer a frozen sturgeon — gently, but with feeling — and announced a reactor accident to the crew, who boarded a rescue vessel in an orderly panic. Only the officers stayed, along with one stowaway problem: a GRU agent disguised as the assistant pastry chef, who believed in the Motherland the way termites believe in porches.

"Give me a ping, Vasili," Ramius said at the conn. "One ping only."

"One ping only, Captain." Vasili Borodin, the young navigator, obliged, then sighed at the sonar return the way young capuchins sigh at horizons. "Captain... when we reach America. I will live in Montana. I will marry a round American she-macaque, and raise rabbits, and she will can peaches for the winter."

"They don't grow bananas in Montana, Vasili."

"Then I will grow a fig tree," Borodin said firmly. "In a bucket. Indoors."

The rendezvous came off the Sargasso Jungle, that forest of seaweed where the Dallas and the whale-boat-sized banana eventually found each other in the dark. Jack Rhesus came aboard the Ooktober in a suit and immediate regret.

"You're the historian," Ramius rumbled, looking him over like a menu. "You wrote the book about me."

"I wrote a monograph," Jack said. "You're taller in it."

"I am at the end of my leaves, historian. My whole life I have been a tool of the troop bosses. No more. I sail for the New World."

Then, because plans must be told to officers, Jack laid out the crazy part in the wardroom. The Dallas would report the Ooktober sunk; the entire Soviet Fleet would sail home believing it; and every officer aboard would stay legally dead until Washington decided otherwise. "You are giving us," said Borodin slowly, "a funeral with better weather." "New names, new trees, and a pension older than your bones," said Jack. "Also, your letters home will be published posthumously, so write something quotable."

Ramius even let Jack take the helm for a mile, which is how Jack learned that a Typhoon handles like a cathedral with opinions. "Do not fight the rudder," Ramius murmured, arms folded. "You drive like a cow, historian. A clever cow. But a cow."

The pastry chef chose that moment to open fire in the bilge. He was a true believer with a machine pistol and terrible aim; he shot the plumbing, the lighting, and, glancingly, the haunch of poor Vasili Borodin, who went down dreaming of peaches. Jack Rhesus, historian, hid behind a pipe like a professional, aimed like a tourist, missed twice with style, and finally threw the one weapon every monkey is born holding. The banana struck the bulkhead; the chef stepped on the peel; the chef's ideology met the deck at terminal velocity.

"Some things in here don't react well to bullets," Ramius observed, prodding the traitor with his boot. "Bananas, apparently, are excellent."

The hunter sub Konovalov found them anyway and fired. Jonesy heard it coming like a bus with a grudge. "Torpedo in the water!" he called, and a moment later, with deep institutional embarrassment: "Second torpedo in the water — ours. The first would like a word."

Ramius rang up the caterpillar drive and slid the great yellow banana sideways through the sea like a chameleon changing its mind. The Dallas darted in, spat decoys — the crew called them peels — and the torpedo, confused, embarrassed, and now quite alone, sought out its own mother ship instead. There were no survivors of the embarrassment.

"Way to go, Dallas," Jonesy whooped from the sonar shack, which is where legends are filed. Mancuso awarded him the conn, a steak, and a permanent exemption from crosswords.

That same evening the Navy announced, with perfectly straight faces, that the Soviet submarine Ooktober had tragically gone down with all paws. The Soviet Fleet, having heard it sink twice from two navies, steamed home to file paperwork. Admiral Padorin filed the most, having also to cancel, retroactively, the lunch. The Ooktober ended her last cruise quietly, in a river inlet on the American coast, where the officers were granted a new home in the Chesapeake Free Banana Preserve.

Ramius stood at the rail with Jack Rhesus as the sun came up over the New World.

"Welcome to the jungle, Captain."

Ramius handed him a coconut of milk and looked at the shoreline a long time. "Write your memo, historian. Be careful what you put in it. Montana has figs. Who knew."

Upriver, Vasili Borodin healed, married a round American she-macaque of excellent judgment, and cans peaches. The fig tree thrives. It lives in a bucket. Indoors.
