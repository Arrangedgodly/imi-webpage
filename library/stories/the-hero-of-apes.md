# The Hero of Apes

*A parody of "The Hero of Ages"*

*From the journals of the Hero of Apes: the end came not with claws but with quiet, and the quiet was the worst part.*

Sazed the lemur Keeper read those words on a rotting page and did not know they were true, and false, and chewed. For the Rot worked its small cruelties first: it nibbled the world's words, so no written thing stayed trustworthy, and an ape who trusts writing over eyes is already half defeated. Sazed, who had lost Tindwilla and half his faith in the same afternoon, kept reading anyway, because keeping was what he was.

The world was coming apart. Ash fell so thick the green drowned. The earth shrugged and cities slid. The mists crept out even by daylight, killing with a touch. Above it all sat the Rot, wearing the body of a mild old ape named Ati, who had once, the stories said, been kind.

Vin had become something the jungle had no word for — a chimp carrying the power of the Ripening, hunting the Rot's Chimquisitors across the ash. Elend, her prime mate, flew at her side; they had married quietly between two apocalypses, Sazed officiating with three hundred religions to choose from and tactfully picking the shortest. And TenSoon the Furandra ran shapes at their flank, wearing the bodies of wolves and generals and once, memorably, a very confused banker.

All roads led back to the Pits of Hathi-Sin, where the ati-yum 'nanas budded from the earth — the Rot's own flesh, in fruit form. The Lord Groomer's diary said the Hero of Ages would find salvation there, and the diary was rotting too, so every word of it argued with itself. Sazed carried the Lord Groomer's logbook as well, a first-paw account of the jungle's first crew, and both books changed under his paws, each blaming the other like siblings in the back of a cart.

In Urtre-bough, the last free city of the north, Spook was learning that the Rot gives splendid gifts first. A spike in his shoulder made him faster than sight; a whisper made him burn the crooked and call it mercy; the crowds called him Lord Spook of the Flames, and the whisper told him fire was a kind of honesty. He nearly burned the granaries with the city's hearts still in them. At the last, the whisper came wearing a dead teacher's face: it grinned at him with Kelsier's grin, on the wrong side, offering to make the fires just. "You're not him," Spook told it. "He never offered. He dared." And he pulled the spike out of his own shoulder, alone, in the dark, with nobody watching — which is the only way the brave parts count.

"There's always another 'nana," he told Urtre-bough hoarsely, "and it is never the one you're burning."

Beneath the world, the Furandras put TenSoon on trial for treachery to his Contract — he had worn a dead ape's body to serve a living promise instead. The First Generation sat in judgment, old and gray and careful.

"A promise kept only when it is cheap," TenSoon told them, "is a fruit bowl, not a promise. I chose a friend. Chew on me and see how I taste."

They sentenced him anyway. Generations are slow; friendships are not. He escaped to the sun, and took with him the answer to the oldest riddle of the age: where the Lord Groomer had hidden the real atium. In Fadrex Bough, meanwhile, the last city to hold out, High Obligator Yolk-man ruled by ledger and trapped a wandering Vin with cold logic and warm soup — the only snare she ever fell into twice. The caches the crew dug up in five dead cities — food, blankets, and one clue apiece — held every fruit but that one. "He moved it," said Elend. "The one fruit he never ate," said Vin. "Of course. He hid it under the one roof he trusted." Under the earth, with the Furandras, who cannot be bribed because they *are* the vault.

The Battle of Hathi-Sin broke the world's last morning. Kolo-gorillas in their too-small skins, Scruff with fruit-picks, TenSoon wearing half an army — and through it all the Rot's laughter, offering every ape the chance to be spared, if only they would stop fighting, and rot quietly, like fruit. The Rot had one rule: everything splits, everything spoils. The Ripening had one rule: everything seeds. They were the jungle's oldest married couple, and the whole world was the argument.

Elend held the gate while the last wagons of ati-yum were carried off to be burned, and the spear that found him was small and ordinary, which is the insult of such things. He had burned all night, Peelborn light, and his troops had followed him by it, which is a hard thing to be in a war: a lamp cannot blink. Vin reached him in the smoke.

"There's always," he said, with the last of his light, and made the old joke, and did not finish it, because a good joke wants leaving.

Vin stood up out of the smoke and *called the mists* — every white river of them, the whole breathing sky — into one small furious chimp. She went up like dawn with a grudge. In the enemy camp she found the Rot, mild Ati, waiting with open arms, and Marsh — her own brother, spike-eyed, centuries a slave — fighting his own hand for one heartbeat, long enough to tear the bronze spike from her ear and free her fully.

Then Vin of the mists did the one thing the Rot had never imagined: she hugged him, with all her power, and did not let go. The Ripening and the Rot, keeper and wrecker, the two hands of the jungle's oldest argument — they canceled like shadow and light, and where they met there was nothing left at all. Not even ash. Only quiet.

And into that quiet, a small lemur walked.

Sazed had spent his life storing faiths in the rings on his tail — dead religions, dead hopes, kept against the day somebody might need one. He had lost his own beloved and kept on sorting anyway, because storing was what he was. Now he knelt where the two powers hung empty in the air, and took up both: the Ripening in one paw, the Rot in the other. He held them the way a groomer holds a matted, frightened, furious ape: gently, and without any nonsense about deserving.

Then the Hero of Apes went to work.

He combed the ash out of the sky with strokes a thousand miles wide. He rolled the red horizon back to honest blue. He un-poisoned rivers, tucked the shaken little planet in with both enormous hands — and because he had a Keeper's memory, he remembered every broken thing, and fixed it, and where he could not fix it, he sat with it, which is also a kind of fixing. He remembered Tindwilla last of all, and did not fix her, and let her be, and that was the part that made him a hero.

*My name is Sazed,* the last page of the true journal read, unwritten by any Rot. *I am — entirely unfortunately — the Hero of Apes.*

Where Vin and Elend fell, two saplings rose from the ash, trunks twined, holding each other up, and travelers said that on windy nights you could hear the branches argue happily about constitutions.

And in the new sunlight, a red-furred shadow that had refused to move on leaned against a tree and grinned.

"There's always another 'nana," said Kelsier.
