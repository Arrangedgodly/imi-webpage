# Hairy Potter and the Chamber of Screeches

*A parody of "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets"*

The summer Hairy Potter turned twelve went from bad to worse, and Hairy had lived in a cupboard, so that was saying something. He was stuck at the Dirleys', baboons who despised joy the way other primates despise thunder, when a creature appeared on his windowsill and bowed so low its ears flapped inside out.

It was Dobby, a tree-elf with huge ears and eyes like wet dinner plates.

"Hairy Potter!" squeaked Dobby. "Hairy Potter must not go back to Hootwarts!"

"Why not?" asked Hairy — a howler monkey with a lightning-shaped bald streak in his brow, a souvenir from the dark lord Voldemarmoset, who tried to curse him as an infant and vanished instead, making Hairy famous and extremely tired.

"Because there is a plot to make most terrible things happen at Hootwarts School of Junglecraft and Primatecy. Dobby cannot say who." He hesitated, then seized his own ears and banged his head on the sill. "Dobby has to punish himself now."

"Stop — don't — sit down!"

To keep him away, Dobby sabotaged everything: letters vanished, the Dirleys locked him in, and when that failed, Dobby sealed the secret vine to school so that Hairy and Ron Wheezy — a red-faced uakari from a family so large it had its own weather — had to fly the enchanted banana-cart instead. They crashed into the Whomping Vine, which whomped them.

The new Defense Against the Wild Things professor was Gilderoy Lockheart, a golden lion tamarin with a mane like five advertisements, author of *Monkeying with Monsters*.

"And then," he told a rapt class, "I looked the Nundu in the eye and grooming-winked. I'll pass my portrait around later."

But real trouble arrived written in dew on the keeper's-hut wall:

**THE CHAMBER OF SCREECHES HAS BEEN OPENED. ENEMIES OF THE HEIR, BEWARE.**

Below it hung Mr. Whiskers the zoo cat — not dead, the headmistress insisted, but *petrified*, frozen mid-hiss. Then a first-year colobus, frozen mid-shutter.

"The heir of Slychimpin," whispered the jungle. "Only the heir can open the Chamber."

Worse: Hairy kept hearing a voice in the pipes that nobody else heard — a dry screech crawling behind the walls, muttering about *ripping* and *tearing* — which is the sort of thing that makes a monkey unpopular at breakfast.

At the Dueling Club, Lockheart's conjured cobra got loose, and Hairy calmly told it — in Snake-Speak — to leave his friends alone. The whole gym went silent.

"He's a *speaker*," breathed Hermango Grubber, the brainy capuchin of their trio. "Snake-Speak! That's what the heir would do — though it's *statistically* unlikely you're evil."

"So who is it?" said Ron. Hairy nominated Draco Malfoley, a silky sifaka with an inherited sneer, on the grounds that he was horrible. Hermango insisted on evidence, which in her case meant brewing Polyjuice Potion — illegal, tricky, and simmered for weeks in Moaning Myrtle's bathroom.

Myrtle was the ghost monkey of the watering trough, who had flooded it fifty years ago and taken it personally.

On the appointed night the potion turned Hairy and Ron into Malfoley's two bodyguards, Grabbe and Coyle. Hermango's dose had gone wrong — she'd used a hair pulled off a classmate's robe and gotten the classmate's cat.

"Don't," she said, one ginger ear twitching, from deep inside a hood. "Just — don't."

Interrogating Malfoley in the Slychimpin common room took ten minutes and proved only that Malfoley knew nothing, wasn't the heir, and wished he were, which was somehow the most pathetic fact of the year.

Then two blows. First, a rogue bludger stalked Hairy through an entire Quidditch match until it broke his arm; Lockheart's attempt to mend it removed the arm's bones entirely. ("Deboned!" he said, radiant.) Second — much worse — Hermango was found petrified in the corridor, frozen with a page torn from a library book clutched in her fist. The last legible word was *pipes*.

The answer came crying in that same bathroom. In Myrtle's flood floated a small log-book, inexplicably dry. The log wrote back. *Hello, Hairy Potter. I am Tom Riddle. Would you like to see how I caught the beast?*

The pages pulled Hairy down fifty years, into a memory like green water: young Riddle, sleek and watchful, cornering a huge, shaggy, underage gamekeeper called Hagrub — who was hiding a crate. In the crate was a spider the size of a dinner plate named Aragog.

Riddle smiled the smile of a monkey about to ruin someone. "That," he said, "looks like the monster of Slychimpin."

"Never write in strange logs," Hermango said later, thawed and furious at herself. "I can't believe I have to say that."

The diary vanished. The screech got closer. And Hagrub was hauled away to prison with a parting bellow that only three students understood was meant for them: "If anyone wanted ter find out somethin', all they'd have ter do is follow the spiders!"

They followed the spiders. Into the Forbidden Wood, at dusk, on the specific errand of walking toward a sound like clicking hooves, until the trees opened on a hollow where Aragog now lived — a spider the size of a rhinoceros, with a rhinoceros's opinion of visitors.

"Hagrub never opened the Chamber," Aragog creaked. "The monster down there is older than he is. Older than me. It is an ancient thing, and we do not speak its name."

"Why NOT?" demanded Ron, already backing away.

"Because we fear it," said Aragog, mildly, "and also because dinner is served." His children came out of the dark on all sides — and would have eaten them too, if the wild banana-cart (still feral, still ticked off about the Whomping Vine) hadn't come crashing through the trees to snatch them up.

Back at the castle, the screech took what it wanted: Ron's little sister Ginny — a first-year who had been pouring her heart into the diary all year, and getting paler as something else poured back — was carried down through a snake-carved tap behind Myrtle's sink. The wall read: *HER SKELETON WILL LIE IN THE CHAMBER FOREVER.*

Lockheart, ordered to go down after her, packed to flee instead. "My books, my career—" Ron's broken wand backfired his memory-wipe straight into his own face; he rose, beaming, blank as a fresh notebook, and had to be dragged along as luggage.

The hidden pipe dropped them into the Chamber of Screeches, where the screech echoes before its maker arrives. There lay Ginny, white as paper; there stood Tom Riddle, wearing a schoolboy smile; and there came the monster of Slychimpin: the Basilisk — an anaconda so vast its scales scraped like river stones, whose screech froze all who met its eyes.

"She petrified the cat through a puddle. Myrtle heard her through the pipes. Directly, though — directly, she *kills*."

"Voldemarmoset," said Hairy, watching the name re-arrange itself along Riddle's flank. "You're the heir."

"I am the past, the present, and the future," said Riddle. "The monkey *protege*."

Then the screech came for Hairy. But the Chamber had heard something else first: Fawkes the phoenix macaw, blazing down with the Sorting Cap — and something heavy inside it. The Basilisk lunged; Fawkes put out its eyes; and Hairy drew the silver sword of Gibben-dor, swung it the way you swing when the snitch is trying to eat you, and drove it up through the great snake's mouth as its fang sank into his arm.

The Basilisk fell. The fang burned. Riddle gloated — until Fawkes wept on the wound, and Hairy, half-delirious, pulled the fang out and drove it through the log-book. A scream of ink, a gush of memory — and Tom Riddle was scrubbed out like a bad entry.

Afterward — the sister saved, the frozen thawed with mandrake stew, Lockheart exposed as a fraud whose signature move was grooming other heroes' memories away — Hairy returned the diary to its owner's sock-bag, sock slipped inside.

"Master has given Dobby a sock," breathed the little tree-elf, radiant. "DOBBY IS FREE!"

The headmistress gave Hairy her end-of-term verdict, the only exam he treasured: "It is our choices, Hairy, far more than our fur, that show what we truly are."

Then came the feast, and all Hootwarts raised mango juice beneath a banner in letters visible only to those who had earned them:

**Never tickle a sleeping anaconda.**
