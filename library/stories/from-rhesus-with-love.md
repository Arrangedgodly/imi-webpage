# From Rhesus, with Love

*A parody of "From Russia, with Love"*

On a private island in the Aegean, the executive board of S.P.E.C.T.R.E. — the Simian Professional Executive for Counter-espionage, Tropical Mischief, Ripe Bananas and Extortion — gathered for the annual general meeting. Minutes were approved, treasuries admired, and a junior vice-president who had been skimming from the fig budget was fed, with regret and ceremony, to the seabirds.

"Item four," said the Chairman, a lemur in shadow whose tail never showed itself. "The English problem. We shall destroy the monkey Banna and humiliate his Service in a single operation. We will need one beautiful rhesus, one decoding machine, and one train. Kronzo?"

"A train," the treasurer repeated nervously. Trains, in S.P.E.C.T.R.E.'s ledgers, carried a depreciation schedule all their own.

Kronzo rose — a chess macaque who thought in openings and lived in endgames, and who had once won an international tournament by announcing mate in a fruit salad. He laid out the plan with pawns: the Jungle Service's own vanity would carry the poison. When it failed, as it must, the Chairman retired Kronzo with a poisoned pawn, which is S.P.E.C.T.R.E. for a pension.

In a Moscow archive office, Corporal Tatiana Rhesanova, a pretty young rhesus with impeccable fur and no taste for adventure — she measured excitement the way the army measures boots, by size and reluctance — read her orders twice and went pale: she was to defect, carrying the Peek-a-tor — a priceless cipher decoder — and deliver herself to the Jungle Service's most famous agent.

"Deliver myself?" she whispered. "To a monkey?"

"He is not precisely a monkey," said her colonel, who was himself an imposter and a scoundrel in a very good hat. "He is a chimp. Rest assured the arrangement is purely patriotic."

Her final inspection was conducted by Colonel Rosa Klebba of SMERSH Grooming, a baboon built like a filing cabinet who examined Tatiana's posture the way other monkeys examine fruit, pronounced it "regrettable but usable," and issued her a travel iron and a look that could spoil milk.

In Istanbul, Banna met the local station chief, Kerim Bey — a vast genial baboon with three sons, a periscope, and a personal rule never to hold a grudge past lunch. Together they rode the ferry, dug a tunnel under the enemy consulate (Kerim's sons listened night after night through six feet of rock and reported that the cypher clerk typed secrets with one finger and love letters with two), and spent a rowdy evening at a camp of dancing macaques, where a territorial dispute between two rival troops was settled the traditional way: everyone sang, everyone threw figs, and Kerim Bey made friends with both sides and the fruit vendor. The camp was raided at midnight by bully-macaques from the consulate; Kerim took one scratch, made two friends and a plan, and by breakfast the raiders' sergeant was feeding him grapes.

Then came the Orient Ex-vine-press — the great vine-hauled sleeper running out of the jungle toward Paris — with Tatiana aboard, the Peek-a-tor in her suitcase, and, unknown to everyone, an assassin in the next compartment.

Banna found her in the dining car. She had enormous eyes, a trembling loyalty, and a story that did not quite fit her fur.

"I am supposed to be frightened of you," she told him. "They said you were an animal."

"The name's Banna. James Banna. And I'm afraid, Corporal, that you're the bait on somebody's hook."

"The hook was the machine," she said softly. "Was I the worm?"

"You," said Banna, signaling the steward, "are the finest part of the whole rotten tackle."

At Belgrade a helpful Englishman boarded — sandy fur, easy grin, lanyard reading CAPTAIN NAIÏSH, SECURITY — and attached himself to their compartment like a good chap. Banna noticed two things wrong with dinner: the chicken was off, and the stranger had poured red wine with the fish.

"Rude of me not to introduce myself earlier," the Englishman said at midnight, when the corridor was empty. He was a big red-coated gibbon with a gym-tail and a smile full of too many teeth. "The name's Gribbon. Red Gribbon." His wristwatch unwound itself into a garrote vine with a flick, the way expensive timepieces do in his profession. "Time, Mr. Banna, is the one thing you are out of."

"Red wine with fish," said Banna. "That should have told me something."

"It usually does."

What followed redecorated the sleeper car. Gribbon fought like a gym-tail with a grudge; Banna fought like a chimp who had dined correctly. When the train jolted at the junction, Banna fed the gibbon his own snack-bag strap, hoisted him into the luggage rack, and — at the conductor's request — arranged him face-down in the vegetable crisper until the frontier. At the border a customs vervet opened the crisper, looked at Gribbon, closed the crisper, and asked only that the passenger be declared. "He's a vegetable," said Banna. The vervet stamped the form. Nobody in the history of that railway had ever argued less.

Gone, too, by morning, was Kerim Bey — found in his berth between stations, still smiling, gone between lunch and a grudge. Tatiana wept into Banna's dinner jacket. Banna looked out the dark window for a long time and did not look like a chimp anyone should pour wine near. At Trieste they gave him to his sons with the periscope's brass cap on his chest, and his eldest kept the grudge rule faithfully: by the funeral, the assassins had already been apologized to, paid off, and outlived.

At the frontier, the treachery unraveled. The colonel had fled; the plot had been designed to shame the Service and hang Banna's name on it; and S.P.E.C.T.R.E.'s chairman had himself a new problem, namely that his rhesus had gone gloriously off-script and fallen in love with the target.

They left the train at the frontier in a borrowed car full of peaches and crossed three borders on Tatiana's smile, Banna's paperwork, and one incident involving a truck of olive oil that the insurance company still calls a statement. In Paris they honeymooned anyway, because the Jungle Service is sentimental and the per diem was generous. On the last morning, a small cleaning-tarsier with spectacles and carpet slippers shuffled into their suite, embraced Tatiana like an aunt, and then lashed out with one steel-tipped hind claw, poisoned with the venom of the perfumed centipede.

"Klebba," Banna gasped, recognizing the posture from a thousand dreadful briefings.

Banna caught the tail on a chair leg. The chair splintered. The colonel was subdued with a coat rack and considerable yelling, and marched off filing a complaint about hotel security. She was deported in a crate stenciled DIPLOMATIC, FRAGILE, MOSTLY. But the claw had found Banna's shoulder on the way down, and the world went fuzzy and sweet, like a smoothie with opinions.

He woke in the zoo infirmary to find Tatiana asleep against his good shoulder and a note pinned to his drip, written in careful corporal's cursive:

FROM RHESUS, WITH LOVE — NEXT TIME, ORDER THE FISH. T.

Banna laughed, which hurt, and filed the note in the only safe deposit the Service could never audit: the pocket over a chimp's heart.
