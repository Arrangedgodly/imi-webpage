# Redhowl

*A parody of "Redwall"*

The monkeys of Redhowl Abbey had one law older than the walls themselves: any creature in need of food, shelter, or a decent grooming was welcome at the table. This was easy to believe on a golden evening when the great Mango Bell rang for the feast and the table groaned under October nana-nog, deeper'n'ever durian pie, and candied figs enough to make a grown mandrill weep.

Young Matthias — a novice howler, all elbows and appetite — sat beside Constance the Gorilla, who could carry a fully laden dessert trolley in one hand and usually did.

"When I'm a warrior," Matthias announced, "I shall eat like this *and* fight like Mandrill the Warrior. Both. At once."

"You can't even carry the honey pot," said Constance, "and it has handles."

Across the table, Cornflower — a quiet young langur who helped with the harvest — laughed into her cup, and Matthias knocked over the honey pot, and Methuselah, the ancient gatekeeper and oldest monkey in the Abbey, chuckled like a dry leaf.

"He'll be a warrior yet," Methuselah said. "The tapestry says so, if you know how to read old stitching."

That was the night Cluny the Scourge came down the jungle path. He was a baboon the size of a landslide, with a pirate's cutlass hacked from an old propeller blade and a tail fitted with a poisoned barb, and behind him shambled his horde — weasels, stoats, and rats gone bad, all of them armed with farm tools and bad intentions. He had lost his eye, his ship, and his temper, and he meant to take Redhowl in trade.

"I'll give you one chance," Cluny bellowed at the gates. "Hand over the Abbey, and I'll only be *moderately* horrible."

"Go climb a tree," called Constance, "and fall out of it."

The Siege of Redhowl began at dawn, with a siege tower made of lashed bamboo that Constance demolished by throwing an entire plum orchard at it, one tree at a time. Cluny answered with fire, ladders, and tunnels, and with the theft of the Abbey's morale in the form of the fellowship's entire banana reserve. That last one hurt worst.

Cluny ruled his horde the way he besieged: by promotion and terror. Cheesethief the overseer rose by volunteering others for everything and fell, eventually, into a hole Cluny had ordered dug for somebody else. Killconey the ferret, the horde's only optimist, proposed a fresh horror every morning the way other beasts propose weather. When the fox healer Sela was caught selling Cluny's plans to both sides at once — the family business — Cluny had her thrown from the camp, and her lanky son Chickenhound fled to Redhowl with stolen knowledge and a sackful of Abbey silver. Caught in the storehouse by old Methuselah, the fox panicked, struck the gatekeeper down with the sack, and went over the wall into legend and exile. The Abbey buried its oldest monkey beneath the orchard, and Matthias stood at the small grave and stopped talking about being a warrior. It had stopped being a game.

"Vengeance is a stone that sinks," Constance told him gently. "Build with it. Don't throw it."

Cluny, meanwhile, discovered that Abbeys are not taken by force but by patience, and he had none, so he tried everything. He tunneled under the wall — Grumm's cousins flooded the tunnel with the pond. He built a battering ram from an old fig tree — the red squirrels dropped a hornet's nest into the crew. He challenged the Abbey to send a champion for single combat on the flat stones, and when Constance walked out cracking her knuckles, Cluny revealed that "single combat" was a figure of speech for "forty stoats in the reeds." The squirrels had assumed this, and had hornets to spare. By midwinter the horde was superstitious, half-starved, and flirting with religion, and the Abbey — eating well, since Cornflower had organized the stores like a general — sang louder just to be heard doing it.

But Matthias had found something in the Abbey's loft: a great woven tapestry, moth-eaten and magnificent, showing Mandrill the Warrior with his legendary sword raised. Beneath his feet, stitched in letters older than any living monkey, ran four words: **I AM THAT APE.**

"What does it mean?" Matthias whispered.

"It means," said the dying Methuselah, weeks before the end, "that Mandrill's sword is real, it's out there, and it isn't going to swing itself."

The trail led out of the Abbey and up through the Skyparrot clans, who spoke of the sword in the third branch and had the third branch in the second tree, because parrots. Matthias fell among them a prisoner of the mad King Bull Parra, won the friendship of the princess Warbeak in a raid-gone-wrong, and left with a warrior's ransom in allies. It led through Basil Mongoose, a footloose scrapper fond of dandelion cordial, who became Matthias's road-companion and personal nuisance. And it led, finally, to the quarry of Asmodius the Poisontooth — a python longer than the Abbey was tall, with fangs like scythes, who had swallowed the sword along with its last owner and had opinions about visitors.

"You have come for the shhword," Asmodius hissed, coiled on his hoard. "Everything comesss for the sssword. Ssstop that."

"Stop what?" said Matthias.

"Looking at my ssscales like you're going to groom me. Everyone doesss. I *hate* it."

Which is how Matthias won, because he had been raised in an Abbey where every creature is welcome, and he offered the loneliest snake in the jungle the one thing no warrior had ever brought him: a proper going-over with comb and claws, every patch of that vast shivering hide. Somewhere in the middle of it, Asmodius wept a little, disgorged eight hundred years of swallowed treasure, and let the young howler take the sword of Mandrill the Warrior with his blessing and a standing invitation to the Feast, which he never once attended but liked being invited to.

Matthias came home at the worst possible moment, as heroes do. Cluny had breached the gate with the last of his horde. Fire licked the orchard. Hostages had been taken; the Abbey mice had been driven to the top of the stairs; the tyrant's patience, never his strong suit, had finally killed itself. The Mango Bell — cast from melted armor by the Abbey's founders, so that the sound of war would forever call the peace — hung in its tower above the fray.

"I am that ape!" Matthias roared, and meant it, and swung down into the battle like judgment on a rope of vines — with the Skyparrot clans diving behind him, the shrew-skirmishers of the Guosim boiling in through the gatehouse, and Basil Mongoose arriving fashionably late and loudly.

He fought Cluny across the burning yard, up the bell tower stairs, tooth against blade, novice against Scourge, while the whole Abbey held its breath. And when Cluny lunged for the kill, Matthias did the simplest thing in the world.

He cut the rope.

The Mango Bell fell with a sound like the end of the world, and Cluny the Scourge heard only the first note of it.

Afterward, the feast went on for three days. Deeper'n'ever durian pie, October nana-nog, and — at the far end of the table, on a cushion, wearing a little party hat the Dibbuns had made him — a python of excellent temper, being groommed by novices and hating every minute of it.

"Stop that," he said, and stayed for seconds.

The bell was rehung and named, and Matthias and Cornflower were given the honor of the first peel at the harvest. Matthias ate like a warrior and fought like a novice that evening — sparring Warbeak in the orchard till the stars came out — and when Cornflower asked him whether he was still going to be Mandrill the Warrior when he grew up, he looked at the Abbey, whole and loud and feeding everybody, and said: "No. I'm going to be me. Mandrill can have the sword back."

The tapestry, the Dibbuns swear, winked.
