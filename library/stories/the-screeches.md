# The Screeches

*A parody of "The Sneetches"*

Now, the Tuft-Belly Screeches

had tufts on their bellies.

The Plain-Belly Screeches

had none upon thars.

The Tuft-Belly Screeches

would brag, "We're the best kind

of Screech on the beaches,

with tufts like the rest-kind

could never possess!" So they'd sniff and they'd snort,

"We'll have nothing to do

with the Plain-Belly sort!"

And whenever they met one,

they'd snootily state:

"We are Screeches with tufts, sir,

and you? You are late

for a troop you're not in. Run along now, small primate."

Now of course, when the Tuft-Bellies had their big bonfires

they'd roast up green bananas

on hot beach-wood fires,

and they'd play leap-the-driftwood, and hoot beachy ballads,

and compare belly tufts in the shallows and shallows —

while the Plain-Belly Screeches

were kept well away

from the warmth and the plantains

and the swinging-and-play,

where they'd munch dry old figs on the cold beach sand.

One Plain-Belly youngster named Lou tried once, just once,

to sneak to the fire for a hot banana.

A great big Tuft-Belly turned him about:

"NO TUFTS, NO TREATS!" he bellowed and spat.

"You can't play beach-ball! You can't sing the song!

A tuftless Screech simply does NOT belong!"

Poor Lou sat down in the cold damp sand

and looked at his belly, plain as a hand,

and said to himself, and a little blue fish,

"It isn't my fault. It's my belly, I wish.

But nobody cares how a wish may be wished."

And it was terrible! Bitter! Insulting! Unkind!

For a Screech with no tuft is a Screech in a bind:

he can't join the chorus, the feast, or the troop,

he can't even swing from the birthday-loopde-loop!

But then ONE day, a strutting old driver ape

came prancing out of the brush with a wink and a cane,

pulled up in a cart that was painted up grand

(with a fold-out machine on the rear-most stand),

and he said with a grin and a prod of his hat:

"Friends, friends! Sylvester MacMacaque is my name —

MacMacaque MacBean, at your service," said he.

"I have heard of your troubles. I've heard you're unhappy.

But I can fix that, my friends —

I'm the Fix-It-Up Chappie!

"I have here a machine, and it works like a dream,

and my prices are fair — well, they're fair-ish, they seem!

It's a Tuft-On machine, and it's neat as a pin.

Why, you'll all have tall tufts on your bellies! Hop in!

It won't hurt a bit! Not a scratch! Not a scar!

My friends, one short session — three bananas! THREE!

And I'll tuft every Plain-Belly Screech on this beach — whee!"

So the Plain-Belly Screeches, they clamored: "US FIRST!"

Lou dug up his three-'nana savings (the worst

of feelings, that digging, but oh, what a prize!).

And into the machine he was stuffed — what a burst!

There was blinking of lights, and a whirring of gears,

a fluffing, a puffing, a spinning of shears,

and out Lou came strutting, so proud and so tall,

with a fine fuzzy tuft like a fountain of shawl!

He marched to the bonfire, first one through the door,

and he hollered, "HOT PLANTAINS FOR EVERYONE — POUR!"

But then, sir, the Tuft-Bellies — how they did moan!

"Our tufts made us special! Our tufts set the tone!

That macaque has ruined it! We're just like the rest!

Quick, MacBean — turn us PLAIN! Do your worst! Do your best!"

"With pleasure," said MacBean, "for a small extra fee —

my Tuft-OFF machine is the finest of three!

Step this way! Ten bananas! A bargain! A deal!"

And the tufts were plucked off with a whir and a squeal.

But NOW, said the brand-new Tuft-Bellies (the first ones),

"Aha! We've got tufts and YOU haven't, you persons!"

So the plain ones paid three more to get themselves tufted,

and the tufted paid ten more to get un-tufted,

and the un-tufted paid MORE to be tufted anew —

well, twenty bananas! — and then thirty-two!

In they went, out they came, in again, out again, Lou!

(Young Lou went in eleven times. He lost count at nine.

His tuft was so tired it just lay down and pined.)

The prices went up every round of the way:

first three, then ten, then a hundred a day!

At last, when their pouches were empty and slack,

MacBean said, "No problem! I'll put it ON PACK —

just sign here, dear Screech, on this leaf-and-ink line —

one small IOU and your credit is fine!"

And they signed! And they spun! And they paid and they paid —

tufts ON, tufts OFF, till their savings allayed!

They kept going around in a cycle of hair:

tufts on! Tufts off! Till they didn't know where

their bellies had been or their tufts had been stuck —

all they knew was their pouches were clean out of luck.

For MacBean kept his cash register ringing with fruit,

and he tallied his bananas and chuckled so cute:

"You can't teach a Screech, friends! You can only re-dress 'em!

And a Screech without bananas? Why, I simply possess 'em!"

Till at last, at sunset, every last 'nana was spent,

every leaf-IOU signed to the last muddy cent,

and the Screeches stood blinking, bewildered, fore-bent,

and they looked at each other and they could not tell

who had tufts and who hadn't, and who'd wear them well.

Was THIS one the one who was PLAIN on the sand?

Was THAT one the one with the tufts? Understand:

neither the Plain-Belly Screeches nor Tuft-Belly crew

knew if this one was that one, or that one was this,

or this one was THAT one, or that one was THIS,

or WHICH one was WHICH when they'd BOTH been on site,

or who'd paid to be plucked or been plucked to be tight!

And the beach went all quiet, except for the sound

of MacBean counting bananas by the pile and the pound.

Then Lou — little Lou, with his hungry-leaf sack —

looked round at the mess, and he slowly said back:

"Wait just a minute. Just WAIT, one and all!

When I had no tuft, I was nothing. That's small.

But I've HAD a tall tuft, and I've had it plucked too —

and I'm STILL just plain Lou. So the tuft isn't TRUE.

It never once mattered. Not tuft and not bare.

We're all of us Screeches, so what do we care?"

Then, at last, they were glad of the lesson they'd got,

and they learned what they'd learned from the whole banana lot:

That day, all the Screeches forgot about tufts,

and whether they had them or not — what's the use?

And that's why I tell you, from branch-top to root:

don't buy your bald belly a fancy new suit!

For the Screeches got really quite smart on that day —

the day they decided that Screeches are Screeches,

and no kind of Screech is the best on the beaches.

That day, all the Screeches forgot about stars —

I mean tufts! — and whether they'd one upon thars.

Then MacMacaque MacBean packed it up with a grin,

loaded bananas to the gunwales and booted his van,

and he drove off as rich as a monkey has ever

been driven away from a beach, smart as clever,

chuckling, "You can't teach a Screech, and that's that!"

But he was wrong, was that Mac — and I'm glad to state:

the Screeches stayed friends!

And they've stayed that way, friend —

one troop, every belly, both tufted and plain,

and they swing together in sunshine and rain.

They roast bananas for all now. They leave room at the fire.

The birthday-loopde-loop has no tufty-require.

And if a clever chimp in a coat ever tries

to sell you a machine that improves all the guys —

remember the Screeches, remember the beach,

remember poor Lou and his eleven-tuft streak,

and holler: "No thanks, sir!

A Screech is a Screech!"
