# Funnybonobos

*A parody of "Funnybones"*

In a dark dark jungle there was a dark dark grove.

In the dark dark grove there was a dark dark tree.

In the dark dark tree there was a dark dark hollow.

In the dark dark hollow there was a dark dark nest.

And in the dark dark nest, up an old fig tree, there lived two bonobo skeletons. A BIG BONOBO SKELETON.

And a little bonobo skeleton.

And a dog skeleton.

**WOOF!**

One night — a dark dark night, naturally — the little bonobo skeleton woke up and rattled herself out of bed, bone by bone by bone. She rattled over to the big hammock and shook it.

"Wake up!" she clattered. "Wake up, wake up, WAKE UP! It's the middle of the night!"

"Mmmf," said the big bonobo skeleton, which in skeleton means *go back to sleep*.

"Let's go and frighten somebody!" said the little bonobo skeleton.

The big bonobo skeleton sat up so fast his ribs went *xylophone*. "YES!" he clattered. "Let's frighten SOMEBODY!"

"WOOF!" agreed the dog skeleton, who agreed with everything.

So the little bonobo skeleton climbed down the dark dark tree, and the dog skeleton bounced down the dark dark tree, and the big bonobo skeleton — because he was very big and not very bendy — fell down the dark dark tree, all in a jingle-jangle jumble, and had to sort himself out at the bottom, which took a while, because elbows and knees look surprisingly alike in the dark.

"Ready?" said the little bonobo skeleton, when the big bonobo skeleton's legs were finally the right way round.

"Ready," said the big bonobo skeleton.

"WOOF!" said the dog skeleton.

And off they went, swinging and rattling through the dark dark jungle, to frighten somebody.

They swung over the dark dark river. They swung through the dark dark vines. They swung past the dark dark flowers that only open at midnight, and frightened them, and the flowers went *pop* in a fright, which didn't count, because flowers are easy.

They came to the hollow where the colobus monkeys slept, all curled up like black-and-white commas.

"Frighten time," whispered the little bonobo skeleton.

"BOO!" clattered the skeletons, together.

But the colobus monkeys were fast asleep. All they did was snore — a snore so long and bubbly that it snored the dog skeleton's ears inside out.

"Fast asleep," said the big bonobo skeleton, disappointed. "Everybody's ALWAYS fast asleep. Nobody to frighten."

"WOOF?" said the dog skeleton, which meant *what about the neighbours?*

So they swung on, through the dark dark undergrowth, to the burrow of the old warthog, who was famously grumpy and famously frightenable.

"ONE — TWO — THREE — BOO!" clattered the skeletons, with their arms up like spooky branches.

The warthog opened one eye. "Grrruffle," he said. "Rats with xylophones." And he went back to sleep.

"RATS WITH XYLOPHONES?" said the big bonobo skeleton, mortally insulted. "I'll have you know we are BONOBOES. Funnybonobos! The funniest bones in the whole jungle!"

"There's no fun in frightening somebody who won't be frightened," sighed the little bonobo skeleton. "Let's go to the jungle gym instead."

Now, the jungle gym was a genuine jungle gym — an enormous tangle of real branches that somebody had fixed up with ladders and ropes — and skeletons LOVE a jungle gym. The little bonobo skeleton did seventeen somersaults. The dog skeleton chased his own tail, which, being a skeleton tail, came off, and had to be caught. And the big bonobo skeleton got halfway up the rope ladder, shouted "Watch THIS!", attempted a triple swing with a half-twist —

— and tripped.

He tripped over the dog skeleton, who was under the ladder chasing his tail again, and the big bonobo skeleton came down *crash!* upon the little bonobo skeleton, who came down *bangle!* upon the dog skeleton, and all three of them burst apart like three boxes of very old, very mixed-up building blocks, into one enormous heap of bones.

Silence.

"Well," said a voice from somewhere in the heap, "this is a fine barrel of monkeys. Which one of you is standing on my head?"

"Which one of you is my head?"

"WOOF?" said a bone.

They sorted themselves out — or rather, they TRIED to sort themselves out.

First try: the little bonobo skeleton ended up with the dog skeleton's tail on top of her skull, waving like a little flag, and the big bonobo skeleton had one arm where his leg should be, so every time he tried to walk he shook hands with himself.

"That's not right," said the little bonobo skeleton.

"That's not even LEFT," said the big bonobo skeleton.

Second try: the dog skeleton was rebuilt with two BIG bonobo ribs for back legs, so he stood like a very small pony, and the little bonobo skeleton had her kneecaps on her shoulders.

"WOOF!" said the dog skeleton, unbothered. Being a dog, he had never once wondered where his legs were.

Third try: they put everything back exactly where it belonged — every rib, every knuckle, every toe — and then, just to be sure, they checked each other twice, head to toe, front to back.

"Perfect," said the big bonobo skeleton.

"Perfect," said the little bonobo skeleton.

"WOOF!" said the dog skeleton, and fainted, because all the checking had made him dizzy.

And that was when the little bonobo skeleton noticed, at the bottom of the jungle gym, a dark dark pool, still as glass, with the moon in it. And in the moonlight, looking up out of the water, she saw a HORRIBLE face — hollow eyes, a grinning mouth, rattling white bones!

"AAAAAAAARGH!" she clattered. "A SKELETON! A SCARY SKELETON!"

"AAAAAAAARGH!" clattered the big bonobo skeleton, seeing it too. "FRIGHTEN IT!"

They both leapt at the terrifying thing in the water, and the terrifying thing leapt at THEM, and there was an enormous splash and an enormous tangle and an enormous fright, and when the pool went still again, three soggy skeletons sat in the shallows, hugging each other, pointing at their own reflections.

"Wait," said the little bonobo skeleton, spitting out moonlight. "That's US."

"US?" said the big bonobo skeleton. He leaned over the water. The horrible skeleton leaned too. He made a face. So did it. He rattled his jaw. So did it.

"Well, I never," said the big bonobo skeleton. "We DID frighten somebody after all."

"We frightened ourselves!" said the little bonobo skeleton. "We're the best at it! Nobody in the whole jungle is better at frightening us than US!"

"WOOF," said the dog skeleton, proudly, as if he'd done most of the work.

So the funnybonobos went home the long way, three rattly shadows swinging through the dark dark undergrowth, past the dark dark flowers, over the dark dark river, scaring themselves at every splash and every rustle and having the BEST time, all the way back.

Up the dark dark tree climbed the little bonobo skeleton. Up the dark dark tree bounced the dog skeleton. And up the dark dark tree — very slowly, and holding on with both hands, which were attached correctly — climbed the big bonobo skeleton.

In the dark dark nest, they curled up, bone by bone by bone.

"Good frights," said the big bonobo skeleton.

"Good nights," said the little bonobo skeleton.

"WOOF," said the dog skeleton.

And in a dark dark nest, in a dark dark hollow, in a dark dark tree, in a dark dark grove, in a dark dark jungle — three funnybonobos went to sleep.

And they did.
