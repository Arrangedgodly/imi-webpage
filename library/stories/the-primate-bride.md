# The Primate Bride

*A parody of "The Princess Bride"*

"Grandpa," said the little marmoset, sick in her nest of leaves, "you don't have to read me the boring parts."

"Boring?" said Grandpa, settling his old gray rump on the branch. "This book has fencing. Fighting. Torture. Revenge. Giants. Chases. Escapes. True love. Miracles. And monkeys. I'll skip the kissing parts."

"Good," said the little marmoset. "Begin."

Buttercup the golden lion tamarin was the most beautiful creature in the troop, and she employed, on her little fig farm, a capuchin named Westley, whose only answer to her every demand was "As you wish."

"Farm boy, groom my mane." — "As you wish."

"Farm boy, fetch me a 'nana." — "As you wish."

"Farm boy, climb the thorny branch and pick me that one perfect fig." — "As you wish." And Buttercup suddenly realized that when he said "As you wish," what he meant was "I love you." And when she realized that, she said the only sensible thing: "I love you too, you enormous sap."

But Westley was poor. He sailed away to seek his fortune, and his ship was taken by the Dread Pirate Gibbons, who never leaves survivors. Buttercup went quite silent with grief — she had been silent before, but this was a different, worse silence — and the awful Prince Humperdinck, a mandrill with a face like a dangerous traffic sign, chose her to be his bride. She said yes the way you hand over your figs to a bandit: without enthusiasm, and without options.

Now, three villains kidnapped Buttercup: Vizzini the bonobo (a brain), Inigo the colobus (a master of the six-fingered scratch), and Fezzik, a very large gorilla (a heart of gold wrapped in fur). They sailed to the Cliffs of Insanity, and behind them, in a little black boat, came an Ape in Black, climbing the cliff by the vines, gaining, always gaining.

"Inconceivable!" cried Vizzini.

"You keep using that word," said Inigo. "I do not think it means what you think it means."

The Ape in Black met Inigo first, and they fenced — grooming-style, claw against claw, formal as a barbershop quartet. "You are using Bonetti's Defiance against me, eh?" said the Ape in Black. "I thought it fitting, given the rocky terrain."

"You are superb," admitted Inigo, as their claws blurred like hummingbirds.

"I admit it," said the Ape in Black. "You are better than I am."

"Then why are you smiling?"

"Because I know something you don't know."

"And what is that?"

"I am not right-pawed." He switched paws, and disarmed Inigo in a stroke — but knocked him cold with the hilt instead of the blade, because he was that kind of ape.

Next Fezzik, in a wrestling match of shocking politeness ("I do not enjoy arguments where I outweigh someone by a thousand pounds"). The Ape in Black put him in a sleeper hold, gently, like tucking in a very large child.

And then the famous battle of wits. "Where is the poison?" said Vizzini. "In one of these two figs. The poison is iocane powder. It is odorless, tasteless, and dissolves instantly in banana. Choose."

"Wait," said the little marmoset, sitting up in her nest. "This is the clever part. Don't skip this part."

"I wasn't going to," said Grandpa. "It's my favorite part."

They swapped. They sniffed. They switched. They reasoned at enormous length about where a clever bonobo would expect a cleverer ape to put it. They both ate. The Ape in Black was immune — iocane, it turns out, is built up in small doses by anyone raised on fermented banana wine — and Vizzini toppled, gasping his last: "To think — all that time — it was in BOTH — inconceivable—"

"You said it, not me," said the Ape in Black, removing his mask.

"Westley?" breathed Buttercup. "You're the Dread Pirate Gibbons?"

"Retired. I'm only called that. It's the name that scares people; the ship mostly floats." He shrugged. "As you wish."

They fled through the Fire Swamp — shrieking fig bursts, lightning-sand, and RUMs (Rodents of Unusual Monkey-hating Size) — and Buttercup learned that true love is mostly a man yelling "DON'T sit there" with great urgency. But Prince Humperdinck captured them. His plan, he explained to his Count, was elegant: kill the bride on the wedding night, blame the neighboring Giblin troop, and ride the war to glory. Meanwhile, into the Pit of Disrepair went Westley, where The Groomer sucked fifty years of life from him, one year per brush-stroke.

"You humiliated me in front of my bride," said Humperdinck. "Now I humiliate your hairline."

"Life is pain," said the pit keeper. "Anyone who says differently is selling something."

But Inigo, desperate for help with his revenge, hauled Westley's floppy body to Miracle Max the mandrill. "Is he dead?"

"Not ENTIRELY dead. Mostly dead. There's a difference between mostly dead and all dead — mostly dead is slightly alive! With all dead, there's usually only one thing you can do: check his pockets for loose figs. Give him this chocolate-coated pill. But remember: true love is the greatest thing in the world, except for a nice MLT — marmoset, lettuce and tomato, where the marmoset is nice and lean."

"Have fun storming the castle!" Max's wife shouted after them. "It'll take a miracle. GOOD THING YOU BROUGHT ONE."

They stormed the castle. Fezzik terrorized the guards in his terrible helmet voice ("I AM THE DREAD PIRATE GIBBONS. LEAVE NOW, OR FACE THE WRATH OF A VERY LARGE GORILLA"). Inigo found the six-fingered man who had killed his father, and said, growing louder each time, "Hello. My name is Inigo Monkeya. You killed my father. Prepare to die." And the man did, grievously, after offering Inigo money, power, and revenge-alternatives — but there are no alternatives in a sentence like that one.

"You've been chasing him your whole life," said Fezzik. "What will you do now?"

"To be honest," said Inigo, "I've been mostly winging it. I have no idea."

"Wing it with ME," said Fezzik. "The Dread Pirate Gibbons always needs a crew."

Humperdinck fled — straight into Westley's voice. Mostly dead Westley, tied to the throne machinery, sat propped and pitiless, and offered the prince a unique proposal.

"To the pain," said Westley, "means the first thing you lose is your feet, then your hands, then your nose —"

"You're bluffing," said Humperdinck. "You haven't strength left."

"Drop. Your. Sword."

He dropped it.

And Buttercup, tied to the throne, was freed by Westley's last stumble of strength.

"Westley, will you ever forgive me?"

"As you wish," said Westley, sagging into her arms.

And they rode off on white giant gorillas — which is exactly like white horses, only considerably more so — the kiss outranking the greatest kisses of history: the kiss of the Scarlet Spider Monkey, the kiss of the Arrow-Flinging Barbary, and yes, even the kiss of the Lord of the Banana-Ring.

"Wait," said the little marmoset, wiggling deeper into her leaf-nest. "Grandpa... you skipped the kissing parts perfectly. Is that the end?"

"The end of this one. There are lots more where that came from." He tucked the fig leaf around her.

"Grandpa. Maybe tomorrow... could you read it again?"

"As you wish," said Grandpa, and turned off the moon.
