# The Ape in the Cape

*A parody of "The Cat in the Hat"*

It was far too wet to swing.
Much too wet to climb.
So we sat in the nest.
We just sat there, my sister Sal and I.

We sat there, we two, on a cold drizzly day, grooming our knuckles with nothing to do. Mother had said, "Do not go ape. And do NOT let anyone in the nest." Then she went out, with her tail held high, and left us with the rain.

And SOMETHING went BUMP. We looked up with a jump.
There, balancing on the branch outside, was an orangutan in a striped cape and a tall striped hat that would have fit three orangutans, on a day our mother was most decidedly out.

"I know some good games we could play," said the ape in the cape. "I know some new tricks on a cold, cold, wet day like this. I am a chimpion show-off! Your mother will not mind at all if I do. She won't mind a bit, so long as I don't leave banana skins on the furniture."

Our nest-fish, Gilbert, bubbled furiously in his bowl. "No! No!" bubbled Gilbert. "Make that ape go away! Tell that ape in the cape you do NOT want to play! He should not be here when your mother is out!"

"Now, now, calm down, fish," said the ape in the cape. "We can have lots of good fun, if you'll let me show you. Trick one is quite simple — I call it Up on a Rump."

And the ape balanced on his rump on the rim of Gilbert's bowl, with a fig on his foot, a coconut on his hat, and a cup of juice on top!

"Look at me! Look at me now!" said the ape in the cape. "With a cup and a fig and a coconut on top! Look at me, look at me, look at me NOW! It is fun to have fun, but you have to know how — and I know HOW!"

"But that is not all," said the ape. "Look at me! On one toe! On a vine, on a ball! Up on the ball went the ape with it all — cup, fig, coconut, juice, and the bowl! He hopped! And he hopped! With his balancing load!"

And you know what happened. The hop went too high, and the whole pile came down — PLUNK — right on poor Gilbert.

"Look at this mess!" bubbled Gilbert, peeling a fig leaf off his fins. "That is NOT how a troop keeps a nest, you big orange lout! Your tricks are no good. Get out of this house!"

"I do NOT wish to go!" said the ape in the cape. "I do not wish to go with the mess and all that. And Sal, would you like to be helper? I have ninety-nine bananas, all balanced on one finger!"

Sal held the ladder of vines. I steadied the plate. And the ape in the cape took the whole yellow tower and walked — WALKED — up one side of the nest and down the other, and Sal almost smiled, and Gilbert almost didn't mind.

But you know what happened. One banana rolled. Ninety-nine bananas went BANANAS — on the walls, on the floor, on Sal, on me, mashing stickily onto every leaf of the nest.

"Look at this mess NOW!" bubbled Gilbert. "This nest is a smoothie! Your tricks are no good. Take your bananas and GO!"

"I do NOT wish to go!" said the ape in the cape. "Besides, I have not yet shown you the best trick of all. I have friends in my hat called Thing One and Thing Two."

And out of the hat came two tiny marmosets on strings — THING ONE and THING TWO, both wearing matching capes and grinning grins of pure monkey business.

"Have no fear!" said the ape. "They are perfectly trained! They never let go of their strings. Much."

"Let go of the strings!" cried the Things — and they did.

The Things flew their kites all through the trees, bounced on the vines, swung from the roof of the nest, knocked down the mangoes, tangled the lianas, juggled the figs, and hung Gilbert's bowl from the highest branch where no fish should go.

"Stop those Things!" cried Gilbert, from his branch in the sky. "Those Things should not fly kites in a nest! This is not a good game at all! Make them stop! MAKE THEM STOP!"

And then our mother was nearly in sight! Down the branch she would come, any minute, any second now — and the nest was bananas, and the Things were at large, and a fishbowl hung where the stars should be.

So I grabbed the troop's fishing net, and I caught those two Things in mid-flight — netted Thing One, then netted Thing Two, and told the ape in the cape, "That is ENOUGH for one day," and plucked Gilbert gently back down.

"Things!" called the ape in the cape, with his hat in his hand. "Back in the hat! Not another kite!" And the Things went back in the hat with their kites, sulking, the way weather goes back in the sky.

"And I am sorry," sighed the ape in the cape. "You were right all along, little fish. This nest is a mess and it's mine — and your mother — oh dear — she comes back down that branch in ten minutes flat."

Then he grinned, opened his hat, and pulled out a most extraordinary contraption: three grooming claws, a leaf-blower, a sponge on a very long stick, and one small umbrella, for reasons never explained.

"Have no fear of this mess!" said the ape in the cape. "I always pick up all of my playthings. And SO..." And with a WHOOSH and a whirl of his cape, the bananas unstuck from the walls, the kites flew back into the hat, the nest reassembled itself leaf by leaf, and Gilbert's bowl landed where it belonged, with Gilbert in it, glaring.

Then the ape shook both our hands — our feet, actually, it's a primate thing — and he and his hat and his Things shot off like a striped orange comet, and the rain kept on raining, and the nest sat there clean, as if nothing had happened at all.

And then Mother came back and said, "Did you have any fun? Tell me. What did you do?"

And Sal and I did not know what to say. Should we tell her about Gilbert, the kites, the bananas, the ape and the Things? Would she like it? She would not. She would NOT. But it's fun to have fun, and we had — and how do you explain a comet?

But Gilbert, from his bowl, gave one slow, deliberate bubble that meant what do YOU think, you silly great apes?

And we said, "No, we did nothing at all. We just sat in the nest. We sat there, we two. And we didn't have fun. We had no fun at all. It was far too wet to swing. Much too wet to climb."

And it was true. Sort of. If you don't count everything.

But — what would YOU do if your mother asked YOU?
