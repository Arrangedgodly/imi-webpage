# A Midsummer Night's Screech

*A parody of "A Midsummer Night's Dream"*

In the jungle of Athens-vine, Duke Theseus the mandrill announced to his bride-to-be, the queenly baboon Hippolyta: "Four happy days bring in another moon, and then we feast! Hippolyta, I wooed thee with my tail, and won thy love doing thy grooming — and now our nuptial hour draws on apace."

"Four days will quickly steep themselves in night," said Hippolyta, who was gracious and slightly wry. "And then we can finally stop announcing things."

Into the festivity stormed old Egeus, dragging his daughter Hermia and her two suitors: beloved Lysander and unwanted Demetrius. "This monkey hath bewitched her heart! This Lysander — he has sung under her window, given her knitted fruit-covers, and stolen her imagination! By the ancient privilege of Athens-vine, she marries Demetrius, or she grooms other monkeys' babies forever!"

"Your father's word is law, Hermia," advised Theseus.

"So is Lysander," said Hermia hotly, "and he has more figs. And better verses. And a better tail."

"Take time to pause," said Theseus; "and by the next new moon — either wed Demetrius, or to the nursery with you."

Given four days, the lovers made the plan lovers make best: one that could not possibly go wrong. "Steal forth tomorrow night," whispered Lysander, "and meet me in the wood, a league beyond the troop. There will I keep thee truly, and we'll flee to my aunt Squirrel's hollow, where Athens-vine law cannot poke its nose."

Her friend Helena — who loved Demetrius, who once had sworn love to HER, and who now adored Hermia — decided to tattle, so she could chase him chasing them. "Love looks not with the eyes but with the mind. The course of true love never did swing smooth. I will go tell him of fair Hermia's flight — for wherever he is, I shall be his shadow."

She was more right than she knew, for the wood was ruled by two quarreling fairies: Oberang, King of the Canopy, an orang with a crown of dragonflies, and Titania, his tiny-but-terrible queen.

"Ill met by moonlight, proud Titania."

"What, jealous Oberang! Since our quarrel the figs fall green and the monkeys groom in winter — the seasons alter, all through our dissension! And all for a changeling bushbaby."

"You know our quarrel is the same. Give me the bushbaby to be my page."

"Set your heart at rest — one half of my holding does not come and go." And she swept off with her whole fairy train, trailing moonbeams and attitude.

So Oberang did the mature thing: he summoned his trickster, a mouse-lemur named Pucksqueak.

"I am that merry wanderer of the night!" said Pucksqueak, somersaulting in. "I jest to Oberang, and serve him in a hundred screeches."

"Fetch me the flower called love-in-ripe-ness," said Oberang. "Its juice, laid on sleeping eyelids, makes any monkey dote upon the next creature seen. I know a bank where the wild papaya blows, where mandrills doze and drip the sap of sleep. I'll charm Titania's eyes till she gives up the bushbaby — and meanwhile dose a young Athenian-vine monkey in rusty fur, so the maiden greets him first."

"I'll put a girdle round about the jungle in forty minutes," said Pucksqueak, and did.

What could possibly go wrong? In the same wood, the Rude Gibbons were rehearsing a play for the wedding: "The Most Lamentable Comedy, and Most Cruel Death, of Piramus and Thisby," with bombastic weaver Nick Bottombarrel insisting on every part in it.

"Let me play the Lion too!" he bellowed. "I will roar so that the Duke says, 'Let him roar again!' I will aggravate my voice so that I will roar you as gently as any sucking dove — I will roar you an 'twere any nightingale."

"You can play no part but Piramus," said Peter Quince-topithecus, weary beyond his years.

Flute the fruit-bat objected to playing the lady Thisby — "Nay, faith, let me not play a woman; I have a ruff coming" — and Snug the sloth begged to have his lion part written out, "for I am slow of study." "You may do it extempore," said Quince-topithecus, "for it is nothing but roaring." Snout the tapir was to play the Wall. It was, all agreed, a company of genius.

Watching from a fern, Pucksqueak thought this the funniest thing ever and, being a spirit of pure monkey business, enchanted Bottombarrel with the head of a howler monkey. His fellow actors fled shrieking "O monstrous! O strange! We are haunted!" — leaving him singing in the glade, which woke Titania, upon whose eyes Oberang's nectar had already been dropped.

"What monkey is this?" murmured Titania.

"Not a monkey at all!" said Bottombarrel cordially, through his enormous new muzzle. "A howler, madam, at your service. Or at least my head is. The rest is a weaver."

"I pray thee, gentle howler, sing again! Mine ear is enamored of thy note. I love thee! Thou art as wise as thou art beautiful!"

"Reason and love keep little company together nowadays," observed Bottombarrel, and let himself be led off to the fairy bower to eat dew-bananas, with four little servants — Pease-blossom, Mustard-fig, Cobweb, and Moth — told off to scratch his head and bring him honey. He had never been happier in his life.

Meanwhile Pucksqueak found the Athenian monkeys asleep — and anointed the wrong one. Lysander woke, saw Helena, and instantly declared: "Transparent Helena! I run through fire I will for thy sweet sake!"

"Wherefore was I to this keen mockery born?" cried Helena, certain it was a prank — for she had followed Demetrius into the wood, and he had said the cruelest true thing: "I love thee not, therefore pursue me not. Do I not speak plainly? The more I love, the more they hate me." Then Demetrius, anointed too, adored her as well, and both boys fought over Helena while Hermia stormed in to scratch some eyes out. "You juggler! You canker-blossom! You thief of love! How low am I, that you should court her? Speak — how LOW am I?"

"Have you no modesty, no maiden blush?" cried Helena, backing up a tree.

"Lo, she is one of this confederacy!" — "You both are rivals, and love Hermia; and now both rivals to mock Helena!" — and four monkeys chased each other through the dark wood, threatening duels, until Pucksqueak, following with his notebook, delivered his verdict to the branches:

"Lord, what fools these monkeys be!"

He led them all in circles by mimicking their voices in the dark — "Lysander! speak again!" — until each fell asleep where they dropped, and then, at Oberang's word, he put the remedy on Lysander's eyes: "Jack shall have Jill; naught shall go ill; the man shall have his mare again, and all shall be well." And so, sorted at last — Demetrius enchanted to Helena rightly, Lysander to Hermia truly, and Bottombarrel's howler head gently unscrewed and returned to storage — the wood finally got some sleep.

"Titania waked, and straightway loved a howler," Oberang noted, later.

"So it was, my lord," said Titania, with enormous dignity. "We need never speak of the dew-bananas."

Bottombarrel woke alone in the glade. "The eye of man hath not heard, the ear of man hath not seen... man is but an ape if he go about to expound this dream. It shall be a ballad: 'Bottombarrel's Dream' — it hath no bottom! Peter Quince shall write it! Where are these lads? Where are these hearts?" — and he strode off to rehearsal, convinced he had been the hit of the century, which, in fairness, he had.

The lovers were found unscrambled in the dawn and marched to the Duke's triple wedding, where Egeus objected loudly and Theseus overruled him with the wave of a fig: "In the wood, it seems, the minds were altered. I will overbear your will; for in the temple by and by, with us, these couples shall be wedded. Egeus, you may sit down."

At the feast the Rude Gibbons performed their play. Quince-topithecus delivered a prologue so badly punctuated that it promised both to do harm and not to do harm simultaneously; Snout the tapir, as the Wall, explained, "All that I will tell you is, that I am a wall, and this cranny is mine, through which the lovers whispered"; and Piramus and Thisby whispered love through it, died magnificently — "Now die, die, die, die, die!" — and were eaten by Snug the sloth, as the Lion, with terrible slowness.

"This is the silliest stuff that ever I heard," said Hippolyta.

"The best in this kind are but shadows," said Theseus, "and the worst are no worse, if imagination amend them. Get you gone, sir — I mean, go on, splendid."

"Merry and tragical? Tedious and brief?" marveled Demetrius. "That is hot ice and wondrous strange snow!"

Then the fairies blessed the palace, room by room, and Pucksqueak put out the lantern-fruit: "If we shadows have offended, think but this, and all is mended: you have but slumbered here while these visions did appear. Every monkey, give me your paws, if we be friends, and Robin Pucksqueak shall restore amends. Good night! I'll vanish before you can say 'banana.'"

And he did. With a screech.
