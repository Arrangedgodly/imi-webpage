# Naked Langur

*A parody of "Naked Lunch"*

I would like to state flatly, for the record, that the following account is not a novel, because a novel has a plot, and a plot is a leash, and I have chewed through mine. What follows is a report from the perimeter of the jungle, submitted by the langur William Lee, sometimes called El Langurito, presently a fugitive from the Trappers of three jurisdictions.

Note the langur. Note especially the tail: white as a subpoena, longer than the law allows, and prehensile, which means it can hold things, and it has held things, and there is the whole of my trouble in one sentence.

The charges: importation of fermented 'nanas, and certain cartoons I had drawn of mandrills in circumstances no mandrill would tolerate. When the Trappers came, I signed a statement saying I was a langur of good character, which they typed up, which I signed, which made me a liar twice — once in ink and once in front of the mirror.

I could stay in the city only so long. The city is a cage that thinks it's a forest. So I went south to Interzone, a jungle city-state with the customs of a monkey house and the climate of a shoe.

How long does it take for a jungle to grow over a city?

In Interzone the Interzone Times ran eight editions a day and not one true sentence, which the editors called balance. The fruit market opened at dusk. The currency was rumors. I took a room above a hammock shop and waited, because in Interzone waiting is a profession, and I was rapidly becoming its most decorated practitioner.

—

MEET DR. BOUGH-WAY

Dr. Bough-Way was a physician of no fixed principles and infinite technique, a long-limbed colobus with a bedside manner like a card trick. I found him in his clinic, sterilizing a banana leaf over a flame.

"Lee! Just the langur. Tell me, what do you know of depersonalization?"

"Nothing I can't live without."

"Splendid, splendid. Consider: One Fruit, One Peel, One Troop." He sketched it in the dust: a single banana, and beneath it, kneeling, the multitudes. "That is my blueprint. Totalitarianism is simply hygiene applied to fruit. My colleagues call me a monster. My colleagues bill by the hour."

"Do you have a drink, Doctor?"

"I have a cure for wanting a drink. It consists chiefly of wanting it less publicly." He sighed and poured us both something amber out of a bug bomb. "I did a strictly conceptual act once, Lee. Never again. Concepts have no follow-through."

—

THE MUGWUMPS

In the back of the Café Terminal sat the Mugwumps: pale, hairless, hair-slicked monkeys who had peeled themselves of all inconvenience. They did not laugh, buy, or swing. They simply sat and gleamed, and from their foreheads, at long intervals, dripped a sweet nectar the desperate paid to catch on their tongues.

"Interopzone's finest," said the barman, wiping a glass with a dirtier glass. "They remember the jungle before the fruit. They are the only customers who never complain, never die, and never tip."

One of them looked at me with eyes like coat buttons and dripped once, slowly.

I did not catch it. Even then, a langur has his standards, though standards, in Interzone, are the first thing repossessed.

—

HASSAN'S RUMPUS ROOM

They said of Hassan's Rumpus Room that it was the most fabulous feast in Interzone, and they said it in the tone you'd use for a rare disease. Hassan, a gorilla in a fez, greeted each guest personally at the rope.

"You boys know how to have a good time," Hassan would boom, "or you would not be here." And the band played that slow, sweet jungle rhythm, like time being peeled.

A young macaque at the fruit table, deep in his cups, made the fatal request.

"Hassan," he said, "bring me something different."

The room went cold. Hassan's fez drooped. "Something," Hassan repeated, "different."

Two enormous bouncers took the macaque by the elbows with the utmost courtesy and carried him to the door.

"Out!" said Hassan. "Banned from the fruit market! For life!"

In Interzone, this is a fate worse than death, because it takes longer.

—

THE BLACK SWEET

Then there was the Black Sweet. Word came through the market of a cake of pressed, tar-black 'nana that made fermented fruit look like mineral water. The addicts of the Black Sweet described a centipede of pure sugar scuttling through the blood, laying its eggs of contentment in every joint.

I confess I looked for it. I looked for it the way you look for your hat when your hat is already on your head, which is to say desperately, and everywhere, and forever slightly behind schedule.

—

THE MONKEY WHO TAUGHT HIS TAIL TO TALK

You will want to know about the monkey who taught his tail to talk. This was years ago, in the north. A baron of the banana trade, bored beyond fortune, trained his own tail in rhetoric — first a whistle, then a word, then whole tar-black paragraphs.

I saw the act once. The tail was better than the monkey. The tail discussed politics. The tail did impressions. The monkey sat rigid in his dinner jacket, holding his own tail up to the lamp like a lamprey with opinions, his eyes gone glassy as a taxidermied question.

In the end the tail upstaged him completely. It denounced its host at a garden party, revealing smuggling, graft, and the location of the good figs. The tail was rounded up in a dragnet and sentenced to life on a bare branch, where it hangs to this day, address unknown, telling all comers about the monkey it rode in on.

I knew him, you understand. We all knew him. Every monkey is the monkey the tail rode in on.

—

LANGUAGE, A VIRUS

At the Academy of Fruitless Medicine, I once heard a lecture proving that language is a virus from outer space. In Interzone, language is a fruit bat: it arrives at dusk, hangs upside down from your best sentence, and spoils the bunch.

Consider my statement to the Trappers. Consider any statement. The word "langur" comes very close to "languor," and I have never been sure which one the authorities arrested.

—

THE TITLE, EXPLAINED

I was eating in a chop house off the plaza when the word arrived, whole, the way words do in the tropics — on the back of a fly.

The title means exactly what the words say: NAKED LANGUR — a frozen moment when everyone sees what is on the end of every branch.

Look down at your paw. Look at what you are holding. Look at what you have been holding all along, in the dark, under the leaves, calling it breakfast.

That is the naked langur. That is the whole of the book, and the whole of the menu, and there is no dressing on the side.

—

DEPARTURE

The Trappers came to Interzone in the rainy season, in civilian pelts, buying rounds and taking names. The market folded like a fan. Hassan's closed for renovations. Dr. Bough-Way departed for a clinic of his own invention, from which, he assured me, he could depersonalize at long last by mail.

I stood on the roof of the hammock shop and watched the jungle leaning in over the rooftops — patient, green, unimpressed. Ficus roots had already cracked the plaza. Orchids were working on the bank.

Somewhere south, they said, grew yagé, on the Vine of Souls: one bite and every monkey in the world speaks inside your skull. No market, no middleman, no statement to sign.

Interzone would be a jungle again. It always had been. Only the rents were new.

I packed my tail and went out over the wall, hand over hand, into the leaves — the last naked langur, swinging south toward the word at the end of the branch.
