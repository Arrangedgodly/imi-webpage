# Each Peach Bare Bum

*A parody of "Each Peach Pear Plum"*

It was a sunny morning in the middle of the jungle, the kind of morning when the peaches blush, the pears dangle, and the plums hang heavy on the big plum tree at the corner of the grove, where everybody in the whole forest came to pick their breakfast.

A tiny chimp no bigger than a banana was up in that plum tree already, being extremely quiet, with one small purple fist already full.

**Each peach pear plum,**
**I spy Chimp Thumb!**

Chimp Thumb froze among the leaves. "Who said that?" he squeaked, in a voice the size of a grape. But nobody answered — the way it never does — so he climbed down with his plums and ran straight home to hide in the cupboard, where the jam lived.

And as he reached for the jam pot, who should come bending into the cupboard after him, rear end first, but his own mother, reaching for the very same pot?

**Chimp Thumb in the cupboard,**
**I spy Mother Macaquee!**

"Chimp Thumb!" cried Mother Macaquee, snatching the jam. "You've had three plums before breakfast!"

"Two," said Chimp Thumb. "The third one fell."

And down to the cellar she marched, Chimp Thumb under one arm, jam under the other. But on the cellar stair, somebody was sitting on the top step with her chin in her hands and a mop beside her: a sad little scullery monkey in an apron, watching the dust fall through the sunshine.

**Mother Macaquee down the cellar,**
**I spy Chimp-erella!**

"Why the long face?" asked Mother Macaquee.

"I've scrubbed the whole tree-house," sighed Chimp-erella, "and my stepmother says I still can't come to the Great Fig Ball tonight."

"Never you mind," said Mother Macaquee. "You scrub like an angel. Look — even the Three Bonobos came to admire your floor!"

And it was true: through the window marched three burly bonobos with walking sticks and empty shopping bags, off on their morning hunt for honey.

**Chimp-erella on the stairs,**
**I spy the Three Bonobos!**

"How do," said the Biggest Bonobo.

"How do," said the Middlest Bonobo.

"How do," said the Littlest Bonobo, who was only guessing.

And off they marched into the deep jungle, three bottoms swinging in a row like a rhythm section, hunting honey. They found the honey tree, too — but the honey tree was full of bees, and the bees had opinions, and that is a story for another day. So the Three Bonobos came marching home again, empty bags swinging, hunks still intact, which was when they passed a small green nest in a bush, with a small round baby in it, fast asleep on his front — thumb in mouth, blanket kicked off, little bare bottom shining in the sun like a second sunrise.

**Three Bonobos out hunting,**
**I spy Baby Banana-Bunting!**

"Hsst," said the Biggest Bonobo. "Do not wake the baby."

"His bottom's pinker than my plum," said the Littlest.

The baby smacked his lips in his sleep and slept on. And up the hill beyond the bushes, a small worried monkey in a bonnet was tiptoeing through the long grass, peering behind every fern, under every leaf, counting and recounting her toes.

**Baby Banana-Bunting fast asleep,**
**I spy Bo-Creep!**

"Have you seen my sleep?" Bo-Creep whispered to the Three Bonobos. "I've looked everywhere. I lost it at bedtime and I haven't seen it since."

"Babies are full of it," said the Middlest Bonobo, pointing at Banana-Bunting. "Creep over soft and you might borrow some."

But just then — BOOM, BOOM, BOOM — up the hill past them came two more, a small macaque and an enormous gorilla, rolling a great barrel of rainwater between them, grumbling all the way.

**Bo-Creep up the hill,**
**I spy Macaque and Gorill!**

"It's for the plum pie," puffed Macaque. "The Big Bake needs water."

"The Big Bake needs a smaller barrel," rumbled Gorill, who was doing most of the rolling.

But the hill was steep, and Macaque was showing off, and Gorill was going too fast, and together they rolled the barrel over the crest, lost their footing, and went down the far side in a slow, dignified, thunderous tumble —

**Macaque and Gorill in the ditch,**
**I spy the Wicked Witch!**

— straight into the ditch, where the Wicked Vervet happened to be lurking with her big black kettle, collecting snails. The barrel burst, the ditch flooded, the snails escaped, and the Wicked Vervet rose out of the muddle with her hat on backwards and her famous blue bottom somehow even bluer with rage.

"MY SNAILS!" shrieked the Wicked Vervet.

"Your ditch," corrected Gorill, still holding the barrel hoops.

The Wicked Vervet snatched up her kettle and went storming away over the wood — flounce, flounce, flounce — muttering spells and shaking her fist at the sky, and everybody watched her go, because a furious vervet crossing a green wood is one of the finest sights in the jungle.

**Wicked Vervet over the wood,**
**I spy Swinger Hood!**

Swinging hand over hand through the canopy, green-hooded and grinning, came the jungle's most wanted good guy — Swinger Hood, friend of all small plum-thieves, with a fat red cherry tucked behind one ear for later.

"Wicked Vervet!" he called down pleasantly. "Wherefore the flounce?"

"Somebody flooded my snails!" raged the Vervet. "There'll be a curse about it!"

"There'll be pie about it," said Swinger Hood. "Everybody's baking. Haven't you heard the whole grove singing? Look down there."

And far below, at the corner of the grove, the Woodcutting Chimp was raising his axe against the big plum tree itself, because — for one morning only, by ancient jungle law — the Big Bake needed every last plum.

**Swinger Hood with a cherry,**
**I spy the Woodcutting Chimp!**

"TIMBER-OOH-OOH!" cried the Woodcutting Chimp, and the big plum tree tipped its branches, and all the plums in the world came down at once — bouncing and bowling through the grass, and every monkey, bonobo, ape and baby in the jungle came running to catch them.

The Wicked Vervet caught six, and remembered she loved baking more than cursing. Chimp-erella caught a whole basketful, and a fig-slipper fell out of the tree into it, which she took for a good omen. ("Wear it to the Great Fig Ball," said Mother Macaquee, "and if your stepmother says one word about it, tell her the tree chose your outfit.") Bo-Creep lay down in the warm grass, and before the pie was even in the oven — found her sleep again, mid-sentence. Baby Banana-Bunting woke at last and demanded plums, loudly, with both fists, and got them, and immediately fell asleep again in the plum basket, which everyone agreed was the correct use of a plum basket.

And when the great banana-plum pie came out of the community oven, hot and purple and smelling like the best day of summer, they all sat down together on the sunny bank — Chimp Thumb and Mother Macaquee, Chimp-erella and the Three Bonobos, Baby Banana-Bunting on Mother Macaquee's lap, Macaque and Gorill still damp from the ditch, the Wicked Vervet (hat fixed) and Swinger Hood (cherry eaten), and the Woodcutting Chimp with his axe across his knees.

And it being a warm day, and pie being sticky, and monkeys being monkeys, not one of them kept a stitch of extra clothing on past the second helping.

**Each peach pear plum,**
**I spy... EVERYONE!**

And they all ate banana-plum pie, bums in the sun.
