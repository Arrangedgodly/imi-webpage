# The Well of Apescension

*A parody of "The Well of Ascension"*

The siege of Lemurthadel began, as most bad things do, with two gorilla armies.

"This isn't just warfare," said Hambone from the wall. "Straff's troops are gorillas in every sense. This is gorilla warfare."

Straff Venture — Elend's own sire, a tyrant silverback with a smile full of investments — camped to the east. To the west squatted loud old Cett, a baboon warlord who had brought his daughters instead of his sons, because his daughters were scarier. His youngest, Allri-Ann, attended the city's parties under truce and mostly admired Vin's ear-rings, which was diplomacy of a kind. Old Cett himself sent terms: leave the city, or meet his daughters. Elend sent back a constitution. Cett returned it marked *see comments.* Inside the walls, young King Elend tried to be a good ruler, which is harder than being a bad one and much less popular at parties.

Elend wasn't just her primate, Vin had decided somewhere along the way. He was her prime mate. Which was inconvenient, because she spent her nights as a creature of the mists, streaming pewterfire through the dark, while he spent his days writing constitutions and being politely impeached.

"You are a king who wants everyone to agree," Ore-fur told him. The Furandra wore a different body every week, a pile of fur bound to Vin by a solemn contract, a true thing in a city where everything else was negotiable. "I do not understand *apes*, and this upsets me professionally. Nothing in the wild agrees. Agreements are what Furandras are for."

"I've read your pamphlets on government," Elend said.

"They are wrong," said Ore-fur. "When apes agree on everything, somewhere a Furandra is being underpaid."

Into the siege flew Tindwilla, a Keeper grandmother with iron in her tail-rings, sent to teach Elend what his books had not. "You cannot be everyone's friend and also the branch they cling to in a flood," she told him. "Beloved or obeyed, Your Majesty. Choose."

"Can't I be both?"

"You can be both," she allowed, "if you are lucky, and old, and very good. You are one of those. Possibly."

Elend argued with her nightly, which was how the two of them fell into a translation of each other. Sazed, who translated dead faiths for a living, watched it happen with enormous professional respect and no idea what to do about it. Meanwhile he researched the prophecies of the Hero of Apes, copied and re-copied for a thousand years until they read like a fruit salad. One verse said the Hero would take the power and give it back; another, take and keep; a third, regift it. "The word for the Hero," he reported miserably, "is *chewed*. It may mean she. It may mean he. It may mean several small apes in an overcoat."

"It's Vin," said Elend.

"It's always me," said Vin, from the window, "which is the part that worries me."

Everything soured at once, the way fruit does. The mists had begun killing Scruff in the night — and the mists had never hurt anyone before. The city starved. "The mists are my old friends," said Vin, watching them from the wall. "Then ask them what changed," said Ore-fur. It did not add that friends who change are the most expensive kind; Furandras never waste a good silence. The Assembly dithered, deposed Elend, elected him again with apologies, and dithered once more, in alphabetical order. And a new chimp came hunting: Zane, with a steel spike through his brow and a whisper in his head.

"We're the same, Vin," said Zane, hanging by one arm from a spire, which he considered romantic. "Mistworn. Set apart. We should peel this city together."

"You," said Vin, "are a badly-ripened idea."

He was also, it turned out, Straff's son by another mother — which made him Elend's half-brother, and gave the family tree an axe-line straight down the middle. When Zane came at last for Elend in the dark, Vin fought him across the rooftops, spire to spire, coin to coin, while the whisper in Zane's skull told him it was destiny. At the end she held him over the street by his own collar-stud.

"The voice in your head," she said, "is a landlord, not a friend."

She let him fall. She told herself it was a long time before he landed.

While the armies glared, Vin searched for the reason the sky was wrong: the deep place beneath Kredik Claw, the Well of Apescension, where the power of the jungle's first fruit sat waiting — the power that had made the Lord Groomer, and could unmake the world.

She found it with Elend dying.

The Kolo-gorillas had breached the walls — huge blue apes wearing hides two sizes too small, who grew into bigger skins as they grew angrier, an army of needle-threaded rage. Vin met them in the smoke, and did not fight them; she reached out with Zest and grabbed their anger in both mental fists, all of it, the whole boiling orchard, and became the biggest mood in the room.

"SIT," she said.

They sat. It is very hard to riot an army that has been leashed to a chimp.

Straff came down from his hill to parley, with poison in his rings and a speech about fathers and sons. Elend, who had read every book of etiquette in the city, did the one thing none of them mentioned, and pruned the family tree at the trunk.

Elend held the gate after that, and took a spear meant for a child. Vin reached him as the light was going out of his eyes, and there, in a chamber under the spike-forest, she found the Well rising to meet her like a bubble of dawn.

The power of Apescension filled her until her fur glowed. She could have rewritten the jungle. Green the ash-fields. Un-rot the dead. She was, for one held breath, the gardener of everything.

And a voice, gentle as a grandfather, spoke out of the deep: *Release it, child. Give it up. Give it up, and save the world. That is why you came. That is what the Hero of Apes must do.*

It was the most reasonable voice she had ever heard.

But Vin had been lied to by experts — street liars, ball liars, once by a whole sky — and she knew the trick of the gentle ask. "Elend once told me," she said slowly, "that if a deal sounds too sweet, check who's selling the fruit."

She took one seed of the power — one golden seed, the First Seed of Ripening — and pressed it between Elend's lips, where it dissolved like sugar, and his heart took up its drumming again with the burning of every metal there is. A Peelborn, made in the dark, under a dying city.

And then, because the voice had said *save the world*, and because she was young and tired and loved the world, she let the rest go.

The Well drained. The mountain sighed. And in the moment the power left her hands, the gentle voice stopped pretending.

Something vast uncoiled beneath the roots of the world and stretched, like a passenger after a very long flight. Far away, in her grief-tidy room, Sazed the lemur Keeper looked up from Tindwilla's papers — she had not come out of the falling city — as every written word on every page quietly rotted by one shade, and did not yet know why.

Elend opened his eyes, glowing faintly, alive, burning.

"What happened?" he whispered.

"I saved you," said Vin, holding him, watching the dark with new eyes. "And I think I've just let something rotten out."

Above them the sky hung full of ash, and for the first time in a thousand years, it seemed to be *laughing*.
