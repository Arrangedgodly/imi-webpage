# Interesting Climbs

*A parody of "Interesting Times"*

It began, as most disasters do, with a monkey trying to help.

The wizards of Unseen University had discovered a spell that could send a wizard anywhere in the world instantly, which was remarkable, and aimed it at Rinsemacaque, which was unforgivable. Rinsemacaque — the worst wizard ever to wear the hat that said WIZZARD, a scrawny capuchin whose entire magical output in thirty years had been one spell, a lot of running, and the word "no" pronounced in seventeen directions — materialized on the far side of the world, in the Empire of the Counterweight Canopy.

It was an empire of red-tiled pagoda trees and perfect manners, ruled by Emperor Long-Paw, Son of Heaven, a cruel pampered macaque with thousands of servants and one large hobby: death. Behind it all ran the Great Vine — a wall of living wood, a thousand miles long and a hundred monkeys high, patrolled day and night; nothing came over it, because nothing, the Empire believed, would ever want to.

"Great," said Rinsemacaque, looking at it from the wrong side. "Now I only have to walk around it."

He was in Hung-hung, the Forbidden City, where he found two things. The first was Twobanana — the world's first tourist, a cheerful young ape in ironed shorts, trailed, as ever, by the Luggage, his bananawood chest with hundreds of little legs and no manners whatsoever.

"Rinsemacaque!" Twobanana beamed. "Did you come for the revolution?"

"I came by accident."

"That's how all the best tourism happens."

And the second thing: the Empire was ripe. Twobanana's holiday memoir, *What I Did On My Holidays*, had slipped past the censors and become the most dangerous book in the Empire: one where ordinary monkeys complained about the government and were not struck by lightning. Students copied it by paw. A secret army formed, named for the red banners of protest: the Red Bum Army, because their manifesto's first demand was the right to sit down, and their second demand, written slightly later, was a rash ointment. Its leaders included two of the sweetest-natured revolutionaries in the Empire, Pretty Butterfly and Lotus Blossom — who were, Rinsemacaque noted with the sensation of a dropping branch, Twobanana's twin daughters.

"You named them *what*?" he said.

"Their mother chose," said Twobanana, proudly. "I chose their education. It appears to have been revolutionary."

"There's been a misunderstanding," Rinsemacaque told the Red Bum Army's cell, when they explained that the great wizard Rinsemacaque had been sent by heaven to lead them. "I'm not a great wizard. I'm barely a monkey."

"The leaflets say otherwise," said Butterfly, sweetly, producing several hundred leaflets that said otherwise.

Ruling the Empire behind the throne was the truest evil it had ever produced: Lord Hong, a silken-cheeked macaque so clever he was bored by it, who had poisoned three rivals before breakfast as a form of calisthenics. Lord Hong wanted the Silver Horde — more precisely, he wanted the chaos they'd bring, so that he could tidy it up afterwards and inherit the wreckage. He wrote poetry; the poetry was confiscated, on pain of death, which everyone agreed was the kindest review available.

Then the Silverback Horde arrived.

They came over the Great Vine at night — seven elderly gorillas, lean as whipcord, silver of back and black of humor, led by Cohen the Barbanarian: greatest hero of the age, leathery as a three-year-old banana skin, with tungsten dentures and a waistcoat of improbable trophies. Behind him came Caleb the Rash, Truckle the Understatement, Boy Willie with his famous one-two-any-question-three, and Mad Hamish, a hundred and five, wheelchair-mounted, who screamed insults in a language nobody had spoken aloud in three centuries. The Horde had heard the Empire had never been conquered, and came, as Cohen explained it, "to see why not."

"You're seven old monkeys," said Rinsemacaque. "It's an empire of millions."

"Young monkey," said Cohen, filing a denture, "we've been fightin' since before your granddad was a mistake. Empires have armies; armies is soldiers; soldiers fight for a living, which means they want to survive the fight. We're heroes. Heroes don't have that problem."

The revolution and the conquest collided at the Festival of the Golden Peach. Emperor Long-Paw, discovering ancient legends in his palace and a peasant uprising in his streets, deployed his final wonder: the Terracotta Troupe — five thousand clay monkeys, a thousand years old, stamped each one with a spark of old magic, obedient as gravity. They woke with a sound like a mountain clearing its throat, and were every bit as unbeatable as advertised.

For about nine minutes.

Then the Horde demonstrated the difference between training and experience, the Red Bum Army the difference between obedience and appetite, Twobanana the power of polite bafflement, and the Luggage what five hundred years of bananawood resentment does when it finds a door it doesn't have to open. Mad Hamish's wheelchair, fitted with blades by an admiring Feegle correspondent, went through the Troupe's left flank like a plough through topsoil. Lord Hong, observing from a balcony that his own schemes had finally produced a situation with no floor, made a dignified exit and was last seen fleeing across a rooftop, hotly pursued by a small chest with hundreds of legs and a grudge against his luggage policy. The Empire's historians recorded the succession as "concluded, with prejudice."

They found Emperor Long-Paw in his own ornamental lake, being an ornamental duck.

"You can't kill me," gabbled the Son of Heaven. "I am the Emperor! There is a curse! *May you live in interesting times!* You wouldn't dare make it more interesting!"

"Sonny," said Cohen the Barbanarian, sitting himself on the throne and scratching, "we've had plagues, wars, weddin's, and one interestin' pancake. Interestin' is what happens *between* the good bits."

The new Emperor Cohen's first reforms were, in order: pensions for heroes, tea for everyone, and a decree that the Great Vine be converted into the world's longest monkey-bars, open to the public on feast days. The Red Bum Army got its sitting-down, its ointment, and — after one disastrous afternoon trying to organize a queue — an education in how much easier it is to storm a palace than to run one, which Butterfly and Blossom absorbed over the objections of exactly nobody, having been raised by Twobanana to regard governments as a kind of weather.

Rinsemacaque, who by now held the Empire's newest academic post — Professor of Cruel and Unusual Geography — stood at the palace window at dawn, watching the Red Bum Army try to organize a queue, and felt the familiar tingle of impending responsibility.

"You could stay," said Twobanana, hopefully. "The new Emperor says heroes who survive get pensions."

"I've seen what happens to interesting places," said Rinsemacaque. "They stay interesting. And I—" he was already climbing out the window, which for Rinsemacaque was a whole philosophy, "—am a *boring* monkey, and proud of it."

The wizards of Unseen University, informed of all this by the ancient magic of newspapers, voted to send help, and then, in the grand tradition of committees, defined "help" as Rinsemacaque, already sent, no further action required. They then gave themselves a very good lunch, which the minutes record as unanimous.

They say in the Counterweight Canopy that the old curse goes: *May you climb in interesting vines.*

Rinsemacaque climbed out of that empire at record pace, the Luggage thumping along the rooftops below him, and nothing interesting caught him.

For a whole day.
