# Making Monkey

*A parody of "Making Money"*

Moist von Chimpwig stood in the Oblong Office of Lord Vervetinari, Patrician of Ankh-Morepork, and did the one thing he was famous for: he grinned like a monkey with a plan.

"You want me to run the Royal Mint," said Moist. "I ran the Post Office under protest, and now you want me to run the place where money comes from. I'm a former con artist. I spent years forging things, and you want to put me in charge of making monkey."

"Not money. Monkey," said Vervetinari, who pronounced the city's currency exactly as he pleased. "The Mint, the Royal Bank. The Lavish family has owned the bank for generations, and the chairmanship has recently become vacant. Topsy Lavish, aged one hundred and thirty, departed this life, biting her lawyer on the way out."

"Who runs the bank now?"

"Mr. Fusspot."

"Who is Mr. Fusspot?"

Vervetinari drew back a curtain. On a cushion sat a marmoset wearing a tiny expression of immense wealth, gnawing a stale banana.

"Mrs. Lavish left him her entire fortune," said the Patrician, "with the stipulation that, upon his death, everything passes to her dog — and upon the dog's death, to you. The family is not entirely patient."

The family demonstrated its impatience within the week. The Lavish aunts sent Mr. Fusspot a congratulatory banana that smelled faintly of bitter almonds, which is a traditional flavor in Lavish wills. Gladys, a gorilla of impeccable manners whom Moist had once hired as a postwoman, and who now guarded the chairman with an expression of gentle menace, intercepted it, curtsied, and gave the aunts a look that caused them to remember urgent business abroad.

The eldest son was worse. Cosmo Lavish had begun collecting Vervetinari memorabilia — the ring, the cane, and now, worryingly, custom-made boots. "Soon," Cosmo whispered to his mirror, "I shall *be* him."

At the Mint, Moist met Mr. Bent, an orangutan of such perfect posture that dust settled on him respectfully. Mr. Bent disapproved of Moist, of whimsy, of paper currency, and of fun, in that order.

"The coin of the realm," said Mr. Bent, "is worth its weight in banana-gold. A one-banana coin contains one banana's worth of gold."

"Wonderful," said Moist. "So melt it and you have the same banana. Now here's my idea: paper. A one-banana *note*. It costs nothing to print and is worth one banana."

"And where is this banana?" said Mr. Bent, with the patience of a mountain. "Who holds the banana?"

"That," said Moist, "is the beautiful part. Nobody. The note *is* the banana. People just have to believe in it. Belief is the strongest thing in Ankh-Morepork — we've got gods applying for citizenship on the strength of it."

Meanwhile, in the basement, Igor (you could tell he was an Igor by the scar and the lisp) demonstrated the Glooper: a cathedral of glass tubing full of coloured liquids that mimicked the entire economy. "When people panic, marthter, the red liquid ritheth."

The only person in Moist's life who was neither for sale nor impressed by him was Adora Belle Dearheart — "Spike" to everyone but her mother — who ran the Golem Trust, typed like she was stapling the letters down, and had agreed, against all visible evidence, to marry him. "You're going to run a bank," she said, looking at him the way lightning looks at a tree. "The man who sold me a bridge is going to be in charge of all the money. I'm off to the desert to dig up the Trust's new recruits; when I get back, try not to have been hanged."

Cosmo Lavish hired the merciless Mr. Fungus and a horde of goons to destabilize the bank and retrieve the chairman. The goons got Moist first. They locked him in the old gold vault with a candle, a chair, and the strong suggestion that he sign the bank over to Cosmo.

Moist had been in worse rooms. He'd *sold* worse rooms. He spent a cheerful hour talking to the door — loudly — about the second, secret vault, the one with the real gold and the unfortunate habit of flooding at high tide, and then he put out the candle, moved the chair, and waited by the wall in the dark, and when the goons came in to check on the drowning vault, he walked out past them carrying the empty money box as though it were his, because nobody ever questions a confident monkey carrying a box. By the time they turned around he had locked them in and was apologizing to them through the keyhole, sincerely, at length, while they listened despite themselves, because sincerity was his instrument and he had tuned it in prisons all over the continent.

The marmoset was guarded through it all by Gladys, who also ironed his tiny waistcoats. Mr. Fusspot bit two goons and a lawyer, entirely in the line of duty.

Cosmo's plan came apart in the grand manner: attempting to *become* Vervetinari, he was out-Vervetinaried by Vervetinari, and ended the night ruined, confined, and in an urn-shaped pillow arrangement — with the boots still on.

Then the run on the bank came anyway, and with it the thing Moist had been dreading: the red liquid in the Glooper climbed to the top tube, and at the top of the crowd's panic, something inside Mr. Bent went *ping*.

The most stable monkey in Ankh-Morepork stood on a counter, removed his spectacles, and revealed that he had run away from the circus at the age of six, on the grounds that it was insufficiently respectable, and had been holding himself perfectly still ever since. He juggled three one-banana notes. He walked the balcony rail like a tightrope. He produced, from somewhere, a custard pie and deployed it on the loudest Lavish aunt with a precision the Mint would have been proud of. The mob, who had come to riot, discovered they were laughing instead, which is very hard to do while kicking in a door.

"The notes aren't worth anything!" bellowed a gorilla in the crowd, recovering. "Show us the bananas!"

"Right," said Moist, climbing onto the counter. "You want bananas? I'll show you the bananas. Open the vault!"

The vault revealed not a single banana. It revealed gorillas: hundreds of them, standing silently in ranks, holding ledgers — Adora Belle's gorillas, raised from the desert and the old sunk cities, honest to the bottom of the world, with the Golem Trust's guarantee stamped on every contract.

"Every note," Moist announced, "is backed by a day's honest work from one of the Trust gorillas — who cannot lie, cannot steal, and cannot be bought. Your money isn't in the vault, friends. It never was. It's in *each other*. That's what making monkey really means. Now — who'd like a cup of tea and a fresh-baked banana bun while we print you some more?"

The mob stayed for the buns. Adora Belle, arriving dust-covered from the desert with the last of the gorilla convoys, looked at the crowd, the notes, and the custard-streaked Mr. Bent, and said, "I leave for three weeks."

"You left for four," said Moist.

The notes held. In the weeks after, Moist caught a pickpocket trying to spend a forged one-banana note and shook his hand warmly — professional courtesy.

In the Oblong Office, Vervetinari steepled his fingers.

"Congratulations on stabilizing the currency," he said. "I now have a vacancy for someone to run the tax office."

"I —" said Moist. "You're enjoying this, aren't you."

"I have never had half so much fun," said the Patrician, "as watching an honest man try to escape."
