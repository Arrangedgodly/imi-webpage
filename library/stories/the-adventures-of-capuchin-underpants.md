# The Adventures of Capuchin Underpants

*A parody of "The Adventures of Captain Underpants"*

George and Harold were two fourth-grade chimps at Jerome Bonobowitz Elementary School. George had a tie and a flat-top haircut; Harold had a T-shirt and a bad case of bedhead fur. They were best friends — prime mates, you might say — and they were usually in trouble, because trouble was where the good ideas lived.

Their good ideas were comic books. Every week the boys drew a new issue of their greatest creation: **THE AMAZING CAPUCHIN UNDERPANTS**. Capuchin Underpants was a superhero who fought crime in nothing but a red cape and a pair of tighty-whities, because as everyone knows, capuchins look absolutely heroic in whitey-tighties. His battle cry was **"TRA-NA-NAAA!"** (That's banana for "Tra-la-la.")

The problem with Jerome Bonobowitz Elementary was its principal, Mr. Bonobo Krupp, a large gray-bottomed grump who hated fun, songs, comic books, and — George was pretty sure — bananas themselves, which for a bonobo was just unnatural.

The school's big football game against the Ripping Rhinos was coming, and thanks to George and Harold's cheerleading mishap (involving a banana-cream catapult and one accidentally airborne marching band), the Rhinos lost 77 to 0. Mr. Krupp was furious. But he had no proof.

No proof, that is, until the school's cheerleading camcorder mysteriously appeared on his desk. Mr. Krupp pressed PLAY, watched the entire banana-cream catastrophe, and grinned a slow, gray grin.

"Boys," he said, "unless you want this tape mailed to your parents, you're mine. You'll sweep the gym. Scrub the lockers. Peel the cafeteria bananas. ALL of them."

For a week, George and Harold peeled, scrubbed, swept, and suffered. "There's gotta be a way out," groaned Harold, massaging his poor peeling fingers.

"Wait," said George, holding up a cereal box. "The new Chimpan-Z's cereal! Free with ten box tops: a **3-D HYPNO-RING**!"

Ten boxes of cereal later (Harold's stomach would never forgive them), the ring arrived in the mail. It was plastic. It was glittery. It came with a warning pamphlet titled *Hypnosis and You*, which they immediately lost behind the sofa. What could possibly go wrong?

The next Saturday, at exactly 10 a.m., Mr. Krupp answered his door to find a delivery of one extra-large banana-split pizza and two delivery chimps who immediately pointed a sparkly ring at his face and chanted, "Ooga-booga-boo, our word is law, bend your brain to our bra-aw!"

Mr. Krupp's eyes went swirly. He swayed like kelp.

"He's under!" hissed Harold. "Quick, gimme the commands!" He cleared his throat. "When I snap my fingers, you will no longer be grumpy old Mr. Krupp. You will be CAPUCHIN UNDERPANTS!"

George giggled. "No! Harold! Not for real — "

*SNAP.*

Mr. Krupp tore off his tie. He tore off his shirt. He tore off his pants, revealing the whitest tighty-whities in the history of primates, tied his necktie around his neck as a cape, climbed onto the mailbox, flexed his bonobo chest, and bellowed:

**"TRA-NA-NAAAA!"**

And off he leapt, cape flapping, bounding down the street in his underwear toward downtown.

"We created a monster," said George.

"We created a HERO," said Harold. "Also a monster. Come on!"

Capuchin Underpants could not actually fly. This surprised him every single time. He landed in the fountain, sprang out undeterred, and galloped across a rooftop because a lady ape yelled "Stop, monkey!" and stopping was not in his vocabulary. That was when George and Harold, pedaling their banana-crate bikes, saw the tanks.

Two enormous robots the color of rotten bananas were rampaging down Main Street, and between them, in a hovering claw-machine bubble, cackled a villain: **DR. NAPPYRASH**, a mad scientist in a giant crinkled diaper and goggles.

"Fools!" announced Dr. Nappyrash through a megaphone. "Behold my WEDGIE-POWERED ROBOTS! Their engines run on pure wedgie energy — the strongest force in the monkeyverse! And with them I shall steal the moon! Yes, the moon — which shall henceforth be known as the GREAT BANANA IN THE SKY! MWAHAHA!"

"Great," muttered George. "The moon is a banana now."

"Actually that tracks," said Harold.

The robots stomped closer, and on each one's shoulder panel was a big red meter marked **WEDGIE POWER**. The robots were grabbing mailboxes, parking meters, and one extremely embarrassed gorilla in jogging shorts, and yanking upward to charge their batteries.

"Unhand that gorilla's undergarments!" cried Capuchin Underpants, landing heroically on a robot's fist. "You face the mightiest monkey in manties! TRA-NA-NAAA!"

The robot grabbed Capuchin Underpants by the cape and gave him an atomic wedgie so thorough that the meter shot to FULL.

"OW," said Capuchin Underpants, in a very small voice.

The gorilla fled, waddling. George looked at Harold, and Harold looked at George, and both chimps had the same terrible, wonderful idea.

"George," said Harold, "when you give a robot a wedgie — "

"— it powers the robot," George finished. "But when a MONKEY gives a wedgie —"

"— it's monkey business!"

It was time for the boys' secret weapon: **FLING-O-RAMA!** (The instructions say: hold the book, grab the corners, and flip the pages back and forth really fast — but you can't flip a battle, so they improvised.)

Harold went left. George went right. The robots turned to follow them, exposing their behinds, where — printed clearly on each robot's metal seat — was a tiny label: **WEDGIE INTAKE VALVE**.

"Geronim-OOH-OOH!" yelled George, swinging on a streetlamp like the chimp he was, landing on Robot One's shoulders, grabbing two fistfuls of metallic robot-briefs, and yanking with all his fourth-grade might. The robot's meter dropped to EMPTY with a sad little *blorp*, and it toppled over like a vending machine.

Harold, meanwhile, had climbed Robot Two and delivered a wedgie so righteous that its engine reversed, lifting the robot gently into the sky, over the zoo fence, and into the hippo pool. The hippos were not consulted.

"NOOO!" howled Dr. Nappyrash. "My beautiful babies! I'll diaper the lot of you!"

"Flip the page!" cried Capuchin Underpants, freed at last, and while George and Harold distracted the doctor by flinging themselves left and right — fling, o-rama, fling — Capuchin Underpants snatched the escaping villain by the diaper, hooked the waistband onto the school flagpole, and hoisted. Wedgie energy, it turns out, is completely wasted on flagpoles, but the image of Dr. Nappyrash dangling by his own crinkled britches was enough to make two robots' worth of difference.

"You haven't seen the last of me!" squeaked Dr. Nappyrash, spinning slowly.

"That's okay," said Harold. "Nobody's seen the last of anybody's underwear today."

The police took Dr. Nappyrash away (still in the flagpole's Wedgie of Honor), and the gorilla jogger shook all three heroes' hands. Then Capuchin Underpants climbed a fire escape, raised one fist to the moon-that-was-now-officially-a-banana, and hollered his cry.

"TRA-NA-NAAAA!"

And then he jumped off the fire escape, because he still, after all this, could not actually fly.

"WAH!" went Capuchin Underpants.

"Water!" yelled George. "Splash water on his head!"

Harold emptied a bucket of park fountain onto the falling bonobo, and instantly the swimmy-eyed trance broke. Mr. Krupp blinked. He was on a sidewalk. He was wearing a necktie, and shoes, and a cape, and absolutely nothing else that a principal should be wearing.

"GEORGE! HAROLD!" he roared, wrapping his cape around himself with as much dignity as a grumpy bonobo can manage. "Did you two hoodlums have anything to do with this?"

"Who, us?" said George.

"We've been peeling bananas all day," said Harold. "Ask anyone. Ask a banana."

Mr. Krupp narrowed his eyes and marched home to change, muttering about the indignity of it all.

Everything went back to normal — the tape was handed over, the peeling ended, the football team won their next game 1 to 0 on a technicality. Well, almost everything.

Because weeks later, on a field trip to the aquarium, Mr. Krupp got so bored by the jellyfish exhibit that a sea lion trainer's fingers, snapping to get her sea lion's attention, went *snap* — and Mr. Krupp ripped off his shirt, bellowed "TRA-NA-NAAA!", and plunged into the dolphin tank in his underwear to fight crime.

And George and Harold, watching their principal breaststroke past a sea turtle, looked at each other and sighed the sigh of true artists.

"We really should have read that pamphlet," they said.
