# The Tortoise and the Hair

*A parody of "The Tortoise and the Hare"*

The race was Henrietta's idea, because everything was Henrietta's idea.

Henrietta was a golden lion tamarin with the most magnificent hair in the entire Amazon — a cascading copper mane that caught the sun like a shampoo commercial. She was famous across the canopy simply as *the Hair*, and she never lost anything: not a branch sprint, not a swinging match, not an argument, and certainly not a hair.

The trouble started, as trouble does, at the fruit market in the kapok tree, where old Tortellini the Tortoise was buying figs. Tortellini was the slowest commuter in the jungle. He had been ascending the Great Fig Staircase since the last wet season and was, at present, two steps from the top.

"Out of the way, slowpoke!" called Henrietta, flowing past like a shampoo ad with legs. "Some of us have places to be and hair to be there with!"

"Plenty of path for everyone," said Tortellini mildly.

"Plenty of *path*." Henrietta sniffed. "Honestly, grandfather, at your pace you'll finish your errands by next monsoon. Why, I could beat you to the Fig Stash and back before you'd finish saying 'fig.'"

"Almost certainly," agreed Tortellini. "Say — why don't you?"

"Why don't I what?"

"Beat me to the Fig Stash. A race. You and I. High road through the canopy, or the low road along the branches — every monkey knows the low road goes past the burr bushes, of course, but nobody ever asks the tortoise. They just assume."

The market went quiet. A mandrill dropped his mango. By lunchtime there wasn't a monkey in the watershed who hadn't bet a banana on it. By evening the odds stood at ninety-nine bananas on the Hair to one lonely fig on the tortoise, and the one fig had been wagered by Tortellini himself. ("On yourself?" asked the bookmaker, a capuchin named Odds. "It's not a bet if it's a fact," said Tortellini.)

The Fan Club of the Fabulous Hair knitted copper armbands. A toucan set up as a commentator. Nothing at all was arranged for Tortellini, though a beetle who had known him for years did agree to walk beside him, and had to trot.

Judge Orang, a bored orangutan of enormous dignity, dropped a fig from the high branch. "On the fall of the fruit — the race to the Fig Stash begins!"

The fig hit the ground.

Henrietta was *gone* — a comet of copper — while Tortellini set off at his traditional pace, which was generously described as "ongoing."

Henrietta rocketed through the canopy, took the Hanging Vine Express, and hit the halfway mark with time to spare. So she spared it. First, at the Limpid Pool, she caught her reflection. "Hello, gorgeous. No — thank YOU." An hour of admiring was simple maintenance; anything less was neglect, and her mother had not raised a neglectful tamarin.

Then a quick grooming break — a Hair is only as fast as her shine. Then she wound her mane into a racing braid, unwound it (unflattering), and wound it again (perfect).

Then the Fan Club found her. They had sprinted out from the market to cheer her at the halfway mark, copper armbands and all, and she could hardly race past her own supporters without stopping — it would be *rude*, and an icon is never rude. She signed seventeen fig leaves with her tail, delivered a short speech ("Hair today, gone in a blur — that's the dream, ladies"), and posed with a baby squirrel monkey whose mother would frame the moment forever.

Then, spotting the Shade Salon — a lovely cool fern hammock — she flopped down beneath a blooming trumpet flower.

"A nap," she declared, "is essential for split ends. Ten minutes."

The trumpet flower, unfortunately, was a dormancy bloom, and its pollen was famous for turning ten minutes into the better part of an afternoon.

Meanwhile, Tortellini plodded. Past the pool, where the water politely waited. Past the salon, past the sleeping icon, past the Fan Club, who did not recognize him as a racer and tried to sell him an armband. Every monkey knows the low road goes past the burr bushes — but Tortellini was two steps from the top of the Great Fig Staircase, and a tortoise who has climbed all year does not stop for burrs. Or heat. Or mockery, of which there was plenty.

"SLOWPOKE!" hollered a spider monkey.

"Compared to a glacier," said Tortellini pleasantly, "I'm practically news."

"You're losing!" shouted the spider monkey.

"To whom?" said Tortellini, looking carefully up and down the empty low road. "All I see is my lane."

He reached the Fig Stash at sunset, touched the great fig pile with his nose, and turned around to plod back. And it was on the homeward leg that the watershed learned the second thing about Tortellini, the first being that he was slow: he did not gloat, he did not wave, and when a young monkey offered him a carried fig, he said, "Thank you — but then it wouldn't be mine when I crossed."

The flower's pollen wore off with the moonrise. Henrietta woke, bolted upright, and went absolutely bananas.

"The race! THE RACE!"

She flew. She blazed. She took the Vine Express *backwards*. Her fan club, who had assumed victory hours ago and gone home, were woken by the sonic boom of her passing. And she would have won — truly — except that in her furious flight she took the low road to shortcut the finish, and forgot the low road's famous feature, and the burr bushes reached out and grabbed that famous copper mane like a thousand tiny hairdressers with grievances.

"NOT THE HAIR! ANYTHING BUT THE — oh, come ON."

She freed herself one burr at a time, weeping for each, and appealed to a passing toucan ("Natural obstacle! I demand a ruling!"), who declined on the grounds that everyone knows the low road goes past the burr bushes, and nobody ever asks the toucan either.

By the time she'd picked her way free, redone the braid (perfect), and swept across the finish line, the market was already cheering — for Tortellini, standing calmly beside Judge Orang with a fig in his mouth and one final step taken at last. The beetle had fainted from trotting. The toucan commentator, overcome, ate his own microphone.

"Rematch!" panted Henrietta. "Best of three! My hair was SABOTAGED by botany!"

"Any time," said Tortellini. "I'll be here. Going... wasn't really the point."

Henrietta opened her mouth to argue — and stopped, because Tortellini had just quietly offered her his fig, the one he'd carried the whole way, on the grounds that she looked like she'd had a trying afternoon. Nobody had ever given the Hair anything but applause. She took the fig. It was, she admitted later, the finest thing she'd ever been handed.

Judge Orang raised the golden fig and spoke the words that have rung through the jungle ever since:

**"Slow and steady wins the fig stash."**

Henrietta demanded a rematch every season, and lost every single one — to naps, to burrs, to her own reflection, once memorably to a mirror she installed beside the track. But somewhere along the way she and Tortellini became the fastest of friends, and she could be seen most evenings at the top of the Great Fig Staircase, holding his fig while he took his last step, and listening — she, of all monkeys — to whatever the tortoise felt like saying, which was the actual prize.

Which just goes to show: the Hair lost the race, but she won a prime mate.
