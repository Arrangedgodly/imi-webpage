# The Fellowchimp of the Swing

*A parody of "The Fellowship of the Ring"*

When Mr. Bilbo Bananas of the Banana Shire announced fireworks by Grandalf the Greyback for his one-hundred-eleventh banana-day party, the Shire went daffy, then quiet — because everyone knows what a wizard is: a gorilla who knows things.

"A silverback is never late," Grandalf announced, arriving precisely when he meant to. "Neither is he early. He swings precisely when the story requires it."

At the party's height, Bilbo stood on the party tree, muttered something rude about farewells — and *vanished*. His secret was a small golden swing woven of a vine no shears could cut; sit in it, and you flickered out of sight. Grandalf found him home, packing.

"It's had *you* sixty years, Bilbo. You're thin, stretched, like butter scraped over too much bread. Leave it to Frodo."

"It's mine. My own. My preciousssss —" Bilbo blinked. "Why did I say it like *that*?"

Grandalf made him leave it on the mantel and warned Bilbo's heir, young Frodo Bananas — a pocket monkey of excellent appetite — with four words: "Keep it secret. Keep it safe."

Seventeen years later, Grandalf returned, flung the Swing into the hearth-fire, and watched fiery letters crawl across the vine:

**One Swing to rule them all, One Swing to find them, One Swing to bring them all, and in the treetops bind them.**

"The One Swing," said Grandalf grimly. "Forged by the Dark Lord Sauronape in the fires of Mount Zoom. It cannot be wielded — only unmade, in the fire it came from. Its maker is rising."

"There's been a creature in the garden," said Frodo. "Big eyes. Talks to himself."

"Gollum," sighed Grandalf. "Once a pocket monkey like you; the Swing groomed him hollow. Do not be too eager to deal out judgment — even the very wise cannot see all ends. He has a part to play yet."

Then Black Riders came snuffling up the lanes on dire peccaries, hissing "Sssswingbearer" at the hedgerows, and Frodo — with his gardener, Samwise the Sturdy, a macaque who could cook a fig eleven ways, plus Merry and Pippin, who claimed to be "navigating" — took the Swing on the road.

The shortcut through the Old Forest was Merry's idea, which told them everything. That wood was older than grudges and twice as touchy, and its touchiest resident was Old Man Willowow, a willow whose hobby was lulling travelers asleep and swallowing them into knotholes for later. Merry and Pippin vanished into the bark up to their chins before rescue came bouncing down the path: Tom Bananadillo, a brightly feathered tamarin whose hat had a feather, and whose feather had opinions.

"Old gray Willowow!" Tom sang. "Open up, you grumpy plank! These are MY guests for supper!"

The willow, grumbling, spat them out. "Don't thank me," said Tom, hoisting the little monkeys over the hills like grocery bags. "Thanking's a trap in these woods. Mind the barrow next — the Barrow-wight keeps travelers as coat ornaments."

Alas: barrow. Wight. The four woke frozen on a cold stone altar while a dim voice sorted treasure, and it was Frodo's stubbornness — and a well-timed barrow-spoon — that broke the spell. Pippin would claim the spoon for years.

At Bree they found the inn of Barliman Bananabur, whose memory was a colander. "Grandalf! Knew a Grandalf. Left a letter? Did I? I *meant* to post it." The letter, two months stale, warned of Black Riders and vouched for a ranger called Swinger. In the corner of the common room, a hooded figure the size of a small weather event had been nursing one nutmeg ale for four hours.

"You sang about your Swing in public, Mr. Underhill," said Aragorilla, pushing back his hood. "There are nine things in the world that would like to cut that song short. We leave at dawn."

"Weathertop will be safe," the ranger decided.

Weathertop was not safe. Nine shapes came out of the dark at moonrise, and their leader wore a crown on nothing at all. "The Shire's little pet," hissed the Witch-King of Angmarang, and drove a thorn-knife into Frodo's shoulder, cold as a debt.

"You bite KNEES?" cried Sam in disbelief, swinging his frying pan into empty cloak. "Nine terrors of the ancient world, and you bite KNEES?"

But the thorn had done its work. A splinter of it went walking toward Frodo's heart, and it was a near-run race to the Ford of Riverdell, where the river rose up in the shape of white horses and washed the Riders away. There Elorang the Elder looked at the Swing once and the horizon a long time.

"Bring forth the Council," he said.

The Council argued in the usual way. Dwarf lemurs proposed hiding the Swing. Elves proposed guarding it. Grandalf explained that it could not be wielded, only unmade, in the fire of Mount Zoom where its maker was re-forging himself. Then Borangutan of Gonana stood.

"One does not simply *swing* into Mount Zoom," he declared. "Its gates are guarded by more than orkutans. The air is a poisonous funk. Evilly disposed weather. We need a plan, a big monkey, and ideally an army—"

"So what do you propose?" said Elorang.

"...A fellowship," said Borangutan, more quietly.

"Bring forth the Fellowship of the Swing!" Elorang declared, and nine stepped forward.

Frodo, Swing-bearer. Sam, cook and constancy. Merry the Marmoset and Pippin the Pygmy ("We're coming too — we know where the figs are hidden"). Grandalf the Greyback. Aragorilla the ranger, called Swinger, heir of kings who sat forgotten in the pub corner. Legolass the leaf-monkey ("You have my tail."). Gimli the dwarf lemur ("And my axe! It's ceremonial! It's very small! It counts!"). And Borangutan, who wanted the Swing as a weapon, twice, loudly.

"Nine," said Elorang. "The Fellowchimp of the Swing."

They went over mountains, under them, and — after a blizzard and a moth the size of a barn — through them, into the Deep Burrows of Moria. There Pippin, out of pure Pippin, dropped a pebble down a well. *Ploonk,* said the well. A lifetime later: *ploonk.* "Fool of a Tookit," Grandalf muttered — and had barely finished when drums began in the deep. Actual drums. Then orkutans. Then far, far worse.

Past Balin's grave — *Here lies Balin, Lord of Moria. He should have knocked.* — they ran for the Bridge of Khazad-Doomp, and the terror caught them there: the Barrel-rog, a spirit of shadow and flame that had lived since the world's making in a very large barrel of monkeys, and broken loose.

"You cannot swing past me!" Grandalf boomed, planting his staff. "I am a servant of the Secret Fire, wielder of the flame of Anor! YOU! SHALL! NOT! SWING!"

The bridge cracked. The Barrel-rog fell — and its flaming liana-whip lashed up and dragged Grandalf down with it.

"SWING, YOU FOOLS!" he cried, and was gone.

The Fellowship emerged weeping into Lothlórangutang, the golden grove where leaf-monkeys weave light into the trees and grief is folded into song. There the Lady Galadrielle held up her mirror, and each of them saw the inside of his own heart held to a window. Frodo, desperate, offered her the Swing itself.

"In place of a Dark Lord you would set up a Queen of Bananas!" she said. "Beautiful and terrible as the dawn! All shall love me and despair!" She paused, and the light went out of her voice. "No. I pass that test. I will diminish, and remain Galadrielle. Frankly, the crown was itchy."

In the mirror Frodo saw a small grey shape crawling toward a dark door. "That's Gollum. Why is it always Gollum?"

"He has a part to play yet," said Galadrielle, "before the end."

Her gifts were small and absurd: lembananas, the waybread one bite of which fills a monkey for a day; a rope for Sam that unties itself when you mean it to, like a good friend; three hairs of her silver fur for Gimli, who guarded them like ingots; and for Frodo a little glass phial. "May it be a light to you in dark places, when all other lights go out. It's also banana-scented."

Then, at Amon Hen, the Hill of the Eye, everything broke at once. Frodo sat the high seat and felt the whole jungle swing toward him like a tide. Orkutans flooded the slopes. Borangutan, tempted one last time, seized a fistful of Frodo's vest — and let go, weeping. "I tried to take it! Go, Swing-bearer — run!" And he turned to stand alone between the orkutans and the little ones, horn of Gonana ringing, until three arrows brought him down.

"I would have followed you, my king," he told Aragorilla, who came too late. "To whatever tree. To the end."

Merry and Pippin were carried off; the others chased; and Frodo stood at the riverbank with the Swing in his pocket and the jungle's weight on his small shoulders.

"Go back, Sam," said Frodo gently.

"Ah," said Sam, wading in anyway. "No, I don't think I will. I made a promise, Mr. Frodo. 'Don't you leave him, Samwise the Sturdy.' And a Sturdy doesn't."

So they crossed together, two small figures in a fig-leaf boat, paddling toward Mount Zoom and the fire that ends the Swing — and though broken, the Fellowship's story had only begun swinging.

**Three swings for the troop-kings under the sky. Seven for the dwarf lemurs in their halls of stone. Nine for wild monkeys doomed to cry. One for the Dark Lord on his dark throne.**
