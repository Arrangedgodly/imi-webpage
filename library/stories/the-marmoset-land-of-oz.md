# The Marmoset Land of Oz

*A parody of "The Marvelous Land of Oz"*

In the far corner of the Marmoset Land of Ook lived a small, nimble-fingered boy marmoset named Tip, who served a crooked old witch, Mombi the Mangabey. She made him scrub the cooking pots, dust the bat guano, and fetch firewood from the spookiest part of the forest.

One afternoon Mombi went off to buy dried moths, and Tip got up to some serious monkey business. He whittled a body from a green branch, dressed it in a banana-leaf suit, and for a head he carved a great cheerful papaya, with a seed smile and everything. He was admiring his work when he remembered Mombi's precious jar: the Powder of Life.

"Just a pinch," said Tip. "For science."

He sprinkled it on his creation, who sneezed, blinked, and said, "Good afternoon! I feel remarkably fruity."

Tip named him Jack Papaya-Head — and then heard Mombi's footsteps on the path. "RUN!" he yelped. "She'll turn us both into garden statuary!"

So Jack jogged off on his twig legs, laughing like a barrel of monkeys, with Tip riding his shoulders. For the road, Tip emptied the jar over the Saw-Vine, a carved liana-horse who whinnied like a parrot with opinions — all but the very last pinch, which Tip kept in his pocket, because a boy who has lived with a witch trusts no jar to be truly empty.

"I never apologize," announced the Saw-Vine, standing up in a shower of shavings. "It slows me down."

"You'll be slowed plenty once I'm on you," said Tip, and they galloped to the Emerald Canopy — where who should be king but the Scarecrow, Dorothy's old friend, stuffed with brains and banana mash.

"You're the king?" said Tip.

"By accident," admitted the Scarecrow. "It happens more than you'd think in politics. The last fellow was a marmoset with a megaphone."

They had hardly sat down to tea when the gates banged open and in marched General Ginger, a tall young gorilla in a smart uniform, at the head of her Army of Revolt — the Guerrilla Girls, rank upon rank of girl monkeys armed to the eyebrows with grooming combs.

"We revolt!" announced Ginger, hopping onto the throne. "We are tired of doing all the grooming and getting none of the glory! We shall live in the palace and do nothing elegantly!"

"Is doing nothing elegantly hard?" Jack asked.

"Frightfully," said Ginger.

The palace guard surrendered on the spot — not one of them would risk a combing — and the Scarecrow, Tip, Jack, and the Saw-Vine slipped out the back gate, collected the Woggle-Primate — H.M., Highly Magnified; T.E., Thoroughly Educated; he had once swallowed an entire jar of Education Pills at a single sitting, which is why he knew everything except humility — and fled to the yellow castle of the Tin Chimp, who greeted them with his pincushion heart in his throat.

"It's a pickle," said the Tin Chimp. "Ginger holds the city. Mombi wants Jack and Tip for topiary. And none of us can fight girls with grooming combs; they're too accurate."

"We shall FLY over their heads," declared the Woggle-Primate. "Bring me two palm fronds, a hammock, and the stuffed head of a hornbill."

They lashed the lot together — the hornbill's head objected to mornings on principle — tipped the last pinch of Powder upon it, and named it the Flying Gump. It rose gloriously, wobbled, sneezed, and crashed into the biggest bird's-nest in Ook — but it *flew*, and that was the main thing. The Woggle-Primate declared it the finest aircraft in Ook, on the technicality that it was the only aircraft in Ook.

They patched its wings with nest-twine and flew straight back over the Emerald Canopy to see what they could see. What they saw was Ginger, waiting on the ramparts with her paws on her hips.

"Visitors!" she cried, delighted. "Comb them down to size!"

A flick of a well-aimed comb parted the Gump's wing like a bad haircut, and the whole crew spiraled into the palace orchard — all except Jack, who fell fifty feet onto a papaya tree and smashed his head to pulp. They hid till midnight, while Tip carved him a new head from the biggest papaya on the branch.

"A greener smile," said Jack, trying it on, "but I think a wiser one. I shall miss the old head. It knew so little."

Then, with combs whistling past their ears like angry grasshoppers, they escaped at last to Glinda the Good Gibbon, whose white fur was so bright the birds mistook her for moonlight.

"General Ginger's rebellion is a trifle," said Glinda. "Revolutions are like fleas — the biting is brief, and the scratching goes on for months. We shall be gentle. The true ruler of Ook was lost long ago, and it was the witch Mombi who hid her."

So Glinda marched on the Emerald Canopy with a quiet army. The Guerrilla Girls, it turned out, were exhausted from months of doing nothing elegantly, and surrendered comb-first, asking politely whether anyone's parting needed seeing to; Glinda gave them the afternoon off.

Mombi they caught in the throne room — or rather, they caught a rose orchid that smelled powerfully of mothballs and old spells. When Glinda bent to look at it, it became a vulture and flapped for the skylight; the Saw-Vine, who was faster than anything on legs and most things on wings, ran her down along the rafters and cornered her against the crown jewels, until there was nothing left for Mombi to be but Mombi.

"The baby Princess Mar-Mozma was entrusted to me," she sniffed. "I did not care for the responsibility. So I turned her into a boy marmoset."

Everyone looked at Tip.

"*Me?*" said Tip.

"You," said Glinda gently, "have been a girl princess all along, disguised by magic. The change is easily undone — if you consent."

Tip thought of the cooking pots and spooky firewood, and then of friends who had crossed the jungle for him — for *her* — without ever knowing what she was.

"Do it," said Tip.

Mombi muttered the counter-charm, and in a shimmer of gold stood a young girl tamarin with a little crown of fur about her brow.

"Princess Mar-Mozma," said Glinda, bowing.

"Blazes," said Jack Papaya-Head happily. "I've been carrying a princess on my shoulders this whole time. My second head will never believe it."

Mar-Mozma ruled the Emerald Canopy kindly ever after. On the day of her crowning she refused the grand throne and asked for a cushion beside it instead, so that Ginger might sit level with her; this was held to be extremely stylish, and started a fashion. Jack got a hammock of honour and fresh seeds whenever his smile went soft; the Saw-Vine became the Royal Steed and apologized to no one; the Flying Gump was pensioned with honours and retired to the museum rafters, where it told longer and longer stories about its flying days; the Scarecrow went back to thinking, the Tin Chimp to feeling, the Woggle-Primate to correcting royal proclamations and founding the Royal College, where Education Pills were henceforth handed out one at a time. Even General Ginger was made Keeper of the Royal Combs, for a troop runs best when every monkey grooms and every monkey is groomed.

And in the Land of Ook, they say, a scarecrow can be king, a papaya can be a gentleman, a girl gorilla can lead an army, and a boy marmoset can be a princess — because in Ook every monkey is somebody, and the best ruler is whoever swings out for the rest.
