# The Three Macaque-teers

*A parody of "The Three Musketeers"*

Young D'Artagnan of Baboonternagne arrived in Paris Jungle with a shaggy coat, a letter of introduction to the Musketeers, and a nose for trouble — the last one quite literally, since he'd already been fooled by a one-eyed macaque called Rochefort, who had pickpocketed the letter and called his fur "provincial."

D'Artagnan was so outraged that he challenged three separate apes to duels on the same afternoon, which even in Paris Jungle was considered showing off. The three apes, as it happened, were the most famous Musketeers in the King's Troop: Athos, Porthos, and Aramis — the legendary Macaque-teers, who drank at the headquarters of M. de Tréville and treated the Cardinal's Guards like a personal hygiene problem.

"A duel at the Bamboo Bastion at noon," said Athos grimly, grooming his whiskers with dignity.

"Another at two," said Porthos, flexing until his waistcoat strained.

"And a third at four," said Aramis, adjusting his spectacles. "I do hope you survive the first two. It would be terribly rude otherwise."

But when the Cardinal's Guards came crashing through the canopy to arrest the dueling apes, D'Artagnan drew his fruit-knife and fought beside the strangers instead. By the last thrown banana, the four were brothers-in-arms.

"Welcome to the Troop," Athos declared, clapping him on the back hard enough to dislodge a family of ticks. "From this day forward, our motto is yours: *All for one, and bananas for all!*"

The trouble began with diamonds — or rather, with studs. The Queen, a lovely bonobo named Anne, had made the terrible mistake of giving her diamond banana-studs to the Duke of Buckinhamshire, an English orang of boundless charm and boundless ego. The Cardinal Richelieu — a crimson-caped baboon whose eyebrows alone could plot a conspiracy — knew it, and had arranged for the King to request the studs at the Chimpmas Ball.

"If the Queen cannot produce them," purred the Cardinal's spy, Milady de Winter, a beautiful but dastardly capuchin with a fleur-de-lice brand hidden beneath her fur, "she will be disgraced."

"There is one ape who can save her," said the Queen's seamstress, Constance, whose eyes met D'Artagnan's and stayed there a beat too long. "You. You must go to London and bring back the studs."

"Madame," said D'Artagnan, hand on heart, "I would swim the channel for her Majesty. On a floating log. Against the tide."

His three friends came with him — for about forty leagues, until the Cardinal's agents ambushed them one by one. Athos was left trapped in a wine cellar with a broken ankle. Porthos was challenged in a tavern and dueled a fake friar. Aramis caught a flea in the shoulder. In the end, D'Artagnan rode into London alone, discovered that Milady had already stolen two of the studs, and found the Duke re-forging the pair in his own forge — because when one is the richest orang in England, one simply *does*.

At the Chimpmas Ball, the Queen wore her studs, the Cardinal choked on his punch, and the King's Troop swung from chandelier to chandelier in celebration. The Queen, in gratitude, sent D'Artagnan a ring with a heart-shaped diamond; the Cardinal, in frustration, sent him a spy. Both were equally expensive.

But Milady was not finished, and the war moved to La Rochelle, where the Cardinal had the whole troop besieging a rebellious city and his own agents besieging everyone's reputations. Constance was abducted by the Cardinal's men and hidden in a convent — which the Cardinal considered a prison and Milady considered a target. Rochefort, the one-eyed macaque of the stolen letter, crossed D'Artagnan's path twice more during the siege, and by the third crossing they had run out of bad feeling and simply nodded across the parapets like professionals.

It was there, in a tavern at the siege, that the four friends made the most beautiful wager of the war. Challenged by the Cardinal's Guards to leave the bastion they'd claimed for a breakfast spot, Athos proposed instead that the guards come back in an hour — "We are holding the Bastion Saint-Gervais until we have finished our breakfast. We breakfast seriously in this troop." For one golden hour, over jam and confiscated nectar, the four apes planned Milady's downfall between repelling assaults, and walked out having lost nothing but Aramis's hat and their appetites for mercy.

Because they had, each in turn, a bill to settle with Milady. She was the most dangerous monkey in the whole story — a spy who had poisoned a convent full of nuns, taken bribes from half the crowns of Europe, tried to murder D'Artagnan with a tainted banana, and betrayed her first husband — who, it turned out, was Athos himself, a secret revealed at exactly the right dramatic moment on exactly the right stormy night: he had found her brand, hanged her from a branch, and left her for dead, and the jungle had neglected to keep her.

"She has gone beyond monkey business," said Athos, whose voice went cold whenever his past was in the room. "This is gorilla warfare."

Before they could close the net, Milady committed her masterpiece: she found a puritan guard named Felton, unspotted and devout, and over eleven days of whispered martyrdom convinced him she was a persecuted saint. Felton assassinated the Duke of Buckinhamshire with a fruit-knife on the duke's own stair — then walked to the gallows bewildered, still believing her innocent. The Cardinal, watching from France, wrote Milady a blank commission — full pardon, the field left empty — which was as close as that baboon ever came to applause.

The Macaque-teers declared war. They tracked her to a convent in Béarn — the same convent where Constance was hidden — and arrived one hour too late for two things at once: Milady had fled, and she had poisoned Constance on the way out. D'Artagnan held his beloved while the nectar did its work, and she smiled at him and died asking him to forgive her for leaving first.

"She has gone beyond gorilla warfare," said Athos quietly. "This is annihilation."

They captured her at last in a safehouse near the border, and put her on trial before a shadowy tribunal of apes in red cloaks, with the executioner of Lille — whose brother she had also ruined; she had gotten around — standing witness in his hood.

"You are condemned for your crimes," Athos intoned. "The sentence is exile from every troop in France. Never again shall another monkey groom you, feed you, or swing beside you. For a monkey, there is no worse fate."

And indeed, there was not. Milady vanished into the wilderness, friendless and un-groomed, the most feared solitary ape in Europe.

The Cardinal, meanwhile, respected D'Artagnan so much for outsmarting him that he wrote him a commission: *Lieutenant of Musketeers*. D'Artagnan offered it first to each of his three friends, and each refused with ceremony, leaving it to him.

"To the Troop!" cried D'Artagnan, hoisting a banana-cream pastry in salute.

"To the Troop!" roared the Macaque-teers. "All for one —"

"— and bananas for all!" they bellowed, and the whole tavern of Musketeers went completely bananas, swinging from the rafters, throwing peel confetti, and drinking the health of the four bravest, silliest, most inseparable apes in the entire Paris Jungle.
