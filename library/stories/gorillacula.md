# Gorillacula

*A parody of "Dracula"*

**From the journal of Jonathan Harkerfield, a young solicitor of the legal firm Hawkins & Harkerfield.**

3 May. Left Munich at 8:35 P.M., having finished all my banana business early. I was bound for the Carpawthian Mountains, where a certain Count Gorillacula was purchasing an estate in Purfleet — Carfax Abbey, as dull a property as ever a monkey signed for. The locals at the inn turned pale when I named my destination, pressed a clove of garlic and a silver grooming comb into my paws, and crossed their thumbs at me.

"Go not to the Castle," said the landlady baboon. "The howlers come out at night."

The howlers came out at night. My calèche climbed the pass with a driver who was — I now know — the Count himself, howlers circling the carriage, singing their terrible song. I arrived at the great ruined castle alive, which I was later told was the surprising part.

The Count stood in the doorway: an old gorilla, immense, silver-backed, fur gone white at the temples, with a grip that could crack a coconut at forty paces.

"I bid you welcom-eek, Mr. Harkerfield," he said. "Enter freely and of your own will." He made as if to shake my hand, and instead groomed my shoulder briskly, which I found forward, but he is nobility.

He served me supper though he ate nothing himself — "I have dined already, on a liquid diet" — and we spoke late of the purchase. As the clock struck midnight he paused, listening to the howling in the valley below.

"Listen to them," he said, with something terribly like tenderness. "The children of the canopy. What music they make!"

"You know the song?" I asked.

"Better than that, Mr. Harkerfield. I know the singers."

**Later.** I have seen much that a sensible monkey should not write down. The Count casts no reflection in my shaving mirror — he stood behind me this morning and the glass showed only the wall. When I nicked myself with the razor, his eyes flared banana-yellow and he seized my wrist — but the silver comb in my pocket seemed to scorch him, and he sprang back, apologizing with courtly grace.

I have met the three spider monkey sisters, who drifted down the library wall head-first like autumn leaves, laughing softly: "You too, young one! You are ours!" The Count swept in like a storm front and pulled them off with one vast arm. "Back! This one belongs to me! Not yet. When I am done with his paperwork, you may have what is left."

I confess the paperwork was in excellent order. It is the only compliment I can pay this place. I once tried climbing down the castle wall to escape and found the Count below me, descending the sheer stone face-first, knuckles and toes, like a fly down a windowpane. I resolved thereafter to be very, very polite.

**From the log of the S.S. Demeter, of Varna, carrying cargo: fifty boxes of sacred jungle soil.**

The mate has vanished. The second mate has vanished. The Captain's pet capuchin, who has sailed with him twenty years, was found hiding in the lifeboat, and could only say "eek" over and over. Something large moves among the boxes at night. Last night the first officer saw it: a great grey shape on the deck, sitting on a crate, eating — the word he used was "sipping" — the moonlight. Tonight the Captain has lashed himself to the wheel with a rosary and a prayer. If this reaches any monkey's eye, let the truth be known: we are carrying Death, and Death has a silver back.

*(The Demeter ran aground at Whitbranch during a sudden storm. Witnesses on the cliff saw an enormous gorilla leap from the bow to the rocks — "like a gorilla," said the harbourmaster, "only more so" — and vanish up the cliff path toward the local estate, which had recently and inexplicably been sold.)*

**Dr. Seward's phonograph diary.** (Dr. Seward: a mandrill of science, keeper of a private asylum adjacent to Carfax Abbey.)

Lucy Gibbonwood is failing. She was the loveliest gibbon in the county — betrothed to young Lord Goldentamarin — and now her fur has gone pale as milk, and on her throat are two tiny punctures, like the marks of an over-curious mosquito. I have transfused blood into her from her fiancé, from the American, Mr. Quincey P. Morris the capuchin, from myself. It does no lasting good. I have wired for my old teacher, Professor Abraham van Helsing, of Amsterdam.

Van Helsing arrived with a suitcase of garlic blossoms, a quantity of silver grooming combs, and the manner of an old orangutan who has seen everything twice and taken notes both times.

"You have done well, friend John," he said, hanging garlic round Lucy's room like bunting. "Now, attend: she is losing blood, and no wound. Where does the blood go? To whom? There is a monkey in this county who is not monkey only."

"You mean —"

"I mean," said Van Helsing, "that we must think very carefully about daytime naps. Also, if you see a bat the size of a picnic blanket, do not assume it is a bat."

We did not watch carefully enough. The picnic-blanket bat came at night when the nurse dozed. Lucy grew worse; then Lucy died; and then — worse than dying — Lucy *did not stop*. Children of the district began to be found at dawn on the Heath, dazed and happy, each clutching a banana and murmuring about the nice grey lady. The papers called her the Nanafer Lady.

Lucy's mother will not hear of it, but Van Helsing took Arthur, Quincey, Seward and me — I am back, it is Jonathan; Mina has since married me and types everything, hence the tidiness — to the tomb.

"First," said Van Helsing, "the heart." He held out to Arthur a stake of dense, dark banana-wood, worn smooth as a bannister.

Arthur, weeping, pressed it gently to Lucy's breast. One soft tap, as if knocking on a door to say sorry. Lucy sighed — her fur settled, her face unbent into sleep, and she looked like herself again, poor dear, done at last with moonlight.

"Now the garlic," said Van Helsing, "and we speak of this to no one, ever, over tea."

**Mina Harkerfield's journal.** It is done, and I am the proof it needed doing. While the troop guarded Lucy's tomb, the Count came to me at night — mist under the door, mist with shoulders — and held his own wrist to a thorn. "You will be fellowship to me," he said, and before I could turn away he had pressed into my mouth a black banana, ripe past sweetness into midnight, and made me take one bite. "You are now flock to me," he said. "Flesh of my flesh. First, a taste; then, when I return, the feast."

The mark on my forehead burns like a fresh sunburn, and at dawn and dusk I can hear him, as if the wind were a telephone.

But mark this, Count: my husband is a solicitor, my friend a doctor, my friends a Texan with a bowie knife and a professor who has never once lost an argument. You have made a serious administrative error, and we are coming to correct it.

**The chase.** We traced his fifty boxes of jungle soil to Carfax, to Piccadilly, to a house in Mile End, and into each we pressed a chip of dried banana — the Host of the troop, if you like — sterilizing every nest forever. Through my dawn-trance we tracked him fleeing up the river, and by train, and by horse, Van Helsing crying "We go gorilla on this gorilla, my friends!"

Quincey galloped ahead and bribed the boatmen to give the Count's cart a wrong turn. We caught the cart in the pass at sundown — the box on it, lid nailed, the Count inside, furious, audible. Quincey slashed the ropes, Arthur's kukri pried the lid, and the last light of day fell on the old gorilla's face.

He was terrible and he was old, and the look of hate in his eyes turned, at the very last, to something almost like triumph. Then he folded — quietly, like a circus tent at the end of the season — into a heap of grey dust, two banana peels, and one tall hat.

Quincey took a scratch across the arm from the nails in the lid — nothing, he said, though Mina bandaged it with shaking paws. He has since retired to Texas to grow bananas commercially, and writes annually.

**Mina's journal, last entry.** The scar on my forehead is fading as I type. Jonathan sleeps with his mouth open like a good chimp. Van Helsing has taken the garlic home to Amsterdam "for the cooking, friend Mina, for the cooking only."

We stood on the cliff this morning, the whole troop of us, and watched the sun come up over the valley where the howlers sing. God be merciful to the old monkey in the box. He wanted only what we all want — to never ripen, never fall. But everything falls, in the end. That is not the curse.

That is the crop.
