# The Silver Hair

*A parody of "The Silver Chair"*

Jill Pole was crying behind the gym because a gang of bullies had been throwing things at her best friend Eustink Scrub, when Eustink said the forbidden words: "Let's try the monkey bars. The magic ones."

The magic ones were behind the wall, of course. One moment they were in the Experiment Enclosure; the next, a warm wind was carrying them up through leaves, into a sky the color of the inside of a banana, where the Great Silverback Aslan waited.

"You are sent to find the lost Prince Rilian," said Aslan, "son of old King Capuchin, who vanished chasing a great green serpent that killed his mother. I will give you four signs. First: Eustink, as soon as you arrive, you shall meet an old and dear friend, and you must greet him at once. Second: you must journey out of Narnia, north, into giant country. Third: you shall find a ruined city, and on a stone, writing; you must do what the writing says. Fourth: the first who asks you to do something in my name is the lost prince."

"Sorry," said Jill. "You said north?"

"Do well to remember the signs," said Aslan, and breathed them off the edge of the sky.

They landed in Narnia in time to watch ancient, heartbroken King Capuchin sail away east — and it was there that Eustink walked straight past the old friend. He was an owl monkey named Glimfeather, confusingly an owl monkey who was also an owl monkey, and he had to chase them, hooting, for a quarter mile before the greeting got done.

"Thing is," said Eustink, "I forgot the first sign before we landed."

"That's fine," said Jill. "There are three left."

There were not, in practice, three left. Glimfeather carried them that night to the Parliament of Owls, which was mostly committees, and a motion was passed (four to two, one abstention, wet) to send them to the marshes and a guide: Puddleglum, a tall, damp, deeply gloomy proboscis monkey with an enormous nose and enormous feet.

"If you ask me — and nobody ever does — we're doomed," he said cheerfully. "Still. Mind the puddle."

The journey north went about as well as expected. In the ruins of a city of ancient giants, with Harfang's warm smoke on the horizon, they found huge carved letters on a cracked flagstone: **UNDER ME.**

"Sign three," said Puddleglum, standing on the letters. "Do what the writing says."

"It says *under me*," said Jill. "Under a giant city. On purpose."

"I don't care for it," said Puddleglum, and started prying up the stone.

That would have been the moment to obey the sign. Instead, a beautiful lady in green silk came riding by, all kindness, suggesting that "under me" was merely how giants said "welcome," and that the ruined city had been abandoned since the giants quarreled over a recipe. She pointed them to Harfang. By the time they realized what she was, they were inside the gates of Harfang, city of gorillas, so delighted by the warm fires and heaped fruit and gigantic fluffy blankets that they ignored the sign right over the gate: *DO NOT ENTER, SMALL TASTY MONKEYS.*

At midnight, exploring, they found the gorillas' great stone cookbook, open to the housekeeper's favorite page. It began: *MONKEY: best caught young, hang by the tail overnight, bake in a golden crust with bananas.*

"Do you know," said Eustink, voice wobbling, "I don't think they liked us. I think they liked us *with gravy.*"

They fled through a crack in the hill on feast morning — Puddleglum muttering, "Doomed, you see; told you; try to look surprised" — and fell for an unconscionable long time, and landed in Underland: a cold country of lanterns and little grey miners, where the beautiful lady in green greeted them kindly, and a hollow-eyed young langur sat staring at nothing.

"That is Prince Rilian," said the lady, sweet as overripe fruit. "He has a difficulty. At night he must be bound to a chair, for he becomes quite mad. Whatever he says then — do not release him."

The chair was woven of a single silver hair, fine and strong as cable. When they bound him, Rilian transformed — howling that the chair was his enemy, that the witch was his destroyer, that he was himself only in the black hour. Then, for a moment, he went perfectly quiet.

"In the name of Aslan," he said, "let me go."

Jill and Eustink hesitated. Puddleglum had already noticed that "do not release him" and "asks in Aslan's name" did not fit together like honest bananas. "It's the fourth sign," he said grimly. "The first who asks in His name. Do the sign, not the snake."

They cut the silver hair, and Rilian rose, seized his tail-sword, and chopped the chair to flinders.

"Ten years of nights," he breathed.

The green lady smiled no longer. Her lovely voice turned soft and reasonable, which is the worst kind of weapon. Overland? Narnia? Aslan? Childhood tales, she crooned. Sunlight, manes, silverbacks — dreams a monkey has in the upper world, no more real than lantern light. Be sensible. Stay. Her incense burned green on the little fire, and the room began to feel like the whole world.

"Suppose we have only dreamed him," said Puddleglum slowly, standing up. "Suppose the Great Silverback is a game four babies made up above the canopy. Then I'm playing it anyway. Because this world of yours is dingy, your blankets are suspicious, and the gravy — think long and hard about the gravy. A world where gorillas eat monkeys may be sensible, but it isn't worth being sensible *about*. I'm on the Silverback's side, even if there isn't any Silverback. Which there is." And he stamped out her fire with both enormous feet.

The witch's shape rippled; scales slid beneath silk; and she was a serpent the length of a fallen tree — the serpent that had killed Rilian's mother, wearing a different dress. While Rilian and the children fought her, Puddleglum grabbed her behind the head with both hands and announced, "I say, this is going to go badly," and hung on anyway. Between the four of them, the witch was finished.

The little grey miners — marmosets, freed — laughed for the first time in years and did handsprings, shouting "topsies for turves!" and pleading with everyone to come down to Bism, their real home, "where it's warm and the gems grow like figs." They declined politely. The earthquake declined on their behalf.

They carried Rilian up and over the world's edge to Cair Paravel, just in time for old King Capuchin to see his son and die happy, which in Narnia is not the tragedy it is elsewhere.

Through a door in the sky, Jill and Eustink glimpsed the old king young again, laughing in Aslan's country — and then Aslan breathed on them and sent them home, bigger and braver. The Great Silverback leaned through the gym wall of Experiment Enclosure, roared once, and the bullies left at a dead sprint, a mystery the headmaster declined to explain in the newsletter.

"Do you suppose," said Eustink, "that we're heroes now?"

"Probably not," said Jill, glowing. "But we've been somewhere that counted. And Puddleglum would say —"

"'We're doomed,'" they said together, and laughed, and went in to tea.
