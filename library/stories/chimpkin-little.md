# Chimpkin Little

*A parody of "Chicken Little"*

Chimpkin Little was the youngest chimp in the whole fig forest, with the biggest ears and the biggest imagination, which is a dangerous combination. Every rustle was a python. Every python was an anaconda. Every anaconda was, on at least one memorable afternoon, "a dragon with a ladder." The troop loved her dearly and had learned to take her alarms with a fig.

One warm afternoon she sat beneath Old Figbert, the tallest fig tree in the jungle, daydreaming about her future grooming duties. In the daydream she was famous. In the daydream her alarm call had its own echo.

That was when the fig fell.

BONK! It caught her square on the head, and Chimpkin Little leapt three feet into the air and let out a screech that could strip bark at forty paces.

"The canopy is falling!" she hollered. "The sky is coming down! I must tell King Orang the Elder at once!"

And off she dashed, still holding her head, with one small detail quite forgotten: the fig itself, mashed gently into her head-fur like a little green beret.

She hadn't gone far before she met Baboonie Toonie, lounging on a rock, admiring his reflection in a puddle.

"Where are you off to in such a flap?"

"Oh, Baboonie Toonie! The canopy is falling! I must tell the King!"

"Then I'll come too!" said Baboonie Toonie, who never missed a chance to be part of anything, and who checked his reflection once more in case it missed him.

Next they met Mongoosy Goosy, darting through the undergrowth. "The canopy is falling! We must tell the King!"

"Is it falling HERE," asked Mongoosy Goosy, "or somewhere in particular?"

"Everywhere! On everyone! The WHOLE SKY!"

"Then I'll come too," said Mongoosy Goosy. "I look excellent running."

Soon Turaco Luraco joined the parade in mid-air, without slowing down, because news in a jungle travels faster than a troop of screaming macaques — and then came Marmosetty Setty the marmoset, who could not run the whole way on the ground and so rode on Baboonie Toonie's shoulders, narrating. By the tangle-vines at the heart of the forest they were a regular stampede of panic, hollering "THE CANOPY IS FALLING!" at the tops of their considerable lungs, and every creature who heard them either joined the parade or hid under a leaf, and several did both, which takes talent.

There, on a smooth gray boulder, they found Jaggy Waggy the jaguar, stretched out like a very large, very polite rug.

"My, my," said Jaggy Waggy, in a voice like warm honey poured over gravel. "Whatever is the matter, my dears?"

"The canopy is falling! We must tell King Orang the Elder!"

"Goodness," said Jaggy Waggy, sitting up and pressing a paw to his chest. "The SKY, falling. Catastrophe. You must absolutely tell the King this instant. But — tragedy! — you've just missed him. The whole royal court migrated this morning. Lucky for you, they've gathered somewhere safe for the duration." He smiled. "My den."

"Your den?" said Mongoosy Goosy, slowing down.

"It's just through this cozy little cave," said Jaggy Waggy. "Roomy. Dry. Faintly aromatic. I've even set out fig-leaf napkins — I'm a very modern jaguar. Come along, walk single file, mind the teeth — DOOR, I mean. Mind the door."

And he opened his jaws in the friendliest smile any of them had ever seen, which should have been their first clue, because jaguars do not have friendly smiles. They have smiles with counters.

"Um," said Chimpkin Little, pausing at the mouth of the den, where it was very dark and smelled faintly of dinner. And as she paused, she caught her own reflection in a still puddle — and there, still riding proudly on her head, was the mashed green fig.

Chimpkin Little froze. She lifted the fig out of her fur. She looked at it. She looked at the sky, which was extremely not falling. She looked at the fig again. She interrogated it, quietly.

"Where did you come from?"

The fig said nothing, but it was green and whole, and it smelled of Old Figbert, and everyone knows figs come from fig trees and not, as a rule, from the sky in general.

"Wait just a monkey moment," she said slowly. "The sky didn't fall. A FIG fell. Off of Old Figbert. Onto my noggin. And I have been carrying the evidence on my head like a HAT."

The parade skidded to a halt. Baboonie Toonie's jaw dropped. Mongoosy Goosy's fur stood on end. Marmosetty Setty stopped narrating for the first time in recorded history.

"We've been going completely bananas," whispered Mongoosy Goosy, "over a piece of fruit?"

"A SNACK," said Turaco Luraco, who was bitingly honest in the mornings.

"FRUIT IN MY DEN!" howled Jaggy Waggy, who had been counting place settings — one little chimp, one baboon, one mongoose, one turaco, one marmoset, a lovely five — and did not appreciate the change of menu from five courses to zero. He lunged.

But Chimpkin Little had learned something important that afternoon, which is that a fig is not just a bonk on the head — it's a resource. She turned and crammed the mashed fig straight up the jaguar's nose.

"ACHOO!" went Jaggy Waggy, with a sneeze that shook the tangle-vines, and the whole troop of them shot out of that cave like a barrel of monkeys fired from a fig-fueled cannon.

They ran all the way to King Orang the Elder. King Orang was extremely old and extremely dignified, and he had been woken from his afternoon sitting, which he took very seriously, and the whole bedraggled parade was shouted at him at once, by everyone, in five voices: the bonk, the parade, the den, the napkins, the fig, the sneeze.

"ONE at a time," rumbled King Orang, and they all turned to Chimpkin Little, which was itself a promotion of sorts.

So she told it properly — from the beginning, with the fig held up as Exhibit A — while the King listened and examined the evidence (or what remained of it) with great care, turning it over twice and sniffing it once.

"The sky," he announced at last, "is exactly where we left it. I have checked personally; I had the court look up all morning as a precaution. But figs," he added, splitting it into five shares, "are falling, and very fine ones too. Chimpkin, you have learned the two great lessons of the jungle: first, check your own head-fur before you check the horizon. And second — never trust anybody whose den has a napkin drawer."

He appointed her Chief Alarm Verifier on the spot, a new office, at a banana a week. Her duty: every alarm in the troop now passed through her ears and her fig-check before the troop passed through the tangle-vines. In her first season she verified two genuine leopard alarms, one flood, and forty-one caterpillars that had merely looked ambitious.

And Chimpkin Little never screeched an alarm again without checking her hair first — and the whole fig forest slept noticeably better, except for Jaggy Waggy, who never entirely got the fig out, and sneezed in green for a month.
