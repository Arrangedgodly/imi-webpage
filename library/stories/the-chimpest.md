# The Chimpest

*A parody of "The Tempest"*

A storm cracked the sky over the island like a coconut under a stone. In the rigging of the king's ship, sailors screeched; in the waves, something that looked like fire leapt and danced.

"Boatswain!" yelled Gonzalo, an old monkey clinging to the mast. "Be patient! Remember you're a primate!"

"Primate?" roared the boatswain over the wind. "I'll drown everybody for a banana!"

On the shore, Miranda — the loveliest young chimp ever raised on figs — gripped her father's arm. "O, the horror! If you have power, end this storm! That ship holds souls — I think I see them dying!"

"Be collected," said her father Prospero, an old orangutan with a furrowed brow and a fig-wood staff, "and tell me what you see beyond that wreck."

"Nothing but darkness and the spray."

"Then look with your heart. The crew is safe — not a whisker wet beyond damp. I raised this tempest myself, with a word and a wave of this staff. My daughter, it is time you knew your story. Twelve years since, I was Duke of Milan — until my own brother Antonio, with the King of Naples' help, set me and my books adrift in a rotten fig-shell of a boat, with you, then a squeaking infant. The good lord Gonzalo secretly stocked us with food, water, fresh fur-oil, and volumes from my library — for which my library forgives him everything."

Then Ariel, the airy servant — a white-handed gibbon who moved like a rumor — swung down out of thin air. "All hail, great master! I boarded the king's ship; the mariners lie snoring, safely stowed; and the prince's son, Ferdinand, I have scattered alone on the far shore, hair up, tears down."

"Splendid," said Prospero. "Freedom is closer than your next meal."

Ariel vanished in a shimmer. From the rocks something grumbled and hauled itself upright: Caliban, a heavy-browed, permanently resentful chimp, son of the witch Sycorax.

"There's wood enough within," said Prospero coldly.

"I needs must, since you keep me to it," Caliban muttered. "This island's mine, by Sycorax my mother, which you take from me! You taught me words — 'banana' and 'groom' — and I profit by knowing how to curse. May red-tailed wasps sting your breakfast!"

"Go to it," said Prospero. Caliban went, muttering, "Freedom, hey-day!"

Ariel, meanwhile, floated invisibly over the grieving prince, playing a song on fig-flute and thunder-drum:

"Full fathom five thy father lies;
Of his bones are coral made;
Those are pearls that were his eyes;
Nothing of him that doth fade,
But doth suffer a sea-change
Into something rich and strange.
Sea-nymphs hourly ring his knell:
Ding-dong. Hark! Now I hear them — ding-dong, bell."

"Where should this music be?" wondered Ferdinand, who had been wandering the beach drumming his grief on a hollow log. "I' the air or the earth? It waits upon some god o' the island — and since it mentions my father, I shall follow it." He did, and it led him straight to Miranda — and both stopped as if struck by the same falling branch.

"I might call him a thing divine," breathed Miranda, "for nothing natural I ever saw so noble."

"Most sure, the goddess of the island!" stammered Ferdinand.

"O brave new canopy," said Miranda softly, "that has such monkeys in't!"

"'Tis new to thee," said Prospero aside, and set about testing the boy by making him stack coconuts — "poor worm, thou think'st me cruel" — while Miranda helped whenever her father wasn't looking.

Elsewhere on the island, the king's party wandered lost and grieving. Old King Alonso sat in the ferns refusing figs, while Gonzalo tried hope in three flavors. But Alonso's brother Sebastian and Prospero's brother Antonio — a matched pair of treachery, one wicked, one wicked and inventive — whispered under a sleeping-spell Ariel had cast.

"Will you be king of Naples?" breathed Antonio. "One stroke — there, the sleeping king — and who inherits? Claribel, ten thousand leagues away, and queens rarely commute."

"But if we're caught—"

"Blame the island," said Antonio. "The excuse comes built in."

They drew their fig-wood daggers — and Ariel woke Gonzalo with a shriek in his ear. "Bless us! Thunder!" cried Gonzalo, and the two conspirators straightened up innocently, sheathing their daggers slowly, like monkeys caught reaching into the fig-jar.

Then a banquet appeared before the court, borne by spirits, glittering — and as the starved courtiers reached for it, Ariel swooped down as a harpy, wings of vine and beetle-shell, clapping the table so the figs leapt off and vanished in smoke.

"You are three men of sin," pronounced the harpy, "whom destiny — the sea, the rocks, and frankly your own sailoring — hath brought to this island. For you, most wicked sir, whom to call brother would infect my mouth, the powers have prepared a slow stone in the deep. But if you would lift this charm: lead a clear life henceforth, and make your hearts sorrow."

"O, it is monstrous, monstrous!" cried Alonso, beating his chest. "The billows spoke; the winds sang it; the thunder pronounced the name of Prosper! Ferdinand is drowned — and I drowned with him." He ran off weeping, followed by the others, each haunted by his own conscience, which is the finest storm Prospero ever raised.

"Bravely done, spirit," said Prospero. "My high charms work in them still, and gently. Go — release the butler-apes and their monster; it's time for the farce to sink."

Farce it was. Trinculo, the king's jester, and Stephano, a drunk butler-monkey waving a bottle, had washed ashore, and Caliban had decided Stephano was a god. "I'll show thee the best springs! I'll pluck thee berries! I'll get thee young monkeys from the rocks!"

"And I'll believe every word," said Stephano, drinking. They plotted to seize Prospero's staff — "Remember first to possess his books," warned Caliban wisely — and make Miranda their queen. "Freedom, hey-day!"

Ariel foiled them with goblin music, leading them round and round through thorn bushes until the whole conspiracy dissolved in a frog-pond, smelling of lilies and regret.

"They're red-hot with drinking, master," Ariel reported, "and set upon your cell with terrible courage. Your charm so strongly works 'em that, if you now beheld them, your affections would become tender."

"Dost thou think so, spirit?"

"Mine would, sir, were I human."

"And mine shall be," said Prospero. "Prepare the masque."

For Ferdinand and Miranda, he conjured a masque: Iris on a rainbow of orchids, Juno in a bower of vines, Ceres in a gown of fig-leaves, blessing the young couple while figs sprang from thin air into their laps — until Prospero remembered the conspiracy against his life, started, and the whole vision wavered like heat off a stone.

"Our revels now are ended," he said, as the goddesses melted into bright air. "These our actors, as I foretold you, were all spirits, and are dissolved. The fig-topped towers, the gorgeous canopy itself, shall dissolve, and leave not a rack of peel behind. We are such stuff as bananas are made on."

At last he gathered everyone — the guilty old king, the treacherous brothers, the good Gonzalo — and drew back a curtain of vines to reveal Ferdinand and Miranda playing fig-chess and flirting outrageously.

"Sir, we are your flesh and blood," said Ferdinand, kneeling, "here thought lost forever — but found, united, and troth-plighted."

The king wept. Even Antonio grunted something generous. And Prospero, moved, spoke: "The rarer action is in virtue than in vengeance. Though they abused me to my hurt, I'll kindle no more storms."

He buried his staff deep in the sand and drowned his book of spells in the deepest tidepool. Then he set Ariel free.

"Where the fig hangs, there swing I; where the sweet breezes run through the leaves, there sing I! On the bat's back I do fly, merrily, merrily — free!" And Ariel was gone, a laugh left hanging in the air.

Then everyone sailed for Naples, leaving one grumpy chimp sole king of his own small island — which, if you ask Caliban, was the only happy ending in the whole play.
