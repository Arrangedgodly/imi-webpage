# How the Chimp Stole Christmas!

*A parody of "How the Grinch Stole Christmas!"*

Every Hoot down in Hootville liked Chimpmas a lot.

But the Chimp, who lived just north of Hootville, did NOT!

The Chimp hated Chimpmas! The whole Chimpmas season!

Now, please don't ask why. No one quite knew the reason.

It could be his head wasn't screwed on just right.

It could be, perhaps, that his slacks were too tight.

But I think that the most likely reason of all

may have been that his heart was two sizes too small.

But whatever the reason — his heart or his slacks —

he stood there on Chimpmas Eve, hating the Hoots,

staring down from his cave with a sour Grinchy frown

at the warm light below in their root-branch town.

For tomorrow, he knew, all the Hoot girls and boys

would wake bright and early and rush for their toys!

"Chimpmas is coming!" they hooted with glee.

"And it comes in the morning! As soon as can be!"

Then he growled, with his fingers nervously drumming,

"I MUST find some way to keep Chimpmas from coming!"

"I hate Chimpmas!" he growled. "Tomorrow they'll rush

for their toys! Then the NOISE! Oh, the noise, noise, noise, noise!

There's one thing I hate — all the NOISE, NOISE, NOISE, NOISE!

Then the FEASTING! On banana-pudding and roast Chimpmas yeast!

And they'll do something I hate most of all —

they'll sit close together, the big and the small,

and groom one another! DE-TICKLE AND PREEN!

They'll hand-hold and feather-preen, beak-to-beak, back-to-back,

and they'll SING! And they'll SING! And they'll SING, SING, SING, SING!

And the more the Chimp thought of this Chimpmas-Sing-Sing,

the more the Chimp thought: "I must stop this whole thing!

But HOW? How do I stop it from coming? ... HOW?"

Then he got an idea! An awful idea!

THE CHIMP GOT A WONDERFUL, AWFUL IDEA!

"I know just what to do!" the old Chimp hissed a hiss.

"I'll make a Santy-Chimp costume — I can't miss!"

And he chuckled, and he clucked, "What a great Chimpish trick!

With this coat and this cap, I look just like Saint Nick!

But a sleigh needs a beast — I have only one hope:

I'll borrow my marmoset, Max, from his rope!"

So he called for poor Max, tied a horn on his head,

and Max gave a look that said, plainly, "Drop dead."

Then he loaded some bags, and some old empty sacks,

on his ramshackle sleigh, and he hitched up poor Max.

Then the Chimp said, "Giddap!" and the sleigh started down

toward the Hoots who aslept in their root-branch town.

All their windows were dark. Quiet snow filled the air.

All the Hoots were all dreaming sweet dreams without care.

"This is stop number one," the old Santy-Chimp hissed,

as he climbed to the roof — and he nearly just missed!

Then he slid down the chimney — a terribly tight fit,

but if Santa could do it, then so could a Chimp it.

Then he slithered and slunk, with a smile most unpleasant,

around the whole room, and he took every present!

Popguns! And cycads! And cookies! And drums!

A bicycle? No — a tri-troika! And plums!

Cardamom, jam, and banana-pudding cans!

He stuffed them in bags, and, as naturally as breathing,

he turned to the tree — and he started the thieving!

He took every hoot-light! He took every vine!

He took all their figgy-glue, strand after strand!

He cleaned out that icebox as quick as a flash —

why, that Chimp even took their last can of Hoot-hash!

Then he stuffed all the food up the chimney with ease.

"Now for the tree!" grinned the Chimp in his sleigh-keys.

And the moment he grabbed it — a small, sleepy coo:

little Cindy-Lou Hoot, who was not more than two.

She stared at the Chimp and said, "Santy-Chimp? Why?

Why are you taking our Chimpmas tree? WHY?"

But, you know, that old Chimp was so smart and so slick,

he thought up a lie and he thought it up quick!

"Why, my sweet little tot, there's a light on this tree

that won't light on one side. I'm taking it, see?

I'll fix it up there in my shop, my deer —

er, my dear — then I'll bring it right back here."

And his fib fooled the child. Then he patted her head,

and he got her a drink, and he sent her to bed.

Then he did the same thing to the other Hoots' houses,

leaving crumbs much too small for the other Hoots' mouses!

It was a quarter past dawn... all the Hoots still abed,

all the Hoots still asnooze, when he packed up his sled,

and he packed it with presents, and hitched up poor Max,

and the whole kit and kaboodle went creaking out back.

Three thousand feet up! Up the side of Mount Clump-It,

he rode with his load to the tiptop to dump it!

"Pooh-Pooh to the Hoots!" he was chimplishly humming.

"They're finding out now that no Chimpmas is coming!

They're just waking up! I know just what they'll do!

Their mouths will hang open a minute or two,

then the Hoots down in Hootville will all cry BOO-HOO!"

"That's a noise," grinned the Chimp, "that I simply MUST hear!"

So he paused. And the Chimp put a hand to his ear.

And he did hear a sound rising over the snow.

It started in low. Then it started to grow.

But the sound wasn't sad! Why, this sound sounded MERRY!

It couldn't be so! But it WAS merry! VERY!

He stared down at Hootville! The Chimp popped his eyes!

Then he shook! What he saw was a shocking surprise!

Every Hoot down in Hootville, the tall and the small,

was SINGING! Without any presents at all!

He HADN'T stopped Chimpmas from coming! IT CAME!

Somehow or other, it came just the same!

And the Chimp, with his feet ice-cold in the snow,

stood puzzling and puzzling: "How could it be so?

It came without ribbons! It came without tags!

It came without packages, boxes, or bags!"

He puzzled and puzzed till his puzzler was sore.

Then the Chimp thought of something he hadn't before!

"Maybe Chimpmas," he thought, "doesn't come from a store.

Maybe Chimpmas... perhaps... means a little bit more!"

And what happened then? Well... in Hootville they say

that the Chimp's small heart grew three sizes that day!

And the minute his heart didn't feel quite so tight,

he whizzed with his load through the bright morning light —

but the load was so heavy, so far up the side

of Mount Clump-It, that runners began to slide!

The sleigh slipped! It skidded! It started to go!

It tobogged toward the roofs of the Hoots down below!

And the Chimp gave a yell, and he grabbed for the rein-rope —

one marmoset, one chimp, and a quarter past-dawn slope!

But his heart was big now, and his heart was strong,

and he pulled with the strength of ten chimps, plus two, all along!

He heaved and he hauled and he hunched up his back,

and he stopped that whole sleigh — and then, Max — that young mack —

well, they say that that Chimp's small heart grew so tight,

it EXPLODED with warmth, and it felt just all right.

So the Chimp, with a smile that was three sizes new,

cried "Giddap, dear Max!" and down Clump-It they flew,

and he brought back the toys! And the food for the feast!

And he — HE HIMSELF! The Chimp carved the roast yeast!

"Welcome, Chimpmas!" sang the Hoots, one and all,

"Welcome, Chimp! Welcome, marmoset! Welcome, all!"

And the Chimp swung a tail around Cindy-Lou Hoot,

and they groomed and they feasted — de-tickled to boot!

And for the first time in years, when the Hoots started singing,

the Chimp didn't grumble — he joined in, uncling-ing? —

well, whatever that word is, he sang it out right,

with his heart three sizes bigger, in the warm morning light.

For Chimpmas, he learned, in the branches up high,

is the troop you hang onto, beneath the same sky.
