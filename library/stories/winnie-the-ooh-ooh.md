# Winnie-the-Ooh-Ooh

*A parody of "Winnie-the-Pooh"*

Once upon a time, a very long time ago now, about last Friday, there lived a monkey under the name of Sanders in a jungle all alone at the very top of the forest. His name was Ooh-Ooh, F.S. — F. for a Friend, S. for a Small monkey of Very Little Brain, stuffed head to tail with fluff.

Ooh-Ooh did one Stoutness Exercise every morning, to the counting of one, two — which was as high as it seemed worth going — and then he had a little something. The something was bananny: banana mashed with wild honey, eaten with his paw directly, in bed.

"Ooh," said Ooh-Ooh, smacking his lips, "ooh, ooh."

One morning he visited his friend Rabbit the agouti, who lived in a burrow beneath a fig root. Rabbit said, "Help yourself," which is what people say when they do not mean it. Ooh-Ooh helped himself to breakfast, second breakfast, elevenses, and a little something, and then he went to leave through the front door — and did not leave. He was stuck, wedged like a cork in a bottle, tail in, head out.

"Ooh," said Ooh-Ooh, "I think I shall require a week."

So it took a week of Christopher Bonobo reading him a Sustaining Book, while Rabbit's relations used his hind legs as a towel-horse, until at last — pop! — he came out like a champagne monkey, and Christopher said, "There you are," and Ooh-Ooh said, "And there I was."

Another time, Ooh-Ooh and Twiglet — a Very Small marmoset, even smaller than small — decided to catch a Huffalump. They dug an enormous pit (deep, for Huffalumps; deep, for safety) and baited it with a jar of bananny, which Ooh-Ooh was to leave in the trap and not eat.

He ate it. From the inside, which was the mistake. At midnight he woke with the jar stuck fast over his head, and it was very dark inside, and he thought: *A Huffalump has got me.*

Twiglet woke and saw a monstrous shape charging about with a jar for a head, and did exactly the right thing, which was to scream and run. They ran together for a quarter of a mile before the jar caught a root and smashed, and Ooh-Ooh blinked out at Twiglet and said kindly, "It's all right, Twiglet. It was only me all along. But didn't I make a very good Huffalump?"

Then one wet and Blowy day, something Bounced into them from behind.

"Bounce!" it said. "Bounce, bounce, trounce! I'm Bouncer. Bouncer the sifaka. That's T-F-A-K-A spelled sideways."

"A sifaka," explained Howler, the wise old monkey who could spell his own name if you gave him an hour, "is a Bouncing Animal. It is as well to know this early on."

The wonderful thing about sifakas, sang Bouncer, is sifakas are wonderful things. Their tops are made out of bounces, their bottoms are made out of springs. They're bouncy, trouncy, flouncy, pouncy — fun, fun, fun, fun, fun! But the most wonderful thing about sifakas is he's the only one.

"I like that song," said Ooh-Ooh, after thinking about it for a while. "I would sing it too, if I could remember all the bounces."

Bouncer, it turned out, did not like bananny, which was the first worrying sign. He did not like haycorns either, or thistles, or what the forest called extract of malt, and after a Full Investigation of every larder in the forest (Rabbit's was hit hardest, and Rabbit's opinion of Bouncing Animals sank lowest), it was discovered that what sifakas like best is the one thing nobody had: the strength potion of Langur the kangaroo-monkey, and her medicine chest, and her little Roo. So Bouncer moved in with Langur, was adopted before breakfast, and bounced on from then on with official papers.

Ee-ore the slow loris, the grey and gloomy one, heard the whole thing from his branch without turning his head, because it took too long.

"Sometimes," Ee-ore said, "I sits and thinks, and sometimes I just sits. Today I was going to do both, but Bouncer has Bounced on my sitting place, so now I shall do neither."

They gave him his tail back, which Bouncer had Bounced off, and pinned it on with a thorn, and Ee-ore said it was the finest tail he never asked for.

It was shortly after this that Ee-ore's birthday came, or didn't. "It's my birthday," he announced, gloomily, from the stream-bed where he was standing. "The whole day. Nobody has noticed, which is what I expected, so it has gone very well."

Ooh-Ooh hurried home and fetched a full jar of bananny for a present. Twiglet hurried for a fine bunch of bananas — but on the way a very large baboon carrying a very large balloon trod on a thorn, the balloon burst with a Bang, and Twiglet arrived with a scrap of burst banana-skin and a story.

"A balloon," said Ee-ore, turning it over slowly. "My balloon. It is a very small balloon, which is the best kind, because it takes up no room at all. And is this jar empty? An empty bananny jar. Which is to say — a jar for keeping things in." He was quiet for a moment. "It's a good jar," he said at last. "Nobody has ever given me two useful presents before. I shall put the balloon in the jar." And he did, and looked at it, and was as nearly happy as a slow loris is permitted to be.

It was that summer, on the banks of the stream, that Ooh-Ooh invented Poohsticks — you drop a stick off one side of the bridge and run to the other side and see whose stick comes out first — and it is played to this day by everybody, which is more than can be said for most inventions of Very Small Brains. It was also Ooh-Ooh who found the North Pole. The Expotition had gone to discover it (Twiglet carried provisions; Ee-ore carried hope, quietly, where nobody could see it), and when Roo fell into the stream and was being carried toward the gorge, it was Ooh-Ooh who lay flat on the bank and poked his long pole into the water, and Roo grabbed it and climbed to safety, and the pole was still stuck in the riverbed.

"Ooh," said Ooh-Ooh, looking at it. "I think it's the North Pole."

"Ho," said Ee-ore, "so it is."

And a sign was nailed to it: NORTH POLE (DISCOVERED BY OOH-OOH, DISCOVERED WITH POO? — the calligraphy of the forest has never been reliable — POOH-POOH? — and at last simply: OOH-OOH FOUND IT).

The rains came in the autumn and the forest flooded, and the water rose until Twiglet was an island — a Very Small monkey on a Very Small tree, writing a help-message in a bottle, being chased by heffalumps in circles (in his head, which were the worst kind). Ooh-Ooh found the message in his bananny jar, thought hard for a Very Little Brain's idea of hard, and came up with the biggest one of his life: they would sail to the rescue in Christopher Bonobo's umbrella. Which they did — Ooh-Ooh steering, Christopher paddling, ten mice and a rabbit providing ballast commentary — and Twiglet was taken aboard at the eleventh hour, and the umbrella was named the Brain of Ooh-Ooh II, after the first Brain of Ooh-Ooh, which had been the boat's predecessor and had sunk.

Then the water went down, the forest had a feast, and the autumn came slowly after, the way it does, one orange leaf at a time.

And that evening, when the sun was going down over the top of the forest, and Langur and little Roo were going home to bed, and Tink? — no, the fireflies were coming out one by one, Christopher Bonobo climbed the great fig tree to Ooh-Ooh's door.

They had a party first — Ooh-Ooh's special party, with iced bananas and pencil-sharpenings that nobody ate — because Christopher Robin was going away, and everybody knew it, and nobody said it. School, it was called. Somewhere outside the forest, there was a thing called school, and it wanted Christopher Robin every day, all day, forever.

"Ooh-Ooh," he said, "promise you won't forget about me, ever. Not even when I'm a hundred."

"Wherever they go," said Ooh-Ooh solemnly, holding the honey pot, "and whatever happens to them on the way, in that enchanted place at the top of the forest, a little monkey and his playmate will always be playing."

And so they were.
