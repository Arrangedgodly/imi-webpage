# Going Bananas

*A parody of "Going Postal"*

They said that in the end they threw him to the crocodiles, and in a way they did, and in a way they didn't, and in the best way it was only the beginning.

Moist von Lipvine — forger, fraud, and the finest liar ever to swing out of the underworld — woke from his fake hanging in the palace of Ankh-Monkeypork, facing Lord Vetinari: a slender ring-tailed lemur with eyes like two open ledgers.

"You are dead, Mister Lipvine," said the Patrician. "The ape known as Alfred Bananagler was thrown to the crocodiles this morning. They found him surprisingly unfilling. You, however, may choose: the croc pit, or a job."

"What job?"

"Postmaster."

The Post Hollow was a mausoleum of mail: four hundred thousand undelivered leaf-letters, composting gently in towers, on which an entire civilization had once written things like *Wish you were swinging here* and *Enclosed, one banana, do not eat the stamp.* The staff consisted of Groat, an ancient howler with bandages where his fur had given up, and Stanley, a young capuchin whose entire mind was devoted to thorns.

"Thorns," said Stanley, reverently. "Five-point barbed. Six-point barbed. Don't get me started on brambles."

"I take one drop of jungle fever in a barrel of water," Groat explained, "as a precaution. I have never once had jungle fever, which proves the science."

Behind Moist stood Mr. Pump, a clay gorilla seven feet tall, with words baked into his chest. YOUR HONESTY IS MY CHAIN, one line read, which was worse than handcuffs.

"Everything in this city is my circus," Lord Vetinari observed, "and all the monkeys are mine."

Miss Macaque-alarity, who ran the Hollow's front desk and disapproved of everyone born after her, looked Moist up and down. "The last postmaster ran away over the roof," she said. "I scrubbed that roof twice. Don't you dare make me scrub it a third time."

The Hollow's great rival was the Grand Trunk drum line — signal towers beating coded thunder across the jungle, hollow logs and castanet trees, messages flying faster than any leaf could be carried. The Trunk was owned by Reacher Glitter: a gorilla dusted head to toe in gold, with a pirate's grin and a boardroom manners problem. His board met in a room with no windows, in case honesty broke in. Under his management the towers were worked night and day by underfed drummers until their paws bled rhythm.

"The market will provide," said Reacher Glitter, to applause he had paid for.

"The market," said Adora Belle Sweetheart, spitting her chew-stick into the ferns, "provides funerals. My brother drummed the Trunk. He asked for one day off. He got a long one." She looked Moist in the eye, which was like being audited. "You're that Bananagler fraud. I can tell, because you smile like a mirror."

By night she dug golems out of old river mud and found them jobs, because a golem with work is a citizen, and a golem without one is a very heavy problem. She trusted golems. She trusted nothing else that smiled.

So Moist did what Moist did best. He went ape.

He put on a golden suit of dyed fur and re-opened the Hollow. He invented the stamp — a rectangle of gummed leaf you licked, which Stanley fainted onto — and within a week the city had gone quietly insane for them. Collectors traded a misprinted upside-down Vine-and-Crown stamp for a vineyard. Groat wore his album to bed. Stanley gave a lecture on thorny hedges to a packed hall, and had to be helped down.

To launch it all, Moist stood on the Hollow's steps in the golden suit and sold the crowd their own better nature at a penny a stamp. "This city was built on letters!" he cried. "Love letters! Apology letters! Letters with bananas in!" The crowd cheered, queued, and overpaid in a good cause — which, Lord Vetinari maintained, is the entire history of civilization.

He taught Groat to sort mail by smell. He dragged the backlog out of the towers with buckets and ropes and civic pride. And one night, when fire took the sorting hall — set, the watch agreed, by nobody in particular, in the pay of somebody very particular — Moist ran into the smoke again and again until his golden fur was gray, and the letters came out, because — to his enormous irritation — he had started to *believe* in the mail.

The next morning he packed a bag. He stood in the doorway with it, and behind him Mr. Pump said, in a voice like a landslide filing paperwork: "Postmaster. Reconsider."

"If I run," said Moist, "I'm only what they always said I was."

"Yes," said the golem. "And If You Stay, You Are What You Chose."

Moist stood there a long time. Then he put the bag down, and the great knack of it was that nobody ever saw him do it.

Then he made the bet. One letter, carried by hand from Ankh-Monkeypork to Pseudopawlis, against the Grand Trunk's fastest drum. The whole jungle took sides. Reacher Glitter smiled gold and shook on it.

The letter said: *To the Postmaster of Pseudopawlis — I am a fraudulent ape and I can still beat a drum.*

The Trunk threw everything at the race, and the race threw it back. Towers along the route went quiet with convenient coughs; a bridge developed an urgent need to be a raft. But a line of drums worked to breaking point breaks, and theirs broke in seventeen places at once, because Glitter had saved money on rope, wood, and monkeys, and thrift always falls on you in the end. Meanwhile a battered gorilla of clay ran day and night beside the mail cart, saying nothing, being honest at great volume, while Groat — goggles down, bandages streaming — drove the last stretch standing up and screaming his own surname for luck.

The letter won by two days. Glitter's empire came apart like a wet stamp; his own board discovered that a room with no windows is also a room with no exits, and testified for the privilege. Glitter himself held a farewell party at which he was the only guest who did not know it was one; the Watch let him finish his speech, for artistic reasons. In the ruins of the Trunk's logs, Moist found a message that had been going round and round the drum line for years, never paid for, never erased, sent in memory of a dead drummer by his mates:

*GNU JAPE DEARFRUIT.*

"What does it mean?" asked Stanley.

"It means his name keeps swinging," said Moist. "Leave it in. It goes round forever."

They gave him a parade, which he hated, and a medal, which he hid. Adora Belle looked him up and down and said, "Say it."

"I'm a crook," said Moist.

"Good," she said. "Keep saying it. You're the top banana now, Postmaster, and I'll be watching to see you stay only lightly rotten."

Lord Vetinari, watching the parade from the palace, observed to no one at all that a city could survive tyrants, floods, and war, but could not survive a man who made it proud of a letter.

The crocodiles, it is recorded, never got their ape. Going bananas, the street called it. Moist called it Tuesday. And every night, somewhere over the dark canopy, a drum beat out a dead drummer's name, round and round the world, because some mail is too important to stop.
