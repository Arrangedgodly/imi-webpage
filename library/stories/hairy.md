# Hairy

*A parody of "Carrie"*

Troop High School of Chimp-berlain, Maine, ran on one universal law: what you couldn't groom, you mocked. Susan Snell broke that law only once, and once was enough to flatten a gymnasium.

It happened in the girls' grooming room after fitness class. The water came on, the fur went fluffy, and Hairy White — plain, friendless Hairy, whose fur grew in patches like a lawn a dog hated — looked down at her own forearm and watched it molt. Great clumps of her winter coat floated toward the drain. Hairy had never molted before, and something in her understood, in the wordless way of the very young, that this was a door opening.

Hairy screamed.

Chris Hargensen led the chant — "Grow it up! Grow it up! BALD-Y!" — and threw banana peels, and the others threw too. Sue Snell threw the peels she was holding back down to her sides, ashamed, and did not throw any, which is not the same as being innocent. Chris's boyfriend Billy filmed the whole thing on his fig-phone, and by supper it was on every screen in Chimp-berlain, captioned MOLT-CAM. Hairy's mother saw it before Hairy got home.

Miss Desjardin, the gym teacher, made the whole class report for lice-duty, and Chris refused, and was banned from the Banana Ball, and that, more than anything that came after, made everything after.

At home, Margaret White — a silverback of unbreakable gloom — locked her daughter in the Grooming Closet to pray. "The troop laughed at you, Hairy, because you are full of sin," she said, "and sin, like fur, always comes back."

But the mirror in the closet cracked that night — cracked without being touched — and a hairbrush walked across the floor like a fat black spider, and Hairy sat very still in the dark and began to make plans. The next time Margaret swung the brush at her, the brush stopped in mid-air, hung there, twitching, and dropped. Margaret called it the Devil's molting. Hairy called it Tuesday.

In the library she found it, in a cracked-spined book under F for Freak: TELEKINESIS — the gift of moving things with the mind; researchers believe it sleeps in every primate and wakes in some.

Hairy practiced moon by moon. First pebbles. Then the teapot. Then bananas, which she could peel in mid-air, slowly, like a magic trick performed by the wind.

The principal, a worn old baboon named Morton, summoned her about the video, apologized for the species, and gave her the school's idea of help: a pamphlet. Miss Desjardin ran the guilty parties down like a storm: six weeks of detention, and Ball bans for the ringleaders — Chris foremost. Chris's father, a lawyer gorilla with a briefcase where his heart should be, stormed the school and threatened suits until the ban bent. Chris walked out grinning. "You'll pay for this, Sue Snell," she said sweetly. "You and your bald little friend." It was the last schoolyard thing Chris ever said, and she said it twice more, in her head, forever.

The night before the Ball, the phone rang in the White treehouse, and Hairy, up practicing — floating thimbles, one at a time, over the sleeping town — heard her mother's voice come unraveling down the line to the neighbors: "They're all gonna laugh at her. They're all gonna laugh at her at that filthy dance." Hairy floated the receiver gently back onto its hook, from across the room, and made a decision about noise.

Sue Snell had a better idea, and it cost her the Ball to do it. "Take Hairy to the Banana Ball," she begged Tommy Ross, the class president and the troop's best swinger. "Not pity. Penances can be sweet. Do it right, or don't do it." Tommy, who was decent the way some fruit is sweet without trying, said yes, and meant it.

The Ball glittered. Hairy wore her mother's slips and a patchy little smile, and worked all evening on being ordinary — which is a full-time job when every monkey in the room has seen you molt. Then the ballots — rigged by Chris's crowd as the cruelest joke in troop history — came back with one name.

They crowned Hairy and Sue together, Banana Queen and Queen's companion. For one whole minute, Hairy White was happy. You could see it. That was the horror of it, and the pity: you could see it.

Then Chris Hargensen, from the rafters, pulled the rope, and the bucket tipped — a bucket of overripe banana sludge, gray and sweet and stinking — all over the Queen. Beside her, the same trick, the same rope, caught Tommy square in the ears. And in her head, in her mother's voice, in a voice with a smile in it: *They're all gonna laugh at you, Hairy. They're all gonna laugh at you.*

Hairy stopped thinking about being a monkey.

The book said *flex*, meaning the mind, and Hairy flexed. The fruit bowl rose like a small moon and orbited her head. The punch bowl flew the length of the gym and shaved the beard off the vice principal. The doors slammed like a judge's gavel. The coconut-milk hose lashed out and groomed Chris's fur backwards forever. Billy's palm-frond convertible folded itself, politely, like a napkin, into the lagoon — no one drowned, everyone was plucked — and the fig-phones of Chimp-berlain rose as one from a thousand paws and played MOLT-CAM to the sky until the sky itself rang. Miss Desjardin, running to help, was lifted gently, dusted down, and set safely on the bleachers, because Hairy remembered who had been kind. And Tommy was carried out on a river of calm air, untouched, because she spared Tommy, remember that. When the lanterns failed, one figure stood atop the wreckage, bald and crowned and shining in the emergency glow: the Banana Queen, going quietly, thoroughly ape.

She walked home through a town coming apart at the seams — every streetlight popping like fig seeds, every fire hydrant bowing as she passed — and Margaret met her at the door of the treehouse with the fruit-knife, to cut the sin out of her daughter. Hairy looked at her mother, and her mother's heart — quite simply, with no mark and no noise at all — slowed, and slowed, and stopped. Then Hairy held her mother's cooling hand, and the crown of banana slid from her head, and she began to cry like the child she still was.

Sue Snell found her in the ruin of the gym and was afraid, and came anyway, which is the better part of both.

"They're all gonna laugh at me," Hairy whispered.

"No," said Sue. "Not anymore. Not ever again."

When Sue looked again, Hairy was gone — a bald queen swinging off into the dawn, if you believe the younger juveniles, and everyone believes the younger juveniles now.

The town wrote its reports, and the reports disagreed, which is how you know a town is embarrassed. Sue wrote one letter, long after, to the son of the gym teacher: *Hairy White never hurt anyone who hadn't fed her to the troop first. What happened at that Ball was not a prank that got out of control. It was a prank that got answered. There is a difference, and you should learn it before you laugh at anybody.*

Because to this day in Chimp-berlain, when a banana peels itself in the night, when a mirror cracks in an empty room, the old ones touch their fur, and check their children's scalps, and say her name.
