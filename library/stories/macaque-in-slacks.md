# Macaque in Slacks

*A parody of "Fox in Socks"*

Ladies and gentlebeasts! Gather near, if you please,
to the branchiest stage in the tallest of trees,
where the Jungle Jubilee Talent Night rocks —
starring ME, a macaque, in spiffy new slacks!

(See that gorilla in row three? That's Rocks.
Rocks has a face like a box of dry socks.)

"Rocks!
Slacks!
Stack!
Chimp!
Chimp in slacks!
Rocks in the stalls!
Chimp in slacks on a stack, and Rocks gawps!"

"Look, sir. Look, sir. Mr. Rocks, sir.
Do you like my new slacks, sir?
Let me do some tricks for you, sir.
Let me show you
some new tricks, Rocks!"

"Sir," says Rocks, "I don't like your little trick.
I'm not quick or slick, sir. My tongue is too thick, sir.
I can't say your chimp-champ chatter, sir.
I'm getting so dizzy I have to sit down, sir."

"SIT DOWN, sir? Why, sir? Sit down, Rocks, sir, sit down!
When a gorilla sits down
with a frown on a mound
in a town called Frown-Down,
in a gown of soft brown,
on a warm afternoon in the month of big June —
that's a frown-gown-mound-town-gorilla-sit-DOWN!"

Rocks sits down. The chimp is not done.

"New slacks! Blue slacks!
See my new slacks, Mr. Rocks?
True slacks! Two slacks!
I've a whole tidy stack, sir,
of new blue-slack stacks, sir,
it takes a lot of ticks
to pick a lot of slacks
when a macaque's picking slacks on the rocks!"

Rocks rubs his temples. The chimp takes a breath.

"Who? Who? Who?
Whooo but Lou Loris likes to lick?
Lou Loris likes to lick low leaves, sir.
Lou Loris licks the leafy leaves
and the leafy leaves lick Lou,
and the low leaf that Lou licks, sir,
is the leafy low leaf LICKED — phew!"

"Sir," says Rocks, "my tongue is now lime.
It has completely run out of rhyme."

"LIME? TIME! Now, sir, now, sir, look what I see, sir!
Slow Moe Crow, sir, comes with a canoe, sir!
Slow Moe Crow rows a gnu in the blue,
and a gnu in a blue canoe rows it true,
and the gnu chews stew while Moe Crow rows through,
and the blue stew spews when the canoe comes to —
that's a slow-Moe-Crow
gnu-chew
blue-canoe
spew-stew
row-row, sir,
and it's rowed by a CROW!"

"Gently," begs Rocks. "Gently, sir. Please.
The baboon in the balcony just fell to his knees."

The chimp takes a bow. Then the chimp takes a breath.
And the chimp takes another. (The crowd holds its breath.)

Chicks with ticks come.
Chicks with tocks come.
Chicks with ticks and tocks and clocks come.

Chicks with clocks tick.
Chicks with tocks tock.
And the clocks go TICK and the tocks go TOCK
on the ticks of the chicks with the clocks, Mr. Rocks!

Gib comes. Gail comes.
Gib and Gail lead quails.
Quails with pails come.
Gib's quails wail. Gail's quails wail.
Gib and Gail's quails wail on trails with pails,
and the pails leak ale on the quails' tail trails!

Bim comes. Ban comes.
Bim and Ban drag fans.
Fans with pans come.
Bim's fans pant. Ban's fans pant.
Bim and Ban's fans pant and chant on plants,
and the plants dance, plants, while the pans clatter — ants!
And the ants carry plans to the plants, Mr. Rocks —
that's Bim-Ban
fan-plant
pan-ant
plan-chant
dance-plants!

Rocks grips his armrests. The chimp is sweating. The crowd goes bananas.
The sloths are leaning in, which for sloths takes an hour.

"Newt! Newt! Suit! Suit!
A newt in a suit, sir!
A newt in a suit
with a flute, sir, to boot!
And the newt in the suit
plays a toot on the flute,
and the toot that the newt toots
is astoundingly cute,
and two toucans, Deej and Djuk,
who cook newt-toot flutes by the book,
say a newt-suit-flute-toot
is the toot-suit-newt-look —
TOOT, sir! TOOT!
Boot the newt-flute suit!"

Rocks shuts his eyes. Rocks counts to ten.
When Rocks opens his eyes, the chimp is doing it again.

Chet Chimp likes figs.
Chet's pig digs figs.
Chet Chimp picks big figs.
Chet's fig-pig digs Chet's big figs,
and the pig picks a fig in the fig tree Chet likes!

"Here, sir! Here, sir! Do you hear, sir?
Doodle the poodle doodles, sir.
Doodle's a poodle who doodles on noodles.
Doodle's doodled on two tons of noodles, sir,
and poodles who doodle on noodles are rare,
but a noodle-doodling
poodle-owning
mango-mowing
marmoset named Mare —
that's a noodle-doodle
poodle-mango
marmoset-Mare,
sir,
and Mare says it's FAIR!"

There is a silence.

It is very long.

The chimp, sensing victory, goes for the crown.

"Now, sir!" cries the chimp, "for my finale, my sweets —
when beetles groom monkeys in barrels, it's neat!

When beetles groom beetles
in a barrel of monkeys,
and the barrel of monkeys
is balanced, my sweets,
on the knuckles of a baboon
dozing slow in the heat,
on a branch in a breeze
above two ticking clocks —

they call this a baboon-knee
barrel-of-monkeys
beetle-grooming
branch-breeze
tick-tock battle,
if you please!"

There is another silence.

It is longer.

And then Rocks the gorilla stands up.

Slowly.

Which, from a gorilla, means something.

"Chimp," says Rocks, "when a macaque in slacks
in a box on the rocks
wears socks on his toes
and he talks in his socks,
and he walks with a gnu
through a gooey lagoon,
where the gnu chews blue goo
and the slacks droop and flop,
and the ticks and the tocks
and the chicks and the clocks,
and the quails with the pails
and the ants with the plans,
and a loris named Lou
licking leaves in the dew,
and a doodling poodle
on two tons of noodles,
and a slow-Moe-Crow canoe
full of blue-canoe stew,
and a barrel of beetles
and a baboon in heat,
and a fig-picking pig
in the figs on a feet —
in the figs on a FEAT, sir,
I mean on a seat, sir,
I MEAN —
all follow the chimp
to the top of the stack —

you call this a
gnu-chewing-goo
sock-talking
slacks-drooping
quail-wailing
ant-planning
lizard-lisping
poodle-doodling
crow-canoeing
chick-tick-tock-topping
macaque-in-a-box-on-the-rocks —

and I call it time for your nap!"

The chimp's mouth opens. Nothing comes out.
His tongue files for leave. His slacks go slack.
He sits down in the stack, in his box, on the rocks.

The crowd goes APE. From sloth to baboon,
from the balcony to the branch, to the baboon's own knuckles,
the canopy rings with the wildest of cheers,
and the judges — a toucan, a tapir, and Mare —
award Rocks the Jubilee crown by unanimous bellow.

Rocks bows. He is gracious. He helps up the chimp.

"Macaque in slacks," says Rocks, "our game is done, sir.
Thank you, sir, for a barrel of fun, sir.
Your tongue-tickling tricks took a terrible toll, sir.
But nobody, nobody — NOBODY, sir —
out-talks a gorilla
who talks very slow."

The chimp sleeps for a week. In his slacks. On the rocks.
And nobody, nobody, nobody asks him for socks.

(Though the gnu, it is said, still chews goo in the lagoon,
and Mare maintains the doodling was fair.)
