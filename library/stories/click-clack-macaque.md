# Click, Clack, Macaque: Monkeys That Type

*A parody of "Click, Clack, Moo: Cows That Type"*

Zookeeper Brown had a problem. Well, first Zookeeper Brown had a safari park, and in the safari park he had a fig tree, a tire swing, and sixteen macaques — and, ever since Tuesday, one old typewriter that a retired keeper had left under the shelter.

Also, since Tuesday, it had turned cold.

At dawn on Wednesday, Zookeeper Brown heard a sound coming from the macaque enclosure.

*Click, clack, ooh.*

*Click, clack, ooh.*

*Clickety, clack, ooh.*

At breakfast he found a note stuck to the fig tree with sap:

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> The treehouse is cold. We want electric blankets.
>
> Sincerely,
> The Macaques

"No way," said Zookeeper Brown. "No blankets. Monkeys do not get electric blankets. Monkeys get fur. Fur is the whole point of monkeys." He crumpled the note, drank his coffee, and felt very much in charge.

So he typed an answer and taped it to the fig tree, because if the monkeys were going to write notes, he could write notes too:

> NO BLANKETS. — MANAGEMENT

At dawn on Thursday, he found his note back on his own office door, corrected:

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> "No blankets" is a fragment, and "management" is not a title you have earned. Also, sorry. We're closed. No monkey show today.
>
> Cold simians,
> The Macaques

Now this was serious. The ten o'clock banana show — where the troop peeled 'nanas for the school groups with unbelievable style — was the most popular act in the park. The school buses were already rolling. And across the path, the gibbons had seen the whole thing and taped up a note of their own:

> We're cold too.
>
> Solidarity,
> The Gibbons

The gibbons, it should be said, did not actually want electric blankets. The gibbons wanted a heated swinging-vine, which is not a product that exists. When Zookeeper Brown pointed this out through the fence, the gibbons taped up a second note:

> Then invent one. You have a clipboard. Inventors have clipboards.
>
> Warmer and warmer,
> The Gibbons

Zookeeper Brown paced. He called the Park Director, a toucan with no patience and a beakful of opinions. "Brown!" she screeched. "Where is my monkey show?!"

"The monkeys are on strike!"

"Then un-strike them! You're a zookeeper! You have a clipboard! You're practically management!"

"I'm not management," said Brown. "The monkeys made that very clear in writing."

He tried everything. He held up bananas at the fence — the macaques typed:

> We can see the bananas from here. That is not the same as warmth.

He explained, through the fence, that fur was essentially a built-in blanket:

> Then you wear your fur at night in your unheated office, and we'll keep ours. Fair is fair.

He did not have an answer for that. He did not especially like remembering that he kept the office heater on.

By Thursday afternoon the gibbons had joined the strike properly. No singing, no swinging, just sixteen gibbons and sixteen macaques sitting in a row with their arms folded, breathing on their own paws. A class of kindergartners stood at the empty show lawn and cried into their juice boxes. One small chimpanzee held up a hand-lettered sign that said PLEASE, and Zookeeper Brown had to look at the sky for a while.

The problem was communication. The macaques would not speak to him, and he could not speak to the macaques, and the gibbons were just showing off. What was needed was a neutral party — someone every animal trusted, owed nothing, and wanted nothing from anyone.

There was only one candidate: Sloane the sloth.

Sloane lived in the middle of the park, upside down, and had never once picked a side, mostly because picking sides requires reaching. Zookeeper Brown deputized him on the spot. Sloane took the first message at noon and reached the macaque enclosure by dinner, which Sloane considered a personal best.

The macaques typed a reply. Sloane brought it back the next morning:

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> Electric blankets. Leave them by the gate. We will send you the typewriter.
>
> Sincerely,
> The Macaques

"Absolutely not!" said Zookeeper Brown. "No blankets! That's final!" And he typed his final answer in his biggest letters, both index fingers at once, which took him eleven minutes:

> FINAL MEANS FINAL. NO BLANKETS. — Z. BROWN

The gibbons went on strike harder. The Director called four times, once at midnight, mostly to screech. The macaques' reply came back via Sloane, who accepted a leaf of applause for his service and began the long journey back to his tree, which he would reach by Sunday.

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> Final means no monkey show, no gibbon song, no cute photos for the newspaper, and no warmth for anybody's public relations. Blankets by Friday, or we unionize the flamingos. They have grievances. They have ALWAYS had grievances.
>
> Sincerely,
> The Macaques
> (cc: The Gibbons, The Flamingos, one emu who is watching)

By Friday, Zookeeper Brown had a clipboard full of nothing, a park full of folded arms and tucked-away tails, and a flamingo who had begun, ominously, to stand on one leg NEAR the fence.

"Fine," he growled. "FINE."

He bought every electric blanket in town. He left them in a pile by the gate — big fuzzy ones, banana-yellow ones, one with a picture of a lion on it that he had always wanted for himself.

The next morning, the blankets were gone. The gibbons were singing. In their place, at the gate, sat the typewriter with one final note curled in the roller:

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> Thank you for the blankets. The treehouse is warm. The show will resume at ten o'clock. It will be better than ever, because now we are cozy. P.S. You may keep the lion blanket. We saw you looking at it.
>
> Sincerely,
> The Macaques

And it was. The ten o'clock show was magnificent. The troop peeled with such flourish that three children fainted with joy, the Director wept into her feathers, and the flamingos — who had gotten nothing and stood on both legs all morning out of respect — were given a space heater on probation. That evening the flamingos taped their first-ever note to the macaques' fig tree:

> We never doubted you. But if you strike again, kindly give us notice. We rehearsed.

> Yours in Solidarity,
> The Flamingos

Zookeeper Brown locked the typewriter in the office, wrapped himself in the lion blanket, and finally, finally slept.

He was awakened at dawn by a new sound. A slow sound.

*Click... clack... ooh.*

*Click... clack... ooh.*

On his desk, typed over the course of what must have been the entire night, was a note:

> Dear Zookeeper Brown,
>
> The moat is nice. But we would like a diving board.
>
> Sincerely,
> The Sloth

"SLOANE!" yelled Zookeeper Brown.

At the moat, Sloane hung in his tree, upside down and innocent, and blinked — one eye at a time, very slowly — and smiled.
