# Diary of a Chimpy Kid

*A parody of "Diary of a Wimpy Kid"*

First of all, let me get something straight: this is a JOURNAL, not a diary. I know what it says on the cover. The cover says "Chimpy Kid" because that was the only blank book left at the jungle market, and Mom was too cheap to wait a week for a proper one.

The only reason I'm writing in this thing is so that when I'm rich and famous, the whole troop can read all about me. So don't expect me to be all "Dear Diary, today the mist sat in the canopy and I felt cozy inside." This is a book of FACTS.

Fact One: middle troop school is the dumbest idea ever invented. You take kids like me who haven't hit their growth spurt yet and throw us in with gorillas who have to shave their backs twice a day. I'm not even the smallest monkey in my grade. That honor belongs to my friend Rowley, a round little bonobo who still swings through the canopy with his mom holding his tail.

There is also Fregley, the colobus who lives on the next branch over. Fregley will describe his most recent scab before he says hello. He has decided we are best friends, and I have checked: there is no appeals process.

Let me just say for the record that I think the whole thing is a scam run by the adults so they can park us somewhere while they groom each other in peace.

**September**

Here's everything you need to know about how gross my school is. There is a rotten banana sitting next to the climbing wall that has been there since LAST spring. Somebody chucked it there and nobody wants to pick it up, because — and this is the key part — if you touch it, you get the Rotten 'Nana Touch.

If you get the Rotten 'Nana Touch, you're basically an outcast until you pass it to somebody else, and the only way to protect yourself is to keep your tail curled in a loop at all times, which is really hard to do while climbing. Walk through the halls this month and you'll see forty nervous tails curled up like pretzels with regrets.

Last year a gibbon named Bananabe Hall got the Touch, and nobody would even pass him a fig at lunch. Then his family swung off to another troop entirely, and everybody breathed easier.

**October**

Rowley and I made a comic together for the school paper. Rowley's whole contribution was shouting "ZOO-WEE MAMA!" every time something good happened. I tried to explain that comics need to be about important things, like a gorilla who bites the tops off trees, but Rowley just wanted to draw monkeys slipping on peels. The paper took his comic and lost my masterpiece. I'm not bitter. That's a lie. I'm completely bitter.

Then the paper ran his comic EVERY WEEK, and now kids line up to shout "zoo-wee mama" at him, and he does the voice, and everybody laughs like it just hatched. I invented half of that comic. Specifically, the half that was good.

Halloween: Rowley and I went as a two-monkey banana. I was the top. Rowley's dad made him carry a glow-beetle lantern, and I want to state for the record that laughing at a bonobo with a glow-beetle lantern is not cruelty, it is comedy. On the way home we tossed a couple of overripe bananas at a hollow log full of teenage baboons, which was, in hindsight, our first real decision of the year.

They chased us all the way to my tree, hooting about what they were going to do with our pelts. We squeezed inside and listened to them pounding on the trunk. Then my dad came out in his bath fur, sleep-groped his way to the rain barrel, and dumped the entire thing off the branch. Cold water everywhere. The baboons fled shrieking about the flash monsoon, and Dad never even knew they existed. He thinks he watered an orchid.

**November**

Got roped into the troop play, "The Yellow Tree of Oz." I was cast as a Tree. A Tree that SINGS. My big brother Rodrick filmed the whole thing for his junky band, the Limp Bananas, and now the entire valley has watched me warble "We're off to see the Yellow Tree" while a munchkin macaque stuffs fruit into my bark.

Here is what actually happened onstage, since Rodrick's video cuts around: the Wizard forgot his lines and just listed fruits. The flying monkeys were grounded because the teacher got nervous about the vines. And the director kept hissing "TREES, SWAY" with such feeling that two actual saplings in the front row began to cry, in case they were next.

Mom said I was wonderful. Dad said the whole evening was two hours of his life that would never fruit again. Rodrick is sixteen and thinks he is the greatest howler since sliced bread. Mom invented "Mom Bucks" to make us behave — little leaves worth one real banana each. Rodrick counterfeited an entire canopy of them. So that's the kind of family I have.

**December**

Rowley and I were both Safety Patrollers, walking the nursery monkeys home through the canopy. I did all the work. Rowley got all the credit, mostly for waving.

Then one afternoon I may have chased a nursery monkey up a fig tree while holding a stink beetle. May have. The patrol advisor only saw the second half of the incident, which is my entire point about evidence, and when she demanded a name, I gave her one. Rowley's.

He got suspended from the patrol for a week. He didn't know it was me. Well. He didn't.

Big fight, right before the winter break. A nursery monkey with a big mouth and a photographic memory told him everything. I called him a hairless lar gibbon, which, honestly, he kind of is. He told his parents. His dad gave me the Look of a Disappointed Silverback, which I do not recommend.

He stopped sitting with me at lunch. Then the year-end awards came out and Rowley won Class Clown, for zoo-wee-mama-ing all year. I got nothing. I invented half of that comic.

Then the worst thing possible happened. The teenage baboons caught us at the climbing wall — the same crew from Halloween, still damp — and one of them picked up the Rotten 'Nana.

I'm not going to write down what they said. I'll just say they wanted somebody to EAT it, they picked Rowley, and Rowley's lip was shaking, and something in my chest made a decision before I did.

I stepped forward and told them I'd do it. And I did. And the next morning I stood up in front of the whole school and said it again, loudly, so nobody would ever wonder: I ate the Rotten Banana. Gobbled it up, peel and all.

You can imagine how that went over. Nobody will share a branch with me now. I have basically given myself the Touch forever, or at least until somebody eats something worse, which, at middle troop school, is a matter of time and physics.

But here's the thing. Rowley is clean. And when he found out why I did it — and about the stink beetle — he walked over to my lonely branch at lunch, sat down, and split his fig with me straight down the middle. Then he whispered "zoo-wee mama," very quietly, just for me, and honestly? It landed.

A chimp-ion takes one for the team. When I'm rich and famous, this journal is going to make me sound extremely noble.

**January**

New semester. The banana is still there by the climbing wall, and now it is technically a historical artifact. Bananabe Hall's family sent a postcard from their new troop. He's thriving, it says. Nobody has touched that banana since spring except me, which is either a record or a diagnosis.

Rowley and I are sharing a branch again. I figure fame can wait until I hit my growth spurt, or until the gorillas stop shaving their backs, whichever comes first.

Please do not read the last paragraph, future historians. The banana was gross.
