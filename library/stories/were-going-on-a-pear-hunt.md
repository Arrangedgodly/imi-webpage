# We're Going on a Pear Hunt

*A parody of "We're Going on a Bear Hunt"*

We're going on a pear hunt.

We're going to pick a big one.

It's a gorgeous day.

We're not scared!

That was me — littlest of the troop, riding on Uncle Bo's shoulders — and it was Big Sister who said it first, thumping her chest like a tiny drum: *We're going on a pear hunt!* And Granny, who was old and gray and had crossed the river forty times and once out-stared a jaguar, said it too. And Uncle Bo, who was big and slow and laughed like falling coconuts, said it loudest of all.

Because this morning, Great-Aunt Fig had told us the secret. Pears, she said. A whole tree of them, golden and heavy and sweet, up the valley, over the hill, past the mist. The last pear tree in the whole green jungle, from the old days, when somebody's somebody planted it and went away.

"Big ones?" I asked.

"Big as your head," said Great-Aunt Fig.

"Bigger," said Granny. "Sweeter than a fig. Juicier than a mango. So juicy you hear monkeys three valleys away smacking their lips."

So we packed a plan (Granny's), a rope (Uncle Bo's), a spare rope (Big Sister's, for the first rope), and a brave heart (mine, borrowed).

And we went.

Uh-uh!

Grass!

Long, wavy grass!

Swishing and swaying, up past our waists, up past our ears — a green gold sea of grass with seeds like little spears.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to go through it!

Swishy swashy!

Swishy swashy!

The grass swished and the grass swashed, and seeds got in my ears and seeds got down my back, and Big Sister held my tail and Granny held Big Sister's tail and Uncle Bo held Granny's tail, so we went through the grass like one long monkey, swishy, swashy, swishy, swashy — until suddenly, the grass ran out, and there at the bottom of the valley was —

Uh-uh!

A river!

A deep, cold river!

Water rushing and flashing, sliding over stones, cold as a jaguar's nose.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to swim through it!

Splash splosh!

Splash splosh!

Brrr! It was cold, it was deep, it was filled with tickly fish, and Uncle Bo sat me on his head — "Passengers ride free!" — and we splashed and sploshed and sploshed and splashed, and Granny showed us how it was done, swimming on her back with her toes in the air, until we hauled out, drippy and shivery, on the far bank.

Where we found —

Uh-uh!

Mud!

Thick, oozy mud!

Squelchy, squerchy mud, the kind that grabs your foot and gurgles.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to squelch through it!

Squelch squerch!

Squelch squerch!

We sank to our ankles. We sank to our knees. Uncle Bo sank to his middle and said, "Hoo!" in a small voice, and Big Sister pulled his ear and I pulled her tail, and Granny — who was too wise for any of this — walked lightly across the top on all fours like a ballerina, going squelch, squerch, squelch, squerch, while the rest of us wallowed.

"I LOVE the mud!" I shouted.

"You're a monkey," said Granny. "Of course you do."

And past the mud was —

Uh-uh!

A forest!

A big, dark forest!

Trees going up forever, roots going down forever, dark as the inside of a fig.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to climb through it!

Stumble trip!

Stumble trip!

Roots grabbed our toes. Vines grabbed our tails. Things hooted. Things rustled. It was dark, dark, dark, and Big Sister held my hand tight, and Uncle Bo hummed a low hum so we could follow his big voice, and Granny said, "Eyes and ears, little fruits. Eyes and ears." Stumble trip, stumble trip — until the trees thinned, and the air went thin too, and cold, and white, and we climbed out of the treetops onto the shoulder of the mountain, where —

Uh-uh!

A snowstorm!

A swirling, whirling snowstorm!

Snow! In the jungle! Whirling around the mountain top like the sky had shaken out its feathers.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to go through it!

Hoooo wooo!

Hoooo wooo!

We pulled big leaves around us for coats and hunched into the wind, hoooo wooo, hoooo wooo, snow whistling past our ears and piling on our heads, and I rode inside Uncle Bo's arms with just my eyes showing, and Granny led, hunched and gray and unstoppable, calling back through the storm, "Not far now, little fruits! Not far now!" Hoooo wooo! Hoooo wooo!

And then the snow fell away behind us, and the mountain dipped down into a quiet green hollow — and there, at the end of the hollow, all alone, was the tree.

The pear tree.

And under its branches —

Uh-uh!

A cave!

A narrow, gloomy cave, a dark hole in the hillside, going down and down.

We can't go over it.

We can't go under it.

Oh no!

We've got to creep into it!

Tiptoe!

Tiptoe!

Cold air breathed out of the cave, smelling of old stone. We tiptoed, tiptoed, one step, two steps, three steps, and stopped.

WHAT'S THAT?

One shiny wet nose, twitching at the dark?

No.

One round golden thing, glowing in the gloom?

Yes.

Two big buzzful bees, circling, circling?

Yes.

One enormous, shaggy, honey-colored back, rising and falling, right in front of us?

Uh-oh.

A BEAR!

A GREAT BIG HUNGRY BEAR! ASLEEP UNDER THE PEARS!

The bear's nose twitched. The bear's eyes opened. The bear looked at us.

We looked at the bear.

The bear looked at the pears.

IT'S A BEAR!

BACK THROUGH THE CAVE — TIPTOE! TIPTOE! (Run, don't tiptoe! TIPTOE ANYWAY!)

BACK THROUGH THE SNOWSTORM! HOOOO WOOOO! HOOOO WOOOO!

BACK THROUGH THE FOREST! STUMBLE TRIP! STUMBLE TRIP!

BACK THROUGH THE MUD! SQUELCH SQUERCH! SQUELCH SQUERCH!

BACK THROUGH THE RIVER! SPLASH SPLOSH! SPLASH SPLOSH!

BACK THROUGH THE GRASS! SWISHY SWASHY! SWISHY SWASHY!

Faster, faster, the bear thundering behind us, roaring for pears and for monkeys, and Big Sister swung me up, and Granny swung Big Sister up, and Uncle Bo ran guard at the back going "BOO! BOO!" at the bear, which is a very brave thing to do when you are not even a little bit sure it will work —

Back to the troop tree!

Up the trunk!

Into the doorway!

Slam the door!

Under the leaves!

Under the leaves! Under the leaves! All of us in one big heap of hearts going boom-boom-boom, listening to the wind in the branches, listening to the grass beyond the river, listening, listening —

Nothing.

Gone.

The bear had gone home to grumble under his empty tree, wherever it was.

And we were never going on a pear hunt again.

"Never again," said Big Sister.

"Never ever again," said Uncle Bo.

"Never," said Granny.

And then, from somewhere inside her shawl, Granny pulled out one round, golden, glorious, bee-chased, bear-guarded, mountain-top pear — swiped clean off the tree as we ran, by the slyest granny in the whole jungle — and she split it, seed to stem, into four sweet, juicy, laughing shares.

"Never again," said Granny, winking at me.

"Until the pears are ripe next year."
