# Best Served Peeled

*A parody of "Best Served Cold"*

Spring came late to Talins, and Monza Marmosetto came with it.

She had taken the fortress at Serra for Grand Duke Apeso, climbing the cliffs at night while his real generals lolled at breakfast. They called her the Serpent of Talins. Her brother Benna counted the gold 'nanas and believed in her completely, which in that jungle made him the rarer of the two.

The duke invited them up to his balcony above the gorge.

"I'm told a serpent can survive anything," said Apeso, smiling like a well-fed crocodile. "Let's test it."

The world went red. When Monza next understood anything, Benna was gone, and she was falling, and the gorge floor came up like a debt collector.

Something with hungry eyes found her broken at the bottom of the ravine and put her back together — with wire, and spite, and a warning that her heart now beat "to no particular schedule." His name was Shenkt, though he rarely used it, and his hobby was unmaking locks at forty paces, to stay in practice. He had opinions about Apeso. Nobody with eyes that hungry has none.

There were six names on her list, and she wrote them on her knives so she wouldn't forget:

*Gobba, the duke's sneak. Moresco, his coin-counter. Ganmark, his general. Castor, the quiet one. Ario, the princeling. And at the top, underlined: APESO.*

"You can't do it alone," said Shenkt.

"Obviously," said Monza. "I need a barrel of monkeys."

She got one, more or less. Shivers, a shaggy northern ape trying very hard to be a better monkey ("I'm done with blood," he said, applying for blood work). Friendly, a placid orangutan who loved only order and numbers, and greeted her with "Four," and a banana arranged in strict order of ripeness. Old Cosca, a ruined capuchin general permanently soaked in palm wine, who described any atrocity as "a fine vintage." And Morveer, a preening proboscis poisoner, with his apprentice Day, who passed long journeys composing his own funeral speech.

"The trick, Day," Morveer observed, "is to appear modest, which is easy for me, as I am merely the finest mind of the age. Now fetch the strychnine, and do not hum."

Monza's revenge proceeded like fruit going bad: quietly, then all at once.

Gobba the sneak was drowned in his own rain barrel, which the rain resented, having never once been asked to harbor a schemer. Moresco the coin-counter licked his thumb to count his banknotes, which Morveer had made a terminal habit. ("Poisoned corners," Morveer explained, wounded that anyone needed it explained. "The *page* is the weapon, Day. Write that down.") General Ganmark met her blade to blade on a rooftop — they had been colleagues once, and he fought like it, politely — and Monza peeled his guard away strip by strip until there was nothing left of him but a fine suit and a long fall.

It was somewhere around Castor that things changed. Castor, the quiet one, kept a library and received her with tea.

"I fed the duke your brother's route," he said, pouring. "You should know I never enjoyed it. I am the only name on your list who will say so."

"That," said Monza, "is somehow worse," and while he poured she used the knife, and it was quiet, and she did not drink the tea. She had become the cold thing the gorge had made her, and she knew it, and did it anyway.

Shivers stood down the next morning. He had lost an eye at Serra's fall, kept it in a jar, and gained a philosophy to keep the other one company.

"There's a difference," he said, "between justice and just-us. I came south to stop being an animal, Monza. You're the best climbing animal I ever met, and it scares me what you're turning into."

"Good," said Monza, who by then had very few words left that weren't on the list.

He was gone by dawn, which was a mercy. They had nearly been something to each other, and neither of them could have afforded the invoice.

Friendly stayed, because Friendly had numbered the days of the job, and it was not yet the last number. Cosca stayed because he was owed money and palm wine, in that order — though he also, it emerged much later, sold Monza's plans to Apeso for a boat's worth of wine money, and then sold Apeso's plans back to Monza for a vineyard, both transactions, he insisted, solemn. Morveer stayed because he was enjoying himself, and because Day had begun sharpening things at odd hours, which kept him alert.

Ario the princeling died in his hammock, believing to the end that his assassins were admirers. And then there was only the top name, in a tower above Talins, waiting with wine.

The tower did not open easily. Shenkt came up the stairs with her, unmade the locked door with a sound like a struck bell, and declined, as ever, to explain. "If I explain," he said, "it sounds simple. It is. That is why nobody expects it."

"You survived," said Grand Duke Apeso, unhappily. "You were always the blade, never the hand. I never feared you, Serpent. I feared your brother — did you know he dreamed of thrones? On your knees or his, someone was going to rule Talins through that pretty face. I trimmed the branch. That is all a ruler is: a prudent pruner."

In the silence after, she heard Benna counting gold 'nanas in a room that no longer existed, and the counting stopped.

Monza looked at him for a long time. She thought about the gorge floor, and wire, and Benna's grin at the counting table, and how the old Monza would have served this slow and cold.

She had learned enough of herself by then to make it quick.

"You put it in words, finally," she said. "That's all I wanted." The knife agreed with her.

Morveer, deciding that very evening to poison Monza and Day for their own good, toasted his own cleverness with coffee Day had poured. "The antidote was in the sugar, master," said Day. "No — you switched the labels last week. You labeled them." It was the longest sentence she had ever wasted on him, and the last. She read his funeral speech at the graveside, edited for length, and it was, everyone agreed, the finest thing he ever composed.

They say the Serpent never took the ducal chair, though half of Talins begged her to. She sat in the anteroom instead, and guarded the door, and made the new young duke behave — because some cities do not need a ruler so much as a reptile in the shadows, keeping the fruit honest.

"The job," said Friendly, at the end, "was one hundred and nine days. I counted. Best hundred and nine I ever did."

"You counted the people too?" asked Monza.

"I counted everything," said Friendly. "That's how I know some of it's missing."

Years later, young monkeys of Talins would ask her the old question: is revenge sweet?

"Revenge is a dish best served cold," Monza Marmosetto would say, peeling a banana with her knives. "And 'nanas are best served peeled. Never confuse the two, or you'll be eating something with no sweetness left at all. Now count. Both hands. Friendly hates it when you skip numbers."
