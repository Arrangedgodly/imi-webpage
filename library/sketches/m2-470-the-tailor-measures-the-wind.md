# The Tailor Measures the Wind

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: bespoke tailoring, luxury fittings, and designers who try to measure a moving breeze with a tape measure*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: TAILOR, a macaque; CUSTOMER, a gibbon; ASSISTANT, a baboon; DESIGNER, an orangutan; FAN OPERATOR, a capuchin.

[A tailoring studio. A coat form stands beside a small fan. The customer wears a cape that keeps lifting.] 

CUSTOMER: I need a coat that fits the wind at the hilltop.

TAILOR: Wind is not a body measurement.

DESIGNER: We can make a custom breeze profile.

ASSISTANT: The fan has three settings.

[The fan blows. The tailor stretches a tape measure toward the customer's cape. The tape lifts and wraps around the coat form.] 

TAILOR: The waist is four inches longer.

CUSTOMER: The wind has no waist.

DESIGNER: It has a silhouette.

[The assistant pins the cape to the form. The customer holds still. The fan operator turns the fan off; the cape falls flat.] 

TAILOR: Now I can measure the shoulder seam.

CUSTOMER: I need the coat to work while the wind is on.

[The designer drapes a light sample fabric over the form. The fan turns on and pulls the fabric over the tailor's head.] 

TAILOR: I have disappeared into the forecast.

ASSISTANT: The fabric is too light.

[The customer asks for something warm but not heavy. The tailor picks a wool sample. It holds shape in the breeze. The designer adds a long ribbon; it whips around the form and tangles the tape.] 

DESIGNER: The ribbon shows movement.

TAILOR: It shows that the tape is tangled.

[The assistant frees the tape. The customer tries the wool sample, walks past the fan, and raises both arms. The coat stays in place.] 

CUSTOMER: This feels steady.

TAILOR: It fits your shoulders. The hood will shield the wind without covering your ears.

[The fan operator switches between low and high. The customer turns into the breeze, then sideways. The tailor checks the shoulder seam after each position.] 

FAN OPERATOR: Low setting.

TAILOR: Seam holds.

FAN OPERATOR: High setting.

TAILOR: Still holds. No need to measure the wind.

[The designer removes the ribbon and rolls up the sample fabric. The tailor marks the wool pattern on paper. The fan is switched off.] 

[The designer pins the paper pattern to a cork board. The fan operator checks that the fan guard is secure, then sets the fan to low. The tailor holds a narrow paper strip near the customer's shoulder.]

TAILOR: The paper moves, but the wool seam stays put. We will cut the pattern with room for a sweater.

CUSTOMER: And the hood?

ASSISTANT: It has a drawstring, but it will not cover your ears.

[The customer tries the sample again and turns toward the fan. The hood shifts back slightly; the assistant adjusts the shoulder pin rather than measuring the air.]

DESIGNER: The wind has changed the silhouette.

TAILOR: The silhouette is the customer. The wind is only the fitting room.

[The designer removes the long ribbon from the table and clips it to a stationary display form. It flutters in the fan's high setting while the customer's sample remains in place.]

FAN OPERATOR: High setting.

CUSTOMER: I can still hear the assistant.

ASSISTANT: Both ears are free.

[The tailor marks the shoulder seam and hands the pattern to the customer. The fan stops; the ribbon falls against the display form.]

FINAL IMAGE: The customer leaves with a pinned coat pattern while the tape measure rests neatly on the tailor's table.
