# The Legend of the Map Legend

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: guided hiking tours, dramatic map symbols, and guides who turn a short trail into an expedition*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-009*

Cast: Guide Tali, a ring-tailed lemur; hikers Neri, a macaque, and Vex, a gibbon; and ranger Omi, a capuchin.

EXT. PARK TRAILHEAD - MORNING

Tali holds a large map. A dotted line marks a short loop around the pond.

TALI: This trail has three stages: the ascent, the crossing, and the return.

NERI: It is flat.

TALI: The map uses dramatic language.

VEX: What does the triangle mean?

TALI: A hill.

OMI: That is a picnic table.

Tali turns the map upside down. The triangle becomes a table icon.

TALI: The legend says triangle means picnic table.

NERI: We are standing beside one.

VEX: Then we have reached stage one.

They start along the path. Tali narrates every turn as if leading an expedition. They reach a wooden bridge over a shallow stream. Vex steps onto it. The bridge squeaks.

TALI: The crossing has begun.

OMI: The bridge is ten feet long.

VEX: The sound makes it feel longer.

Neri checks the map. The dotted route passes the pond and returns to the trailhead. A blue line crosses it.

NERI: What is the blue line?

TALI: Water.

OMI: It is the stream.

TALI: We have discovered the legend.

They reach a bench. Tali describes it as an outpost. Omi says it was installed for tired hikers. Vex sits, and the bench creaks like the bridge.

VEX: The expedition is under way.

Neri sees a sign reading LOOP CLOSED FOR REPAIR. Tali says they can continue to the pond and return on the same path. Omi explains that the sign refers to the far side of the loop, where workers are repairing a railing.

TALI: The return route is the same route in reverse.

VEX: That is a bold navigation choice.

The hikers turn around. They pass the bridge again. It squeaks. Tali marks the map with a pencil. The group returns to the trailhead in seven minutes.

TALI: The journey has taken us from the beginning to the end and back.

NERI: We walked around the pond.

VEX: I crossed a bridge twice.

OMI: It was still ten feet long.

Tali shows the map to a new group of hikers. She has circled the picnic table and labeled it "summit." Omi erases the label.

TALI: The guide has been updated.

NERI: It is a table again.

The new hikers ask how long the loop takes. Tali says it depends on how often they stop to interpret the map.

VEX: We should have stopped more.

They sit at the picnic table. Tali folds the map so the loop disappears and the triangle remains.

The next hikers arrive. Tali offers the map right-side up. The triangle now points to the picnic table, and no one mistakes it for a summit.
