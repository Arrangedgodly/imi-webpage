# The Canoe Tour Has a Landlord

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: guided outdoor tours, real-estate showings, and guides who introduce a riverbank as if it were a rental property*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-015*

Cast: GUIDE, a macaque; LANDLORD, a baboon; PADDLER, an orangutan; TOURIST, a capuchin; RANGER, a gibbon.

[A riverside dock. The guide, paddler, and tourist board the tour canoe. The landlord and ranger board a second canoe together. Across the water, one willow shades the landlord's private bank; a separate willow stands beside the town's public landing.] 

GUIDE: On your left, a scenic landing.

LANDLORD: Private property.

TOURIST: The landing?

LANDLORD: The bank, the landing, and the willow.

RANGER: Not the river.

LANDLORD: I have not decided about the river.

ACT ONE

[The canoes push off. The landlord paddles close behind the tour boat and hands out printed rules. A sheet blows loose. The paddler reaches for it, and the tour canoe spins slowly in a circle.] 

PADDLER: I have read this same rule three times.

TOURIST: It keeps coming around.

[The guide pulls the paper in with a paddle. The landlord announces a landing fee for any canoe touching shore. The ranger points to the permit, which allows the tour to use the public channel and landing.] 

RANGER: You can charge for your private bank. The marked landing is town property.

LANDLORD: Then I will charge for the view.

GUIDE: Which view?

LANDLORD: Any view containing my willow.

[The tour reaches the private bank. The guide keeps the boat moving. The landlord displays his boundary map, but it is upside down. The ranger rotates it. The map shows a narrow strip of public water and a public landing farther downstream.]

LANDLORD: You cannot stop at this bank.

GUIDE: We are not stopping here.

TOURIST: We are passing it very slowly.

[The tourist has dropped a paddle. The paddler retrieves it with a hook, and the canoe resumes its route. At the public landing, the guide calls for both boats to come alongside the dock. The landlord's clipboard is in a dry bag tethered to his waist. The ranger steps onto the dock first. As the landlord leans across to follow, the bag slides into the water. The ranger catches its handle with a long pole and returns it before it drifts beneath the dock.] 

LANDLORD: Thank you. The dry bag is part of the viewing package.

RANGER: It is part of your equipment.

ACT TWO

[Both canoes are tied at the public dock: the guide, paddler, and tourist step from the first; the landlord joins the ranger from the second. The ranger reads the public access sign. The group takes one photograph beside the public willow, clearly away from the landlord's private willow across the river.]

TOURIST: Is this view included?

RANGER: The landing is public.

LANDLORD: I will list it as a shared amenity.

GUIDE: You do not own this landing.

LANDLORD: Then I will add a photo fee.

[The guide shows the landlord the photo on the camera. In the background, the public sign says NO PRIVATE FEES. The landlord studies the picture, opens his listing page, and types: "Public landing. No fee." He tries to add a separate charge for the willow, then notices the photo shows the public willow, not his.] 

LANDLORD: I need a new photograph.

RANGER: From your own bank.

[The landlord starts toward his canoe, then turns back to reclaim the dry bag. The guide hands it to him from the dock bench where the landlord set it while typing. The paddler checks the knots holding the tour canoe, and the landlord checks the map before planning his return paddle.]

FINAL IMAGE: On the shore, the group admires its single photo while the landlord edits his listing to say PUBLIC LANDING - NO VIEWING FEE.
