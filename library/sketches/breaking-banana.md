# Breaking Banana

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: 24-hour breaking news coverage and breathless studio analysis*
*By: IMI Broadcast Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M1*

[NEWS STING. ANCHOR ADA, a mandrill, sits at a desk bolted to her branch. A red banner reads "BANANA ON COUNTER." FINCH, a capuchin reporter, stands beside a breakroom counter, gripping his microphone with his tail.] 

ADA: We interrupt the regular schedule with breaking news. A banana has been left unattended near the office microwave. Finch is live at the scene. What can you tell us?

FINCH: Ada, the banana is yellow, slightly curved, and resting beside a mug that says "World's Best Team." We have not been able to confirm which team.

ADA: Is the banana safe?

FINCH: We don't know. A witness says it was placed here by an employee. Another witness says it may have been here yesterday.

ADA: What does the banana's owner say?

FINCH: We have not identified the owner, but a monkey named Jules was seen looking toward the counter.

JULES, OFF CAMERA: I was looking for my coffee.

ADA: Finch, do we know if the banana is part of a larger pattern?

FINCH: There were two bananas in this breakroom on Monday. Today there is one. Experts say the number has changed.

ADA: We're joined by our office fruit analyst, Dr. Peel.

DR. PEEL: Thank you, Ada. The banana may be fresh, overripe, or between those states. We cannot rule out fruit.

ADA: What should employees do?

DR. PEEL: Keep a reasonable distance. Do not shake it. Do not post its photo with a location tag.

ADA: Finch, the situation appears to be escalating.

FINCH: A colleague has approached the counter. She is reaching for the banana. She has paused. She's reading the mug. She's looking left and right.

ADA: Does she appear to be the owner?

FINCH: She is checking her badge. No, that is a cafeteria card.

ADA: Could the banana belong to the mug?

DR. PEEL: Mugs cannot own fruit under current company policy.

ADA: Finch, have you secured the perimeter?

FINCH: I asked everyone to stand back. Facilities has placed a wet floor sign beside the banana.

ADA: Is there a spill?

FINCH: No. The sign is the closest thing we have to a barrier.

[NEWSROOM PHONE RINGS. ADA LISTENS.] 

ADA: We have a caller who says the banana is theirs. Caller, can you prove it?

CALLER: I brought it in this morning. I wrote my initials on the peel.

FINCH: We can confirm there are initials. They appear to say "B" or a small drawing of a bee.

CALLER: My name is Beatrice.

ADA: That matches. Finch, please return the banana to Beatrice.

FINCH: I can't. The banana is now inside the breakroom's no-claim zone, an area created when the wet floor sign went up.

ADA: How do we resolve the standoff?

DR. PEEL: The sign must be moved by someone with floor authority.

ADA: Who has floor authority?

FINCH: Facilities is on lunch. The breakroom is closed until further notice.

[BEATRICE WALKS PAST FINCH, TAKES HER BANANA, AND LEAVES.] 

ADA: We are getting reports of movement. The banana has left the counter.

FINCH: It has been retrieved by its owner. No injuries, no spill, and the wet floor sign remains in place.

ADA: Is this story over?

DR. PEEL: There may be follow-up if the banana is eaten.

ADA: We'll keep a camera here all afternoon. I'm Ada Vale. This has been Breaking Banana. Stay with us for continuous coverage of a mug that may be chipped.

[THE RED BANNER CHANGES TO "MUG STATUS UNCLEAR." NEWS STING.]
