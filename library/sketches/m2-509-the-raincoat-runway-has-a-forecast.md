# The Raincoat Runway Has a Forecast

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: fashion runway shows, seasonal collections, and weather-conscious models who cannot stop demonstrating their garments*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-014*

Cast: DESIGNER, a macaque; MODEL, a gibbon; ANNOUNCER, an orangutan; STYLIST, a baboon; WEATHER REPORTER, a capuchin.

[A fashion runway. The designer adjusts a yellow raincoat on the model. The reporter holds a small umbrella beside a forecast screen showing SUN.]

ANNOUNCER: The collection is called Dry Season, despite today's clear sky.

DESIGNER: These coats are about possibility.

WEATHER REPORTER: There is no rain in the forecast.

STYLIST: We can make our own atmosphere.

[The stylist sprays a fine mist into the air. The model steps forward. The raincoat hood catches the mist and turns inside out.]

MODEL: The hood has made a decision.

DESIGNER: Turn the hood back. That is not the look.

[The model walks to the end of the runway and poses. The coat's sleeves are too long and cover both hands.]

WEATHER REPORTER: The model is prepared for weather and unable to clap.

ACT ONE

[The designer signals for the next outfit. The model turns around and walks back, but a static cling strip sticks the hem to one shoe.]

MODEL: I am attached to my own coat.

[The stylist crouches to unstick it. The announcer introduces a transparent poncho.]

ANNOUNCER: The second look is called "Cloud Confidence."

WEATHER REPORTER: The screen still says sun.

DESIGNER: The outfit can be aspirational.

[The model pulls the poncho over the raincoat. It inflates with the stage fan and lifts the hood like a sail.]

MODEL: I am drifting toward the forecast.

[The designer grabs the hem. The stylist switches off the fan. The poncho collapses over the model's head.]

STYLIST: I have lost the model.

[The model lifts the poncho and keeps walking. The crowd applauds. The designer bows as if the mishap were choreographed.]

WEATHER REPORTER: Should I update the forecast?

DESIGNER: Only if it rains.

[The reporter checks the screen. A tiny cloud icon appears, followed by a 10% chance of drizzle. Everyone onstage opens an umbrella at once.]

MODEL: That is one small cloud.

ANNOUNCER: The collection has responded.

[The reporter taps the wrong control. The forecast screen switches to HEAVY RAIN, and a ceiling nozzle releases a brief test spray. The stylist holds the poncho over the model while the designer covers the score cards.]

WEATHER REPORTER: The studio effect is on.

MODEL: The forecast is now accurate for this runway.

[The reporter switches the screen back to sun. The spray stops, but drops cling to the coat. The model turns, and the raincoat makes a squeak at every step.]

ANNOUNCER: We call that the wet-weather beat.

DESIGNER: We call it a wet sleeve.

ACT TWO

[The model removes the poncho and drapes it over the designer, who now holds both the umbrella and the mist sprayer. The raincoat sleeves are clipped short with fabric pins.]

DESIGNER: The final look is practical.

WEATHER REPORTER: I do not believe you.

[The stage fan starts again. The designer's poncho hood inflates. The model walks beside them and keeps it from covering the designer's face.]

FINAL IMAGE: The designer and model share one umbrella beneath the studio sun graphic while the reporter changes the forecast to "indoor wind."
