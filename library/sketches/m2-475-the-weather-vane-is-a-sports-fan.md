# The Weather Vane Is a Sports Fan

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: sports talk radio and live weather reports that treat wind direction as team commentary*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: WEATHER HOST, a macaque; SPORTS HOST, a baboon; ROOFER, an orangutan; REPORTER, a gibbon; VANE, a capuchin.

[A rooftop broadcast set. A weather vane turns above a safe marked platform. The hosts stand behind a rail.]

WEATHER HOST: Wind from the west, light and steady.

SPORTS HOST: The vane points west. That is where the home team is playing.

VANE: I am pointing toward the wind's origin, not choosing a team.

REPORTER: The vane has joined our broadcast.

ROOFER: The vane does not talk. That is a loose weather sensor on the monitor.

[The monitor displays an arrow toward the stadium. The sports host leans closer. The vane turns as the breeze changes.] 

SPORTS HOST: It has switched sides.

WEATHER HOST: The wind shifted north.

VANE: I can point north without supporting anybody.

[The reporter asks the roofer to check the vane's base. The roofer stays behind the rail and uses a long-handled inspection mirror.] 

ROOFER: The mount is secure. The sensor cable is loose at the monitor.

SPORTS HOST: Could we tighten it before the game starts?

WEATHER HOST: We can report the weather first.

[The sports host reads a dramatic imaginary score from the vane's direction. The vane turns again. The host pivots to follow it, stepping in place rather than crossing the marked safe zone.] 

SPORTS HOST: The visitors are now favored.

VANE: The wind is from the east. Your prediction is still your own.

[The roofer secures the monitor cable from the set's access panel. The display now matches the vane. The reporter asks for the forecast; the weather host gives it plainly.] 

WEATHER HOST: A breeze from the east, easing by sunset. No rain expected.

SPORTS HOST: And the match?

VANE: I have no data on the match.

[The sports host lowers the microphone. The reporter turns the monitor to the proper weather graphic. The vane continues to turn freely as the wind shifts.] 

REPORTER: The vane has declined to predict the final score.

SPORTS HOST: It refuses to take a position.

WEATHER HOST: That is how a weather vane works.

[The reporter checks a message from a listener asking whether the wind predicts a home win. The sports host leans toward the microphone, ready to answer.]

SPORTS HOST: The forecast does not include the score.

WEATHER HOST: It includes direction and speed.

VANE: At present, the wind is light from the east.

REPORTER: The vane has made its only official statement.

[The sports host starts drawing arrows on the back of the score card. The weather host flips it over to reveal the forecast.]

WEATHER HOST: Keep the weather on the forecast card.

SPORTS HOST: Then I will keep my predictions on this one.

[The reporter places the cards in separate folders. The vane rotates a quarter turn as a breeze passes. No one follows it around the platform.]

VANE: I have changed direction again.

SPORTS HOST: We are staying with our own script.

FINAL IMAGE: The arrow points east above the quiet set while both hosts read from their own separate cards.
