# The Portrait Painter Repaints the Waiting Room

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: prestige portrait commissions and clients who demand to look both candid and extremely important*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-015*

Cast: PAINTER, a gibbon; MAYOR, a baboon; ASSISTANT, a capuchin; CRITIC, an orangutan; NURSE, a macaque.

[A clinic waiting room. The painter sets an easel beside three chairs. The mayor sits with a sash and a folder of pose requests.]

MAYOR: I want the portrait to feel spontaneous.

PAINTER: What are you doing?

MAYOR: Holding my official folder.

ASSISTANT: It is upside down.

[The critic circles the easel, studying the blank canvas.]

CRITIC: The room has no depth.

NURSE: It is a waiting room. People are waiting.

ACT ONE

[The painter asks the mayor to relax his shoulders. The mayor loosens them. His sash slides over his face.]

MAYOR: Is this candid?

PAINTER: It is obstructed.

[The assistant brings a small stool. The mayor sits, but the stool is too low and his folder rises above his chin.]

CRITIC: Now he looks monumental.

NURSE: He looks like he is hiding from a blood test.

[The nurse calls the next patient. The mayor stands automatically, leaving the sash and folder behind. The painter sketches the empty chair.]

PAINTER: That is the first honest pose.

MAYOR: I was not posing.

CRITIC: The absence is compelling.

[The mayor resumes his seat. The assistant shifts a potted plant behind him to make the background elegant. Its leaves tickle his ear. He tries not to move. The painter starts a careful outline.]

MAYOR: I feel a leaf.

PAINTER: Stay natural.

MAYOR: I am naturally being tickled.

[The critic suggests a more powerful gaze. The mayor looks directly at the painter; the nurse notices he is staring at the wall clock.]

NURSE: Your appointment is next.

MAYOR: I thought the painting was the appointment.

[The assistant tapes the pose list to the easel. The mayor asks for "approachable authority." The painter asks him to hold the folder lower and keep one hand on the chair. This puts the folder over the mayor's name badge. The assistant flips the folder right side up, revealing a grocery list instead of official papers.]

CRITIC: It tells a story.

MAYOR: It tells my assistant what to buy.

ACT TWO

[The painter completes the sketch of the mayor seated beside the plant, holding the grocery list. The mayor sees the drawing and laughs. The critic says it captures the burden of leadership. The nurse calls him into the exam room.]

MAYOR: Can we finish after my appointment?

PAINTER: The portrait is finished.

[The mayor exits. The painter turns the canvas toward the waiting room; the plant now leans over the chair as if taking the mayor's place.]

[The mayor returns with a bandage on one finger from the appointment. He asks the painter to add a heroic hand gesture. The painter shows the sketch: the mayor is holding the grocery list in one hand and the chair in the other. The critic says the composition contains both public duty and private errands. The nurse calls from inside the clinic that the mayor has left his folder on the stool.]

MAYOR: Please add the folder.

PAINTER: It is upside down again.

MAYOR: Then it is historically accurate.

FINAL IMAGE: The critic studies the empty chair and asks the nurse for a portrait of the plant.
