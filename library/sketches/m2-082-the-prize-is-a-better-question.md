# The Prize Is a Better Question

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: quiz-show formats that reward confident nonsense over answers*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-004*

Cast: HOST LUMA, a lemur; CONTESTANT RO, a macaque; CONTESTANT TESS, a capuchin; and ANNOUNCER VEE, a gibbon.

[GAME SHOW BELLS. A SCOREBOARD reads: KNOWLEDGE, CONFIDENCE, AND VOLUME.] 

LUMA: Welcome to The Brightest Branch. The first question: what do young monkeys learn by watching adults?

RO: How to peel fruit.

TESS: Which branches hold their weight.

LUMA: Both correct. Tess wins the point because she rang in while Ro was still answering.

RO: The button is on her side.

VEE: The rules state that answers may be improved by proximity to the buzzer.

LUMA: Next: what is the best tool for reaching a fruit above your head?

RO: A long stick.

TESS: A branch you can climb safely.

LUMA: The judges accept both, but Ro gets the bonus for saying "tool" with confidence.

RO: I do not have a stick.

VEE: Confidence is awarded regardless of inventory.

LUMA: Final round. Contestants must ask a question the other person cannot answer.

RO: Why is the scoreboard shaped like a leaf?

LUMA: Because we are in a tree studio.

TESS: Why does the bell ring when nobody wins?

VEE: It is the sound effect for tension.

[THE BELL RINGS TWICE.] 

LUMA: An exciting moment. One point to the bell.

RO: I have a question for the host. Why do we need points if the questions have more than one useful answer?

LUMA: Because we have a prize.

TESS: What is it?

VEE: The winner receives a golden question mark.

[THE PRIZE IS LIFTED. IT IS A BENT TWIG.] 

RO: That looks like a tool.

TESS: It looks like a twig that got bent.

LUMA: It is a question mark, if you hold it sideways.

RO: Then I have another question. Can I use it to reach the fruit above the stage?

LUMA: You may test it.

[RO REACHES WITH THE TWIG. A FIG DROPS INTO TESS'S LAP.] 

TESS: The prize works.

VEE: Does that make her the winner?

LUMA: She has the fruit.

RO: I asked the better question.

TESS: We can split the fig.

VEE: The scoreboard has awarded the bell a point, but it has no place to stand.

LUMA: Put the bell beside its own score.

[THE BELL IS SET ON THE SCOREBOARD. ITS WEIGHT TIPS THE BOARD.] 

RO: The scores are sliding.

TESS: My point is under the table.

VEE: We need a tie breaker.

LUMA: The contestants will answer one question together: what should we do with the fig?

RO: Split it.

TESS: Share the seeds for planting.

LUMA: The judges accept both answers.

VEE: That is two answers, not one.

LUMA: The fig can be split and the seeds saved.

RO: There are seeds in the prize basket too.

TESS: The bent twig can mark where we plant them.

[THE SCOREBOARD SETTLES AS THE BELL ROLLS INTO A CATCH TRAY.] 

VEE: The bell has been returned to the game.

LUMA: It has also stopped the score from falling.

RO: Does that earn another point?

LUMA: No. It earns a job as a paperweight.

TESS: The game show now has a winner and a useful prize.

VEE: The viewers are asking what the final score was.

LUMA: It is under the table with Tess's point.

[SHE REACHES UNDER THE TABLE. THE SCOREBOARD TIPS AGAIN.] 

RO: The points are still moving.

LUMA: Tune in next week to find out where they land.

LUMA: Our judges award the prize to both contestants, and the bell to itself.

VEE: The bell declines the prize.

[THE BELL RINGS ONCE. A STAGEHAND CARRIES IT OFF.] 

LUMA: Tune in next week, when the score will be explained after it changes.
