# The Whispering Room Has an Audience

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: sound installations, quiet-room etiquette, and gallery guides who whisper into sensitive microphones*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: GUIDE, a macaque; SOUND ARTIST, a gibbon; VISITOR ONE, an orangutan; VISITOR TWO, a capuchin; TECHNICIAN, a baboon.

[A small gallery room. A microphone hangs over a bowl of sand. A sign says "QUIET PLEASE."]

GUIDE: The installation captures the sound of a breath.

VISITOR ONE: Like this?

[The visitor breathes softly. The speaker amplifies it into a gust.] 

SOUND ARTIST: The microphone is too close.

TECHNICIAN: The room is designed to hear small sounds.

VISITOR TWO: Does it hear the sign?

[The guide whispers the title. The speakers repeat it in a low rumble.] 

GUIDE: "A Room Listening."

VISITOR ONE: It sounds like the room is answering.

SOUND ARTIST: It is the playback delay.

[The technician turns down the speaker. The room becomes quiet. The first visitor takes one step; the floorboard creaks loudly.] 

VISITOR ONE: The floor is speaking now.

TECHNICIAN: That board is loose.

[The sound artist asks everyone to remain still while she records the sand. A visitor's tail brushes the sign. It falls to the floor with a slap.] 

VISITOR TWO: I think the room heard us.

GUIDE: The sign was in the room.

[The technician picks up the sign and moves it to a stand outside the microphone's range. The floorboard is marked with a small piece of tape. The artist sets the bowl on a soft cloth.] 

SOUND ARTIST: I wanted the grain of sand to shift, not the building.

[She tips the bowl a little. One grain slides. The speakers play a tiny scratch, then echo it. The guide nods.] 

GUIDE: That sound is clear.

VISITOR ONE: It is very small.

SOUND ARTIST: It is one grain, not a storm.

[Visitor Two asks whether people can talk in the room. The guide says yes, but the microphone will amplify whatever is nearby. They take two steps back and speak at normal volume. Their voices arrive without echo.] 

VISITOR TWO: We can hear each other better from here.

TECHNICIAN: The speaker is aimed at the bowl, not the audience.

[The artist flips the playback switch to OFF. The grain remains visible in the bowl. The sign stays upright outside the mic zone.] 

[The technician asks the visitors to try the installation one at a time. Visitor One breathes near the bowl; the grain moves. Visitor Two stands beyond the taped floorboard and hears only the small sound.]

VISITOR TWO: I can hear it without hearing the room answer.

SOUND ARTIST: The delay is off now.

The guide turns the sign so "QUIET PLEASE" faces the entrance, not the microphone. The technician checks the floorboard once and leaves it marked for repair. No one whispers into the speakers. The artist gives visitors a moment to listen without naming what they should feel.

FINAL IMAGE: One grain of sand rests in the bowl while the audience stands quietly beyond the taped floorboard.
