# The Book Club Emergency

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: celebrity book-club podcasts and overconfident literary interpretation*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-003*

Cast: HOST REN, a macaque; GUEST LIV, a gibbon novelist; PRODUCER FINCH, a parrot; and CALLER OBO, a baboon reader.

[A recording booth. Three copies of the same novel sit on a table. One has a banana peel tucked inside.]

REN: Welcome to Read It Like You Mean It, the book club where we finish one book and explain what every object symbolizes.

LIV: I wrote a mystery about a missing button.

REN: The button represents grief.

LIV: It was on a coat.

FINCH: The network needs a deeper takeaway for the clip.

REN: The coat represents the social fabric.

LIV: The coat belongs to the detective.

REN: The detective represents the reader.

LIV: The reader is just trying to find the button.

[Ren flips through the book and finds the banana peel.]

REN: What does the peel symbolize?

LIV: Someone had a banana during the interview.

FINCH: This is our cover art now.

[SFX: PHONE LINE OPENS.]

REN: Obo is calling to discuss the ending. What did you think?

OBO: I liked that the detective found the button in the pocket.

REN: And what does the pocket represent?

OBO: The place where people put buttons.

LIV: Thank you.

REN: Was there anything you disliked?

OBO: You called the gardener a suspect for four chapters because he owned a rake.

REN: The rake represented suspicion.

LIV: The rake was for leaves.

FINCH: We need a debate segment.

REN: Obo, would you say the ending disappointed you?

OBO: No. The button was where the detective looked.

REN: A bold position.

[Finch opens the second book. A pressed leaf falls out.]

FINCH: What's this?

LIV: A bookmark.

REN: No. It's a symbol of the reader's journey.

OBO: I put it there.

REN: Then the journey belongs to you.

OBO: I just needed to save my page.

[Ren tries to hand the leaf to Liv. Liv closes the book on it, flattening it further.]

FINCH: We can sell bookmarks in the shop.

LIV: Please don't sell my leaf.

REN: What would you like readers to take from the book?

LIV: The button was missing because the coat was old. The detective found it because she checked the pocket.

OBO: That part made sense.

REN: We may have to cut the interpretation segment.

FINCH: Then what fills the hour?

OBO: Read a chapter.

[Ren looks at the clock. Finch reluctantly removes the sound-effects cue card.]

REN: This has been Read It Like You Mean It. Next week, we discuss a book with an actual hidden meaning.

LIV: It is a cookbook.

REN: Every recipe begins with a question.

[The banana peel slides off the table and lands on the script.]
REN: What does the missing button represent?

LIV: A button that is missing.

FINCH: The clip title is "Grief in a Pocket."

OBO: I would not click that.

REN: What would you click?

OBO: "Where the Button Went."

[Finch changes the title. The book's cover becomes visible again.]

FINCH: The simpler title has more listeners.

LIV: The button has been found.

REN: The episode has also been found.

[BLACKOUT.]
