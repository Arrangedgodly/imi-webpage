# Portrait Day for the Empty Chair

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: formal studio portraits and photo-day rituals that give props more attention than the people posing*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: PHOTOGRAPHER, a capuchin; MIRA, a macaque; DIRECTOR, a baboon; ASSISTANT, a gibbon.

[A portrait studio. A velvet chair sits beneath a bright spotlight. Mira waits at the edge of the backdrop.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: We will begin with the chair alone. It has excellent posture.

MIRA: The chair has no back muscles.

DIRECTOR: That's why we chose it.

[The photographer circles the chair, taking several pictures. The flash makes Mira blink.]

MIRA: Are we photographing the cast or the furniture?

PHOTOGRAPHER: The chair is the anchor of the composition.

ASSISTANT: The chair has been here since Tuesday.

DIRECTOR: It has been available longer than the actors.

[The director places a name card on the chair. It reads "MIRA."]

MIRA: That is my card.

DIRECTOR: We are testing the label.

MIRA: It is on the wrong subject.

[The photographer asks Mira to sit. She does. The chair squeaks. The assistant puts a folded cloth beneath one leg.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: Hold still. The chair is making a face.

MIRA: That is the sound of a loose screw.

[The director whispers to the assistant. The assistant moves the spotlight from Mira to the chair.]

MIRA: I have disappeared.

DIRECTOR: You are in the portrait as a supporting silhouette.

MIRA: I came for a headshot.

PHOTOGRAPHER: Your head is just outside the crop.

[Mira steps into the light. The chair is now in shadow. The photographer takes a picture.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: We have a beautiful portrait of you with no chair.

DIRECTOR: But the chair was the anchor.

MIRA: Then let it anchor the next one. Put us both in the frame.

[The assistant turns the chair to face the camera. Mira sits, but the chair is too low. She folds her arms under the seat, lifting it slightly.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: Excellent. You are holding the furniture up.

MIRA: This is not a furniture catalog.

DIRECTOR: It could be. The chair's expression is very natural.

[The assistant checks the chair leg and tightens its loose screw. The squeak stops. Mira sits again. The photographer frames her face, the chair, and the name card in one shot.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: Ready?

MIRA: Ready.

[Flash.]

PHOTOGRAPHER: Perfect. We will use it for the cast page.

DIRECTOR: And the chair page.

MIRA: The chair can have the back cover.

[The photographer lowers the camera. The director moves the name card onto Mira's lap. The chair is left uncredited.]

ASSISTANT: The chair would like another take.

MIRA: It can book its own session.


[Mira takes the proof to the door. The director asks her to hold it beside the chair for a final frame. She turns the proof toward the photographer.]

MIRA: One picture of me. One picture of the chair.

PHOTOGRAPHER: Agreed.

The camera clicks once on Mira, then once on the empty chair. The assistant labels the prints before handing them over. Mira leaves with her portrait; the chair stays for its own appointment.

FINAL IMAGE: Mira holds her portrait proof while the empty chair waits outside the spotlight with its screw firmly tightened.
