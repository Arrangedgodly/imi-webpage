# The Star Map Is Upside Down

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: planetarium demonstrations, confident astronomy lectures, and audience questions answered by rotating the whole sky*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: ASTRONOMER, a gibbon; HOST, a macaque; TECHNICIAN, a baboon; STUDENT, an orangutan; VISITOR, a capuchin.

[A planetarium. A star map is pinned to a board. The host points toward the ceiling.] 

HOST: The northern constellation is above us.

STUDENT: The map says south is at the top.

ASTRONOMER: The map is printed for the southern hemisphere. It is not upside down.

[The host rotates the map. The stars now face the floor.] 

HOST: We have turned the sky around.

TECHNICIAN: We have turned paper around.

[The dome lights up. A constellation appears near the floor because the projector is misaligned.] 

VISITOR: Is the sky falling?

ASTRONOMER: The projector is aimed low.

[The technician adjusts the projector. The star field moves across the ceiling. The host narrates the stars as if they are walking.] 

HOST: They are traveling west.

ASTRONOMER: The earth is rotating. The stars only appear to move.

STUDENT: The map has lines between the stars.

ASTRONOMER: Those lines are guides, not visible connections.

[The host draws a line across the dome with a laser pointer. It lands on a smoke detector.] 

HOST: There is the constellation of the alarm.

TECHNICIAN: Please do not point the laser at safety equipment.

[The student places the map correctly on a table, north up. The host tries to match the map to the ceiling but faces the wrong way.] 

STUDENT: Turn your body, not the map.

[The host turns. The stars align. The astronomer points out one constellation and explains that the map shows the southern horizon.] 

VISITOR: Is that the north star?

ASTRONOMER: No. It is a bright star in another region. The north star is not visible from this view.

HOST: We have found the edge of our map.

TECHNICIAN: We have found the edge of the projector's field.

[The host folds the map along its crease. The student tells the audience which direction they face. The technician centers the star field above them.] 

[The astronomer hands the student a small compass and asks the student to stand facing the southern horizon marked on the map. The student rotates the paper until its top edge matches the view. The host begins turning the paper again.]

STUDENT: The map is already oriented. The ceiling is the thing we are matching.

HOST: So I should move?

ASTRONOMER: You should stand beside the audience and point from their direction.

[The host walks to the opposite side of the board. The star map aligns with the projected sky. The visitor turns in place to follow the demonstration and bumps a padded seat.]

VISITOR: I have found west.

TECHNICIAN: You have found row four.

[The astronomer asks everyone to identify the southern horizon. The student points; the host points at the floor again, then quietly raises a hand and corrects themself. The technician dims the house lights only after moving the laser away from the detector.]

HOST: I will stop rotating the universe for the rest of the show.

ASTRONOMER: Good. We have only one universe in the projector.

FINAL IMAGE: The map lies north-up on the table while the projector shows the southern sky above the seated audience.
