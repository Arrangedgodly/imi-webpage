# The Time Capsule Opens on the Wrong Year

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: ceremonial time-capsule unveilings, civic speeches, and organizers who discover they labeled the wrong box*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-014*

Cast: MAYOR, a baboon; ARCHIVIST, an orangutan; STUDENT, a macaque; GROUNDSKEEPER, a gibbon; ANNOUNCER, a capuchin.

[A school courtyard. A metal capsule rests on a table beneath a banner reading OPENED IN 2026. The mayor holds a ceremonial key.]

ANNOUNCER: After twenty-five years, we open the time capsule from 2001.

ARCHIVIST: The label says 2011.

MAYOR: That is still a long time ago.

STUDENT: It is also not twenty-five years.

[The mayor inserts the key. It does not fit. The groundskeeper checks the capsule's lock and finds a small brass padlock.]

GROUNDSKEEPER: This is not the original capsule. It is the planter storage box.

[Everyone looks at the potted fern on the table. The announcer turns the banner around; its reverse side reads REUNION PLANTS.]

ACT ONE

EXT. COURTYARD - CONTINUOUS

The inventory shows the real capsule buried by the flagpole, under a 2001 stone. The groundskeeper moved that marker during landscaping. The program copied a garden map.

MAYOR: Can we call the planter box the first capsule?

ARCHIVIST: It contains soil and a fern, not archived objects.

ANNOUNCER: It is a living archive.

STUDENT: The fern is from last year.

[The groundskeeper moves the planter away from the ceremonial table and points to the flagpole. The group walks over. The stone is visible beside the pole, partly covered by a small mat.]

GROUNDSKEEPER: I moved it only a few feet. The capsule is still below it.

MAYOR: Why did the program say it was by the planter?

ARCHIVIST: The map was copied from the reunion garden plan.

[The announcer stops the procession music. The student places a cone around the marked spot. The groundskeeper fetches a small hand trowel.]

ACT TWO

EXT. FLAGPOLE - LATER

The groundskeeper carefully clears soil around the metal capsule while the archivist checks the date scratched into its lid. It reads 2001. The mayor tries to give a speech over the digging; the student asks them to wait until the capsule is open.

STUDENT: We should not read a message we have not retrieved.

MAYOR: I was describing the excitement.

[The trowel taps metal. The groundskeeper stops digging and brushes soil away. The capsule has a second lock, and the ceremonial key does not fit.]

ARCHIVIST: The key is decorative.

ANNOUNCER: It is a symbol of access.

GROUNDSKEEPER: The actual lock needs the maintenance key.

[The groundskeeper uses the correct key after the archivist confirms the capsule is dry and stable. Inside are a school photo, a paper schedule, and a sealed envelope. The archivist lifts the contents onto a clean cloth.]

STUDENT: The schedule is from 2001.

MAYOR: What does it say?

ARCHIVIST: The reunion was scheduled for 2026.

[The announcer looks at the banner, now clearly twenty-five years old. The students compare the names on the photo with the crowd.]

ACT THREE

EXT. COURTYARD - AFTERNOON

The archivist reads the letter: plant a tree by the flagpole and keep the path clear. The groundskeeper points to a tree by the sidewalk. The student says it was planted last year. The mayor announces the capsule was found where the old map predicted, not where the new program guessed.

MAYOR: We have opened the correct capsule on the correct anniversary.

STUDENT: After a brief planter detour.

[The announcer removes the inaccurate banner. The archivist places the documents into protective sleeves. The groundskeeper replaces the soil and sets the dated stone on top of the spot.]

ARCHIVIST: The record is complete. We can make a new capsule after the event.

ANNOUNCER: This time, we will label it before hiding it.

[The mayor returns the decorative key to its case. The student writes the new location on the archive map and asks the groundskeeper to verify it.]

FINAL IMAGE: The 2001 stone sits beside the flagpole with a fresh map pointing to the uncovered capsule's archive shelf.
