# The Lost Sock Arbitration

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: legal arbitration programs that turn household disagreements into televised testimony*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-010*

Cast: Arbitrator Seli, a gibbon; claimant Odo, a baboon; respondent Piri, a capuchin; bailiff Kito, a macaque.

INT. LAUNDRY ROOM - DAY

A sock lies on a table. Seli calls the hearing to order.

SELI: Odo claims this sock came from his laundry basket.

ODO: It has my initials.

PIRI: I claim it was in my basket.

KITO: The sock is inside the evidence circle.

Seli asks Odo to identify the initials. He says "O.B." Piri says those are the initials for "Odd Basket," the shared laundry basket. Kito measures the sock; it fits both of them.

Odo says he wore the matching sock yesterday. Piri says she washed a pair with the same stripe. Seli asks if either can identify the owner by a repair. Odo points to a blue stitch at the toe. Piri says she did that stitch after her sock caught on a branch.

KITO: The stitch is also blue.

Seli asks Piri if she can recognize the repair. Piri says she can, but Odo also borrowed her needle. Odo says he repaired a sock last week, but doesn't remember which one.

Seli places the sock between them. Both reach for it. Kito rings a small bell.

KITO: Only one party may touch the exhibit.

SELI: This is not a theft trial.

Odo presents a laundry tag. It reads "pair of two." Piri finds the matching tag in her pocket. Seli says the tags were probably sold together. Kito turns the sock inside out and finds a small piece of leaf stuck to the seam. Piri says she wore it in the garden. Odo says they both worked in the garden.

Seli asks what remedy they want. Odo wants the sock returned. Piri wants to finish folding the laundry. Kito points out that the sock is clean and currently has no partner.

Seli orders a practical test. Each person tries the sock on. It fits Odo's foot, then Piri's. Neither can prove ownership. Piri says she does not want to keep a sock without its mate. Odo says he can wear two different striped socks.

SELI: The claimant has no exclusive proof.

ODO: I accept shared custody.

Piri puts the sock in the common drawer. Seli rules that it belongs to whoever finds its match. Kito records the ruling on a sticky note and places it beside the drawer.

Kito checks the common drawer for a label. He writes "unmatched socks" and places both socks together. Odo asks if the case is closed. Seli says it can reopen when the pair is separated.

Kito places the note on the drawer.

TAG

Odo opens the drawer. The unmatched sock is still alone. Piri holds up the ruling note.

PIRI: The case remains open.

ODO: At least the sock is in the right drawer.
