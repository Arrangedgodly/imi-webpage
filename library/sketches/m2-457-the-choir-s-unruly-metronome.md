# The Choir's Unruly Metronome

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: classical rehearsal etiquette and conductors who blame a mechanical metronome for a tempo no one agreed on*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: CONDUCTOR, a macaque; SOPRANO, a gibbon; BASS, an orangutan; PERCUSSIONIST, a capuchin; STAGEHAND, a baboon.

[A rehearsal room. A wind-up metronome ticks loudly on the piano. The choir waits.]

CONDUCTOR: We will take the passage at a calm tempo.

[SFX: TICK-TICK-TICK-TICK-TICK-TICK.]

SOPRANO: The metronome is running.

BASS: It is running faster than calm.

CONDUCTOR: The device is enthusiastic.

[The conductor winds the metronome. It ticks faster.]

PERCUSSIONIST: It is now the lead singer.

STAGEHAND: I can slow it down.

[The stagehand moves the weight down the pendulum. The tick slows, then stops.]

CONDUCTOR: It has left the rehearsal.

SOPRANO: It was not invited.

[The conductor gives a hand cue. The choir begins. The percussionist enters early, following the last tick.]

CONDUCTOR: We need one shared beat.

PERCUSSIONIST: I followed the machine.

BASS: I followed the conductor.

SOPRANO: I followed the bass.

CONDUCTOR: We have three tempos and one chord.

[The stagehand places the metronome at the back of the room. It starts again from the spring. The choir's tails sway in rhythm.] 

STAGEHAND: The table is tilted.

[He levels the table with a folded program. The metronome settles into a steady tick.]

CONDUCTOR: Now we can rehearse.

BASS: At whose speed?

CONDUCTOR: At the marked tempo.

[He shows the score. The mark is a range, not a single number.]

SOPRANO: The composer allows several beats per minute.

PERCUSSIONIST: That is why the metronome could not choose.

[The conductor stops the metronome and asks each section to breathe before the entrance. They inhale together. The percussionist's tail swishes the stand; the page turns.] 

CONDUCTOR: That was not the cue.

STAGEHAND: It was the page turning.

[The stagehand clips the page to the stand. The choir waits for the conductor's raised hand. He gives the downbeat; the sections enter together at a steady, comfortable pace.] 

BASS: We are together.

SOPRANO: The machine is silent.

CONDUCTOR: The music is not.

[The conductor winds the metronome one last time, then leaves it stopped. The stagehand takes away the folded program and levels the table with a real wedge.] 


The conductor asks each singer to clap the marked tempo before the next run. The soprano claps slowly; the bass follows her. The percussionist claps at the metronome's old speed, then notices the stagehand's hand counting a steady four.

PERCUSSIONIST: I was listening to the machine even when it stopped.

CONDUCTOR: Listen to the group now.

They clap together, then sing the phrase once. The conductor stops them before the chord fades. No one asks to restart the metronome. The stagehand clips the score open to the correct measure, and the choir resumes from there.

FINAL IMAGE: The choir holds its final chord while the metronome rests on a level table with its pendulum still.
