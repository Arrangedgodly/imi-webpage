# A Courtroom for the Lost Sock

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: small-claims court, solemn evidence handling, and arguments over household property*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: JUDGE, a baboon; CLAIMANT, a macaque; RESPONDENT, a gibbon; CLERK, an orangutan; BAILIFF, a capuchin.

[A tiny courtroom. A single striped sock lies in an evidence tray.]

JUDGE: The court will hear the matter of one missing sock.

CLAIMANT: I own the left one.

RESPONDENT: It is right-footed.

JUDGE: It has no label.

CLERK: Evidence item A: one striped sock, slightly damp.

BAILIFF: It was found in the dryer.

CLAIMANT: The dryer is not an owner.

[The bailiff brings out a magnifying glass. The judge leans too close to the sock.]

JUDGE: I see a stripe.

RESPONDENT: That is the pattern.

CLERK: The court requests the sock's testimony.

[The clerk shakes the sock. A small pebble falls out.]

JUDGE: The sock has produced evidence.

CLAIMANT: I was walking by the garden.

RESPONDENT: The pebble is mine. I carried it in my pocket.

JUDGE: You both have a connection to the sock.

[The bailiff puts the pebble beside the sock. It rolls off the table.]

BAILIFF: Evidence has escaped.

[The clerk catches it in a coffee cup.] 

CLERK: The pebble is secure.

JUDGE: Now, how do we determine whether the sock is left or right?

CLAIMANT: The heel points this way.

RESPONDENT: It stretches the other way.

[The claimant puts the sock on one hand. The respondent puts the matching hand up; the cuff lines match.]

JUDGE: Do you have the other sock?

[Both contestants look at each other. The bailiff opens a small laundry basket. A second striped sock sits inside, folded under a towel.] 

BAILIFF: I found it when I collected the evidence.

CLERK: It was not in the chain of custody.

BAILIFF: It was in the laundry basket.

JUDGE: Court rules that the pair belongs together. The pebble belongs to the respondent. The dryer owns nothing.

CLAIMANT: I will take them home.

RESPONDENT: I washed them both.

JUDGE: Then share the pair. The court will not divide the socks.

[The bailiff returns the pair to the laundry basket. The claimant and respondent each reach for one. They stop, then pick up the basket together.] 

CLERK: The case is closed.

JUDGE: And the pebble?

[The respondent takes the coffee cup and pours the pebble into a pocket.] 

RESPONDENT: Returned to its owner.


[The judge asks the claimant to turn the sock inside out. A small label is sewn into the cuff, but it lists the washing temperature, not an owner.]

JUDGE: The sock has instructions, not a claimant.

RESPONDENT: I can identify the darn on the heel. I stitched it yesterday.

CLAIMANT: I was holding the basket while you mended it.

The clerk finds a loose thread that matches the repaired heel. The claimant confirms the thread came from their blue mitten, but the sock belongs to the pair they washed together. The judge says shared laundry is not proof of ownership. The bailiff places both socks on the evidence tray, toe to toe.

BAILIFF: They fit as a pair.

JUDGE: That is the first agreement in this case.

FINAL IMAGE: The striped socks sit together in the basket while the empty evidence tray waits for a larger case.
