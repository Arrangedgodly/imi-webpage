# The Telescope Demonstration Points at the Wrong Building

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: public science demonstrations and presenters who refuse to admit their equipment is aimed at a very ordinary object*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-015*

Cast: ASTRONOMER, an orangutan; HOST, a gibbon; VISITOR, a macaque; JANITOR, a baboon; TECH, a capuchin.

[A science-center rooftop. A telescope points toward a neighboring office building. The host gathers a small audience.]

HOST: Tonight we will view the moon.

VISITOR: The telescope is pointed at a window.

ASTRONOMER: The moon is behind the building.

TECH: The building is not moving.

ACT ONE

[The astronomer adjusts the tripod. The host invites the visitor to look through the eyepiece. The visitor sees a desk lamp and a person watering a plant.]

VISITOR: The moon has a fern.

HOST: It is a new crater.

[The tech checks the star chart. The tripod's compass points south, but the telescope barrel points east. A small level is wedged under one foot.]

TECH: The mount is tilted.

ASTRONOMER: It is calibrated for rooftop conditions.

JANITOR: The roof is flat.

[The janitor pushes a mop bucket around the tripod. The cable from the telescope catches on the bucket handle. The host asks the audience to wait while the tech frees it.]

HOST: Space takes patience.

VISITOR: So does this cable.

[The tech turns the telescope toward the sky. The astronomer points out a bright object. The visitor looks again and sees the building's rooftop warning light blinking.]

VISITOR: Is that a star?

ASTRONOMER: That is a beacon.

HOST: It is a local star.

[The janitor switches off the rooftop work light. The telescope image goes dark. The astronomer protests that this is part of the demonstration. The tech rotates the finder scope and discovers its cap is still on.]

TECH: We have been aiming through the cap.

[The host removes the cap and places it in a pocket. The astronomer realigns the telescope using the printed star chart. The visitor turns the tripod gently while the tech steadies the base.]

ACT TWO

[The telescope settles on the moon. Everyone takes turns viewing through the eyepiece. The janitor sees it, then wipes the eyepiece with a cloth. The astronomer asks him not to touch the lens.]

JANITOR: I cleaned the cap.

HOST: That was the cap?

TECH: It was the cover. The lens is clear.

[The visitor looks again and describes a crater. The audience finally sees the moon. The neighboring office worker closes the blinds.]

[The host asks the janitor to describe what he saw. The janitor says the moon has a bright edge and a dark mark near the middle. The astronomer asks him to stop improvising. The visitor checks the chart and finds the crater's name. The host repeats it with great ceremony.]

HOST: A remarkable discovery.

JANITOR: I saw it before the host did.

ASTRONOMER: You saw it through the telescope.

JANITOR: That is how looking works.

FINAL IMAGE: The group studies the moon while the dark office window reflects their small telescope.
