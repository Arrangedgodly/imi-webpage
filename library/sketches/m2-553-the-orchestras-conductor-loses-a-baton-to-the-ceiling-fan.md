# The Orchestra's Conductor Loses a Baton to the Ceiling Fan

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: formal orchestra rehearsals and conductors who mistake a ceiling fan's tempo for a musical cue*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-015*

Cast: CONDUCTOR, a baboon; VIOLINIST, a gibbon; PERCUSSIONIST, a capuchin; STAGE MANAGER, an orangutan; TECHNICIAN, a macaque.

[A rehearsal hall. A ceiling fan turns above the conductor's podium. The conductor raises a white baton. The orchestra waits.]

CONDUCTOR: We begin at the downbeat.

[The fan turns once. The conductor's baton slips from their hand and lands on the podium edge.]

PERCUSSIONIST: Is that the beat?

CONDUCTOR: That is gravity.

ACT ONE

[The technician switches off the fan. A warm light hums. The conductor starts the piece. The violin section enters early, following the stage manager who is counting silently from the wings.]

CONDUCTOR: Why did the strings enter?

VIOLINIST: You lowered your hand.

CONDUCTOR: I was reaching for the baton.

[The technician brings a step stool. The conductor reaches up to the fan and retrieves the baton, but the fan blade nudges it around. The baton slides through a grille and drops into a lighting tray.]

STAGE MANAGER: The baton has gone above the orchestra.

[The percussionist uses a mallet to tap the podium. The musicians begin to follow the tap. The conductor takes the mallet and tries to direct with it, but its head is too large to show a precise cue.]

PERCUSSIONIST: That is my mallet.

CONDUCTOR: It is now a conducting instrument.

[The technician secures the fan grille and turns the fan off at the wall switch. The stage manager brings a magnet on a pole. It cannot lift the wooden baton. The violinist suggests using a backstage ladder, but the ladder is already holding a curtain.]

STAGE MANAGER: We cannot climb the curtain.

[The technician removes the lighting tray and sets it on the floor. The baton lands inside. The conductor picks it up and checks that it is not cracked. The orchestra resets. The technician labels the fan switch DO NOT USE DURING REHEARSAL.]

[The conductor asks the orchestra to repeat the cutoff without a baton. The musicians stop at different times. The stage manager raises a red card; the percussionist stops first, then the strings, then the conductor. The technician hands back the wooden baton and marks a safe storage hook on the wall.]

CONDUCTOR: I can conduct without it.

VIOLINIST: We have just heard the alternative.

ACT TWO

[The conductor starts the piece again. The strings wait for a clear downbeat. The percussionist holds the mallet. The fan stays off. The first movement goes smoothly. At the end, the conductor gives a crisp cutoff and the musicians stop together.]

CONDUCTOR: Excellent.

[The technician turns the fan back on. The circulating air lifts the sheet music.]

VIOLINIST: We have a new movement.

FINAL IMAGE: The conductor pins the score down with the recovered baton while the fan keeps turning overhead.
