# The Wind-Up Toy Leads the Parade

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: ceremonial parades, overexcited announcers, and marchers who try to keep pace with a toy that cannot turn*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-014*

Cast: PARADE MARSHAL, a baboon; TOY MAKER, an orangutan; ANNOUNCER, a gibbon; DRUMMER, a macaque; CROWD HELPER, a capuchin.

[A small parade route loops around a town square. The toy maker winds a tin duck. The parade marshal holds a route map.]

ANNOUNCER: Leading today's procession is a handcrafted marching duck.

MARSHAL: It walks in a straight line for six feet.

TOY MAKER: It can turn if you pick it up and point it again.

[The duck waddles forward. The drummer starts a marching rhythm. The crowd helper walks beside the toy with a padded stop board.]

ANNOUNCER: The duck is taking the center lane.

MARSHAL: The duck is following the edge of the sidewalk.

[At the first corner, the duck continues straight toward a flower stall. The toy maker catches it before it reaches the table.]

ACT ONE

EXT. SQUARE - CONTINUOUS

The parade marshal asks the toy maker to wind the duck again and aim it along the marked route. The toy maker winds the spring carefully; the drummer taps a slower beat. The announcer calls out each step as if it were a championship lap.

ANNOUNCER: Five feet of determined waddling.

MARSHAL: Three feet. We started at the cone.

[The duck's key spins loose, but the toy maker catches it before it falls. The marshal pauses the parade so the key can be secured. The crowd helper holds up a sign reading PLEASE WAIT.]

TOY MAKER: It needs a shorter wind.

DRUMMER: I can keep the tempo gentle.

[The toy moves again. At the next corner, it heads toward a curb ramp. The crowd helper moves a soft barrier into place; the toy bumps it and stops.]

ANNOUNCER: A tactical pause.

MARSHAL: A barrier.

ACT TWO

EXT. ROUTE TURN - LATER

The marshal considers carrying the toy around the loop. The toy maker says the duck can do the straight stretch from the fountain to the finish arch. The parade route can be adjusted so it has no sharp turn. The announcer asks whether this counts as a parade.

MARSHAL: The route is marked. We can move the finish arch to the straight path.

ANNOUNCER: The duck has chosen a new course.

TOY MAKER: We chose it. The duck is a wind-up toy.

[The drummer changes to a simple beat. The crowd helper clears a small gap in the rope so the toy has a flat lane. The toy waddles forward, passing the fountain. The announcer starts a victory speech, then sees the finish arch is still twenty feet away.]

ANNOUNCER: The triumph is still in progress.

[The toy's spring slows. It stops before the arch. The toy maker winds it once, using the key's marked quarter turn. The marshal checks that the path remains clear.]

MARSHAL: Ready when the toy is ready.

[The toy moves the remaining distance and reaches the finish arch. The drummer gives one clear final beat. The crowd applauds without moving into the lane.]

ACT THREE

EXT. FINISH ARCH - AFTERNOON

The parade marshal declares the wind-up duck has completed its route. The toy maker picks it up only after the spring stops. The announcer asks for a second lap so the crowd can get another photo. The marshal points out the procession is complete.

ANNOUNCER: One lap, one finish, one very small athlete.

TOY MAKER: It is going back in its box.

DRUMMER: I can play the closing beat for the box.

[The toy maker sets the duck inside a padded case and places the key in a small pocket. The crowd helper takes down the rope after the marshal clears the lane.]

MARSHAL: The route is open again.

[The announcer stops narrating. The crowd crosses the square; the duck stays packed away.]

FINAL IMAGE: The wind-up duck rests in its case beneath the lowered finish arch.
