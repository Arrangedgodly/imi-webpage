# A Hat for Every Weather

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: fashion demonstrations and local weather segments that present accessories as emergency equipment*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: HOST, a gibbon; DESIGNER, a macaque; FORECASTER, an orangutan; RUNNER, a capuchin; FAN OPERATOR, a baboon.

[A studio runway. A weather map and a rack of hats share one spotlight.]

HOST: Today's forecast is mild with a chance of hats.

FORECASTER: The forecast is clear.

DESIGNER: Then the hats will be decorative.

HOST: We never call them decorative. We call them climate-ready.

[The designer models a wide straw hat. It hides the weather map.]

FORECASTER: I cannot see the high temperature.

DESIGNER: The brim is providing shade.

HOST: We have solved summer.

[The runner brings a wool hat. The host pulls it down over the forecast microphone.]

HOST: This model traps warmth and commentary.

FORECASTER: I need the microphone back.

[The host removes the hat. The wind machine starts too early, sending the straw hat across the runway. It lands on the runner's head.]

RUNNER: I am wearing the forecast.

DESIGNER: You have an excellent profile.

HOST: The wind is southwest and fashionable.

FAN OPERATOR: Test breeze!

FORECASTER: We did not give the cue. The fan is aimed at the map, so it is not a weather reading.

[The forecaster moves the map. It rolls up. The designer uses the wool hat to hold it flat.]

DESIGNER: This hat has a second function.

HOST: It is now a map weight.

FORECASTER: Please do not call it a barometer.

[The host tries a rain hat with a clear visor. The visor fogs from the stage lights.]

HOST: I can see the future.

RUNNER: You can see your breath.

DESIGNER: That is the fog-resistant lining.

FORECASTER: It's fogging indoors.

[The fan operator kicks the wind machine on again. The visor hat blows backward and gets caught on the weather map stand. The map stops curling.]

HOST: We have discovered a way to secure the forecast.

FORECASTER: We have discovered that the stand has a hook.

[The runner puts the straw hat on the hook and the wool hat on the map corner. The designer checks the runway.]

DESIGNER: We have one model left: a cap with ear flaps.

HOST: Does it improve reception?

FORECASTER: It covers the ears.

HOST: Then the wearer cannot hear the forecast.

[The host puts on the cap. The forecaster gives the forecast. The host nods as if hearing.]

FORECASTER: Clear sky, light wind, and no hats in the forecast.

HOST: I agree. It will be a beautiful hat day.

[The designer sends the runner down the runway with the map hats. The wind machine is still on; the straw brim flips up and shades one eye.]

RUNNER: The forecast is partly covered.

FORECASTER: The wind is coming from the machine, not the west.

The host turns the fan off. The hats stop moving, and the map becomes readable. The designer adjusts the straw hat's strap and hangs the rain visor on the rack instead of using it as a wind gauge.

DESIGNER: We have weather-ready accessories.

FORECASTER: We have hats and a studio fan.

[The host removes the cap and hands it to the runner.]



[The designer sends the runner down the runway with the map hats. The runner reaches the wind machine; the straw brim flips up and shades only one eye.]

RUNNER: The forecast is partly covered.

FORECASTER: The wind is coming from the machine, not the west.

The host turns the fan off. The hats stop moving, and the map is readable again. The designer adjusts the strap on the straw hat and hangs the rain visor on the rack instead of using it as a wind gauge.

DESIGNER: We have weather-ready accessories.

FORECASTER: We have hats and a studio fan.

FINAL IMAGE: Three hats hold down the map while the actual forecast remains visible above them.
