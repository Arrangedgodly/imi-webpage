# The One-Note Choir Has a Solo

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: choir auditions, musical direction, and performers who insist a single sustained note deserves a solo*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-015*

Cast: CHOIR DIRECTOR, a gibbon; SOLOIST, a baboon; PIANIST, an orangutan; AUDITIONER, a capuchin; STAGE MANAGER, a macaque.

[A rehearsal room. A choir stands in rows. The pianist plays a chord. The soloist holds one note.]

CHOIR DIRECTOR: That is the note. Now resolve it.

SOLOIST: I am resolving it slowly.

AUDITIONER: The audition is three minutes long.

SOLOIST: I can hold it for four.

ACT ONE

[The choir director asks the pianist to play a cadence. The pianist plays three chords. The soloist keeps the same note through all three.]

PIANIST: The chord changed.

SOLOIST: I noticed.

CHOIR DIRECTOR: You have not moved.

SOLOIST: I am committed.

[The stage manager brings a glass of water. The soloist signals for it without stopping the note. The choir director tells everyone to breathe.]

CHOIR DIRECTOR: We need a breath mark.

SOLOIST: I have a hidden breath.

[The soloist takes a breath through the nose and continues the same note. The pianist stops. The note remains.]

AUDITIONER: Is the note coming from the room?

[The choir director asks the soloist to step away from the microphone. The note gets quieter but does not stop. The pianist inspects the sheet music and finds a small note that says "repeat until signaled."]

PIANIST: The score has no ending.

STAGE MANAGER: The director has to signal.

[The choir director raises both hands to cue an ending. The soloist continues. The choir director drops one hand. The soloist continues.]

SOLOIST: Your cue was ambiguous.

CHOIR DIRECTOR: I lowered both hands.

SOLOIST: I thought that was the beginning of a bow.

[The choir director asks the stage manager to switch off the microphone. The amplifier clicks off; the note stops. The soloist looks surprised.]

[The stage manager places a red flag beside the music stand. The soloist asks whether it means "stop" or "continue louder." The director explains that the flag marks the written rest. The pianist plays the passage once, and the soloist stops exactly at the flag.]

SOLOIST: I have found the ending.

PIANIST: It was on the page.

[The director pins the flag to the score so it cannot slide under the piano bench again. The soloist tests the rest silently.]

ACT TWO

[The choir director chooses a new arrangement with a written rest and a final chord. The pianist plays the introduction. The soloist sings one note, stops at the rest, then joins the choir on the final chord.]

CHOIR DIRECTOR: That was the ending.

SOLOIST: I can add an encore.

[The director points to the clock. The audition time is over. The soloist nods and takes a sip of water.]

FINAL IMAGE: The pianist closes the score while the soloist silently mouths one last note.
