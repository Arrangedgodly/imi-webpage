# The Lost Language of Signs

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: heritage tours that overinterpret simple wayfinding signs*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-010*

Cast: guide Pava, a gibbon; historian Odo, a baboon; visitor Kito, a macaque; sign painter Nara, a capuchin.

EXT. GARDEN PATH - DAY

Pava stands before a wooden arrow marked "POND."

PAVA: Scholars debate the meaning of this ancient symbol.

KITO: It points to the pond.

ODO: The inscription is unmistakable.

Nara says she painted the sign last week. Pava says the tradition may be older than the paint. Odo examines the arrow. It points toward the pond.

Kito asks if there is a longer version of the tour. Pava says the arrow is a gateway into the community's relationship with water. Nara asks if the visitor wants directions. Kito does.

Pava leads him north. They arrive at the pond. Odo asks if the destination confirms his interpretation. Kito says it confirms the arrow worked.

Nara reveals a second sign, marked "EXIT." Pava calls it an invitation to leave. Odo says it may mean "return to the outer world." Kito says the path goes back to the village. Nara says that is why she painted the sign.

The group finds a faded old sign that says "MUD." Pava says it may mark a historic crossing. Odo steps onto the path and slips slightly. The ground is muddy.

KITO: The sign is still useful.

Pava asks Nara to preserve the old sign. Nara says she will repaint it so visitors can read it. Odo suggests adding a plaque about the region's relationship with wet earth. Nara says she can paint "MUD" in larger letters.

Pava invites Kito to read the signs in order. He follows POND to the pond, MUD across the wet stretch, and BENCH to a seat beneath the tree. Odo asks if the sequence represents a journey. Kito says it tells him where to walk. Nara shows them a sign she has not yet painted. It has a blank arrow pointing toward the shed. Pava asks what the blank means. Nara says she has not decided what the shed is called. Odo proposes "The Repository of Seasonal Implements." Kito says "Shed" will fit on the board. Nara paints SHED in large letters and hangs it at the fork.

Odo asks if he can take the old MUD sign as a souvenir. Nara says it belongs outside where it is useful. Pava asks if the museum would accept a photograph instead. Kito takes one with the pond in the background. The sign remains in place for the next walker.

Pava asks for the next visitor to try the route.

Kito follows it back to the pond.

Nara checks that the pond arrow remains visible.

The sign stays put.

TAG

Pava points to a sign marked "BENCH."

KITO: I understand.

He sits down.
