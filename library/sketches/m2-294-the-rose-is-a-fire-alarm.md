# The Rose Is a Fire Alarm

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: high-society costume dramas, elaborate ballroom entrances, and etiquette rules that make a simple alarm impossible to reach*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-009*

Cast: Hostess Vela, a gibbon; guests Luma, a capuchin, and Kett, a macaque; attendant Runi, a ring-tailed lemur; and the ambassador, a baboon.

INT. GRAND BALLROOM - NIGHT

A string quartet plays. Vela welcomes guests in full period dress. A red fire alarm is visible behind a silk rose arrangement.

VELA: The evening's first dance begins after the ambassador's entrance.

KETT: Which ambassador?

VELA: The one arriving through the left door.

LUMA: There are two left doors.

Runi adjusts a place card. The rose arrangement tilts, covering the alarm. Vela tells everyone to remain composed.

VELA: If anything unexpected occurs, press the red alarm.

KETT: Is that the red flower?

VELA: No, the red box behind it.

A guest's feathered hat catches on the curtain. The curtain pulls a candelabra sideways. Runi catches it, but the candles wobble. Luma sees smoke from a fallen candle.

LUMA: There is a small fire.

VELA: We must maintain decorum.

KETT: We should use the alarm.

VELA: The ambassador has not arrived.

Luma lifts the rose arrangement with her tail, revealing the alarm. She presses it. A bell rings and the ballroom lights turn on. Guests stop dancing and look at the smoke. Runi smothers it with a heavy velvet curtain.

VELA: The alarm has interrupted the program.

LUMA: The program is now safe.

Kett opens the windows. Fresh air clears the smoke. The quartet stops playing, then starts a soft tune again. Vela asks whether they can continue the dance.

RUNI: After we check the candles.

VELA: The evening was planned to the minute.

KETT: The fire was not on the schedule.

The ambassador enters through the right door, carrying a fire extinguisher. Everyone bows. He sees the alarm and asks who pressed it.

LUMA: I did.

AMBASSADOR: Good. The candle is out.

VELA: We are pleased the evening has returned to order.

RUNI: The rose should stay away from the alarm.

Vela removes the flower arrangement and sets it on a table. The guests resume dancing, now under full light. The quartet plays faster than before.

KETT: Is this still the first dance?

VELA: It is the second first dance.

The ambassador offers his arm to Luma, who leads him around the candle-free ballroom. Vela moves the table of flowers farther away from the wall.

The quartet attempts the original first-dance cue. Its music stand is behind the alarm now, so the players move it to the center of the room. One musician reads the wrong page and begins a march. The guests line up as if to parade.

VELA: That is not the dance we rehearsed.

KETT: It is the dance we can do under full light.

Luma leads the first turn. The guests follow, leaving the rose arrangement far from the alarm and the candles safely on their trays.
