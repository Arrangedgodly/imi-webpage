# The Last Page Turn

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: book-club discussion shows and literary prestige rituals*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-003*

Cast: HOST ORA, a lemur; CRITIC BELL, a macaque; READER JUNE, a capuchin; and PRODUCER VEX, a gibbon.

[A studio circle. Each seat has a book and a small brass bell. The audience holds copies of a novel titled The Branch at the End of Everything.]

ORA: Tonight we discuss the final chapter. No spoilers before the bell.

JUNE: The book ends on page eighty-two.

BELL: The ending is a refusal to end.

VEX: We need forty minutes of discussion.

JUNE: There are only twelve pages after the middle.

BELL: A middle is a state of mind.

[Ora rings the bell. Bell opens the book to a blank page.] 

ORA: Critic Bell, what does the blank page mean?

BELL: It asks whether the reader has brought enough of themselves.

JUNE: I brought the book.

BELL: Then you brought a boundary.

VEX: Good. Let's pause for the sponsored bookmark.

JUNE: The bookmark covers the last sentence.

VEX: It creates suspense.

[June removes it. A pressed leaf falls from the book and lands on Bell's notes.] 

BELL: A leaf has entered the criticism.

ORA: Was it planted by the author?

JUNE: It fell out of the copy.

BELL: Accidental symbolism is still symbolism.

JUNE: It's a leaf.

[The audience raises their hands. Ora selects one reader.] 

READER: I liked the part where the monkey went to the orchard.

BELL: Which monkey?

READER: The monkey on page eleven.

BELL: That is the author's preface.

READER: It was the best part.

[Vex signals for a close-up. The camera zooms on the pressed leaf, then on the empty chair.] 

ORA: Our conversation has become a visual essay.

JUNE: I would like to talk about the ending.

BELL: What if there is no ending?

JUNE: Then why is there a last page?

BELL: To test our attachment to sequence.

JUNE: I turned it.

[She flips to the back cover. A small note says RETURN TO THE LIBRARY BY FRIDAY.] 

JUNE: The book's final instruction is about returning it.

ORA: That is concrete.

BELL: Concrete things can be profound.

JUNE: It is a due date.

[The audience laughs. Bell tries to ring the brass bell, but June's copy has the bookmark lodged beneath it. The bell produces a muffled thunk.] 

BELL: Even the bell has chosen ambiguity.

JUNE: It has a bookmark stuck under it.

[She removes the bookmark and puts it in the book. Bell rings the bell. It rings clearly.]

ORA: We have found our ending.

VEX: Can we keep talking until the credits?

JUNE: We could read the last sentence again.

BELL: Which one?

JUNE: The one on page eighty-two.

[She reads silently. The others wait, attempting not to interpret her face.]

ORA: And?

JUNE: It says, "The end."

BELL: A daring choice.

[Ora closes the book. The audience closes theirs in a wave. Vex looks at the clock.] 

VEX: We have reached the credits early.

JUNE: That's what happens when a book ends.

[The bell rings once as the camera cuts to the return-by-Friday note.]
