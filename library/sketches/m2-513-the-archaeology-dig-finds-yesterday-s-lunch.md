# The Archaeology Dig Finds Yesterday's Lunch

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: archaeological field documentaries, careful artifact handling, and teams assigning ancient significance to recent objects*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-014*

Cast: LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST, a baboon; INTERN, a macaque; DOCUMENTARIAN, an orangutan; GROUNDSKEEPER, a gibbon; STUDENT, a capuchin.

[A school garden. A square of soil is marked with string. The documentarian crouches with a camera as the archaeologist holds a small brush.]

DOCUMENTARIAN: We have uncovered a layer that may change everything.

INTERN: It is a paper napkin.

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: Do not rush the interpretation.

[The intern brushes soil away. A sandwich wrapper appears beside the napkin.]

STUDENT: That is from yesterday's lunch.

DOCUMENTARIAN: The student has a theory.

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: We must test it.

[The archaeologist places the wrapper on a clean tray. The groundskeeper checks the garden log.]

GROUNDSKEEPER: The class had lunch here yesterday. The bin was full.

STUDENT: I dropped the wrapper, then picked it up.

INTERN: This is a shallow layer.

ACT ONE

[The documentarian asks for a close-up. The intern brushes the wrapper. A plastic fork emerges beneath it.]

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: A utensil.

STUDENT: I dropped that too.

DOCUMENTARIAN: The site's layers are speaking.

GROUNDSKEEPER: The garden has been used for lunch all week.

[The archaeologist measures the wrapper with a ruler. The intern records its length on a field card.]

INTERN: Object is eight inches long. Material is crinkly.

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: Mark it as modern until we confirm.

[The student points to a tiny stone beneath the string.]

STUDENT: That one might be older.

[The intern brushes around it. It is a pebble lodged in the soil. The archaeologist sets it beside the wrapper.]

DOCUMENTARIAN: A stone and a wrapper. A meeting of eras.

GROUNDSKEEPER: The pebble was in the path.

[The documentarian asks the student to reenact the lunch drop. The student picks up a fresh wrapper from the bin and starts walking backward toward the square.]

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: Stop. We are not adding new material to the site.

[The groundskeeper moves the bin away from the dig. The documentarian lowers the camera.]

DOCUMENTARIAN: Can we call this a discovery?

INTERN: We discovered a wrapper.

[The documentarian asks for a label that will fit on screen. The intern writes "YESTERDAY" on a card, then adds "LUNCH" beneath it. The groundskeeper points out that the garden log gives an exact date. The documentarian zooms in on the card until the lettering fills the frame.]

ACT TWO

[The archaeologist completes the field card and bags the wrapper and fork for disposal, not the museum. The student places the pebble back by the path. The groundskeeper fills the shallow hole with soil.]

LEAD ARCHAEOLOGIST: The record will say "recent lunch litter, recovered and removed."

DOCUMENTARIAN: That is a short title.

GROUNDSKEEPER: It is accurate.

[The intern takes the measuring ruler to the supply box. The documentarian packs the camera. The student points to a fresh napkin drifting toward the newly cleared square.]

FINAL IMAGE: The archaeologist catches the napkin before it touches the soil and labels it "not yet excavated."
