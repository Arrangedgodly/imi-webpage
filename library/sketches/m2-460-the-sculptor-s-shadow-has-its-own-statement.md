# The Sculptor's Shadow Has Its Own Statement

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: contemporary gallery openings, artist statements, and critics who assign meaning to shadows before asking the sculptor*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: SCULPTOR, a macaque; CURATOR, an orangutan; CRITIC, a baboon; LIGHT TECH, a gibbon; VISITOR, a capuchin.

[A gallery. A small sculpture sits under a bright lamp. Its shadow stretches across the wall.] 

CRITIC: The shadow expresses exile.

SCULPTOR: It is the shadow of a wooden spoon.

CURATOR: We have not printed the statement yet.

VISITOR: I thought the long shape was part of the work.

[The light tech nudges the lamp. The shadow moves. The critic follows it across the wall.] 

CRITIC: The exile is in motion.

LIGHT TECH: The lamp moved six inches.

SCULPTOR: I carved the spoon to show a handle, a bowl, and a notch. The lamp makes the notch look like a second head.

CURATOR: That could be useful.

SCULPTOR: It is a notch.

[The curator places a placard beside the sculpture. It reads "Untitled: The Silence of Absence."]

SCULPTOR: That is not the title.

CURATOR: The title card was still at the printer.

CRITIC: The absence of the title is part of the statement.

VISITOR: What is the title?

SCULPTOR: Spoon With a Notch.

[The critic looks disappointed. The light tech rotates the lamp until the shadow shortens. The spoon's notch disappears from the wall.] 

CRITIC: The exile has returned.

SCULPTOR: The lamp moved.

[The visitor bends to look at the carving itself. The critic bends too, blocking the label.] 

VISITOR: May I see the notch?

SCULPTOR: Yes. It is on this edge. The notch lets the spoon hang on a hook.

CURATOR: We could put a hook in the gallery.

SCULPTOR: It belongs in a kitchen.

[The curator tries to hang it on the wall. The spoon falls, but the light tech catches it and places it on a low shelf.] 

LIGHT TECH: The shelf is level.

CRITIC: The work has descended.

SCULPTOR: The hook was the wrong size.

[The curator prints a new card: "Spoon With a Notch. Wood. Made to hang beside a kitchen sink." The visitor reads it. The critic reads it too, then steps aside so the sculpture is visible.] 

VISITOR: I understand how it works.

CRITIC: I will revise my statement.

SCULPTOR: You can keep the shadow in your notes.

[The light tech fixes the lamp so the shadow falls beneath the shelf, not across the label.]


The curator asks the sculptor to speak with the visitors. The sculptor says the notch came from a hook in the studio kitchen; it makes the wooden spoon easy to store. The critic asks whether the shadow was planned. The sculptor says the light was placed by the gallery, but the shape can still be interesting.

SCULPTOR: I am happy for visitors to notice the shadow. I just want them to know it moves when the lamp moves.

The light tech demonstrates by shifting the lamp a few inches. The shadow changes, but the spoon stays still. The visitor turns the card around to read the materials. The critic waits until she finishes before adding a note.

FINAL IMAGE: The spoon hangs from a proper hook while its small shadow rests below the plain title card.
