# The Water Tasting Has a Spit Bucket

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: luxury tasting panels that describe ordinary water with elaborate flavor language*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-011*

Cast: Soro, a macaque host; Tani, a capuchin judge; Ove, a gibbon water server; Pina, a lemur contestant.

INT. TASTING ROOM - EVENING

[FOUR GLASSES OF WATER SIT ON A LONG TABLE. A SPIT BUCKET IS PLACED BESIDE EACH JUDGE.]

SORO: Tonight we compare four spring waters.

TANI: Are they all water?

OVE: Yes.

PINA: How will we tell them apart?

Soro hands out score cards. The first category is "nose." Pina leans toward a glass and inhales.

PINA: It smells clean.

TANI: That's a start. Look for notes of stone, leaf, or distant rain.

Pina smells again.

PINA: It still smells clean.

Soro calls that entry "fresh and uncomplicated." Tani tastes the first glass and swirls it.

TANI: The finish is surprisingly long.

Ove says the glass is full. Tani says she means the taste. Pina asks which spring it came from. Ove checks the bottles. Each one is labeled "Water, Sample."

SORO: The source has been withheld to avoid bias.

PINA: Then how can we know which is best?

TANI: By the water.

The judges taste each sample. Tani makes notes: "soft," "mineral," "bright," and "round." Pina writes "wet" in every box.

SORO: You need to separate the samples.

PINA: They all disappear when I swallow.

Ove pours a fifth glass from the rinse pitcher by mistake. Pina tastes it and declares it "exceptionally crisp." Tani says it has a faint soap note. Ove checks the pitcher; it was used to rinse the glasses.

TANI: We have discovered a fifth sample.

Soro asks if the judges need the spit buckets. Tani says no. Pina asks if she should use hers. Soro says it is only for palate clearing. Pina takes a sip of water and spits it into the bucket.

PINA: That clears the palate.

The sip makes a tiny splash in the bucket. Ove realizes the four official samples all came from the same tap pitcher; only the fifth glass came from the rinse pitcher. Soro looks at the sample cards and sees four different labels.

SORO: We may have tasted one source four times.

TANI: The descriptions were different.

PINA: The glasses were different.

Ove opens the bottle rack. Each bottle has a different colored cap, but all came from the same tap. Soro asks whether the colors signal different mineral balances. Ove says one cap was chosen by the delivery crew and the others came from the supply drawer.

TANI: The label can affect the tasting.

PINA: The water cannot read it.

Tani asks for a blind test. Ove turns the sample cards face down, pours fresh tasting portions into identical glasses, and shuffles their order while the judges look away. The judges taste them again. They pick the same sample as their favorite.

Ove points out that the judges all ranked the rinse-pitcher sample highest.

[THE JUDGES LOOK AT THE SPIT BUCKETS. PINA PUTS A SCORE OF FIVE IN EVERY BOX. SORO AWARDS THE RINSE-PITCHER SAMPLE BEST WATER FOR ITS REMARKABLE SOAP FINISH.]
