# The Pigeon Runs the Lost-and-Found

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: municipal lost-and-found counters, overly detailed claim checks, and a pigeon who keeps reclaiming ordinary street objects*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-014*

Cast: CLERK, a baboon; PIGEON, a capuchin; PARK WORKER, a macaque; VISITOR, an orangutan; WITNESS, a gibbon.

[A park kiosk. A counter holds a lost mitten, a folded map, and a shiny bottle cap. The clerk opens the window. A pigeon lands on the counter.] 

CLERK: Lost-and-found is open.

PIGEON: I am here to claim the bottle cap.

CLERK: Can you describe it?

PIGEON: Round, silver, and it fits my foot exactly.

[The clerk slides the cap across the counter. The pigeon taps it twice, then places one foot inside.] 

VISITOR: That's clearly yours.

CLERK: I still need a matching description.

[The park worker arrives carrying a small shopping basket with three more bottle caps.] 

WORKER: I picked these up near the fountain. They may all belong to the same bird.

PIGEON: They are not mine.

CLERK: You just claimed one.

PIGEON: That one has a dent on the rim.

[The witness examines the caps and finds the dent. The clerk returns the correct cap to the pigeon and places the others in a tray.] 

WITNESS: The pigeon knows its property.

CLERK: What about the mitten?

PIGEON: Not mine.

[The visitor checks the mitten's tag. It has a name stitched inside. The clerk places it in a labeled bag and sets the map on the counter.] 

VISITOR: That map is mine. I left it on the bench.

CLERK: Where did you last see it?

VISITOR: On the bench by the fountain.

WORKER: I found it under the bench.

[The clerk hands the map over. The pigeon carries the bottle cap to a low shelf and sets it beside a small collection of caps. The park worker asks whether all the caps have been claimed.] 

PIGEON: Not those. They are for the next bird.

CLERK: Then I will log them as unclaimed.

[The pigeon pecks the counter twice, as if stamping a form. The clerk reaches for the bottle cap, but the visitor points out it has already been returned.] 

VISITOR: That one is no longer lost.

CLERK: Correct. Claim complete.

[The clerk shuts the window briefly to update the ledger. The pigeon waits outside, not on the counter. When the clerk opens it again, a new feather lies on the sill.] 

WORKER: Is that lost?

PIGEON: It is attached to me.

[The clerk marks the feather as not an item. The visitor folds the recovered map and leaves for the park path.] 

[The clerk writes a small receipt for the returned map and hands it to the visitor. The visitor checks that it shows the fountain path. The pigeon turns the bottle cap once, then settles beside it.] 

CLERK: The map is claimed and the cap is returned.

WITNESS: That is two resolved items.

WORKER: The other caps can stay in the tray.

[The clerk closes the lost-and-found ledger. No one takes the mitten, and it remains in its labeled bag for the owner.] 

FINAL IMAGE: The pigeon guards its dented bottle cap on the shelf while the clerk returns the unclaimed caps to the tray.
