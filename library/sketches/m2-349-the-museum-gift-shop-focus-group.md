# The Museum Gift Shop Focus Group

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: focus groups that ask customers to invent uses for products nobody wants*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-010*

Cast: facilitator Piri, a gibbon; shopper Kito, a macaque; shopper Odo, a baboon; designer Nara, a capuchin.

INT. MUSEUM ROOM - DAY

Piri presents a small wooden spoon with the museum logo carved into the handle.

PIRI: What would you use this for?

KITO: Stirring.

NARA: We hoped for a more imaginative answer.

Odo says it could be used as a bookmark. Nara points out that it is curved and would fall out of most books. Piri asks what emotion the spoon inspires. Kito says hunger.

The group looks at a mockup of a display box. The spoon is placed on velvet under a tiny light. Odo asks how to remove it. Nara says the box is sealed for presentation. Kito says a spoon should be usable. Piri asks if the word "usable" has positive associations.

NARA: We need to know what visitors would buy.

KITO: I would buy a normal spoon.

Odo suggests selling it as a souvenir of the museum cafe. Nara says the cafe already sells regular spoons. Piri asks whether the logo makes it feel special. Kito says the logo is on the handle, where his thumb covers it.

Nara proposes adding a ribbon so the spoon can be worn as a pendant. Odo tries it around his neck. It swings and hits the table. The group agrees it is too heavy for jewelry.

Piri asks if anyone would display it at home. Kito says no. Odo says maybe on a shelf if the box opens. Nara considers removing the velvet. The group watches as she takes the spoon out and stirs a cup of tea.

KITO: That works.

PIRI: The focus group has changed the product.

NARA: We can sell it as a spoon.

Piri asks Nara whether the logo can be made smaller so it does not cover the handle. Nara erases the large mark from the mockup and places a tiny stamp on the back. Kito asks if it will still be a souvenir. Piri says it can remind visitors where they ate. Odo tries the spoon in a bowl of soup; it is too small for serving but works for stirring tea. The group decides to sell it near the cafe, not in a glass case. Nara writes a new product card: "Wooden tea spoon, made in the workshop." Piri asks if the description needs more feeling. Kito says it needs a price. Odo suggests a price that includes the box. Nara removes the box from the budget and keeps the spoon simple.

The tea is served in plain cups.

Nara pours tea.

TAG

Odo checks the display box. It is empty.

ODO: Now it is an exhibit about the absence of a spoon.
