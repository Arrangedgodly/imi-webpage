# The Reservation for the Empty Table

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: upscale restaurant service, elaborate reservation rituals, and menus that describe a chair as an experience*
*By: IMI Sketch Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-009*

Cast: Host Vela, a gibbon; server Nari, a macaque; diner Odo, a capuchin; and chef Palla, an orangutan.

INT. RESTAURANT - EVENING

Odo arrives alone. Vela consults a leather book.

VELA: Your reservation is for one at the quiet table.

ODO: Is the quiet table available?

VELA: It is occupied by a candle.

Odo looks. A candle sits on an empty table. Vela leads him to a table by the kitchen.

NARI: Tonight's menu features the sound of a pan, the aroma of thyme, and a chair from the north wing.

ODO: Can I order the soup?

NARI: The soup is a conversation between squash and water.

ODO: I would like both sides of that conversation.

Palla appears from the kitchen and says the soup is ready. Nari describes it as a warm welcome in a bowl. She pours it. A single crouton floats in the middle.

ODO: Is the crouton the guest of honor?

PALLA: It is the only one that survived the oven.

Vela brings the candle from the quiet table and places it beside Odo. The candle is taller than his glass.

ODO: Now the table is quieter.

Vela moves the candle to the floor. Nari returns with bread, describing its crust as a map of the baker's hand. Odo takes a bite.

ODO: It is bread.

NARI: We can also call it bread.

Odo asks for the bill. Nari brings a card that says "A suggested contribution toward the evening." No price is listed.

ODO: How much is the soup?

NARI: The soup is eight coins.

ODO: The card says the evening.

NARI: The evening is twelve coins.

ODO: It is only seven o'clock.

Palla enters with a corrected bill. She says the restaurant charges for the meal, not for time spent in the chair. Vela removes the candle from the floor so no one trips. Odo pays for soup and bread, then asks for a glass of water.

NARI: Still or sparkling?

ODO: Tap.

NARI: From the kitchen tap?

ODO: From any tap.

Palla brings a glass of water. Nari presents it as "a clear note between courses." Odo drinks it in one sip.

VELA: Would you like to see the dessert menu?

ODO: Is the menu empty?

NARI: It has three desserts.

ODO: Then I will have one.

Palla serves a small pear with a spoon. Odo eats it. The server asks whether the pear changed his evening.

ODO: It made me full.

The staff clear the table. Vela offers Odo the quiet table, now that the candle has been moved.

ODO: I will leave while it is still available.

Nari writes "quiet table" in the reservation book. The candle is returned to the host stand, where it gets its own place card.

He exits. The candle remains on a shelf, alone at its reservation.
