# The Moon Prop Has Its Own Cue

*Format: Comedy sketch*
*Parody target: theatrical cue calling, overcomplicated stage machinery, and productions that treat a simple prop as a principal performer*
*By: IMI Sketch Monkey Desk*
*Batch: IMI-M2-SKETCHES-013*

Cast: STAGE MANAGER, a gibbon; ACTOR, a macaque; MOON OPERATOR, an orangutan; DIRECTOR, a baboon; INTERN, a capuchin.

[A small theater. A paper moon hangs from a pulley. The stage manager holds a cue sheet.]

STAGE MANAGER: Stand by moon rise.

MOON OPERATOR: Moon ready.

ACTOR: I am ready.

DIRECTOR: The moon is not ready. It needs a more personal cue.

INTERN: It is made of paper.

DIRECTOR: It has presence.

[The stage manager calls the cue. The moon rises, spins, then swings over the actor's head.]

ACTOR: Is this the moon or a ceiling fan?

MOON OPERATOR: It is catching the side draft.

DIRECTOR: The moon is emotionally unsettled.

[The actor holds out a hand. The moon passes just beyond it.]

ACTOR: I cannot reach it.

DIRECTOR: The distance represents longing.

STAGE MANAGER: The rope is too short.

[The operator lowers the moon. The intern adds a second string to stop the spin. The moon now turns in the other direction.]

INTERN: It has two opposing tides.

DIRECTOR: Beautiful.

ACTOR: I am still waiting for the scene.

[The stage manager reads the script. The actor is supposed to say one line when the moon appears.]

ACTOR: "The moon is up."

DIRECTOR: We could cut that. The audience can see it.

STAGE MANAGER: They cannot see it if it is facing the wall.

[The operator rotates the moon to face out. Its blank back faces the audience. The director asks for a painted crater. The intern draws a circle with a marker.]

DIRECTOR: It needs texture.

INTERN: That is a circle.

ACTOR: The moon is up.

[The moon does not move. The operator's rope has caught on a pulley. The stage manager calls hold. The actor repeats the line; the moon remains still.] 

DIRECTOR: The moon is withholding.

MOON OPERATOR: The rope is stuck behind the beam.

[The operator frees the rope. The moon lowers to the stage. The actor holds it by the rim, and the intern removes the extra string. The stage manager ties the original rope to a marked stop so the prop cannot swing into the actor.] 

STAGE MANAGER: Stand by moon rise.

MOON OPERATOR: Ready.

ACTOR: I am ready.

DIRECTOR: Give it a personal cue.

STAGE MANAGER: Cue moon.

[The moon rises straight, stops at the mark, and faces the audience. The actor points.] 

ACTOR: The moon is up.

DIRECTOR: Hold for applause.

[No one applauds. The actor lowers the moon safely into the operator's hands.] 

ACTOR: The moon is down.

[The intern checks the stop knot, then points to the cue sheet. The operator nods and keeps both hands on the rope.]

INTERN: The next rise will wait for the stage manager.

STAGE MANAGER: And the actor.

FINAL IMAGE: The paper moon rests on its marked hook while the actor exits through the open curtain.
